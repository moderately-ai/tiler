## User-visible outcome

A program can state that a `[T, 1024]` activation contracts against a `[3072, 1024]` weight over the last axis of both — 197 of the pinned workload's 253 contraction occurrences — and the compiler either accepts the structure or refuses it under a named rule. Today no contraction can be stated at all.

## What ADR 0087 already settled, and what this ticket owes

**Fact.** [ADR 0087](../docs/decisions/0087-model-contraction-as-one-keyed-family-with-an-index-structure.md) accepts one keyed family whose node carries a renaming-invariant canonical index structure, with five structural rules rejecting at construction under their own names and an unsupported structure failing closed at capability resolution. It authorizes the *shape* of that admission and not its start; this ticket is the start, bounded to one index structure.

**Proposal — the profile, from the [L3 realization record](../docs/research/scheduling/first-metal-contraction-realizations.md).** `td,od->to` over `[M, K] x [N, K] -> [M, N]`, F32 operands, accumulator, and result. The canonical encoding by first appearance is operand 0 = `(0, 1)`, operand 1 = `(2, 1)`, output = `(0, 2)`, contracted = `{1}`. **Note that this is not `[M, K] x [K, N]`**: the checkpoint stores every projection weight `[out_features, in_features]`, so the contracted index is the last axis of both operands, and a key shaped for the ordinary matmul would require a transposing `Reindex` on all 197 occurrences.

## Required delivery

- **The key and its inference routine.** One governed `OpKey` in the standard registry, with an `infer` that rejects each of the five structural rules separately — an output index in no operand; a summed index in one operand; an index repeated within one operand; a duplicated output index or an output order that is not a permutation of the free indices; an index in more than two operands — as typed provider diagnostics naming which rule, at construction, never as a generic invalidity.
- **The canonical encoding, with its mutation proof.** ADR 0087 item 1 requires the encoder to be domain-separated, exhaustively encoded, and *mutation-proved*: a perturbation that makes two distinct structures encode equally, or one structure encode two ways, must be demonstrated failing before the encoder is trusted. `ab,cb->ac` and `td,od->to` must produce identical bytes; `td,do->to` must not. The `tiler::strict-serial-sum-f32@1` reduced-axis attribute in `crates/tiler-ir/src/semantic/registry.rs` is the precedent for where the normalization lives.
- **Extent agreement through the accepted path.** The shared `K` resolves through the [shape environment contract](../docs/research/shapes/shape-environment-contract.md)'s three-outcome path, retaining both bindings so a failure names both observed sources.
- **The access relation.** Operand 0's map is `(t, o, d) -> (t, d)` and operand 1's is `(t, o, d) -> (o, d)`. Both are pure projections needing no index arithmetic, so no new access class — but this is the first family whose two operand maps project away *different* iteration coordinates, and the emission must be exercised rather than assumed.
- **The numerical signature.** Computation/input precision, accumulator dtype, result dtype, conversion behaviour, and the order contract, parameterized by the structure per ADR 0087 item 5. The L3 record's table gives every value for this profile. A contraction admitted with only an operand dtype and a result dtype is underspecified against ADR 0009.
- **The support-matrix row**, moved in the same change, with the honest rung.

## Non-goals

Structures 2 and 3 (the attention score and value contractions), any multi-operand form, any lowering capability, any schedule, and any backend. The multi-operand question stays reserved under Q-SEM-015 and rule five is where its future answer lands.

## Closes when

The structure is statable, all five refusals fire under their own names with a test that was watched failing, the canonical encoder's mutation proof exists and was demonstrated, and a contraction occurrence still fails closed at lowering-capability resolution because no capability covers it yet.

## Outcome

**Fact — the key and its structure.** `tiler::strict-tensor-contraction-f32@1` is registered by `StandardSemantics` through `register_standard_contraction` in `crates/tiler-ir/src/semantic/contraction.rs`: exact arity two operands and one result, one required `Record` attribute carrying a `ContractionIndexStructure`. The structure canonicalizes by first appearance across operand 0, operand 1, then the output, so `td,od->to` yields operand 0 `(0, 1)`, operand 1 `(2, 1)`, output `(0, 2)`, contracted `{1}` — exactly the encoding the L3 record derives. `ab,cb->ac` and `ij,kj->ik` produce identical canonical bytes; `td,do->to`, the ordinary `[M, K] x [K, N]` matmul, produces operand 1 `(1, 2)` and different bytes.

**Fact — the five rules, each under its own name.** Every rule is a typed provider diagnostic at construction, reachable both from `ContractionIndexStructure::new` and from a hand-assembled attribute reaching `infer`: `contraction.rule.output-index-in-no-operand`, `contraction.rule.summed-index-in-one-operand`, `contraction.rule.index-repeated-within-operand`, `contraction.rule.duplicate-output-index`, `contraction.rule.index-in-more-than-two-operands`. Rule four's second half — that the output is a permutation of the free indices — decomposes into rules four and one, because the structure's free indices *are* its output set; a third variant would be a check that could never say no, and that derivation is recorded on the variant rather than left implicit. The structure's rules are decided before the occurrence's operand count, which is what keeps rule five reachable under an exact-arity-two schema.

**Fact — refusals outside the five rules**, each derived rather than invented: `contraction.structure.no-contracted-index` (an unsummed structure is an outer or elementwise product, refused for the reason the registered strict `Sum` refuses an empty axis set), `contraction.structure.contracted-set-mismatch`, `contraction.structure.non-canonical-numbering` (refused rather than renumbered, following the reduced-axis precedent), `contraction.structure.operand-count`, `contraction.structure.malformed-attribute`, `contraction.type`, `contraction.rank`, `contraction.extent.disproved`, `contraction.extent.empty-contracted-domain`.

**Fact — extent agreement.** Resolved through the accepted three-outcome path, retaining both bindings: a disproof names both observed sources, as in "contraction index 1 is bound to extent 1024 by operand 0 axis 1 and to extent 512 by operand 1 axis 1". **Measurement boundary — the third outcome is unreachable here.** A semantic `ValueFact` carries a static `Shape` of `Extent`, so every extent an occurrence can present is a compile-time constant and the equality is always proved or disproved; the unresolved arm, a typed host-side pre-dispatch requirement, has no way to arise until a semantic value fact can carry a symbolic extent. That boundary is stated on the check rather than left for a reader to infer from its absence.

**Fact — the access relation.** `a_contraction_emits_two_operand_projections_dropping_different_coordinates` in `crates/tiler-ir/tests/index_region.rs` emits the region: iteration domain `(t, o)`, reduction domain `(d)`, operand 0 read over `[t, d]`, operand 1 over `[o, d]`, write over the full parallel set. The two operand domains share exactly one dimension and each keeps one the other drops — the property no previously admitted family had. Both projections discharge by interval propagation (`BoundsProofView::Interval`) and the write by `WriteOwnershipProofView::CoordinatePermutation`; nothing enumerates. **Inference.** "No new access class" is therefore exercised rather than assumed. It is an index-layer region and not a lowering capability, which stays a non-goal.

**Fact — the numerical signature.** Fourteen unconditional fields on the operation definition: computation/input precision, accumulator type, result type, conversion, contributor sequence, seed, empty-contracted-domain behaviour, reassociation, permutation, distributivity, ADR 0015's contraction permission, canonical NaN bits, NaN-canonicalization site, determinism. Values come from the L3 record's reduction-contract table. Two are decisions this ticket had to make rather than copy, and both are flagged for review: the **empty contracted domain is refused** (an unseeded fold has no empty result, and declaring `+0.0` would be a different operation the L3 record already separates), and **canonical-NaN installation is required after every combine and at the result boundary**, matching the registered strict serial sum. The second closes the condition D-8 states. **Inference.** The stronger obligation was chosen deliberately: a realization satisfying per-combine canonicalization satisfies the boundary-only rule, so a later relaxation is a deliberate widening rather than a silent tightening, and a materialized split-reduction partial carries a canonical payload. `FlushSubnormalsToZeroF32` is deliberately absent — it is a qualified target row's realization, not a property of the operation, and putting it here would make a target fact travel with consumer-neutral semantics.

**Fact — the mutation proof, demonstrated.** `the_canonical_encoder_is_mutation_proved_against_collision_and_double_encoding` carries two deliberately broken encoder twins and asserts each produces its defect: flattening the per-operand framing makes `ab,cb->ac` and `abc,b->ac` — two admitted, structurally different contractions — encode identically, and dropping the renumbering makes one structure encode two ways. Both were also watched failing against the *shipped* encoder by perturbing it: flattening `encode_structure` made `assert_ne!(ab_cb, abc_b)` fail with byte-identical left and right, and removing the renumbering in `Numbering::Canonicalize` failed four tests including the workload's own `(0, 1)`/`(2, 1)` canonicalization.

**Fact — the fail-closed refusal.** `capability::tests::a_contraction_occurrence_resolves_to_no_installed_index_access_capability` asks the *governed* lowering registry — the four index-access capabilities this build ships — for the contraction key at the `[f32, f32] -> [f32]` signature and gets `LoweringResolveError::MissingCapability`, with `multiply-f32` resolving `Ok` in the same test as the positive control. **Fact — and the honest correction.** `compile()` never reaches capability resolution for a contraction program: both installed recognizers require exactly one input over a fixed producer chain, so a two-operand contraction is refused at the request boundary with `phase: "strategy", rule: "signature"`, before any explain trace exists. `pipeline::tests::a_contraction_program_is_statable_and_refused_before_capability_resolution` asserts that refusal and says why it is not the lowering one.

**Measurement — every new refusal watched failing.** Four perturbation runs, each reverted: (A) `derive_contracted` returning early failed all five rule tests, the constructor-reachability test, and the no-contracted-index test — 7 of 9; (B) disabling the contracted-set, numbering, operand-count, extent, empty-domain, and rank checks failed all six of their tests; (C) flattening the encoder failed the mutation proof; (D) dropping the renumbering failed four canonicalization tests. (B) additionally surfaced a real defect: without the operand-count check the extent loop panicked with an out-of-range index, so the loop now zips the structure against the operands and cannot index out of range at all.

**Fact — identity and pin movements.** The semantic-registry snapshot moved, so the explain request digest was rebaselined from `928bbdb50eb505ed` to `e1e95ea1d50a918f` with a comment stating that the subject covers the frozen snapshot and its lowering-registry identity, and that no capability was registered. `policy::operation_capabilities` gained a `tiler::strict-tensor-contraction-f32@1` row consuming all eight realized dimensions — the union of the arithmetic and reduction rows, because a contraction is a reduction *and* its per-contributor step is an adjacent multiply and add, which is the one place ADR 0015's contraction has something to fuse. Without that row a target would be admitted without ever being asked. The `tiler.standard-semantics` provider revision was deliberately **not** bumped, following the precedent set at this ticket's base commit, where thirty-one dtype identities were added without one.

**Fact — the support matrix.** The contraction row moved R1 to R3, and four further roadmap assertions that this change made false were corrected in the same edit: that no admitted operation expresses operands required to disagree, that no admitted operation produces two operand maps projecting away different iteration coordinates, that no contraction operation exists, and the registered-operation counts. The stale bullet claiming L3 cannot be specified until the keyed-family question is settled was also corrected — ADR 0087 settled it before this ticket started.

**Deliberately not done.** No reference evaluator, fusion role, lowering capability, schedule, or backend. Index structures 2 and 3 are untouched. Rule five remains a refusal rather than a multi-operand answer. `docs/decisions/0087-...md`'s traceability sentence still reads that the support-matrix contraction row "stays at R1", and the L3 realization record's D-8 still reads as open; both files are outside this ticket's scopes, and correcting them is filed as [`correct-the-contraction-records-after-admission`](correct-the-contraction-records-after-admission.md).
