## User-visible outcome

The subgroup tier — CUDA warps, Metal simdgroups, WebGPU subgroups — has a target-neutral schedule representation the optimizer's four surfaces cover, so a warp-level reduction or shuffle-based combine becomes one more alternative the existing selection machinery enumerates, checks, and costs without modification.

## Why this is the next rung, and what already exists

**Fact.** The adopted [scheduled-region model](../docs/research/scheduling/scheduled-region-model.md) designs the tier: `Subgroup` and `Lane` bindings distinct from per-thread vector lanes, and a `SubgroupTree(contributors, combine_steps, result_lane, masked_identity)` combine construct. None of it is implemented; the live vocabulary reaches the workgroup tier only (the 2026-08-01 cooperative tile and synchronization authority).

**Fact.** The workgroup rung's landing evidence is the pattern to repeat: representation first with verifier-owned rejections, synchronization kinds statable-and-refused until admitted, target realization as one atomic fact, Metal support downstream. `add-subgroup-memory-scope-when-collectives-land` (deferred) and the refused `Collectives` synchronization kind are the reserved seams this design activates.

**Inference — the tier's model differs from the workgroup tier in ways the design must decide rather than inherit.** A subgroup combine moves values through shuffles, not staged memory, so visibility edges and staging lifetimes may not be the right obligations; convergence within a subgroup is a different (and per-device weaker) guarantee than workgroup convergence — CUDA's independent thread scheduling and Metal's simdgroup semantics are not the same fact; and the subgroup *width* is a target fact (32/64/queried-at-runtime) that the schedule must treat as symbolic or profile-bound, never assumed.

## Questions this must decide, each with its elimination stated

- Whether `SubgroupTree` is a `ReductionTopology` sibling of `CooperativeWorkgroup` (the tile precedent) or a construct inside a cooperative phase (a subgroup combine per staged slot), and what each does to identity.
- What the subgroup analogue of a visibility edge is, if any — a shuffle has no memory to fence, but masked-lane identity and width-tail behaviour are correctness obligations something must own.
- How the subgroup width enters feasibility: an atomic realization fact per (kind, width) in the synchronization-subject shape, or a width-symbolic schedule with a profile-bound resolution — and which the optimizer's feasibility surface can answer without backend code.
- Which numerical permissions a shuffle-tree combine consumes — it is a reassociation by construction; whether masked-identity injection also requires the permutation permission.
- What the CPU story is at this tier: a fixed-vector lane fold is the model's *different* binding, and the design must say why the two stay separate (the adopted record already refuses the synonym) with the consequence for a shared combine-tree shape stated.

## Non-goals

Implementation of any construct; a Metal simdgroup realization row (`qualify-the-simdgroup-matrix-contraction-realization` owns the measured half, and its strict-profile elimination stands); any CUDA or WebGPU backend claim.

## Closes when

Each question is answered with its elimination or explicitly deferred with a trigger, the surviving representation is written with worked examples in the record beside the workgroup tier's, the public drafts it would require are enumerated for Tom, and the outcome is an accepted design, a recorded deferral, or a bounded experiment.

## Outcome

The record is [The subgroup execution tier](../docs/research/scheduling/subgroup-execution-tier.md), written at base `8252312`. Research and design only: nothing was executed, emitted, compiled, or timed, and no crate, profile, or contract was edited. The outcome class is **accepted-track design** — a drafted ADR body written to be landed verbatim, with three of its nine decision items flagged as genuine choices for Tom and the rest derived from primary specifications.

**Q1 — sibling topology, not a construct inside a cooperative phase.** `SubgroupTree` is a `ReductionTopology` variant carrying a partition, a literal width, an ordered combine tree, a lane identity, and a result lane, and carrying no tile, no staging, and no synchronization point. The in-phase candidate is eliminated three ways, any one sufficient: a subgroup-only reduction would carry a `CooperativeTile` whose staging, lifetime, edge-derivation and discharge machinery all range over nothing; the width would have to live on the workgroup-shared-memory construct, making it the identity carrier for a dependency it has no relation to; and the empty tile would need exemption from `SynchronizationRule::SingleParticipant`, whose safety argument it degenerates.

**Q2 — there is no subgroup visibility edge, and the two obligations that replace it are not visibility obligations.** A `VisibilityEdge` over a register is eliminated because `FencedSpaces` has no flag that could be true and its doc says why: invocation-private memory is not shared, so fencing it is not a statement about visibility. What survives is source-lane activity and lane identity, both owned by the combine step. **The `Collective` kind stays refused and the design strengthens the refusal rather than activating it** — MSL Table 6.15 and WGSL §15.7.4 both specify shuffles exactly ("Correctly rounded") and both decline to specify the combine order of their reduction collectives ("Inherited from sum of x for all active invocations"), so the refusal reason turns out to describe the primitive rather than a modelling gap. The subject shape `Collective` would need is stated without admitting it: three fields beyond `SynchronizationSubject`'s five, all three already on the reduction topology.

**Q3 — a literal width in the schedule, an equality against an atomic compile-profile subject, and a preflight confirmation before routing commit.** Width-symbolic is eliminated because the combine steps are the topology's *content*, so a symbolic width leaves combine-tree numerical legality undischargeable at intrinsic verification — a stronger failure than the coverage one that refuses a scalable CPU partition — and by the CPU record's identity argument. The ticket's first candidate, an atomic fact inside the synchronization-subject shape, is eliminated because this tier declares no point; the atomicity argument transfers but the carrier does not.

**Q4 — reassociation always; permutation iff the stride order is descending; identity injection consumes neither.** The ascending-mask butterfly is a balanced binary tree over the ascending contributor sequence and consumes reassociation alone. The descending `shuffle_down` tree — the idiom MSL §6.10.2.1 prints — has a bit-reversed leaf order and additionally consumes permutation; the adopted scheduled-region model already reached that conclusion for the staged form of the same tree, which corroborates the derivation from inside the repository. **The two cost the same, so unlike the CPU tier the numerically legal choice is free.** The ticket's explicit sub-question is answered no: masked-identity injection does not require permutation, because padding extends the sequence rather than reordering it — and it consumes no permission at all, because a true two-sided identity leaves the result bit-identical, which is why it must be a proof obligation rather than a permission bit.

**Q5 — the two tiers cover disjoint parts of the design space.** The CPU tier's headline result, the map binding, has no subgroup analogue needing a construct, since `GlobalLinearInvocation` already assigns one invocation per output; the subgroup tier exists only for the partition case. They differ in width authority, in whether a source may be inactive, and in what the ownership proof asserts, so a shared construct would have to make all three optional. **What may be shared is the tree-order vocabulary** — ascending versus descending stride and the leaf-order test — which is one derivation with one answer.

**Q6 (the sixth, from the dispatch) — registers dissolve the slot-addressing problem entirely, and raise a strictly different obligation.** The workgroup tier needed a per-access active-participant subset because `StagedSpan` is a map from participant to *slot addresses* quantified over the whole participant range. A combine step has no slot: its destination is the reader's own register, so destination disjointness is total and free at every round and the one-writer-per-slot rule has nothing to check. The question "which participants are active for this access" never becomes "which slots does this access touch". **What replaces it is an obligation on the reader's source rather than the writer's extent** — every lane read must be active — and the ascending butterfly discharges it by construction, since participation never narrows and `l ^ m` for `m < W` is always a lane of the same subgroup. A narrowing tree would need the construct; the butterfly does not, which is a third independent reason to admit only the butterfly.

**Worked example A** is the CPU record's program (`x : f32[7,101]`, `y[m] = strict_serial_sum(n, x[m,n])`) at `W = 32`: `32k = 101` has no solution, so the padded sequence is 128 with 27 padding contributors and **six lanes owning no real contributor at all**. Those six are fully participating and identity-valued — masking them would make the lanes reading them read an inactive lane — which is why the adopted model's `masked_identity` field is carried but renamed. At exact bits, on the row whose 101 contributors are all `-0.0` (true sum `0x8000_0000`): `lane_identity_bits = 0x8000_0000` gives `0x8000_0000`, and `0x0000_0000` — which is exactly what the verifier *requires* `empty_identity_bits` to hold — poisons lane 25 at its first padding step and six more lanes entirely, giving `0x0000_0000`.

**Worked example B** is the same tile at the workgroup tier, and the comparison is sharper than expected because **101 is prime**: `ContributorPartition::covers` admits only 101 participants each folding one contributor, since 1 is refused as `SingleParticipant`. Subgroup form: 0 bytes local memory, 0 barriers, 0 visibility edges, 9 `f32` adds on the critical path. Workgroup form: 404 bytes, 1 barrier, 1 edge, 102 adds. Both consume exactly one permission. What the subgroup form newly obligates: the identity and its proof (B dodged it by choosing the participant count; A cannot), a width equality resolved across two phases, a launch divisibility requirement so no lane it reads is an unlaunched hypothetical invocation, and five convergence obligations instead of one.

**Three dispatch premises were checked against primary manuals and refuted**, matching the lane tier's bar. (i) "Metal simdgroups are 32 by spec" — MSL 4.1 states no numeric SIMD-group width anywhere; the only width it fixes is the quad-group's 4, and 32 is an observation of `threadExecutionWidth`. (ii) "The width question is the same shape as fixed-versus-scalable" — it is not: a CPU vector width is chosen by the schedule and a subgroup width is imposed by the device, which changes the authority, the availability phase, and whether identity padding is optional. (iii) "This tier likely activates the refused `Collectives` kind" — it does the opposite.

**A live defect was found in landed public vocabulary and filed rather than absorbed.** `RouteResourceDimension::SubgroupThreads` is a floor (`is_satisfied_by(observed) = self.minimum <= observed`) over "threads one subgroup must execute in lockstep", and lockstep within a subgroup is not a property current GPU families guarantee — the CUDA guide's independent-thread-scheduling text withdraws it explicitly. The relation is also wrong for the routes that would state it. Filed as [`correct-the-subgroup-threads-route-dimension-meaning`](correct-the-subgroup-threads-route-dimension-meaning.md); it is independent of whether this design is accepted.
