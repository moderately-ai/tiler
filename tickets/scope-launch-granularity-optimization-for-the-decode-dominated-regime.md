## User-visible outcome

The launch economics that may dominate small-batch LLM decode — many small kernels, per-launch overhead, and cross-stage synchronization — become a measured, scoped optimization surface: fused / multi-stage program forms, command reuse, and fusion between stages of one explicit kernel program. The kernel-program layer owns that intra-program stage DAG. It does not own the consumer's loop over separately invoked programs, so fusion across model-program or layer invocations remains outside this scope until a larger explicit semantic region or composition contract exists. (Literature may still say "mega-kernel" conversationally; prefer fused kernel / fusion region in normative interfaces per the glossary.)

## Why this exists, and what the fired trigger now permits

**Measurement.** The decoder-layer assembly measures **62 semantic occurrences** at the C1 decode row (`T = 1`; 58 at prefill `T = 10`) in [`the_layer_verifies_at_the_c1_decode_row` and its prefill peer](../crates/tiler-reference/tests/decoder_layer.rs). **Proposal.** The whole-model planning record partitions one forward pass into P1 once, P2 28 times, and P3 once. **Inference.** That proposed partition has 30 program executions per pass. It is not an observed runtime population; the owning record labels the partition `Proposal`, assigns its loop to the consumer, and currently records thirteen fixed-result-extent identities rather than the future symbolic world's three. Occurrences are not kernel launches. **Fact.** [`KernelProgramData`](../crates/tiler-ir/src/program/model.rs) owns an intra-program stage DAG, typed dependencies, derived execution order, materializations, outputs, and ABI. **Inference.** At decode batch sizes the per-launch cost plausibly exceeds any single kernel's arithmetic, making launch granularity worth more than further intra-kernel optimization — but that remains an inference, and this scoping's first obligation is the measurement that confirms or refutes it on the designated idle M3 Pro against an exact encoded-dispatch population.

**Second-review correction — 2026-08-11, exact candidate `2dbfa27d391e4b8312bbeb0d644151451af77b2a`.** The first correction still overclaimed three things. The current proof uses `metal` 0.33.0, whose `CommandBufferRef` exposes `commit`, `wait_until_completed`, and `status` but no GPU start / end timestamp accessors; `G_cb` is therefore unavailable without a separately decided binding or harness route, and a manual Objective-C `msg_send!` would be a new unsafe site under [ADR 0079](../docs/decisions/0079-permit-unsafe-code-case-by-case-at-named-sites.md). No such access or implementation is authorized here. The proposed launch-to-execution-versus-kernel-duration comparison was also unsound for this path: `dispatch_prepared` encodes every `dispatch_threads` before `submit` performs the one later `commit`, so a dispatch-encode-call-to-execution interval can include remaining host encoding, precommit delay, submission, queueing, and preceding GPU commands. Vellaisamy et al. explicitly distinguish minimal-queue launch-bound behavior from queue-dominated GPU-bound behavior where reducing launches provides no benefit; a large combined interval could therefore confirm the wrong regime. That decision rule is withdrawn, and the decode inference is unevaluable until a future Metal-specific observation model separates the contributing clocks sufficiently to test it. Finally, `CostComponent::MemoryTraffic` already gives generic bounds over every scheduled stage, including a `KernelSubprogram`'s intermediate owning write and consumer access; what is missing is separate attribution of materialization traffic, materialization latency, and an intra-subprogram barrier term—not the intermediate bytes themselves. This correction preserves the ICB projection, fixed-work controls, serial-sum non-closure, and Gupta evidence gap.

## Source and literature synthesis — 2026-08-10

**Fact — current repository surface.** The program identity encoder folds stage kernel identities and coverage, typed dependency edges, split and staged-realization records, copies, outputs, ABI expressions, applicability, and routing lifecycle into `CanonicalKernelProgramIdentity`; the searchable anchor is `fn encode_identity` in [`program/model.rs`](../crates/tiler-ir/src/program/model.rs). The artifact decoded view retains the verified `DecodedVariant::execution_order` and full `DecodedVariant::stage_dependencies`, including typed `StageDependencyReason`s. Runtime preparation consumes and narrows that information: `shared_allocations` maps `Data` edges to producer / consumer slot pairs and skips `StorageHandoff`; `Preflight::entries`, `Preflight::shared_allocations`, and the corresponding `RoutedDispatch` accessors expose the ordered entries and derived shared pairs, not the complete edge set or reasons. The proof host's `dispatch_prepared` can therefore encode one compute encoder and dispatch per non-skipped prepared entry, but `Preflight` alone cannot reconstruct the dependency / barrier semantics that a reusable-command experiment must preserve. Such an experiment must operate where the selected decoded variant is still available or first name and decide a future runtime boundary that carries the full edge set; this ticket chooses no public boundary. The current route also does not provide a reusable command object or a model-level program DAG.

**Fact — current cost surface.** [`PhysicalCostEstimate`](../crates/tiler-compiler/src/frontier.rs) has `dispatch_count`, `launched_threads`, and `temporary_bytes`; [`PlanStructuralCost`](../crates/tiler-compiler/src/selection.rs) aggregates those fields and adds only the region cover's `materializations().len()`. [`CostComponent::Dispatch`](../crates/tiler-compiler/src/component_cost.rs) reports selected implementation dispatches, while `CostComponent::Synchronization` reports consumers of satisfied `SelectedPlan::handoffs`, which are cross-region handoffs. `CostComponent::MemoryTraffic` traverses every `scheduled_stages()` entry; `ImplementationBody::Subprogram` returns all of its stages in execution order, so the bounded low count includes every owning write and the high count includes every access, including a split intermediate's producing write and consuming read. A split plan also charges its partial intermediate size through `temporary_bytes`. The remaining gap is attribution and latency: no current component identifies which generic traffic bytes are materialization traffic, prices materialization latency separately, or represents an intra-`KernelSubprogram` dependency / barrier. The accepted [cost-model contract](../docs/compiler/cost-model.md) names warm launch latency as target-calibration data. Therefore any future measured launch-sensitive coefficient belongs in target/profile calibration with its exact host, observable, model, and procedure—not in hard feasibility or as a host-independent constant. A valid plan is filtered first; only valid plans may be ranked by a calibrated cost.

**Primary-source precedent, bounded to what each source actually establishes.**

- [NVIDIA's CUDA Graphs programming guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/04-special-topics/cuda-graphs.html) separates graph definition, instantiation, and repeated launch. It states that advance setup can reduce CPU launch cost, preserves operations as dependency-connected nodes, and requires re-instantiation for topology or node-type changes while permitting bounded parameter updates. This is precedent for an amortized runtime executable and its update constraints, not evidence that a Metal ICB or a Tiler program has the same semantics or cost.
- [Apple's indirect-command overview](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/indirect-command-encoding) states that an `MTLIndirectCommandBuffer` can be encoded on CPU or GPU and invoked repeatedly, unlike a single-use `MTLCommandBuffer`. Apple's [CPU-encoding guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/encoding-indirect-command-buffers-on-the-cpu) motivates ICBs by avoiding repeated allocation, deallocation, and encoding of common instructions. [`MTLIndirectComputeCommand`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtlindirectcomputecommand) can carry pipeline state, buffers, threadgroup memory, dispatch geometry, and an explicit barrier. These are capability candidates. They do not show that replay removes the GPU's kernel boundary, and they do not discharge resource-use declarations, dependency realization, device-family support, or bound-storage lifetime.
- **Evidence gap — inaccessible source.** Kshitij Gupta, Jeff A. Stuart, and John D. Owens, *A Study of Persistent Threads Style GPU Programming for GPGPU Workloads*, 2012 Innovative Parallel Computing (InPar), DOI `10.1109/InPar.2012.6339596`, is indexed at [eScholarship item `3j76d3td`](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3j76d3td). On 2026-08-11 the review attempt reached a robot-verification interstitial. The reviewer stopped there and did not seek a mirror or bypass. Without a copy supplied by Tom, this record does not rely on the paper for particular persistent-thread use cases or performance losses. If the text disagreed with the accessible literature, it could change the literature characterization and experiment priorities below; it would not by itself authorize persistent-kernel implementation or erase the current feasibility gaps.
- Vellaisamy et al., [*Characterizing and Optimizing LLM Inference Workloads on CPU-GPU Coupled Architectures*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11750), observe a low-batch CPU-bound region where their launch-to-execution metric carries minimal queue delay, then a GPU-bound region where queueing dominates the same metric and minimizing launches provides no benefit. The paper's NVIDIA systems and profiler do not transfer a threshold or observation method to Metal; they show why the regime and the terms in a combined interval must be distinguished before selecting fusion.
- Jin et al., [*Event Tensor: A Unified Abstraction for Compiling Dynamic Megakernel*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13327), makes tiled task dependencies and static/dynamic scheduling explicit inside a persistent kernel. Its machinery is evidence that persistent cross-operator execution introduces a finer dependency and scheduling representation; Tiler's existing dispatch-stage DAG alone is not evidence that such an in-kernel scheduler, forward-progress rule, or synchronization ABI exists.

## Bounded idle-M3 measurement plan

**Constraint.** CPU timing and profiling run only on the designated idle M3 Pro. This coordination host is not an evidence host. Record exact commit, macOS and build, Xcode/SDK, repository Rust pin, Metal device name and registry identity, power source, release build, and any counter-set availability. Do not change OS, SDK, Xcode, GPU, or toolchain for the run without authorization.

**Subjects and populations.**

1. The runnable end-to-end subject is the existing serial-sum matrix. For each non-empty routed case, assert from the committed route immediately before timing that `selected` has one encoded entry and zero shared allocations and `materialized` has two encoded entries and one shared allocation. Preserve the bit-exact reference oracle before and after every timing block. Empty-domain cases, whose zero-work stage may be skipped, are correctness coverage but not members of a fixed two-launch timing population.
2. The selected / materialized delta is an end-to-end plan comparison, not an isolated launch coefficient: it also changes intermediate traffic, dependency synchronization, and kernel work. It can validate the harness and measure that plan delta, but it cannot confirm or refute the decode-specific inference.
3. The required harness control has three cells using one queue, pre-created pipelines and buffers, and no timed readback: an empty command buffer establishes the fixed submit / completion floor; one precompiled no-op Metal compute entry with zero bound buffers and a one-thread grid establishes the one-command null-kernel cell; and `N ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32}` ordered instances of that exact entry establish a bounded command-count sweep. Preserve the exact MSL source, compiled-library digest, and observed dispatch census; if the toolchain does not retain a dispatchable no-op, stop and amend the subject rather than silently substitute work. The sweep varies CPU encoding work, GPU command scheduling / serialization, and kernel execution together. Fit each observed clock against the encoded dispatch count and retain residuals, but name the slope **combined per-command incremental cost**, not launch overhead. Do not subtract the empty or one-command cell and relabel the result as launch cost: the command streams and executed work differ, and the available Metal command-buffer clocks do not identify that quantity. Perturb the subject by changing `N` and show that the census and timing row change; do not perturb the timing assertion.
4. Decoder-specific confirmation or refutation is a separate required subject. When runnable P1/P2/P3 artifacts exist, derive the decode-step population from each committed route after ABI binding: count non-skipped `RoutedDispatch::entries`, record each kernel-program identity, and—only if the proposed composition was adopted—compute `D(P1) + 28 × D(P2) + D(P3)`. Never substitute 62 occurrences or the proposed 30 program executions for `D`. A changed identity or bound shape is a new population row, not another repetition of the old one.

**Host clocks and the unavailable GPU clock.** The existing proof contains no timing harness. A future authorized harness can create the command queue, libraries, pipelines, buffers, and route-derived preparation before timing, then record these two safe host-wall intervals without calling either launch time:

- `E_host = t_after_encoding - t_before_command_buffer_creation`: host CPU wall time for command-buffer creation and API encoding. It contains no GPU-execution clock.
- `C_host = t_after_wait_until_completed - t_before_commit`: host-observed commit-to-completion time. It combines driver and queue effects, GPU work, completion notification, and host wake-up.

Apple defines [`GPUStartTime`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtlcommandbuffer/gpustarttime) and the corresponding end property, but the repository pins `metal` 0.33.0 and its `CommandBufferRef` exposes neither. Therefore a whole-command-buffer GPU elapsed interval is **unavailable in the current Rust path**, not a third direct clock. A manual Objective-C message send would add an unsafe site governed by ADR 0079; changing the binding or harness is an implementation / access decision outside this ticket. Even if a future safe accessor is admitted, the resulting whole-command-buffer interval cannot by itself separate command scheduling, waits / serialization, or individual kernel execution.

**Future decoder observation boundary.** The current record has no valid Metal-specific observation model that can decide launch dominance. `dispatch_prepared` performs every `dispatch_threads` call during host encoding, before `submit` later calls `commit`; a naïve encode-call-to-kernel-start interval therefore includes remaining encoding and precommit delay as well as submission, queueing, and preceding GPU commands. [Vellaisamy et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11750) make queueing part of their launch-to-execution metric and then distinguish the launch-bound minimal-queue region from the GPU-bound queue-dominated region where launch minimization provides no benefit. Transferring only their interval comparison would erase the distinction this ticket needs.

Before a future Metal trace, counter, or accessor method can support a decoder verdict, its owner must demonstrate all of these properties on the designated M3 Pro: stable correlation from each routed entry to its CPU encode event and GPU execution; a stated clock domain and synchronization method; separate observation or an identifiable model for CPU encoding / submission, time between encoding and commit, queueing / preceding GPU commands, and per-kernel execution; validation that intentionally varied precommit and queue delays move only the terms they are meant to test; and quantified instrumentation perturbation. This names the required boundary rather than choosing a public API, unsafe site, tool, or decision equation. Until it exists, report `E_host`, `C_host`, the fixed-work combined slopes, and serial-sum deltas only as harness / host-and-command-buffer evidence; the decode inference remains unevaluable.

**Procedure and reporting.** Warm the process with 200 untimed executions per cell. Run at least five independently seeded blocks of 1,000 samples, randomizing the cell order within each block. Record `E_host`, `C_host`, encoded dispatch count, shared bytes and allocation count, and the exact output verdict. Record any future GPU or per-entry observation only after its access and model boundary above is decided and validated, and label its instrumentation separately. Report every cell's median, p05, p95, and a bootstrap 95% confidence interval for paired deltas, plus each fitted combined slope and residual plot / data. Keep raw samples, harness revision, inputs, and the command in a linked `spikes/` directory. A rerun-until-green is not evidence: discard a whole predeclared block only for a recorded external interruption, thermal / power transition, or correctness failure, and report the discarded block.

**No current decode decision rule.** Serial-sum can validate the harness and quantify only its selected / materialized end-to-end delta. The null-kernel controls and `N` sweep can quantify only their host and combined per-command behavior. `E_host`, `C_host`, a whole-command-buffer interval if one is later admitted, or a launch-to-execution interval that includes queueing cannot independently confirm or refute the decode-dominated inference. The inference is **unevaluable**, not confirmed, refuted, or merely low-confidence, until the exact non-skipped decoder population and a validated Metal-specific separation model exist. Do not recommend fused, replayed, or persistent forms from these harness quantities; each alternative requires its own feasible end-to-end comparison after the observation boundary is resolved.

## Current research outcome and remaining evidence

**Fact.** The fired dependency supplies a real multi-stage runtime subject: [`expected_shape`](../prototypes/serial-sum-run/src/proof.rs) pins the selected route to one dispatch / zero shared allocations and the materialized route to two dispatches / one shared allocation; `prove_member` checks those counts before `dispatch_prepared`, and the dependency records bit-exact agreement. **Inference.** That proves the measurement is runnable, not that decode is launch-dominated.

**Bounded experiment outcome — host-harness protocol explicit, decision model unavailable.** The subjects, two safe future host clocks, warm-up, repetitions, noise controls, oracle, and unevaluable condition are explicit above. The current binding supplies no GPU command-buffer timestamps, and the required idle-M3 timing cannot be taken on this coordination host. Reconsider the safe harness run when the designated idle M3 Pro and authority are available, but treat its serial-sum and empty / null-kernel results only as harness, plan-delta, and combined per-command evidence. Closure additionally requires a runnable decoder route and a separately decided, validated Metal observation model that separates encoding / submission, precommit delay, queueing, and per-kernel execution sufficiently to test the inference. Retain raw data and exact commands under `spikes/`. Until then this ticket remains `in-progress`; no isolated launch coefficient, decode verdict, or optimization recommendation is justified.

## Required analysis

- **Measure launch economics on the decoder subject before recommending fusion policy.** Serial-sum multi-stage is available now to validate a safe host harness and measure its own plan delta. Evaluation of the decode inference separately requires the exact routed decoder population and a validated Metal-specific observation model that distinguishes CPU encoding / submission, precommit delay, queueing, and per-kernel execution on the named host and procedure.
- **Name the optimization surface at the kernel-program layer.** What is in and out among fused / multi-stage program forms, command-buffer reuse (including Metal indirect command buffers), and cross-stage fusion — and what identity, feasibility, and ABI consequences each option carries.
- **Separate hard feasibility from estimated cost.** Impossible multi-stage launch plans must fail clearly; cost ranking is only among valid plans under the numerical contract and target profile.
- **End in one of the four research outcomes.** A contract update, an accepted decision, a bounded experiment, or an explicitly deferred question with a reconsideration trigger. Product prose alone is not an outcome.

## Non-goals

Implementing persistent kernels, Metal ICB capture, or cross-stage fusion. Changing decoder-layer assembly, whole-model partition, or runtime multi-entry ABI. Treating occurrence counts as launch counts. Replacing the serial-sum multi-stage subject with a simulated multi-layer model run as the first measurement host.

## Closes when

(a) The idle-M3 serial-sum and empty / null-kernel controls validate the safe host harness and quantify their own plan / combined-command deltas without relabelling them isolated launch overhead. (b) A runnable decoder route supplies the exact non-skipped kernel population, and a separately decided Metal observation model proves it can distinguish CPU encoding / submission, precommit delay, queueing, and per-kernel execution sufficiently to confirm or refute the decode inference; serial-sum and host / whole-command-buffer clocks alone cannot do so. (c) The kernel-program-layer optimization surface is named with in / out for fused multi-stage forms, command-buffer reuse / Metal ICB, and cross-stage fusion, plus identity and feasibility consequences. (d) Remainder work is filed as tickets or explicit deferrals with triggers. Exit is a contract update, accepted decision, bounded experiment, or deferred question—not only an open note.
