### 1. `session::compile`

**Fact — the item.** `pub fn compile(request: CompileRequest<'_>) -> Result<CompilationBatch, CompileFailure>`.

**Ownership.** The compiler owns everything the caller does not state: budgets, shape environment, selection policy, feasibility rules, and the offered provider set. The caller owns the program (borrowed), the ordered contract preference, the target list, and the installed lowering authority. The request is taken **by value**, so it cannot be submitted twice or mutated after the compiler begins reading it; `CompileRequest`'s documentation states that as the reason.

**Naming.** `compile` is the module's one verb, and `crates/tiler-compiler/src/lib.rs`'s module documentation makes the claim structural: "`session` is the one boundary over which a caller compiles; every public module beside it supplies something a request is composed from, never a second compilation path." That is ADR 0069's decision — one general consumer-independent boundary over `SemanticProgram`, no graph-specific entry points and no `experimental` namespace — spelled at the item.

**Validation and identity obligations.** Contract preference is validated before anything else runs (`NumericalContractPreference::ordered`); the offered provider set is frozen into an `Arc<[ProviderIdentity]>` once and shared by every slot; and the compiler's own output is re-checked against the caller's request before it is handed back — `into_compilation_batch` refuses a product whose target cardinality differs from the request (`public-target-outcome-cardinality`) or whose slot names a different profile key **or a different canonical descriptor** (`public-target-outcome-binding`). The descriptor half is the ADR 0043 obligation: two profiles can advertise one key and admit different candidates, so a key alone is not evidence.

**Evidence for.** `public_conversion_rejects_mutated_target_cardinality_and_binding` (`session.rs` tests) perturbs the product five ways — a dropped outcome, an extra outcome, a swap at equal cardinality, a substituted profile, and a same-key-changed-descriptor substitution — and observes each refusal by rule name, so both checks are proved able to say no. `target_outcomes_preserve_request_order_cardinality_and_profile_identity` compiles two profiles where one refuses and asserts the other is not discarded, in request order. `successful_batch_slots_share_the_frozen_provider_set` (`crates/tiler-compiler/tests/caller_target_profile.rs`) asserts the two slots' provider sets are equal *and* pointer-identical, which is the identity claim rather than the equality claim. Out of crate, `compile` is the entry point of every real consumer: `crates/tiler-macros/src/aot.rs`, `crates/tiler-build/src/{metal_plan,metal_declaration,metal_profile}.rs`, `crates/tiler-build/examples/identity_join_producer.rs`, `crates/tiler-build/tests/custom_backend/`, `prototypes/serial-sum-compile`, `prototypes/serial-sum-run/src/proof.rs`.

**Evidence against.** What `compile` *admits* is bounded and the bound is not in its signature: `docs/correctness-and-testing.md`'s conformance paragraph states the admitted subject as `f32` throughout with each declared output produced by a strict serial sum, a strict tensor contraction, or an expression the general elementwise walk can spell. A caller reads no such bound off `compile`; it learns the bound by being refused. **Inference:** that is the correct shape — the alternative is a second description of the recognizer at the boundary, which `crates/tiler-macros/src/aot.rs`'s `rendered_refusal` documentation rejects by name as "a second authority that goes stale in silence" — but it means accepting `compile` accepts a function whose domain is documented only by its refusals.

**Elimination.** The alternatives are a narrower entry point (a graph-specific or profile-specific `compile`), which ADR 0069 decides against explicitly; keeping the general path private and shipping only `compile_governed`, which is the state `prototype-public-compiler-api` was filed to end and which no external frontend can install capabilities through; and deferring until the admitted subject stops widening, eliminated under "Recommendation" on measured grounds. **No question survives.**

### 2. `session::compile_governed`

**Fact — the item.** `pub fn compile_governed(program: &SemanticProgram, contract: NumericalContract) -> Result<Compilation, CompileFailure>`.

**Ownership.** It owns nothing of its own. It composes `TargetRequest::new([TargetProfile::governed()])` and `CompileRequest::new`, calls `compile`, checks the returned cardinality, and unwraps the single slot. Its documentation states the reason for expressing it through the general surface: one path rather than two is what stops the convenient one and the general one from drifting, and it is the cheapest proof the general surface is usable at all.

**Naming.** `_governed` names the built-in profile it selects, matching `TargetProfile::governed` and `InstalledCapabilities::governed`. Consistent.

**Validation and identity obligations.** One: it refuses its own output when `compile` returns other than exactly one slot, as `InvalidCompilerOutput` under rule `public-governed-target-cardinality`. It documents a `# Panics` clause for a governed profile that violates its own construction invariants.

**Evidence for.** It is the entry point of every in-crate success test in `session.rs` (`a_governed_program_compiles_to_alternatives_carrying_kernels`, `the_selection_names_a_retained_alternative`, `a_compilation_renders_its_explain_trace`, `a_compilation_names_its_target_profile_and_its_feasibility_rules`, `an_alternative_names_its_capabilities_and_exposes_its_abi_inputs`) and of both refusal tests that pin the pre-trace boundary (`the_reachable_failure_classes_are_reached_from_the_public_surface`, `a_refusal_before_the_trace_boundary_carries_no_trace`).

**Fact — outside this crate it has three call sites, all in tests, and both real producers document why they left it.** The count is `grep -rn "compile_governed" crates prototypes --include "*.rs" | grep -v "^crates/tiler-compiler/src/"`: eight lines, of which two are imports, three are documentation, and three are calls — `crates/tiler-build/src/metal_plan.rs` (two, in its test module) and `crates/tiler-build/tests/custom_backend/main.rs` (one). Of the three documentation lines, two say the function is deliberately no longer used: `prototypes/serial-sum-compile/src/main.rs`'s `compile_under` records that `compile_governed` "selects the compiler's own governed prototype profile, whose grid, binding, and local memory values are internally usable declarations rather than sourced target facts — and `accept_or_publish_metal_plan` now refuses a plan compiled under any profile but the declared one, so a slip back to it fails closed rather than silently publishing an artifact attributed to the wrong target", and `prototypes/serial-sum-run/src/proof.rs`'s `compile_under` gives the routing half of the same reason. **Inference:** the convenience's fixed argument is the wrong target for any producer that publishes an artifact, which is why a downstream fail-closed gate now stands behind it; what remains is a convenience for tests and examples where no authoritative declaration exists.

**Evidence against — and this is the one item where it is a defect rather than a bound.** **Fact (signature-level, independent of any profile):** the general path returns `TargetCompileFailure`, which carries `class()`, `explain()`, **and** `refusal() -> Option<&TargetCompileRefusal>` — the recoverable typed detail for a refusal that precedes the trace boundary. `compile_governed` ends in `outcome.map_err(|failure| failure.failure)`, which projects that value onto its inner `CompileFailure` and discards `refusal`. The convenience path therefore cannot report a numerical-contract or dtype-dispatch refusal's structure at all, and its documentation does not say so — a doc comment is a claim, and this one is silent about a loss rather than wrong about it. **Inference, with its exact check:** the loss is reachable today with vocabulary Tom accepted this morning. `TargetProfileBuilder::governed` declares dispatchability for `F32::resolved_type()` and nothing else, so a pure-BF16 program stated under `NumericalContract::STRICT_BF16` reaches `RequestError::DTypeNotDispatchable` (or, at the numerical gate, `NoResolvableNumericalContract`); `target_compile_failure` populates `TargetCompileRefusal` for exactly those two request errors; and `compile_governed` then drops it. The assertions in `crates/tiler-compiler/tests/bf16_numerical_contract.rs` — `a_strict_bf16_contract_is_refused_by_the_measured_sign_preserving_flush` reads `failure.refusal()` and walks the subject, means, and honoured behaviour — are assertions the convenience path's return type cannot express. That file drives `compile` with a caller-declared profile, so this is an inference from reading rather than a measurement; the one-line refutation is to write the same request against `TargetProfile::governed()` through `compile_governed` and find a `refusal()` accessor on what comes back.

**Sub-question (atomic).** Is `compile_governed` accepted as it stands, accepted with its error type widened from `CompileFailure` to `TargetCompileFailure`, or declined?

- **Widen, then accept.** *Enables:* the convenience path reports what the general path reports, so a caller that starts here and later needs structured refusal detail changes nothing. `TargetCompileFailure` implements `Error` with the inner `CompileFailure` as its `source` and exposes the same `class()`, so nothing is lost in the other direction. *Prevents:* nothing. *Costs:* one type in the signature; the three call sites read `.class()` or propagate, so each needs at most a one-token change.
- **Accept as-is.** *Enables:* closing this node today with no code change. *Prevents:* a `compile_governed` caller from ever branching on a typed refusal. *Counterpoint that deserves weight:* ADR 0075 records that reversing a public surface here costs one internal edit and `cargo check` enumerates the sites, and the loss has never bitten because all three call sites read only the class.
- **Decline.** *Enables:* removing a default target that both producers migrated away from, and with it the only entry point that bakes a profile choice into a function name. *Prevents:* a compiler-internal or documentation example from compiling anything without first assembling a `TargetRequest`; and it removes what session.rs calls "the cheapest proof that surface is usable at all", though the prototypes and integration tests now supply a better one by driving `compile` directly. *Costs:* three test call sites, each becoming three lines.
- **Recommendation: widen, then accept.** Accept-as-is is cheaper precisely because it drops the part that makes the answer actionable, which AGENTS.md names as the shape to be suspicious of, and an accepted lossy signature is a claim the next reader builds on while the widening is one type. Decline is a defensible second answer and is *not* eliminated here — its cost is small and its footgun argument is real — but the governed profile remains the right target for the compiler's own tests and for an example that has no authoritative declaration to name, so removing the entry point solves a producer problem that a downstream fail-closed gate already solves. If Tom prefers accept-as-is, the loss must be written into `compile_governed`'s doc comment in the same change, because the current silence is the actual defect.

### 3. `CompileRequest` and its installation methods

**Fact — the items.** `pub struct CompileRequest<'a>`; `CompileRequest::new(program, contract, targets) -> Self`; `CompileRequest::preferring(program, contracts, targets) -> Result<Self, CompileFailure>`; `CompileRequest::with_capabilities(self, capabilities) -> Self`; and the public bound `MAX_NUMERICAL_CONTRACT_PREFERENCES`.

**Ownership.** The caller owns four inputs and no more. The type's own documentation states the boundary and its reason: budgets and the shape environment stay internal because each has exactly one governed admissible value, and target declaration is accepted only through `crate::target::TargetProfileBuilder`, which validates and freezes a whole profile before it can enter a request. **Fact:** that division matches `docs/status.md`'s statement that shape-environment choice and planning budgets remain internal, so accepting this item does not quietly accept a wider caller surface than the contracts describe.

**Naming.** `new` is the one-contract spelling and `preferring` the ordered-list spelling; `with_capabilities` is a consuming builder method, so a request cannot acquire an authority after `compile` starts reading it. `preferring`'s name states the semantics the documentation defends: the list is part of what the compilation *is* and is bound into the request subject, not a retry policy outside it — two requests accepting different fallbacks are different requests even when they resolve identically.

**Validation and identity obligations.** `preferring` refuses an empty list (`compile.request.numerics.unstated`), a list longer than the bound (`compile.request.numerics.too-many`), and a duplicated entry (`compile.request.numerics.duplicate`), each as `CompileFailureClass::InvalidRequest` carrying the refusing check's stable key. It takes `MAX_NUMERICAL_CONTRACT_PREFERENCES + 1` items from the iterator and no more, so an unbounded iterator cannot be drained by a validation path. Order is the caller's and is preserved into request identity.

**Evidence for.** `a_stated_preference_is_carried_and_both_halves_are_readable` (`session.rs` tests) compiles a two-entry preference, asserts the whole stated list is retained rather than only the winner, asserts a reversed list is a *different* stated list, and asserts the empty list refuses as `InvalidRequest`. `numerical_preference_cardinality_and_uniqueness_fail_as_invalid_requests` (`crates/tiler-compiler/tests/caller_target_profile.rs`) pins both the duplicate rule key and the exhaustion bound by counting the iterator: it asserts `consumed.get() == MAX_NUMERICAL_CONTRACT_PREFERENCES + 1`, which is the check that the bound is enforced by not-consuming rather than by trimming afterwards. `target_request_cardinality_is_bounded_and_stops_at_the_first_excess_profile` covers the same property on the target side of the request.

**Evidence against.** `new` is infallible while `preferring` is fallible, so the surface has two constructors with different failure behaviour for what a reader may see as one act; the derivation is that a one-element list cannot violate any of the three rules, so `new` has nothing to refuse. `MAX_NUMERICAL_CONTRACT_PREFERENCES` is a public re-export of an internal constant, which means the bound's *value* becomes observable — a caller may branch on it. That is deliberate (a caller cannot otherwise know why a list refused) and it is exercised at `caller_target_profile.rs`, but it is a commitment: the number is now a fact about the boundary rather than about the request module.

**Elimination.** Dropping `preferring` and requiring the caller to retry is refuted by the type's own argument, restated as a test: a caller-side retry produces an answer the compiler never recorded as a preference, so the request subject cannot distinguish two requests accepting different fallbacks. Dropping `new` leaves every one-contract caller writing a `Result` for a case with nothing to refuse. Making `with_capabilities` a constructor argument rather than a consuming method would force every caller to name `InstalledCapabilities::governed()`, which is exactly the default that makes `compile_governed` expressible through this same path. **No question survives.**

### 4. `InstalledCapabilities`

**Fact — the items.** `pub struct InstalledCapabilities(CompilerCapabilitySnapshot)`; `InstalledCapabilities::governed() -> Self`; `InstalledCapabilities::installed(lowering: FrozenLoweringCapabilityRegistry, scalars: FrozenScalarRegistry) -> Self`.

**Ownership.** The caller owns the pair — a lowering registry and the frozen scalar authority it was registered against. The compiler owns the realization law: request preflight derives law authority from the *program's* exact semantic registry, so an installer cannot replace the law that defines an operation while installing its lowering. The wrapper is opaque, so the internal request model behind it stays private and the caller's obligation is only the pairing.

**Naming.** `governed` names what the build ships; `installed` names what the caller supplies. The distinction is the one ADR 0078 item 4 asked for by name.

**Validation and identity obligations.** A mismatched lowering/scalar/program triple is refused rather than reconciled; the resolution record is attributed at the installing provider's own revision, not the governed one; and the offered set the compilation reports is the compiler-minted set rather than the selected subset, so an assembler cannot reconstruct the compilation environment from what was selected.

**Evidence for — the strongest on this list, because it is out-of-crate by construction rather than by assertion.** `prototypes/serial-sum-compile` depends on `tiler-compiler` and sees only its public surface: `an_out_of_crate_caller_installs_its_own_capability_registry` composes and installs a registry there, and `an_installed_registry_missing_a_family_fails_closed` omits one family and observes the refusal — the negative case that would catch a `with_capabilities` silently ignoring its argument, which that test's own comment names as the failure it exists to exclude. Inside the crate, `an_externally_registered_lowering_provider_drives_the_compile_path` (`crates/tiler-compiler/src/pipeline/conformance.rs`) substitutes an out-of-crate provider for one governed family and asserts every retained alternative records that provider *and* its capability revision as the occurrence's lowering authority; `a_lowering_cannot_replace_the_semantic_providers_realization_law` and `equal_semantic_snapshots_cannot_substitute_the_programs_law` are the two fail-closed siblings; `contended_lowering_capabilities_fail_closed_with_a_distinct_error` pins contention as its own disposition. `docs/architecture.md` already describes this exact three-call path as implemented.

**Evidence against.** The registry this type installs can carry two provider families and only one of them is reachable through this boundary. **Fact:** `crates/tiler-compiler/src/capability.rs` exposes `LoweringCapabilityRegistryBuilder::register_scalar_lowering` and the `ScalarLoweringProvider` trait, and [ADR 0078](../docs/decisions/0078-name-the-intended-public-extension-seams.md)'s seam inventory rates that family at *implemented support* — "registration and resolution exist and are tested, and no compile-path caller resolves the family" — which `docs/correctness-and-testing.md` repeats as a gate gap and [`resolve-or-retire-the-scalar-lowering-provider-seam`](resolve-or-retire-the-scalar-lowering-provider-seam.md) owns. So a caller can install a registry half of whose registrable content nothing consumes, and no installation evidence exists for that half.

**Elimination — and why the scalar gap is not a reason to hold this item.** `InstalledCapabilities::installed` takes `FrozenLoweringCapabilityRegistry` **opaquely**. Retiring or wiring the scalar family changes what `crate::capability`'s builder accepts and what the frozen registry contains; it does not change this signature, this type, or its two constructors. The gap is therefore an argument about `capability`'s surface, which is a separately-declared reviewed-draft module and not one of this node's six items. **No question survives here**; the scalar seam stays where its own ticket holds it.

### 5. `Compilation` and its accessors

**Fact — the items.** `pub struct Compilation` with eleven accessors: `stated_numerical_contract_keys`, `resolved_numerical_contract_key`, `offered_providers`, `target_profile_key`, `target_profile`, `target_profile_descriptor`, `feasibility_rule_set_key`, `feasibility_rule_set_revision`, `alternatives`, `selected`, `explain`.

**Ownership.** The compiler mints every identity this type reports and hands each over whole: the capability key and revision, the feasibility rule-set key and revision, and the target profile's canonical descriptor *bytes* rather than a digest of them. Each accessor's documentation states the same reason — a consumer composing its own key from parts would be a second derivation of one identity, which is the drift this boundary exists to prevent. `PlanAlternative` is a borrowed view carrying its owning `Compilation`, so a caller cannot pair a program from one compilation with the profile, rules, or provider environment of another.

**Naming.** `stated_*` versus `resolved_*` is the load-bearing pair: "what I would have accepted" and "what I got" are different facts, and a reader seeing only the second cannot tell a compilation that got its first choice from one that fell back. `alternatives` versus `selected` is the second such pair — both the fused and the materialized alternative are exposed because the offline slice compiles the selected program *and* keeps the materialized one as its numerical reference.

**Validation and identity obligations.** `feasibility_rule_set_revision` is documented as always nonzero because zero is reserved for "unset" at the artifact boundary; `target_profile_descriptor` is the descriptor identity rather than a summary of it; `selected` returns `Option` rather than asserting a compiler-owned invariant into a caller's process.

**Evidence for.** `a_compilation_names_its_target_profile_and_its_feasibility_rules` asserts both halves of the profile reference and both halves of the rule-set reference, asserts the two keys differ (fusing them would make an artifact claim it was assessed under rules named after a target), and checks the descriptor against the constant this crate publishes rather than a literal restating it. `an_alternative_names_its_capabilities_and_exposes_its_abi_inputs` asserts every capability key is governed and parseably versioned, that the ABI arena is replayable front-to-back, that every position the entries name is inside the arena, and that the prepared-entry requirements are one per kernel in entry order. Downstream, these accessors are what artifact identity is built from: `crates/tiler-build/src/plan_artifact.rs` consumes `selected_capabilities`, `prepared_entry_target_requirements`, and `abi().kernel_program()`, and `crates/tiler-build/src/metal_plan.rs` consumes `alternatives`, `selected`, `is_fused`, `kernels`, and `entries`.

**Evidence against — two accessors with no out-of-crate call site.** **Fact:** `stated_numerical_contract_keys` has no consumer outside `crates/tiler-compiler` (the check is a workspace grep for the name excluding `crates/tiler-compiler/src/`; it returns nothing). **Fact:** `Compilation::explain` likewise has none — the six out-of-crate `.explain()` call sites are all `CompileFailure`/`TargetCompileFailure::explain` on the *failure* path, two of which (`crates/tiler-build/src/metal_plan.rs`) render an `ExplainReport`. So `VerifiedCompilationExplain`, the success-path composite, is exercised only by `a_compilation_renders_its_explain_trace` in this crate. **Fact:** that type is `pub use`d into `session` from the private `mod explain`, and its own public surface is two methods — `semantic_candidate_count` and `render`.

### 6. The `CompileFailureClass` refusal vocabulary

**Fact — the items.** `pub enum CompileFailureClass`, `#[non_exhaustive]`, five variants: `InvalidRequest { rule }`, `UnsupportedCapability { rule }`, `NoFeasiblePlan`, `BudgetExhausted`, `InvalidCompilerOutput`.

**Ownership.** The classes are the compiler's own and deliberately coarse; the caller branches on *which boundary refused*, never on text. The `rule` payload is the refusing check's stable diagnostic key, minted by `rule_of` from the internal `RequestError` and never composed by a consumer.

**Naming.** ADR 0069 requires the taxonomy to distinguish at least invalid requests, valid programs lacking a capability, infeasible plans, exhausted bounded search, and failures of compiler-produced IR verification; the five variants are that list one-to-one. The split of `InvalidRequest` from `UnsupportedCapability` is documented at the mapping site with the derivation for why it was un-merged: the two imply different actions — fix the request, versus install a provider — and telling them apart under one class meant matching `rule` against strings, which is the thing this enum exists to avoid.

**Validation and identity obligations.** `class_of` is exhaustive at every level **with no wildcard arm**, so a new internal class must be classified deliberately rather than absorbed; its comment states why that matters more here than at most mapping sites (reporting a Tiler defect as an unsupported program tells a caller to change a program that was fine, and the converse sends a correct refusal to the wrong owner). `#[non_exhaustive]` is justified against ADR 0074's amended convention 5 clause 5a at the type, with the parallel versioned schema view considered and eliminated rather than deferred.

**Evidence for — this is the item with a real consumer contract behind it.** `crates/tiler-macros/src/aot.rs`'s `rendered_refusal` matches the class **exhaustively** — all five variants plus a wildcard arm whose comment records that the enum is `#[non_exhaustive]`, so a class added later reaches a consumer as a refusal rather than as a pattern-match failure — and renders each into a distinct actionable compile error carrying the stable rule key. Its own doc comment states the point: the rendering is derived from `CompileFailureClass` rather than from the refusal's `Debug`. **Correction to this node's Decision-boundary citation, re-checked at this base:** `grep -n "fn rendered_refusal" crates/tiler-macros/src/aot.rs` returns `446` here, not the `438` the Decision boundary records, and the import is at `:214`; the function name is the durable citation and the line is not. In-crate, `the_failure_classes_are_distinct` asserts all ten pairs rather than two spot checks, so a class merged back into another fails it; `the_reachable_failure_classes_are_reached_from_the_public_surface` records which classes the public surface can actually produce and why the other two cannot, which is a different claim from "untested".

**Evidence against.** **Fact, from that test's own documentation:** two of the five are not reachable from the public surface — `BudgetExhausted` needs a program built specifically to exceed the governed budgets, and `InvalidCompilerOutput` is unreachable by construction from a valid call because reaching it would mean shipping the defect it exists to report. So the vocabulary is accepted with three of five classes demonstrated end to end. Second: the `rule: &'static str` payload is an observable that reaches a human — `rendered_refusal` interpolates it into the diagnostic text — while the enum's documentation tells callers not to branch on it. Nothing enforces that asymmetry; it is a convention carried by documentation.

**Elimination.** Collapsing the two `rule`-carrying classes is refuted by the mapping site's own recorded history. Publishing a versioned schema view beside the enum is eliminated at the type on convention 3 grounds (a projection cannot fail closed when its source grows) and because ADR 0075 records that Tom rejected the compatibility premise outright. Dropping the `rule` payload would leave `rendered_refusal` unable to name the check that refused. **No question survives.**
