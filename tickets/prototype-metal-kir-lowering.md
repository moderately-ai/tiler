Lower verified structured KIR plus explicit target facts to deterministic MSL for the bounded proof profile. Emit every required entry point and deterministic helpers, with typed diagnostics, golden/negative tests, and no graph-pattern reconstruction or hidden semantic special cases.

## Outcome

Recorded 2026-07-24.

**Fact — what exists.** `tiler-metal` grew from a six-line stub into four modules: `target.rs` (explicit Metal target facts), `emit.rs` (the translation itself), `record.rs` (the emitted translation unit, its entry points, and its binding tables), and `diagnostic.rs` (typed fail-closed rejections), plus `src/tests.rs` and four checked-in golden fixtures under `crates/tiler-metal/goldens/`. The single entry point is `emit::emit_translation_unit(&[&VerifiedKernel], &MetalTargetFacts) -> Result<MetalTranslationUnit, MetalEmitError>`. No manifest, lockfile, or non-`tiler-metal` source changed; the crate's already-declared `tiler-ir` and `tiler-artifact` dependencies sufficed.

**Fact — the KIR claim holds.** Emission consumes exactly one vocabulary, `OperationView`, plus signature accessors (`buffers`, `admitted_builtins`, `numerical`, `canonical_identity`, `scheduled_region_identity`, `value_type`, `value_constant`, `buffer`). Nothing in `emit.rs` names `ScalarProgram`, `ReductionTopology`, `LogicalAccess`, `BoundsProofKind`, `OwnershipProofKind`, `ExecutionBinding`, `TailPolicy`, `IndexRegion`, or `ScheduledRegion`; a test reads the module's own source and fails if any of those identifiers appear. The tail guard is the IR's `Predicated` region, the reduction order is the IR's `SerialLoop`, the contributor address is the IR's index arithmetic, and the NaN normalization is the IR's `ConvertOp`. There is no branch keyed on kernel kind, so the pointwise and reduction fixtures traverse the same code.

**Fact — determinism.** Entry points are ordered by canonical kernel identity bytes and deduplicated by identity, so the source is a function of the kernel *set*, not the caller's order. Entry-point symbols are `tiler_kernel_<16 hex>` over a FNV-1a digest of the canonical identity, and helper symbols name the exact NaN bit pattern they realize. Local names come from a per-kernel counter advanced by a fixed pre-order walk. Only `BTreeMap`/`BTreeSet` are used. Two independent lowerings of one scheduled region — which carry different global builder ownership tags — emit byte-identical source, which is the test that would catch handle identity leaking into a name.

**Fact — numerics.** `f32` immediates are emitted as `as_type<float>(0x…u)`, never decimal, so no literal parsing can round a declared value. Every arithmetic operation is its own statement, so no contraction can form across two structured operations even under `-ffp-contract=on`. `ConvertOp::CanonicalizeF32Nan` becomes an explicit call to a helper realizing the kernel's own `canonical_arithmetic_nan_bits`. The unit reports the compiler flags it depends on as `MetalNumericalRequirement::{SafeMathMode, NoFloatingPointContraction}` rather than assuming them; `-fmetal-math-fp32-functions` is deliberately not claimed, because this emission contains no accuracy-mode-dependent library call. Emission rejects a canonical pattern that is not a NaN encoding, since the scheduled-region contract does not require one and the helper would otherwise compile and compute the wrong thing.

**Measurement — the emitted MSL compiles.** All four goldens and a four-entry-point portfolio unit compile with `xcrun --sdk macosx metal -Wall -Wextra -target air64-apple-macos13.0 -std=metal3.1 -O2 -fmetal-math-mode=safe -fmetal-math-fp32-functions=precise -ffp-contract=off`, with zero diagnostics, on Metal 32023.883 — the exact toolchain row the Metal backend contract governs. The portfolio also links: `xcrun metallib` produces a 14,620-byte library containing all four `tiler_kernel_*` symbols. Two language facts were measured the same way: declaring `[[thread_position_in_grid]]` as `ulong` is rejected (`type 'ulong' … is not valid for attribute`), which is why the launch index is widened explicitly from `uint`; and an unused `static inline` helper warns under `-Wall`, which is why helpers are collected on demand rather than emitted unconditionally.

**Measurement boundary — this is not in the gate.** Those compilations were run by hand from a shell. `tiler-metal` does not depend on `tiler-metal-aot`, because adding that dependency would edit the workspace manifest and `Cargo.lock`, both outside this ticket's `implementation/metal` scope. Nothing in `scripts/check_repository.py` invokes `xcrun`. A passing golden test therefore proves only byte-stability and structure; it is not evidence that any future emitted kernel compiles. Wiring the goldens through the AOT driver is the natural next validation and needs either a scope addition (`implementation/metal-aot`, `implementation/workspace`, `implementation/cargo-lock`) or a follow-up ticket. `scripts/check_workspace.py` pins the exact integration-test target set per package and has no entry for `tiler-metal`, so every test here is a unit test inside `src/`; adding `crates/tiler-metal/tests/*.rs` also requires the workspace scope.

**Fact — a KIR gap: a buffer handle has no recoverable signature ordinal.** `OperationView::Load`/`Store` name a `VerifiedBufferId`, but `VerifiedKernel::buffers()` yields `BufferParameter` values only, `VerifiedBufferId` exposes no ordinal, and no accessor pairs the two. A backend therefore cannot map a load's buffer handle to its `[[buffer(N)]]` position. Matching by parameter value is unsound (two parameters can be value-identical at different indices), and relying on the derived `Ord` to mean ordinal order depends on an undocumented field layout. This backend instead assigns argument-table indices in body first-use order, reports exactly the table it emitted, and rejects a signature with a parameter the body never references (`MalformedKernel { rule: "unreferenced-buffer-parameter" }`) because such a parameter has no position to occupy. The emitted subscripts and the reported table therefore agree by construction, but the index is the backend's choice rather than the IR's. **Proposal.** `tiler_ir::kernel` should expose the pairing — `buffers()` yielding `(VerifiedBufferId, BufferParameter)`, or a `buffer_id(position: usize) -> Option<VerifiedBufferId>` — after which this backend can use the signature ordinal directly and drop both the first-use assignment and the unreferenced-parameter rejection.

**Fact — a second KIR gap: `MemoryScope` cannot name subgroup visibility.** `ExecutionScope` has a `Subgroup` variant but `MemoryScope` has only `Workgroup` and `Device`. MSL's `simdgroup_barrier` establishes SIMD-group visibility, which no governed memory scope can express, and no in-kernel Metal barrier establishes device-wide visibility. Every `ExecutionScope::Subgroup` barrier is therefore rejected today as `BarrierRejection::MemoryVisibility`. This is a gap in the portable vocabulary, not a Metal limitation, and it is reserved-and-rejected rather than silently widened to a workgroup claim. **Proposal.** Add `MemoryScope::Subgroup` when subgroup collectives enter the profile.

**Inference — the `#[non_exhaustive]` discipline pays off here.** Every governed `tiler_ir::kernel` enum is `#[non_exhaustive]`, so this backend must carry an explicit reject-unknown arm for each; a widened IR reaching an already-compiled backend fails closed with a typed rejection instead of being mis-emitted. Conversely `NumericalPermission` and `SubnormalMode` are *not* `#[non_exhaustive]`, so `realization_requirements` matches them exhaustively and widening the numerical contract becomes a compile error at that exact site. Both properties are deliberate; the asymmetry is the one ADR 0074 §5 records.

**Fact — what is reserved but unreachable.** Barriers, `AddressSpace::Constant` parameters, and `KernelType::Bool` immediates have mappings and unit tests but no end-to-end coverage, because the bounded profile's verifier admits neither a barrier nor a constant-space buffer. Their rejections and spellings are tested through the crate-private mapping functions. Unknown-variant rejections (`UnrecognizedOperation`, `UnsupportedType`, and the `UnsupportedOperation` family arms) cannot be tested at all: the variants do not exist yet.

**Proposal — open for review.** The whole public surface of `tiler-metal` is a draft under ADR 0074 §7, and every module says so in its documentation. Three items need Tom's decision in particular: whether `emit_translation_unit`'s portfolio form (`&[&VerifiedKernel]`) is the right shape; whether `MetalTargetFacts::buffer_binding_limit` belongs on an emitter input at all, given that the Metal backend contract assigns resource limits to the target-profile authority; and whether `MslLanguageVersion`, `MetalPlatform`, and `MetalDeploymentMinimum` should stay duplicated here and in `tiler_metal_aot::input` or move to one shared owner. The duplication is currently forced: `tiler-metal-aot` has no dependencies by design, and an MSL language version is not target-neutral enough for `tiler-artifact`.
