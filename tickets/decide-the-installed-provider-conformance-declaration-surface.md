# Decide the installed-provider conformance declaration surface

## Goal

A decision-ready public-boundary packet for binding provider-owned subjects and obligations to the exact dynamic provider environment one compilation actually receives.

## Work

1. Read the complete lowering and physical-provider traits, installation, registry freezing, compilation, selection, explain, receipt, and external-provider conformance-harness paths.
2. Separate the impossible global universe of every linkable third-party provider from the exact configured universe of one frozen request.
3. Compare a required provider declaration method, a declaration passed separately at installation, an owner adapter, a compilation-emitted snapshot, governed-only qualification, and deferral.
4. Eliminate any option that lets provider provenance disagree with declaration identity, lets a caller substitute a manifest, exposes executable implementations, or makes self-declared evidence authoritative.
5. State compatibility for existing implementers, strategy enumeration, revision rules, receipt binding, host/memory cost, and typed refusal for undeclared providers.
6. Reconcile this subject-declaration need with the separate ticket that currently recommends no public third-party conformance harness facade.

## Non-goals

- Do not implement a trait method or accessor.
- Do not expose the conformance harness, planner internals, or rendered explain schema.
- Do not claim a provider declaration is evidence that its implementation is correct.

## Stop conditions

Stop for Tom on every public trait/type/method change. Stop if arbitrary configured environments cannot be qualified without inventing authority or if the prior provider-harness decision conflicts materially.

## Acceptance

- The exact configured denominator is bound to installation and compilation receipts.
- Governed-only and arbitrary-provider profile consequences are explicit.
- Every survivor is top-tier on correctness and fail-closed strictness.
- The packet asks one concrete public-boundary question if a real frontier remains.
