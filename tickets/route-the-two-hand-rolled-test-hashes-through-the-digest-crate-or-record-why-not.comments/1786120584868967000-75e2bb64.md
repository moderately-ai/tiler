The population is three, not two, and the closing check does not reach the third.

Found 2026-08-07 by `survey-what-belongs-in-the-conformance-crate` while reading the conformance candidates. Reproduce:

```sh
grep -rn "fn sha256_hex" crates/ prototypes/
```

It returns `crates/tiler-compiler/src/governed/contraction_conformance.rs:612`, `crates/tiler-reference/tests/contraction_profile_cells.rs:1114`, and **`prototypes/serial-sum-run/src/proof.rs:4132`** — a third transcription of the same FIPS 180-4 body, carrying the same self-check against the two published vectors. This ticket's `Closes when` greps `crates/` only, so the third copy satisfies it while still existing.

Two consequences for whoever takes this:

- **The scopes are short by one.** The third site is under `prototypes/serial-sum-run/**`, which is `implementation/runtime`. This ticket declares `implementation/compiler` and `implementation/reference`.
- **`tiler-digest` cannot absorb any of the three, and that is by its own charter rather than by oversight.** Its rustdoc states there is deliberately no entry point that digests an arbitrary run of bytes: `DigestAlgorithm::digest` takes one domain separator and one run, and `digest_qualified` takes a domain, fixed-width qualifiers, and one trailing run. All three sites reproduce a bare `CC_SHA256` over the raw little-endian `f32` bytes of a device result, in the probe's own undomained digest domain, so routing them through `tiler_digest` would either change the value or require widening a surface the crate refuses on purpose. The reachable answer is a `sha2` dev-dependency at each site, or the recorded reason this ticket's second arm allows.

Suggested edits, left to the ticket's owner rather than applied here: add `implementation/runtime` to the scopes, name the third site in the finding, and change the closing grep to `grep -rn "fn sha256\|Sha256::" crates/ prototypes/ | grep -v tiler-digest`.
