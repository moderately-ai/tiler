## User-visible outcome

The question [ADR 0095](../docs/decisions/0095-decline-a-distributivity-permission.md) answered is put to Tom again against evidence that did not exist when he answered it, so that the flash-class one-pass softmax is either reachable under a stated permission or is refused for a reason that survives the new caller. **This is Tom's decision and nothing here presumes it.** A reaffirmed decline is a complete and useful outcome, and it would then be a decline that has answered its counterexample rather than one that never met it.

## Why this exists

**Fact.** ADR 0095 declined a distributivity permission, and its load-bearing ground was stated plainly: "**No caller exists.** A permission's whole content is that a caller may authorize a freedom; admitting one nobody can spend adds a dimension to every contract … while changing no program's meaning and enabling no rewrite anyone has asked for." The record also states that the vocabulary carrying no caller-less permissions is "the principle the decline rests on rather than a cost estimate".

**Inference, derived in [the certified-bounds record](../docs/research/numerics/certified-bounds-as-rewrite-permissions.md) and reproducible from its Part 2.** The online-softmax rescaling fold — Algorithm 3 of Milakov and Gimelshein, the fold every flash-class attention kernel uses — consumes distributivity. Unrolling `d_j = d_{j-1} * exp(m_{j-1} - m_j) + exp(x_j - m_j)` gives a Horner nesting whose expansion is a sum of products, so reaching it from `sum_j exp(x_j - m_V)` requires exchanging a product of a sum for a sum of products. That is ADR 0080's dimension exactly. It is therefore **not** a reassociation and not an operand permutation, and under [ADR 0011](../docs/decisions/0011-per-operation-numerical-permissions.md) no existing permission reaches it: the rewrite is illegal under every numerical contract Tiler can currently express.

**So a caller exists for the dimension.** That does not make the decline wrong — it removes the premise the decline was argued from, which is a different and weaker claim, and the difference is the whole reason this is a ticket rather than a supersession.

## What is NOT claimed

**ADR 0095's reopening trigger did not fire, and this ticket does not assert that it did.** That trigger is "the first workload whose natural spelling is a directly regroupable **contraction chain**", with the attention block's operations 19–21 as the worked negative case. The softmax normalizer is an elementwise-scaled reduction, not a contraction chain, so it sits outside the trigger's stated subject rather than being a candidate the trigger admits. Do not open this by claiming the trigger fired; open it by noting that the trigger and the decline's *ground* are two different things, and it is the ground that this evidence reaches.

## What this ticket must produce

- The one atomic question for Tom, with a small concrete tensor-program example — the softmax normalizer at a stated `V` — showing what admitting the permission enables and what declining it forecloses, per AGENTS.md's decision-escalation shape.
- **The price, which is what makes this decision priceable rather than a matter of architectural taste.** The certified-bounds record derives the rewrite's worst-case cost as `(V-1)(u + eps_exp)` to first order, about `2^-14` at `V = 512` in binary32 with a correctly rounded `exp`, and measures it against 22 adversarial cases. A permission whose numerical cost is bounded and small is a different proposition from one whose cost is unknown, and ADR 0095 was decided without that number.
- The re-run elimination against ADR 0095's other three arguments, each of which stands or falls independently: that admitting later is additive and costs nothing now; that a permission admitted without evidence would have to guess its own shape (one permission or two, which [`decide-whether-distributivity-directions-share-one-permission`](decide-whether-distributivity-directions-share-one-permission.md) owns and which becomes live under an admission); and that contraction ordering stays a planning question. **The additive argument is the strongest survivor and it may well decide this again** — if admitting later costs nothing, waiting for the rewrite to be wanted rather than merely derivable is still defensible.
- A second freedom this rewrite consumes that no dimension names, tracked separately at [`name-the-elementary-identity-rewrite-dimension`](name-the-elementary-identity-rewrite-dimension.md). **Both gates must open before the rewrite is legal**, so an admission here alone does not make the softmax rewrite reachable, and the question to Tom must say so or it overstates what it buys.

## Reassessment packet — 2026-08-06

**Nothing below is a decision, and nothing below presumes one.** This packet is prepared for Tom under AGENTS.md's explain-then-recommend shape: it states the question, works the example, labels what has changed since [ADR 0095](../docs/decisions/0095-decline-a-distributivity-permission.md) by evidence class, tests ADR 0095's own grounds one at a time against that evidence, prices each of the four joint outcomes, gives point and counterpoint, and closes with **the worker's** recommendation. A reaffirmed decline is a complete outcome and the recommendation below is one; the ticket's own outcome text says so, and the recommendation is written so that Tom can refute the derivation rather than only the conclusion.

### The question, stated atomically

**Does the online-softmax rescaling fold change the answer ADR 0095 gave?** Concretely: admit a distributivity permission — which, to make the fold reachable, means admitting it *together with* [ADR 0101](../docs/decisions/0101-treat-elementary-function-identities-as-a-fourth-numerical-dimension.md)'s elementary-identity permission in one identity-domain step — or reaffirm the decline with the caller argument answered rather than assumed?

### The example, at a stated `V`

A caller states one softmax row as registered semantic operations. `tiler::softmax-f32@1`'s pinned meaning is the two-pass safe form, `V = 512` contributors in binary32:

```text
x : [512] f32                       one row of logits
m  = max_k x[k]                     reduction
d  = sum_j exp(x[j] - m)            reduction over an elementwise-scaled exponential
y[i] = exp(x[i] - m) / d
```

The rewrite every flash-class attention kernel performs is Milakov and Gimelshein's Algorithm 3, one pass carrying the pair `(m_j, d_j)`:

```text
m_j <- max(m_{j-1}, x_j)
d_j <- d_{j-1} * exp(m_{j-1} - m_j) + exp(x_j - m_j)
```

Their Theorem 1 proves the two compute the same `m_V` and `d_V` **over the reals**. In floating point they are different computations, and reaching the second from the first consumes exactly two freedoms:

- **Distributivity**, [ADR 0080](../docs/decisions/0080-treat-distributivity-as-a-third-numerical-dimension.md)'s dimension. The fold is a Horner nesting whose unrolling is `d_V = sum_j [ exp(x_j - m_j) * prod_{k>j} exp(m_{k-1} - m_k) ]`; reaching it from `sum_j exp(x_j - m_V)` exchanges a product of a sum for a sum of products. The two contributor sequences share no floating-point value, so no reassociation and no permutation permission reaches it.
- **Elementary-function identity**, [ADR 0101](../docs/decisions/0101-treat-elementary-function-identities-as-a-fourth-numerical-dimension.md)'s dimension, accepted 2026-08-06. The equality of the two forms rests on telescoping `exp(x_j - m_j) * exp(m_j - m_V) = exp(x_j - m_V)`, a functional equation of the exponential that floating point does not satisfy.

**Both gates are conjunctive.** An admission on this ticket alone leaves the fold refused, naming the missing elementary-identity dimension. That is the third row of the table below and it is the row a "yes" that reads only this ticket's title lands in.

**The price, which is what makes this priceable rather than a matter of taste.** [The certified-bounds record](../docs/research/numerics/certified-bounds-as-rewrite-permissions.md)'s Part 2 derives the online fold's worst-case relative cost over the two-pass fold as `(1 + eps_exp)^(V-1) * (1 + gamma_{2(V-1)}) / (1 + gamma_{V-1}) - 1`, first order `(V-1)(u + eps_exp)`. **The logit spread cancels** — it appears in each fold's own bound and not in their difference — so the price is a function of contributor count, format, and the target's declared `exp` accuracy, and of no input value. At `V = 512`, binary32, `eps_exp = u` (a correctly rounded `exp`): `P = 6.09e-5`, about `2^-14`. A caller stating a `2^-13` relative tolerance would admit it; one stating `2^-16` would refuse it.

### ADR 0095's reopening trigger, tested against its own words

ADR 0095's trigger reads: "**The first workload whose natural spelling is a directly regroupable contraction chain — one where the regrouping that consumes distributivity is what the workload asks for, rather than one an optimizer might speculatively want.** The distinguishing test is stated so it can be applied rather than argued: a workload that merely *contains* a contraction chain does not fire it; the chain must be one whose profitable form requires exchanging a product of a sum for a sum of products. The attention block's operations 19 through 21 are the worked negative case … and a candidate that cannot be distinguished from it does not fire the trigger either."

**The subject clause excludes the softmax fold outright.** The trigger's subject is a *contraction chain*. The softmax normalizer is a reduction over an elementwise-scaled exponential; it is not a contraction chain, so it is neither a candidate the trigger admits nor a candidate resembling the worked negative case. It is outside the trigger's stated subject rather than failing its discriminator.

**The discriminator clause is genuinely ambiguous here, and the ambiguity does not resolve to "fired".** Read one way, "the workload" is the published algorithm class, and every flash-class attention kernel's natural spelling *is* the online fold, so the regroup is what the workload asks for. Read the other way, "the workload" is the program a caller states to Tiler, and a caller writing attention in the registered semantic operations writes `tiler::softmax-f32@1`, whose pinned meaning is the two-pass form — making the online fold precisely "one an optimizer might speculatively want". The second reading is the one Tiler's own boundary supports, since the trigger is about what a *workload* spells and Tiler's workloads are semantic programs. Neither reading rescues the subject clause.

**So the trigger did not fire, and this packet does not claim it did.** What the reassessment stands on instead is that ADR 0095's *ground* and its *trigger* are two different things, and the ground is what this evidence reaches. ADR 0095 nowhere states that its trigger is the only thing that reopens it; it states its grounds "so the elimination can be refuted rather than only the conclusion", which is an invitation to test the grounds directly. That is what the next section does.

### ADR 0095's grounds, tested one at a time

**Ground 1 — "No caller exists." Answered, and it was the load-bearing one — but its literal form survives in a narrowed version that cuts the other way.** ADR 0095's sentence is that "admitting one nobody can spend adds a dimension to every contract … while changing no program's meaning and enabling no rewrite anyone has asked for". A caller for the *dimension* now exists and is derived rather than speculated, so the premise as ADR 0095 wrote it is refuted. **But the narrowed claim survives intact: nobody can spend a distributivity permission admitted alone**, because the same and only identified rewrite independently consumes ADR 0101's dimension, for which no permission is admitted. A distributivity-only admission changes no program's meaning and enables no rewrite — which is ADR 0095's own sentence, still true, now for a different reason. This is the sharpest thing in the packet and it bears specifically against outcome 3 below, not against outcome 4.

**Ground 2 — "The pinned workload contains no chain the regroup is asked for." Unaffected in its own subject, and no longer load-bearing.** The SwiGLU analysis (a SiLU and an elementwise multiply between its contractions, so no regroup is statable) and the attention operations 19–21 analysis (a regroupable pair whose factored form contracts `V` against a `W_o` slice per grouped head, work the direct order never performs) are both untouched by this evidence, and both remain correct. What changed is their *function*: they were offered in support of ground 1, whose subject is the dimension rather than contraction chains, and the dimension acquired a caller elsewhere. Do not read this as softened — the contraction-chain half of ADR 0095's reasoning survives entirely, and outcome 3 below is the only one that acts on it.

**Ground 3 — "Admitting later is additive and costs nothing now." Survives, and it is the strongest survivor.** Nothing in the new evidence reverses it. The derivation, the definition, and the rejection wording are still exactly the material an admission would build on, and the corrected registered fact and the derived admission-rule shape are two further pieces of that material that now exist. Two refinements, neither of which weakens it: (i) "costs nothing now" is a property of the *decline*, and admitting is never free — it is an identity-domain step at both widths whenever it is taken, so the choice is between paying now and paying later, not between paying and not; (ii) admitting both dimensions in one step costs one identity-domain step where admitting them sequentially costs two, which is an argument about *sequencing* that applies equally to "both now" and "both later, together".

**Ground 4 — "A permission admitted without evidence would have to guess its own shape." Partly answered; its core is unaffected.** What is answered: the corpus now has a derived default. ADR 0101 decision 3 refused the analogous directional cut for elementary identity on ADR 0014's capability-asymmetry standard, citing ADR 0095's own "arbitrary cut presented as caution", so "one permission covering both directions" is the derived answer under absence of asymmetry evidence rather than a coin flip, and [`decide-whether-distributivity-directions-share-one-permission`](decide-whether-distributivity-directions-share-one-permission.md) would open with a default to test rather than an open field. What is unaffected: no capability asymmetry between factoring and expansion has been established, and the caller identifying *which* direction it needs is not evidence that the two are separately capable. The guess is smaller than it was; it is still a guess.

**Decision clause 3 — "Contraction ordering remains a planning question within one semantic contraction." Wholly unaffected under every outcome.** The softmax caller says nothing about contraction ordering, and ADR 0087's keyed family is untouched by anything here.

**Outcome 2 — admit elementary identity alone.** *Enables:* nothing. It pays a full identity-domain step to authorize a rewrite this record's decline independently refuses. **Outcome 4 — admit both, as one step.** *Enables:* the online-softmax fold becomes reachable, subject to the derived bound, the certified-bounds record's five admission obligations, and ADR 0101 decision 7's sixth (that the rewritten program's elementary arguments are proved to lie inside every accuracy clause's declared domain — for this fold the discharge is short and stated in the elementary record's Part 4: the running maximum is non-decreasing and includes `x_j`, so both `x_j - m_j` and `m_{j-1} - m_j` stay non-positive). Contraction-chain exploration becomes legal in the same step. *Costs:* everything below, doubled in dimension count and quadrupled in injectivity space, plus three open prerequisites named in the gaps section.

**The identity-domain price, counted rather than waved at.** A permission is a twelfth (or, for outcome 4, a twelfth and thirteenth) entry in the canonical dimension vector, and ADR 0101's Consequences enumerate the consequences of exactly that step:

### Point and counterpoint

**Point, for admitting (outcome 4).** The ground the decline was argued from has met its counterexample. The rewrite is not speculative: it is the single most-wanted rewrite in this problem space, it has a standing p2 owner, and two of its three architectural components have landed in the last week. Its price is derived in closed form, measured against 22 adversarial cases, small at the shapes attention uses (`2^-14` at `V = 512`), and — the load-bearing property — instantiable at compile time from a shape and a target profile with no knowledge of any input value, which is exactly what an admission rule inside a search needs and what a per-instance analyzer provably cannot deliver at the cost the search imposes. The slot the permission would occupy is designed: the admission rule's position, shape, three-way answer, fail-closed direction, and six obligations are all derived. And the sequencing argument is real: because ADR 0101's permission is parked behind this decision, admitting both together costs one identity-domain step where admitting them apart costs two, and outcome 3 taken alone buys the smaller of the two gains at the full price.

**Counterpoint, for reaffirming (outcome 1).** Ground 3 is untouched, and it is the argument that already decided this once: nothing about admitting today is cheaper than admitting on the day a rule exists to consume the permission, and several things are more expensive. The permission would be admitted with no rewrite rule anywhere in the tree to spend it, no operation capability to declare the algebraic property, no numeric `eps_exp` retrievable to instantiate the bound (the record's own trust-boundary section names a plausible hard-coded constant as "the one a reviewer is likeliest to wave through"), a bound derived only for the sequential fold when every real flash kernel merges `(m, d)` pairs in a *tree* — a case the record states does not transfer by substituting the height — and a target-profile declaration resting on zero measurements. Nothing establishes that the rewrite is even profitable; the certified-bounds record says so in its own closing section, and the cost authority that would prefer the rewritten candidate is a bounded structural model with no calibration behind it. **So ADR 0095's principle survives under a new fact pattern rather than being defeated by it:** the permission is unspendable today, not because no caller was ever identified, but because the caller cannot yet be built — and that is a strictly better-evidenced version of the same refusal.

**The counterpoint's own weakness, stated so it can be weighed rather than accepted.** "Wait until the rule exists" risks the ordering problem the elementary record names: a rule cannot be built against a permission that does not exist, so someone eventually has to move first. The honest reply is that the ordering is not symmetric — the *bound*, the *rule shape*, the *obligations*, and the *refusal wording* can all be built and have been, entirely under a decline, and only the final contract field requires the decision. That is the sequence the corpus has actually followed.
