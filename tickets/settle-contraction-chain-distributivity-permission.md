`docs/compiler/optimizer.md` now admits the tensor-contraction association rewrite only under an effective reassociation permission, and the Milestone 6 framing in `docs/roadmap.md` calls regrouping `(AB)C` into `A(BC)` a floating-point reassociation in ADR 0014's sense. That makes the reassociation permission necessary. It does not establish that it is sufficient.

`docs/numerical-semantics.md` defines reassociation as changing grouping while preserving logical operand order, and states the reduction-order contract over one reduction's contributors. A contraction-chain regroup is stronger than that. For output `[i, l]`, `(AB)C` forms the rounded partial `T[i, k] = sum over j of A[i, j] * B[j, k]` and then multiplies `T[i, k]` by `C[k, l]`; `A(BC)` never forms those products at all. The two agree over the reals by distributivity, which floating-point multiplication does not satisfy, so the rewrite redistributes products across sums rather than only regrouping one sum's contributors.

Decide whether the reassociation permission covers this, or whether a contraction chain declares a distinct algebraic capability and consumes a distinct permission alongside it. `docs/numerical-semantics.md` is the normative owner and would carry the answer; the logical-exploration rule in `docs/compiler/optimizer.md` and the future contraction schedules in `docs/compiler/fusion-and-scheduling.md` cite it and would be updated on closure. Until then the optimizer rule fails closed rather than assuming the weaker permission covers it.

Nothing observable depends on this today: every numerical contract the compiler registers — `StrictF32NumericalContract::governed_profile` in `crates/tiler-compiler/src/request.rs` — sets `reassociation` to `NumericalPermission::Forbidden`. **Corrected by `correct-the-surviving-stale-one-contract-claims`.** This previously read that `governed` is the *only* contract the compiler registers. That was true when written and stopped being so on 2026-07-25 at 08:12, when `aa7c4f0` unified admission behind `governed_profile` and registered `governed_flush_to_zero` beside it. The conclusion is unaffected because both entries forbid reassociation; stating the invariant over the profile rather than counting the constants is what keeps it from going stale a third time. The question becomes reachable when a contract permitting reassociation is registered, or when the semantic half of Q-SEM-015 admits a tensor-contraction family, whichever comes first.

"Contraction" is the tensor sense throughout — summation over indices shared by two or more operands — and never ADR 0015's fused-multiply-add permission.

Found by `qualify-contraction-association-reassociation-permission`, which owns the optimizer rule but does not hold `contracts/numerics`.

## Outcome

A correctness-derived contract update. Reassociation permission does not authorize a contraction-chain regroup, and the answer follows from the contract's own definitions rather than from a preference.

**Fact — the definition is not broader than this ticket assumed, checked by reading rather than by grep.** `docs/numerical-semantics.md` defines the two order-contract dimensions as "**reassociation** changes grouping while preserving logical operand order" and "**permutation** changes logical operand order". Two further sentences in the same section fix the scope: "Reassociation without permutation may combine only contiguous contributor intervals in order", which presupposes a fixed contributor *sequence* to take intervals of, and the exact-transformation rule that normalization "must not reorder floating-point operations merely because they are associative over real numbers", which names associativity specifically. ADR 0014's context states the same transform in scalar terms, `(a + b) + c` to `a + (b + c)` — three operands, same order, different grouping. Every reading available in the corpus is the narrow one.

**Inference — a chain regroup is not a regrouping of any contributor sequence.** For output `[i, l]`, `(AB)C` sums the contributors `T[i, k] * C[k, l]` over `k`, where `T[i, k] = sum over j of A[i, j] * B[j, k]`; `A(BC)` sums the contributors `A[i, j] * U[j, l]` over `j`, where `U[j, l] = sum over k of B[j, k] * C[k, l]`. The two sequences share no value, have different lengths in general, and are indexed by different axes, so neither is a grouping of the other and no common sequence exists of which both are groupings. Not one rounded product is shared: the first forms `A[i, j] * B[j, k]` and `T[i, k] * C[k, l]`, the second forms `B[j, k] * C[k, l]` and `A[i, j] * U[j, l]`. The identity relating them is distributivity, `(x + y) * c = x * c + y * c`, which round-to-nearest floating-point multiplication does not satisfy.

**Inference — the conclusion is independent of the multi-operand-node choice `docs/roadmap.md` reserves for Tom.** Were a multi-operand contraction node defined as the flat sum over `(j, k)` of `A[i, j] * B[j, k] * C[k, l]`, its contributors would be triple products that neither association ever computes, and factoring `C[k, l]` out of the `j`-sum would again be distributivity. So settling this does not preempt that reserved decision.

**Inference — distributivity is additional to reassociation and permutation, not a substitute.** Routed through the flat form, the chain regroup also changes the nesting order over the flat reduction domain, and grouping the canonical lexicographic contributor order by the outer axis combines non-contiguous intervals — which the contract's own rule assigns to permutation as well. The predecessor's "necessary but not sufficient" therefore resolves to: reassociation is necessary, permutation is generally necessary, and a third dimension that does not exist in the vocabulary is necessary.

**Inference — that third dimension is forced to be distinct by accepted decisions, so its shape is not a preference either.** ADR 0011 requires every semantic rewrite to declare which effective permission it consumes, and `docs/numerical-semantics.md` states that one permission never implies another. ADR 0015 settles the structurally identical question for fused multiply-add: "Contraction permission is independent of reassociation and does not authorize regrouping an expression to create additional contraction opportunities" — a permission over an existing pattern does not authorize manufacturing a new one. Reusing reassociation to authorize distributivity would contradict all three.

**Fact — the observability boundary is sharper than this ticket stated.** No contract the compiler registers permits reassociation: every member of `StrictF32NumericalContract::governed_profile` sets it to `Forbidden`. `normalize_serial_sum` in `crates/tiler-compiler/src/request.rs` independently rejects any program whose `input_count()` is not exactly `1`. The rule is unreachable in three independent ways, and none of them is why it fails closed. **Corrected by `correct-the-surviving-stale-one-contract-claims`.** This previously read that `NumericalPermission` in `crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/numerics.rs` "has exactly one variant, `Forbidden`", so no contract Tiler can *express* permits reassociation. That was true when written and was falsified by `widen-numerical-vocabulary-and-complete-identity`, which added `Permitted` under ADR 0076 item 1 — changing which contracts are expressible, not which the compiler registers. The unreachability conclusion survives on the registered set rather than on the vocabulary, which is the invariant a widened enum cannot silently falsify.

The contract changes accordingly. `docs/numerical-semantics.md` gains a `Distributivity is outside the order contract` subsection under Reductions that carries the worked counterexample, names the dimension, derives its independence, and records that no permission grants it — so the rewrite is rejected under every registrable contract and its rejection names the missing dimension rather than reporting a forbidden reassociation, which would be inaccurate and would imply a reassociation-permitting contract admits it. Two adjacent misreadings are closed in the same document: the relaxed-policy list now says each name means the dimension this document defines rather than a broader vendor reading of the same word, and the fused-multiply-add section now states that ADR 0015's permission governs exactly one thing about a *tensor* contraction, its per-contributor `accumulator + a * b` rounding, and says nothing about that reduction's order.

`docs/compiler/optimizer.md` moves from pending to settled: the third logical-exploration rule now names all three permissions it consumes, the residue paragraph records the classification and the required rejection wording, the `StrictF32NumericalContract` paragraph adds the one-variant and one-input facts (the one-variant half was later removed by `correct-the-optimizer-one-variant-permission-claim` and the paragraph now rests on `governed_profile`), and the equivalent-expressions example notes that no expressible policy holds for the third group, so it names a reserved equivalence group rather than an available one. `docs/compiler/fusion-and-scheduling.md` gains the matching statement that a schedule may not select a contraction order at all today, and that this is a legality boundary rather than an unimplemented search.

What is *not* settled here is a product choice and was deliberately left to Tom: whether to admit a distributivity permission at all, and if so whether one permission covers both directions of the identity or the factoring and expanding directions are cut apart. ADR 0014 split reassociation from permutation on the evidence that some combiners have one capability and not the other; no analogous asymmetry has been established here, so cutting the dimension in two is not derivable. [`decide-whether-to-admit-a-distributivity-permission`](decide-whether-to-admit-a-distributivity-permission.md) carries both questions with the trigger — the accepted decision admitting a tensor-contraction family, or any earlier proposal to register a reassociation-permitting contract.

An ADR is needed and is not written here, because this ticket does not hold `contracts/decisions`. It should decide that a contraction-chain regroup consumes distributivity as a numerical dimension independent of reassociation and operand permutation, that no permission currently grants it and the rewrite therefore rejects explicitly naming it, and that admitting such a permission is reserved to the decision that admits a tensor-contraction family. It sits directly alongside ADR 0014 and ADR 0015 in the `numerical-operations` catalog group, applies to `tiler.contract.numerical-semantics`, and supersedes nothing — neither ADR claims exhaustiveness over the dimension set, so no accepted decision is contradicted.

Two documents outside this ticket's scopes state the superseded reading and need routing. `docs/roadmap.md` (`contracts/navigation`) line 316 infers that regrouping `(AB)C` into `A(BC)` "is a floating-point reassociation in ADR 0014's sense and requires both an operation capability supporting regrouping and an effective numerical permission to use it"; that inference is now known to be incomplete and should name distributivity. `docs/open-questions.md` (`contracts/navigation`) Q-SEM-015 lists two choices the framing reserves for Tom and should list the distributivity admission as a third. `docs/glossary.md` and `docs/ir.md` were checked and need no change: both enumerate permissions with "such as" or an open list rather than asserting sufficiency.
