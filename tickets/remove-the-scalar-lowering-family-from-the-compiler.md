## User-visible outcome

`tiler_compiler::capability` registers exactly one lowering family, and it is the one the compile path resolves. The public seam that today registers, resolves, and is tested while nothing on the compile path reaches it is gone, and the registry's own mechanics keep every test they have.

## Authority

[ADR 0105](../docs/decisions/0105-retire-the-scalar-lowering-provider-seam.md), accepted by Tom on 2026-08-06 (provenance in [`accept-adr-0105-retire-the-scalar-lowering-seam`](accept-adr-0105-retire-the-scalar-lowering-seam.md)), decides the retirement and supersedes [ADR 0078](../docs/decisions/0078-name-the-intended-public-extension-seams.md)'s item-2 row for `ScalarLoweringProvider`. That record's decision 3 is what this ticket executes; its decision 4 is what this ticket must **not** execute. The derivation — the two-candidate elimination, the law-comparison argument, and the symmetric question showing why the index-access seam is not retired by the same reasoning — is [`resolve-or-retire-the-scalar-lowering-provider-seam`](resolve-or-retire-the-scalar-lowering-provider-seam.md)'s Outcome, and the three findings below are carried verbatim from it because each is normative rather than descriptive.

This ticket is unblocked: the acceptance node is `done`, not `awaiting-decision`, so nothing is pending.

## The normative findings, verbatim from the deriving ticket

**Fact — the removal is not thirteen deleted tests, and reading it that way would be the mistake.** `capability.rs`'s test module holds fifteen `register_scalar_lowering` sites and six `resolve_scalar_lowering` sites, and the scalar family is the *vehicle* for ten tests of the registry's own mechanics rather than their subject — each would read identically against any family: `snapshot_identity_is_independent_of_registration_order`, `duplicate_registration_of_one_provider_is_a_collision`, `one_operation_admits_more_than_one_registrable_signature`, `a_second_signature_for_one_family_and_operation_is_refused`, `contradictory_providers_resolve_to_a_deterministic_ambiguity`, `two_revisions_of_one_provider_resolve_to_an_ambiguity`, `a_missing_capability_resolves_to_a_typed_diagnostic`, `registration_rejects_an_operation_without_semantic_authority`, `registration_is_transactional_and_leaves_no_partial_state`, and `capability_revision_participates_in_snapshot_identity`. Every one of those must be **ported** to `register_index_access`, not deleted; several are load-bearing for ADR 0072 (`two_revisions_of_one_provider_resolve_to_an_ambiguity` is cited in ADR 0078 item 3 with its own coverage measurement). Only three are genuinely about the scalar family and go with it: `registers_two_families_and_resolves_each_to_its_provider` narrows to one family, `a_resolved_scalar_provider_emits_through_the_canonical_builder` goes, and `legality::tests::a_scalar_lowering_capability_is_not_an_index_refinement` goes together with the `RefinementError::WrongFamily` variant it is the sole constructor of. A retirement that deleted the ported ten would silently drop the registry's collision, ambiguity, and transactionality coverage, and the implementation ticket must say so.

**What retirement removes, from a full read of `capability.rs` and `legality.rs`.** `LoweringFamily::ScalarLowering` and its `key_token`, `tag`, and `Display` arms; the `ScalarLoweringProvider` trait; `ScalarLoweringContext` and its five methods; `ScalarLoweringResults`; `LoweringImplementation::ScalarLowering`; `LoweringCapabilityRegistryBuilder::register_scalar_lowering`; `FrozenLoweringCapabilityRegistry::resolve_scalar_lowering`; `ResolvedLoweringCapability::scalar_provider`; and, in `legality.rs`, `RefinementError::WrongFamily` with the now-unsatisfiable family guard at `:760-764`. Two consequential shapes fall out and are the implementation ticket's to decide rather than this record's: `LoweringImplementation` becomes a single-variant enum that may as well be `Arc<dyn IndexAccessLoweringProvider>`, and `LoweringFamily` becomes a single-variant `#[non_exhaustive]` enum whose `key_token` must survive because the governed capability key spells it. Collapsing either is a public-boundary change and is reserved to Tom under ADR 0075, so the record proposes the family's removal and deliberately not the enum's.

**Fact — retirement is identity-preserving, which is the one thing that could have made it expensive and does not.** `encode_capability_key` (`capability.rs:1831-1841`) writes `key.family.tag()`, and `LoweringFamily::IndexAccess` is tag `1`. Removing the `ScalarLowering` variant leaves that tag unchanged, so every frozen registry that exists today encodes to the same `CanonicalLoweringRegistryIdentity` bytes before and after. No ledger, golden, or identity pin is recomputed. `LoweringFamily::key_token` likewise keeps `"index-access"`, so no governed capability key moves.

## What this ticket must not do

**Do not collapse either type that becomes single-variant.** ADR 0105 decision 4 reserves both to Tom under ADR 0075: `LoweringImplementation` becomes a single-variant enum that *could* become a bare `Arc<dyn IndexAccessLoweringProvider>`, and `LoweringFamily` becomes a single-variant `#[non_exhaustive]` enum whose `key_token` must survive because the governed capability key spells it. Leave both standing, in their reduced form, and say so in the Outcome. A second lowering family would want both back.

**Do not recompute any identity pin, ledger, or golden.** The removal is identity-preserving by the Fact above. A pin that moves is evidence the removal was executed wrongly — stop and diagnose rather than regenerate. State the check that nothing moved.

**Do not touch the index-access seam, the semantic-registry seam, or the reference-capability rows.** ADR 0105 changes none of them, and its own alternatives section records why the same argument does not retire the index-access family: it is mandatory, it is the named authority in the artifact construction plan, and it completes an external semantic registration.

`contracts/optimizer` and `contracts/numerics` are held here because the deriving ticket's landed corrections describe a live gap that this ticket closes. `docs/compiler/optimizer.md`'s maturity boundary and `docs/correctness-and-testing.md`'s conformance-gate evidence each state the finding — that the family carries nothing to install and that the removal is routed to Tom. ## Outcome — 2026-08-06, executed

**The inventory is removed and the population is empty.** Everything the deriving ticket enumerated is gone: `LoweringFamily::ScalarLowering` with its `key_token`, `tag`, and `Display` arms; the `ScalarLoweringProvider` trait; `ScalarLoweringContext` and its methods; `ScalarLoweringResults`; `LoweringImplementation::ScalarLowering`; `register_scalar_lowering`; `resolve_scalar_lowering`; `ResolvedLoweringCapability::scalar_provider`; and `legality`'s `RefinementError::WrongFamily` with the family guard `refine_index_region` opened with. `grep -rn 'resolve_scalar_lowering\|register_scalar_lowering\|ScalarLoweringProvider\|ScalarLoweringContext\|ScalarLoweringResults\|WrongFamily' crates/ prototypes/ spikes/` went from **54 hits in 3 files to 0**, and the wider `grep -rn 'ScalarLowering\|scalar_provider' crates/` is also empty. The one site outside `capability.rs` and `legality.rs` was `lowering.rs:538`, a doc sentence naming `register_scalar_lowering` as the public registration route for the governed-key grammar example; it now names `register_index_access`, which is the route that example actually has.

**The reserved types are reduced, not collapsed.** `LoweringFamily` is a single-variant `#[non_exhaustive]` enum whose `key_token` still returns `"index-access"`, and the stored provider handle is still the family-typed `LoweringImplementation` rather than a bare `Arc<dyn IndexAccessLoweringProvider>`. `ResolvedLoweringCapability::index_access_provider` keeps its `Option` return for the same reason — the answer is a family question, and a second family makes `None` reachable again without the signature moving. `tag()` gained a comment recording that tag `2` is spent rather than recyclable, because a recycled tag would let two registries encode alike across a version of the crate.

**Test census: 10 ported, 1 narrowed, 2 deleted; workspace 2891 → 2889, fully accounted.** `cargo nextest list --workspace` diffed before and after shows exactly three lines changed and nothing else in the workspace moving.

The ten registry-mechanics tests were re-expressed against `register_index_access` keeping their names and subjects: `snapshot_identity_is_independent_of_registration_order`, `duplicate_registration_of_one_provider_is_a_collision`, `one_operation_admits_more_than_one_registrable_signature`, `a_second_signature_for_one_family_and_operation_is_refused`, `contradictory_providers_resolve_to_a_deterministic_ambiguity`, `two_revisions_of_one_provider_resolve_to_an_ambiguity`, `a_missing_capability_resolves_to_a_typed_diagnostic`, `registration_rejects_an_operation_without_semantic_authority`, `registration_is_transactional_and_leaves_no_partial_state`, and `capability_revision_participates_in_snapshot_identity`.

Two deleted, each genuinely about the family: `capability::tests::a_resolved_scalar_provider_emits_through_the_canonical_builder`, whose whole body drives a `ScalarLoweringProvider` through `ScalarLoweringContext`; and `legality::tests::a_scalar_lowering_capability_is_not_an_index_refinement`, the sole constructor of `RefinementError::WrongFamily`, deleted with the variant and its fixture `ScalarMultiply`.

**One deviation from the three-deletions reading, stated rather than taken silently.** The deriving ticket and ADR 0105 both count `registers_two_families_and_resolves_each_to_its_provider` among the three that "go with the family", but both spell its disposition as *narrows to one family* rather than *goes*. It was narrowed, not deleted: it is now `registers_a_family_and_resolves_it_to_its_provider` and still asserts `capability_count()`, `family()`, `provider()`, and the provider handle. Deleting it would have left `ResolvedLoweringCapability::family` and `::provider` with no direct assertion anywhere, which is the coverage loss the normative finding exists to prevent — so the literal word was followed over the summary count.

**Two ports changed an assertion's subject and both were watched failing.**

- `two_revisions_of_one_provider_resolve_to_an_ambiguity` is the case ADR 0078 item 3 cites with its own measured coverage claim, and the measurement was re-run on the ported test rather than assumed to survive. Simulating newer-wins inside `resolve` (highest provider revision wins when contenders differ only in revision) makes it **FAIL** — `called Result::unwrap_err() on an Ok value: ResolvedLoweringCapability { ... provider: ProviderIdentity { namespace: "example", name: "aardvark", revision: 2 } }` — while `contradictory_providers_resolve_to_a_deterministic_ambiguity` and `duplicate_registration_of_one_provider_is_a_collision` both stay green. That is the exact discrimination the test's own doc comment claims, preserved across the port. Perturbation reverted.
- `snapshot_identity_is_independent_of_registration_order` needed two capabilities for order to be observable, and with one family they are two *providers* of it (`aardvark`, `zebra`) rather than two families. `capability_count() == 2` was added to both halves so a collapsed population cannot look green, and the assertion was watched failing by dropping the second registration: `assertion left == right failed, left: 1, right: 2`. Perturbation reverted.

`a_second_signature_for_one_family_and_operation_is_refused` lost its third block, the per-*family* probe, because probing it needs two families. The per-provider probe is intact and the whole-value `assert_eq!` on `ConflatedCapabilityKey` now names `LoweringFamily::IndexAccess`. The doc comment records that the family scoping survives in `register`'s comparison with no probe, rather than leaving a reader to infer the guard was weakened.

**Identity verification: nothing moved, and the whole suite is the check rather than the argument.** `encode_capability_key` writes `key.family.tag()` and `LoweringFamily::IndexAccess` is tag `1` before and after, so every frozen registry encodes to the same `CanonicalLoweringRegistryIdentity` bytes. The evidence is that `cargo nextest run --workspace` is **2889 run, 2889 passed, 7 skipped** with no pin, golden, ledger, or descriptor test touched or recomputed — including `the_declared_profile_states_one_barrier_realization`'s byte pin and the artifact/kernel-program identity suites. The before/after test-name diff moving exactly three lines is the second half of that check: no identity-bearing test was renamed, added, or dropped.

**Contracts now state the end state.** `docs/operation-extensions.md`: the status line says the family is *removed from the crate* rather than awaiting removal; the three-claim paragraph, the seam-table `Fact`, and the registry-lifecycle `Fact` all state the removal as landed, the last one naming the ported ten and the two reserved types. `docs/compiler/optimizer.md`: the maturity boundary no longer carries the "registers and resolves and nothing resolves it" sentence and says the installation guarantee covers the whole surface; the following paragraph is now a **Fact** that the family is gone rather than an **Inference** routed to Tom. `docs/correctness-and-testing.md`: the conformance gate's owed-evidence paragraph states that the row is eliminated rather than owed.

The module-header paragraph declaring the family "not covered by that acceptance" now records Tom's 2026-08-06 acceptance under `accept-the-evaluation-order-preservation-target-fact`, in the house form the sibling accepted surfaces use, enumerating the seven accepted items and the four accepted exclusions. All five per-item `**Labelled draft (ADR 0075)**` markers became `**Accepted public surface**`. `grep -rni 'labelled draft\|awaiting a boundary decision' crates/` now returns only the `ScalarArithmetic::new` paragraph, which is a different acceptance node and correctly still a draft. **Nothing to route to `implementation/build`:** no draft label for this family exists in `crates/tiler-build/src/metal_declaration.rs` — its three evaluation-order mentions are a test and its diagnostic strings.
