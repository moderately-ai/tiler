## User-visible outcome

A CPU backend can consume schedules that express fixed-width SIMD (AVX/NEON) and width-agnostic (SVE-class) lane parallelism through the same neutral vocabulary and optimizer surfaces the GPU tiers use — so the second target family Tom prioritized on 2026-08-01 grows past the scalar vertical without a parallel scheduling stack.

## Why now

**Fact.** Tom's target-device priorities are Metal on macOS and CPU. The [bounded scalar CPU vertical](../spikes/target-profiles/scalar-cpu-vertical/README.md) proved the contracts end to end — its own governed profile, backend family, representation, payload, one-way commit, bit-exact agreement including exceptional values — and deliberately installed no physical provider and used no parallelism. Nothing represents a CPU vector lane in the implemented schedule vocabulary.

**Fact.** The adopted scheduled-region model designs the tier: `FixedVectorLane` and `ScalableVectorLane` bindings, explicitly distinct from `Subgroup` lanes ("Subgroup lanes and per-thread vector lanes are different bindings"), with legal width and alignment as profile facts. This ticket carries that design into a decidable shape the same way the workgroup tier was carried: representation and verifier obligations first, target realization as atomic facts, backend lowering downstream.

## Questions this must decide, each with its elimination stated

- What the lane tier's verifier obligations are: tail handling (extent not a multiple of width) is the lane analogue of the workgroup tail, and masked-versus-scalar-epilogue is a numerical question when the operation's contract forbids reassociation — decide what a strict-order fold may vectorize at all, citing the reduction-order contract rather than assuming lane order is free.
- How width enters the schedule: fixed width as a literal the profile must match exactly, versus width-symbolic schedules resolved at feasibility — and whether the two binding kinds share one answer.
- Whether a CPU worker-thread scope (the workgroup analogue) enters in the same design or stays explicitly out — the scalar vertical ran one thread; a threaded CPU backend needs the scope-kind distinction the model reserves, and conflating it with this tier would repeat the synonym the model refuses.
- What the CPU target profile declares: per-ISA atomic realization facts (an AVX2 row is not an SVE row), their provenance (compile-target attribution versus runtime CPUID observation — different availability phases, per the landed phase vocabulary), and what the feasibility surface can answer without executing backend code.
- Where lowering lands: the existing structured-kernel vocabulary emits scalar MSL today; state what a CPU emission target consumes (the KIR as-is with a lane-annotated schedule, or new constructs) and defer any new construct to its own implementation ticket.

## Non-goals

Implementation of bindings, emission, or a threaded runtime; any claim about auto-vectorization by the host compiler (a schedule that *relies* on LLVM vectorizing is not a schedule that states lanes); benchmark claims.

## Closes when

Each question is answered with its elimination or deferred with a trigger, the surviving design sits beside the workgroup and subgroup records with worked examples (one strict-order fold, one reassociation-permitted fold, both at a tail-bearing extent), the public drafts are enumerated for Tom, and the outcome is an accepted design, a recorded deferral, or a bounded experiment.
## Outcome

**The design is complete and all five questions are answered with stated eliminations.** The derivation, both worked examples, the public-boundary list, the deferrals with triggers, and an ADR body written to be landed verbatim live in [The CPU vector-lane tier](../docs/research/scheduling/cpu-vector-lane-tier.md); the target-declaration half lives in [CPU vector realization facts](../docs/research/target-profiles/cpu-vector-realization-facts.md). Research and design only: no crate gained a line, nothing was executed, emitted, or timed.

### The finding that reframes the ticket

**The ticket's hint is right and is not the whole line.** "A lane fold over the reduction axis is a reassociation; a lane spread over output elements is not" separates two cases; there is a third, and it is the one that decides what construct the vocabulary gains. A schedule owns the **map** and the **contributor partition** and does *not* own the **combine order within a partition**. A vector load feeding an *order-preserving* horizontal accumulate changes neither of the first two — it is the declared left fold executed out of a vector register — so it earns no schedule construct at all, and a single "reduce across lanes" construct would have to cover it and the tree-ordered case together, and would then be both admitted and refused under one strict contract.

**That case is not hypothetical, and the ISA proves the distinction rather than illustrating it.** Arm SVE ships `FADDA` ("Floating-point add strictly-ordered reduction… Vector elements are processed strictly in order from low to high") *and* `FADDV` ("recursive pairwise reduction") as two instructions, because they compute two values. **And `FADDV` substitutes `+0.0` for inactive lanes** — a hardware instance of exactly the padding-identity defect derived below, in the reduction instruction a CPU backend reaches for first.

### The five answers

1. **Verifier obligations.** *A lane over the map consumes no numerical permission*, because output positions are pairwise independent by the region's own `OwnershipProofKind::OneGlobalInvocationPerOutput`, so lane assignment changes no operand, no rounding site, and no combine order. **A strict-order fold is therefore vectorizable — across its outputs — under a contract forbidding reassociation outright**, which is a positive answer where the question's framing invites a negative one. *A lane over the partition* is a `ContributorPartition` and consumes reassociation by the same required check `verify_cooperative_semantics` already applies (`!*permits_reassociation`); **a strided layout additionally consumes permutation**, because its combine tree's leaves read in permuted order — which forces the numerically legal layout to be the gather one and the cache-friendly one to be illegal. *Tails are three different target requirements, not three spellings*: `Exact` needs divisibility; `Predicated` needs a fault-suppressing masked load; `ScalarEpilogue` needs every execution path realizing the region's arithmetic to honour its one declared realization; `IdentityPadded` needs a stated padding value proved to be an identity. **The padding value is `-0.0` and the `empty_identity_bits` the verifier already requires is `+0.0`** — two roles, two values, and reusing the second is silently wrong on exactly the rows whose true sum is `-0.0`.
2. **Width.** *`FixedVectorLane` carries a literal*, forced twice over: a symbolic width would give two programs one `CanonicalScheduledRegionIdentity`, and the tail obligation is intrinsic (`N mod W`) so a symbolic width moves a hard rejection into target feasibility or drops it. *`ScalableVectorLane` is width-symbolic*, forced because a literal forfeits the property the mechanism exists to provide. **They do not share one answer, and differ at three obligations, not one**: the tail vocabulary (a scalable binding admits `Predicated` alone — "tail" does not survive translation); the partition (`ContributorPartition::covers` is a product over `partitions = W`, undecidable symbolically, **so a scalable lane may not bind the fold even under a contract permitting everything**); and cost-versus-legality (the concrete length enters no legality predicate).
3. **The worker-thread scope is explicitly out, and the argument is orthogonality rather than deferral.** A lane binds *within* one execution instance, raises no cross-instance visibility edge, and consumes no synchronization authority; a worker thread is *another instance*, and `LaunchPlan`/`GlobalLinearInvocation` already express that — a threaded CPU backend needs **a profile declaration, not a new binding**. Including it would add the unneeded construct while the needed one stays missing, and would be the first thing to compete for the `SynchronizationScope::Workgroup` spelling — the synonym **ADR 0043 refuses in its accepted text**. Trigger: the first CPU schedule requiring a cross-instance handoff.
4. **Profile facts.** *One atomic `VectorRealizationSubject` matched by equality*, never a `CapabilityAxis` — and the elimination is unsoundness rather than fit: the relation a width would take is `AtMost`, and `satisfies(AtMost, 4, 8)` is `true`, so an 8-lane profile would admit a 4-lane schedule it may not realize. **ADR 0043 already fixes the dimension list** ("vector legality depends on operation, dtype, masks, address space, width, and alignment"); what this record proposes is only their *atomicity*, and the counterexample that settles it is NEON, which realizes 4-lane `f32` arithmetic and no masked load at all. *Provenance is compile-target attribution* at `CompileProfile` under `ExternalProfile`, never a host observation; runtime feature detection is **variant eligibility, not feasibility**, and that mechanism already exists and is already device-free. *Feasibility is answerable with no backend code executed, for both bindings.* **And the lane tier does not fire the missing host-process availability phase** — a deliberate negative result, because the scalable length is a cost input and cost never enters the phased model.
5. **Lowering.** *KIR as-is with a lane-annotated schedule is eliminated*: the kernel verifier proves predicate dominance and bounds-witness provenance over the body, so a body whose real predication lives on the schedule is verified against something it is not. New constructs: fixed and scalable lane types (two, not one with an optional count), a mask type distinct from `Bool`, masked load and store naming their mask beside their witness, a `WHILELT`-shaped mask generator and a lane broadcast, and element-wise widening of the existing arithmetic ops rather than a parallel family. **And deliberately no horizontal-reduce operation**, because answer 1 puts an ordered accumulate below the schedule boundary and its spelling already exists as the `SerialLoop`. All deferred to [`admit-lane-typed-values-and-masked-memory-into-the-kernel-ir`](admit-lane-typed-values-and-masked-memory-into-the-kernel-ir.md).

### The worked examples, and where the options actually differ

Both use `y[m] = strict_serial_sum(n, x[m,n])` over `x: f32[7, 101]`, which bears a tail on **both** axes at `W = 4` — `7 mod 4 = 3` and `101 mod 4 = 1` — so which tail exists depends on which case the binding is, and that is the first thing the pair demonstrates.

- **A, strict contract.** A1 (map, `Predicated`) is `Proven` on an AVX profile and **`Rejected` on NEON**, because lane 3 addresses past the bounds proof and NEON has no masked load — same schedule, same contract, two targets, two verdicts, legality not cost. A2 (map, `ScalarEpilogue`) is admissible on NEON where A1 is not, and pays instead with a second execution path under one declared realization. A3 (partition) is **rejected twice independently**: the topology requires `permits_reassociation`, and `covers(101)` has no solution for `4k`.
- **B, reassociation permitted.** B1 is *unchanged* from A — which is the check on answer 1, since the map's obligations would have moved if it had consumed a permission. B2 is admissible in principle and then constrained in order: permutation forbids the strided layout, `covers` refuses the ragged one, identity padding is the only repair, **and the padding value must be `0x8000_0000` rather than the `0x0000_0000` sitting on the scalar program labelled "identity"**. B3 is refused on `ScalableVectorLane` under the identical contract on the identical program with no target involved — the sharpest available demonstration that the two bindings are two answers.

### Three ISA claims a draft asserted were wrong, and are corrected in place

Verified against Arm DDI 0602 (2026-06) and DDI 0487 M.c, Intel SDM 325383-085US and 253665-078US, the RISC-V unprivileged manual, and IEEE 754-2019 §6.3.

- **`VMASKMOVPS` is `AVX`, not `AVX2`.** Only `VPMASKMOVD`/`VPMASKMOVQ` are AVX2. A profile keyed on the wrong feature declares a row it cannot license.
- **SVE's vector length is a *power of two* from 128 to 2048 bits, not any multiple of 128.** The multiple-of-128 wording is DDI 0487 H.a's and Arm narrowed it; `grep -c "multiple of 128"` over M.c returns 0. Nothing here consumes the length, so nothing depends on it — but a cost model enumerating lengths would.
- **There is no AArch64 "Advanced SIMD always flushes subnormals" rule.** That is AArch32's (§E1.3.5.1). On AArch64 `FPCR.FZ`/`FIZ` govern scalar FP, Advanced SIMD, and SVE alike, and on x86 one `MXCSR` governs SSE/AVX packed and scalar alike. **So the packed-versus-scalar divergence this design first asserted as a class distinction does not exist as one**; what survives is per-instruction and per-path — the `FPCR.AH` reciprocal-estimate family, the min/max output-flush exemptions, `FZ16`, and x87 having no `FTZ`/`DAZ` at all. The scalar-epilogue obligation survives with a correct shape ("every path realizing the arithmetic must honour the one declared realization") and is usually discharged by one row; **the honourability-key widening the first draft called required is now a question for Tom**, and the profile record and its ticket both say so.

Also stated rather than absorbed: **ADR 0007's own text names none of `FixedVectorLane`, `ScalableVectorLane`, `SubgroupTree`, `WorkgroupTree`, or `block_reduce`** — those are in the adopted research record it cites as evidence. Both records now attribute quotations to the model and cite ADR 0043 where an accepted decision is available, which turned out to be more often than expected: ADR 0043 decides the scope-kind non-aliasing, the fixed/scalable distinction, and the vector-legality dimension list outright.
