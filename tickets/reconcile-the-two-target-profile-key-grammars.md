## User-visible outcome

An artifact arriving from outside a governed producer cannot carry a target-profile key that the compiler's own constructor would refuse, or the asymmetry is recorded as deliberate with its reasoning at both sites.

## Why this slice exists

**Fact, at `e6a47d9`.** Two different types are spelled `TargetProfileKey`.

`tiler_compiler::target::TargetProfileKey` (`crates/tiler-compiler/src/target.rs:210`) validates non-empty, at most `MAX_TARGET_PROFILE_KEY_BYTES` (128), and an alphabet of ASCII lowercase, digits, `.`, `-`, and `_`, returning a typed `TargetProfileKeyError::InvalidByte { index, value }`.

`tiler_artifact::program::TargetProfileKey` is generated by the `governed_key!` macro at `crates/tiler-artifact/src/program/keys.rs:105` and validated by `validate_key` at `keys.rs:73`, which checks non-empty and at most `MAX_GOVERNED_KEY_BYTES` (256). There is no alphabet, case, separator, or namespace check anywhere in the crate. Reproduce the absence with `grep -rn "is_ascii\|InvalidByte" crates/tiler-artifact/src/`, which returns nothing; the positive control is the identical grep over `crates/tiler-compiler/src/target.rs`, which returns six lines including the admitted-byte closure at line 226. `BackendKey`, `RepresentationKey`, `FeasibilityRuleSetKey`, `CapabilityKey`, and `RouteFeatureKey` share that same permissive validator.

`crates/tiler-build/src/metal_plan.rs:333` is the laundering site: `TargetProfileKey::new(compilation.target_profile_key())` converts the strict spelling into the permissive one.

**Inference.** The strict grammar is a producer-side property and not an artifact-level guarantee. That is a real asymmetry rather than a naming nuisance, because the artifact layer is what validates bytes it did not produce.

The [consumer-neutral backend-provider composition record](../docs/research/extensions/backend-provider-composition.md) surfaced this while correcting the CPU vertical's finding 2, which reports both types as validating "length and alphabet only" because it used one of each and described them in one sentence.

## Implementation keys

- Decide the intended contract before changing a byte. Either the artifact layer enforces the alphabet — in which case say why 128 versus 256 bytes also differ — or the permissiveness is deliberate and both sites say so.
- Neither crate depends on the other, so a shared constant is not available and a change means checking both, exactly as `keys.rs:69-70` already records for the descriptor bound.
- Tightening the artifact grammar can refuse an artifact that decodes today. Establish whether any retained fixture or golden carries a key outside the strict alphabet before changing the validator.
- Consider whether the shared spelling itself warrants a glossary row; `docs/glossary.md` already indexes names denoting several unrelated subjects, and this pair is not among them.

## Closes when

Either the two grammars agree, or each site documents the deliberate difference and the glossary indexes the shared name; and any fixture affected by a tightening is identified rather than discovered by a failing gate.
