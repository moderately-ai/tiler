## User-visible outcome

A live-extent access states its bounds obligation in a spelling that cannot be confused with a statically empty one — under an accepted public spelling and an accepted identity consequence, rather than a worker's improvisation.

*(Restated 2026-08-23. The prior wording — "carries a bounds proof that states its reach, instead of the zero-length `LinearRange` the verifier emits today" — presupposed that the reach is a quantity the schedule could state. 2. **Different relations, different tags.** `TAG_GATHER_SOURCE = 0x0C` against `TAG_LIVE_ROW_MAJOR_SOURCE = 0x0A`, `TAG_LIVE_ROW_MAJOR_CONSUMER = 0x0B`, and `TAG_CONTRACTION_OPERAND = 0x05`.

**What is shared is a precedent, and it is the most useful thing this re-derivation found.** The gather landing answered the general form of this ticket's question, and answered it in *both* directions at once: the statically **proved** gather entered `BoundsProofKind` as a variant carrying its closed proof, while the **undischarged** gather deliberately got no spelling in the schedule at all — it carries a `GatherIndexValidationRequirement` at the index layer instead, and `BoundsProofKind::GatherSource`'s doc records why (anchor `spelling at this layer at all`): that is what stops a schedule from ever representing an undischarged data-dependent read. A live access is structurally the second case — its reach is not knowable at schedule formation — and the landed answer for that case is to keep the unprovable fact out of the schedule and carry it where it can be discharged. That is exactly what the relation-plus-`InputExtent` chain already does. The precedent therefore argues **against** a reach-bearing variant and **for** at most an honest spelling of the absence.

This is a graph finding, not a merge finding: no edge needs adding, and neither ticket subsumes the other.

## Decision packet — how the schedule spells a live access's bounds obligation

**Status: complete at `069873f4`. Independent review before queueing; then Tom.**

### The ground, re-derived

An admitted live access is verified against a bounds proof today, and the proof it must carry is `BoundsProofKind::LinearRange { element_count: 0 }` — `crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/builder/proof.rs` refuses any other value for `LiveRowMajorSource`, `LiveRowMajor`, and a `ContractionOperand` under `LiveContraction`. `crates/tiler-ir/src/kernel/verify.rs`, anchor `pub(super) fn access_elements(`, then ignores the stored count for those relations and returns `Ok(0)` from the *access map*, so the count is inert as well as false. The honest quantity lives one layer up, as an `AbiRoot::InputExtent`-rooted expression resolved at `LiveDevicePreflight`.

So the only question this ticket can still decide is: **what does an admitted live access's bounds proof say, given that its reach is not a schedule quantity?** Today it says "zero in-range positions", which is not true and is not what any consumer reads.

### Eliminated before ranking (gate rule 3)

- **A reach-bearing variant — `BoundsProofKind::LiveRowMajorReach { outer_elements, inner_axis }` or any spelling carrying fields.** Both fields are already derivable from the region: `outer_elements` is the `iteration_shape` element product, and `inner_axis` is the unique `LiveRowMajorSource` marker's axis (or the `LiveContraction` `live_axis`). Restating them creates a second authority that can disagree with the first — precisely the failure mode the accepted fieldless `LiveRowMajor` consumer design refuses by name (`crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/model.rs`, anchor `handle here would be a second authority`), where the argument is that a mismatch should be *unrepresentable* rather than representable-and-refused. Conflates authorities; eliminated.
- **A symbolic `LinearRange` — widening `element_count` to static-or-live.** Either it names the symbol, which drags a `ShapeSymbol` — an interface-side notion the schedule deliberately does not carry, since `LiveRowMajorSource` names only an axis — into schedule identity and reverses the accepted record's stated intent that the live inner extent is not a schedule identity value; or it names "live" without a symbol, in which case it is the fieldless option below with a field position nobody writes. Either misplaces authority or is a dominated cosmetic variant; eliminated both ways.
- **A symbolic `ByteWindow` length at the kernel-program layer.** Sound, and it is the honest home of the vacuous agreement check — but it is a `tiler.kernel-program` redesign, not a schedule-vocabulary decision, and folding it in here would decide two boundaries under one question. Split out rather than eliminated: filed as [`prove-the-symbolic-accessible-range-agreement-at-program-assembly`](prove-the-symbolic-accessible-range-agreement-at-program-assembly.md).
- **Further bounded research.** Not a distinct option: the research that would move this is the end-to-end proof, and it already exists as [`prove-one-live-extent-artifact-payload-and-pipeline-at-two-n`](prove-one-live-extent-artifact-payload-and-pipeline-at-two-n.md). Presenting it as a choice would pad the list.
- **The complete replacement — retiring `LinearRange` and re-spelling every bounds proof.** No live-extent problem requires it, every static arm is correct today, and it would move every schedule identity value in the workspace for nothing this ticket needs. Knowingly dominated; eliminated as padding.

### The frontier

**Option A — keep the zero convention, and state it as a decision.** `BoundsProofKind::LinearRange { element_count: 0 }` stays the live spelling. The change is documentary: record in the `BoundsProofKind` and `LiveRowMajorSource` records that a live access's reach is deliberately not a schedule quantity, that the schedule's witness is the *relation*, and that the reach is published by the program layer as an `AbiRoot::InputExtent`-rooted expression. No identity value moves, no golden is recomputed, no tag is consumed.

- *Strongest counterargument.* The proof states something false. A reader holding a `BoundsProof` alone — a public type with public fields — is told there are zero in-range positions when the truth is "not stated at this layer". The pairing that rescues it lives in a private verifier function, so the honesty is a property of the checker rather than of the type, which is the opposite of the arrangement the repository prefers everywhere else. It also leaves the live arm of `bounds_proof_refines_access` satisfiable by accident: a caller whose static count came out zero through an overflow or an empty shape passes the live check for the wrong reason.
- *What would reverse it.* A consumer outside `tiler-ir` reading `BoundsProofKind` for a reach — none exists today; `grep -rn "BoundsProofKind" crates/` finds 326 lines, all construction sites, test fixtures, or doc examples, and none classifies by exhaustive match, which is the `#[non_exhaustive]` justification's own claim.
- *Perturbations that would test it.* Construct a live region whose bounds proof carries a non-zero `LinearRange` and show `bounds_proof_refines_access` refuses it, quoting the diagnostic; construct a *static* region whose `LinearIdentity` access owns zero output positions and show it carries byte-identical proof bytes and a different access-map tag, which is what keeps the two identities distinct today.

**Option B — a fieldless `BoundsProofKind::LiveExtentReach` at bounds-proof tag `0x14`.** The variant states that this access's reach is the region's live extent and that this layer does not state it. Fieldless deliberately, on the `TAG_LIVE_ROW_MAJOR_CONSUMER` precedent where the tag alone is the whole encoding: with no fields there is nothing to disagree with the relation, so the second-authority failure the eliminated options carry is unrepresentable rather than refused. `bounds_proof_refines_access` gains an arm requiring this variant for exactly the three live relations and refusing `LinearRange` for them; `access_elements` returns `Ok(0)` from the variant rather than by special-casing the access map.

- *Strongest counterargument.* It buys typed honesty and one narrow fail-closed tightening, and buys no correctness the tree lacks today — a live region and a static-empty region already differ in their access-map tag, so no identity conflation exists to fix. Against that it moves every live region's schedule, kernel, kernel-program, and artifact identity **value**, and it spends a tag from a sixteen-value family run of which three are used, to describe a fact the layer's whole design is that it does not know.
- *What would reverse it.* Evidence that the live population is about to grow a relation whose reach *is* statically derivable, which would make a single "live" spelling lossy; or a measured `MAX_*_IDENTITY_BYTES` pressure that makes a new tag non-free. Neither is visible at this base.
- *Perturbations that would test it.* Pair a live relation with `LinearRange { element_count: 0 }` and show the region no longer builds, quoting the refusal — this is the load-bearing one, because it is what makes the accidental-satisfaction hole close. Separately, pair the new variant with a `LinearIdentity` access and show that refuses too. Recompute `LIVE_ROW_MAJOR_SCHEDULE_IDENTITY_HEX` and `LIVE_ROW_MAJOR_KERNEL_IDENTITY_HEX` and show the retired `0x09` constants beside them stay unequal to both, which is the collision guard that file already exists for. Confirm `STRICT_F32_REGION_IDENTITY_HEX` is byte-unchanged, which is the injectivity claim's negative control. The `variant_count`-sized enumeration in `crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/parametric.rs` (anchor `fn every_bounds_proof_kind_tag_is_distinct_and_pinned(`) becomes a build error at the array until the new tag is pinned, so the census cannot silently stop covering its domain.

### Comparison of the frontier (gate rule 4)

Both are top-tier on **correctness**: neither can return a wrong reach, because neither touches the reach — that quantity is the ABI expression's under both options, unchanged. On **fail-closed strictness** B is strictly better by one narrow margin: it makes the live arm unsatisfiable by a mis-derived static count, which A leaves satisfiable. On **long-term maintainability** B is better — the honesty moves from a private verifier arm into the type, and `access_elements`'s access-map special case disappears — at the cost of one identity migration and one census of the live construction sites. On **compatibility** A is free and B is cheap: pre-production, no external consumer, no domain step (argued below), and the delta rule already forces pin recomputation on the merged tree. On **Tiler host runtime and memory** both are unmeasurable: B's variant is fieldless, so `BoundsProofKind` stays at its measured 88 bytes, which the `ReductionDomain` arm sets. Kernel performance is untouched by construction under both.

**Unsupported population** is unchanged by both: every population outside the admitted live one keeps its exact named refusal, and neither option admits anything new. **Public surface**: A adds nothing; B adds one variant to an `#[non_exhaustive]` enum, which ADR 0075 places on the no-approval list — so B's public-surface cost is not what makes it Tom's. **Schema**: neither touches a wire grammar; the artifact and manifest domains are untouched by both, because a bounds proof is folded into schedule identity and never written to the envelope structurally.

Neither dominates. A is better on cost and on leaving a working thing alone; B is better on typed honesty and on one fail-closed arm. That is a genuine trade-off, so it goes to Tom as one question rather than being taken here.

### Identity consequence, in detail — and it is the reason this is Tom's

**Under Option A: none.** No value moves.

**Under Option B, exactly:**

- **Tag.** `0x14`, the next free value in the bounds-proof `0x1X` family run. `0x11` (`TAG_LINEAR_RANGE`), `0x12` (`TAG_REDUCTION_DOMAIN`), and `0x13` (`TAG_GATHER_INDEX_BOUNDS`) keep their tags and their field layouts. **Not `0x13`** — this ticket said `0x13` on 2026-08-22 and that value has since been consumed.
- **Domain steps: none.** Not `tiler.schedule.v7`, not `tiler.kernel.v9`, not `tiler.kernel-program.v13`, not `tiler.artifact-program.v22`, and no manifest major. The argument, stated so it can be attacked: `0x14` is a byte no earlier region can carry at the bounds-proof position, so no new identity can equal an old one; every earlier proof keeps its tag and field layout, so no earlier region's bytes move; and the one narrowing — `0x11` with a zero count can no longer belong to a live access — creates no collision, because a live region and a static-empty region already differ at the access-map position (`0x0A`, `0x0B`, or `0x05` against `0x01`). The precedent is `TAG_LIVE_ROW_MAJOR_SOURCE`'s own comment, which moved every live identity value at the fieldless-marker replacement and deliberately did not step the domain. **This is the claim in this packet most worth an independent derivation**, because being wrong about it silently admits two programs under one identity.
- **Values that move.** Every region containing a `LiveRowMajorSource`, a `LiveRowMajor`, or a `ContractionOperand` under `LiveContraction`, and every kernel, kernel-program, and artifact identity nesting one.
- **Pinned goldens that move: exactly two, both in one file.** `LIVE_ROW_MAJOR_SCHEDULE_IDENTITY_HEX` and `LIVE_ROW_MAJOR_KERNEL_IDENTITY_HEX` in `crates/tiler-ir/src/kernel/tests/live_row_major.rs`. Census method, stated because a census is only as complete as its vocabulary: `grep -rlE '"[0-9a-f]{200,}"' --include='*.rs' crates/` returns eight files of byte-preimage goldens, each read here; only that one pins a live region. The two `RETIRED_CONTEXTUAL_` constants beside them are retired `0x09` values and stay unchanged by construction. The 64-hex digest pins — 65 literals across ten files, including `metal_plan.rs`'s `ARTIFACT_IDENTITY` and `CACHE_SUBJECT` — are all over static, wholly literal, or non-schedule fixtures and do not move; `crates/tiler-build/src/metal_assembly.rs`'s live test (`one_live_extent_payload_identity_is_stable_and_unequal_to_a_baked_neighbour`) asserts only relative equalities and is invariant.
- **Construction sites to migrate.** `grep -ro "element_count: 0" --include='*.rs' crates/ | wc -l` gives **23 occurrences across 14 files**, and **that number is not the population, and errs in both directions**: it over-counts, because some are genuinely empty static accesses, and it under-counts, because a live proof can be spelled `LinearRange { element_count }` over a bound variable — `crates/tiler-metal/src/tests.rs` does exactly that. The population must be sized from the *relation* side, and the type makes that mechanical: after the change, every live construction site is a compile error at the pairing arm. Sizing it by literal grep is the anti-pattern; sizing it by the type is the check.

### Recommendation and the one question

**Recommend Option B**, narrowly. The repository's stated priority order puts correctness and maintainability ahead of cost, and prefers "typed, explainable failure over a silently wrong fast path"; a bounds proof that states zero in-range positions for an access that reaches N is the silently-wrong-value shape of that hazard even though nothing today is misled by it. The precedent from the gather landing points the same way: the undischarged fact stays out of the layer that cannot prove it, but the access still carries an *honest* statement of what it does and does not know. The cost is one identity migration over a population the type will enumerate, plus two goldens.

The strongest reason to choose A instead is that B spends an identity migration and a tag to fix a documentation problem — nothing is wrong today, no consumer is misled, and the same honesty could be bought by writing it down.
