## User-visible outcome

An ordinary compiler/runtime consumer can explicitly compose accepted statically linked backend components and state allowed or required backend policy without global discovery, forks, or registration-order behavior.

## Implementation keys

**Corrected 2026-08-08 against accepted [ADR 0090](../docs/decisions/0090-compose-backends-per-responsibility-rather-than-per-backend.md), which this body predates.** The body was authored at `646a37f6` (2026-07-31 11:23) and the record was accepted at `e78b9ab3` the same day at 22:08; the 22:09 correction sweep `622cf620` that carried the no-registry model into the sibling tickets touched only this ticket's `dependencies` line and never reached its keys. Two keys below therefore named mechanisms the record had already eliminated. The **User-visible outcome above survives unchanged** — a consumer still composes accepted statically linked components and still states backend policy without global discovery, forks, or registration-order behaviour — exactly as it survived for [`route-a-custom-backend-through-an-independently-selected-adapter`](route-a-custom-backend-through-an-independently-selected-adapter.md). What changed is the mechanism, not the goal. Per-key verdicts are recorded inline so a later reader can see which claim was repaired and against what.

- **Verified, and largely discharged since.** Build the facade from the already exercised component seams rather than introducing a second set of traits. The seams exist and are driven from out of crate: `CompileRequest::with_capabilities` and `CompileRequest::with_physical_providers` in `crates/tiler-compiler/src/session.rs`, `InstalledCapabilities`, `InstalledPhysicalProviders`, `assemble_plan_artifact`, and `route_with_adapter`.
- ~~Let consumers register complete backends or partial provider components only in combinations the accepted composition contract can validate.~~ **False, and eliminated rather than merely unimplemented.** ADR 0090 item 4 states "There is no `BackendProvider`, no provider bundle, no emitter registration, and no runtime-adapter registration, and this record proposes none", and its *Alternatives considered* eliminates the bundling trait on three independent grounds. One of those grounds also refutes the "combinations … can validate" clause: the pieces are fixed at four different times — profile at compile time, emission at build time, artifact at publication, adapter at run time — so there is no moment at which a cross-phase combination could be validated at all. A component claims rows of the responsibility matrix and owes exactly those rows' obligations; **a partial provider is the normal case, not a permitted degenerate one.**
- ~~Freeze compiler/build/runtime registries independently and make their canonical identities available for explain and cache/request provenance.~~ **False for build and runtime; verified and landed for the compiler.** Build has no registry by decision — ADR 0090 item 11 promotes the existing backend-neutral closure `assemble_plan_artifact` and names the registering-trait alternative as the larger answer to the smaller question. Runtime has no registry by decision — item 4's Fact records that row 12 "has no registry either, and that is not a gap of the same kind — independent selection *is* its mechanism, and the record eliminates giving it one." The compiler half is real, is frozen per session, and already reaches durable provenance: `crates/tiler-build/src/plan_artifact.rs` builds the artifact's `CompilationEnvironment` from `compilation.offered_providers()`. **Correction — 2026-08-10.** The documentation half of the offered-environment subject is discharged: `Compilation::offered_providers` is documented as the *lowering* environment and points readers at `offered_physical_providers` for the physical half. The remaining remainder owned by [`disclose-the-physical-provider-environment-a-compilation-was-offered`](disclose-the-physical-provider-environment-a-compilation-was-offered.md) is durable artifact provenance — `assemble_plan_artifact` still mints `CompilationEnvironment` from lowering providers alone — not a live "docs disagree with code" bug on this parent.
- **Verified as the remaining work, and imprecise about its prerequisite.** Express allowed, required, or fallback-only backend-family policy as typed input outside semantic graph meaning. No such vocabulary exists today. It is not merely unwritten: `ExecutionEnvironment` (`crates/tiler-runtime/src/load/host.rs`) states exactly one `BackendKey` and one `RepresentationKey`, so a policy ranging over backend families has at most one family to range over and **could not change any routing outcome** — it would be a guard that cannot fail. A typed family policy therefore presupposes that a loading host can state a *set* of executable environments, which is a public-boundary and host-model question this ticket must put to Tom before building on either answer.
- **Verified, and already discharged.** Do not add an unrestricted arbitrary scoring callback; use governed policy and cost identities. `GOVERNED_PHYSICAL_COST_MODEL_KEY` is a reading surface only, `PhysicalCostEstimate::structural` is the sole public constructor and attributes to it, and a `compile_fail,E0624` doctest in `crates/tiler-compiler/src/physical_provider.rs` pins that a provider cannot name a model of its own.
- **Partly discharged.** Reject missing required pieces, duplicate authority, incompatible component identity, and policy that permits no executable route before work. Duplicate authority and incompatible component identity landed as `PhysicalProviderInstallationError::DuplicateIdentity` and `::GovernedIdentity`, each a refusal rather than a replacement and each with a test that distinguishes two *revisions* of one provider from one identity twice. "Policy that permits no executable route before work" is unbuilt and depends on the key above.
- **Verified as an inherited constraint with no deliverable of its own.** Keep inline proc-macro provider visibility bounded by host dependencies and explicit invocation declarations under ADR 0045. ADR 0090 item 13 inherits that trust and linkage model whole and adds no linkage mode; ADR 0045's decision text is unchanged. Nothing in this ticket may widen it, and there is nothing here to build.
- **Partly discharged, and none of the four is covered in the spelling this key asks for.** Provide compile-pass examples for standard Metal, custom Metal specialization, CPU only, and an allowed Metal-or-CPU set. What exists is weaker in a way worth naming rather than rounding up, because `tiler-compiler` cannot host a Metal example at all: its `Cargo.toml` depends on `tiler-ir` and three numeric crates and does not know Metal exists. So `crates/tiler-compiler/tests/external_physical_provider.rs` is a **backend-neutral** partial provider — its own header says it "specializes one schedule axis of whichever regions this compiler already spells" — and is the analogue of the custom-Metal case, not that case; the Metal-specific one is the out-of-gate spike `spikes/extensions/forkless-physical-provider`. `crates/tiler-build/tests/custom_backend/` is a custom backend that is deliberately **not Metal** and is the analogue of the CPU case; the CPU one is the out-of-gate spike `spikes/target-profiles/scalar-cpu-vertical`. Standard Metal has no composition-facade example. The Metal-or-CPU set is blocked on the family-policy key and on the host-model question it raises.
- Present every public crate/module/trait/type/call-site boundary to Tom.

## Closes when

**Corrected 2026-08-10 for terminal honesty with the Outcome audit.** This parent closes when the consumer-facing composition seams land without duplicating ownership: `CompileRequest` installs capabilities and physical providers per session, `assemble_plan_artifact` and `route_with_adapter` stay the build and runtime joins, governed cost identity and installation refusals (`DuplicateIdentity`, `GovernedIdentity`) refuse rather than replace, no global registry or complete-backend bundle type appears, and the undelivered backend-family selection policy plus the standard-Metal/custom-Metal/CPU and Metal-or-CPU example portfolio are split onto named successors rather than left as silent undelivered keys on this node. It does **not** close on this ticket producing “all example policies” or the multi-family host policy itself — those obligations live on [`express-the-typed-backend-family-selection-policy`](express-the-typed-backend-family-selection-policy.md), [`decide-whether-a-loading-host-may-state-several-backend-families`](decide-whether-a-loading-host-may-state-several-backend-families.md), and [`exercise-standard-metal-custom-metal-and-cpu-providers-in-one-portfolio`](exercise-standard-metal-custom-metal-and-cpu-providers-in-one-portfolio.md).

## Outcome audit — 2026-08-09

This parent is `done` because its corrected, still-admissible composition work landed and its one surviving undelivered key was split onto an explicit blocked path; it is not a claim that the original pre-ADR-0090 bundle-and-registry design landed.

- The compiler-side composition seams, governed cost identity, duplicate-identity refusal, and governed-identity refusal described above are delivered. ADR 0090 eliminates the proposed complete-backend bundle and independent build/runtime registries, so those original keys have no implementation remainder.
- The backend-family policy is **not** delivered. [`express-the-typed-backend-family-selection-policy`](express-the-typed-backend-family-selection-policy.md) owns it and depends on [`decide-whether-a-loading-host-may-state-several-backend-families`](decide-whether-a-loading-host-may-state-several-backend-families.md), because the current `ExecutionEnvironment` still states one backend and one representation. Tom must decide whether the loader accepts several environments or the consumer facade owns the policy before that public surface is built.
- The standard-Metal/custom-Metal/CPU and Metal-or-CPU examples are likewise not being rounded up as delivered. [`exercise-standard-metal-custom-metal-and-cpu-providers-in-one-portfolio`](exercise-standard-metal-custom-metal-and-cpu-providers-in-one-portfolio.md) depends on the policy successor and owns the end-to-end proof; [`publish-the-backend-provider-conformance-suite`](publish-the-backend-provider-conformance-suite.md) depends on that portfolio.

The split preserves the original user-visible goal while making the board truthful: this node records the delivered per-responsibility composition boundary, the decision node owns the unresolved host model, the implementation successor owns the typed policy, and the portfolio ticket owns the examples. None of those successors should be bypassed by reopening this terminal parent.
