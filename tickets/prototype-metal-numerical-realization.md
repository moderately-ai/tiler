Map the strict proof profile to explicit MSL operations and offline compiler flags. Preserve canonical arithmetic NaN, signed zero, materialization conversions and reduction order; prohibit unlicensed contraction/reassociation; record exact toolchain realization and fail closed for unsupported realizations.

Test these semantics independently of the offline compiler's fast/safe-math flags: the realization must be provably carried by explicit generated operations rather than inherited from a flag setting, so flipping the math-mode flags cannot silently change conforming results. This obligation previously lived in the Metal AOT integration body and is owned here now that the AOT slice is an integration gate.

## Outcome

Recorded 2026-07-24.

### The measurement environment

**Measurement — environment.** Every measurement below was taken on an Apple M4 Max under macOS 27.0 (build 26A5388g), Xcode 26.6 (build 17F113), SDK `macosx` 26.5 (build 25F70), `Apple metal version 32023.883 (metalfe-32023.883)` and `AIR-LLD 32023.883`. Offline compilation used `xcrun --sdk macosx metal -target air64-apple-macos13.0 -std=metal3.1 -O<level> -fmetal-math-mode=<mode> -fmetal-math-fp32-functions=<fp> -ffp-contract=<contract> -c <src> -o <air>` followed by `xcrun --sdk macosx metallib <air> -o <lib>`. Device execution used a hand-written Objective-C host (`MTLCreateSystemDefaultDevice`, `newLibraryWithURL:`, one thread per output, `MTLResourceStorageModeShared` buffers, bit patterns in and out). Runtime compilation used `newLibraryWithSource:options:` with `MTLCompileOptions.mathMode` set to `MTLMathModeSafe`/`Relaxed`/`Fast`. The kernels dispatched were the crate's own regenerated golden fixtures plus scale/bias variants in the emitter's exact output shape.

### The headline answer: yes, flipping the math mode changes a conforming result

**Measurement — signed zero diverges by math mode.** The emitter produces, for `ScalarProgram::MultiplyThenAdd { scale_bits: 0x3f800000, bias_bits: 0x00000000 }`, the statements `float v5 = v3 * as_type<float>(0x3f800000u);` and `float v8 = v6 + as_type<float>(0x00000000u);`. Dispatched with the operand `0x80000000` (negative zero), that kernel returns `0x00000000` under `-fmetal-math-mode=safe` and `0x80000000` under both `relaxed` and `fast`. IEEE-754 round-to-nearest requires `0x00000000`, because `(-0.0) + (+0.0) = +0.0`. The divergence reproduces at `-O0`, `-O1`, `-O2`, `-O3` and `-Os`, and under both `-fmetal-math-fp32-functions=precise` and `=fast`. The mechanism is LLVM's `nsz` licence, which `-S -emit-llvm` shows applied to every emitted `f32` operation under `relaxed` (`reassoc nsz arcp afn`) and `fast` (`reassoc nnan ninf nsz arcp afn`) and to none under `safe`.

**Measurement — contraction diverges by contract setting.** A multiply and an add written as two separate statements over `scale = 1.5`, `bias = 1.0` return the separately rounded `0x3fc58f9e` for the operand `0x3eb97ef9` under `-ffp-contract=off` and `-ffp-contract=on`, and the fused `0x3fc58f9d` under `-ffp-contract=fast`. The per-statement emission rule is therefore a measured defence against `on` and measurably not a defence against `fast`.

**Inference.** Neither signed zero nor cross-statement contraction can be carried by any emitted operation: they are properties the compiler applies to the operations themselves. `MetalNumericalRequirement::SafeMathMode` and `::NoFloatingPointContraction` are consequently real, load-bearing preconditions, not documentation. The ticket's obligation is satisfiable only for the subset of the contract that a *non-floating-point* operation can express, and the honest deliverable is to move that subset into the operations and to state precisely what remains a flag precondition.

### The `isnan` premise in the brief did not survive contact with the toolchain

**Fact — what the brief predicted.** The dispatch brief carried the previous author's judgment note that a bitwise NaN test would survive fast math whereas `isnan` under a relaxed mode could be folded away, and asked me to find a case where a relaxed realization changes a conforming result through the canonicalization.

**Measurement — no such case exists on this toolchain.** The emitted golden's canonicalization returns `0x7fc00000` for the operands `0x7fabcdef`, `0xffc00001` and `0x7f800001` in all fifteen combinations of `-O0`/`-O1`/`-O2`/`-O3`/`-Os` with `safe`/`relaxed`/`fast`, for the `isnan` form and for the bitwise form alike. `-S -emit-llvm` explains why: the Metal front end lowers `isnan(v)` to `bitcast float to i32`, `and i32 %x, 2147483647`, `icmp ugt i32 %y, 2139095040` at `-O0` and under every math mode, so the shipped AIR contains no `fcmp` and no `llvm.is.fpclass` for any of them. The `nnan` licence is attached to the `fmul`/`fadd`, never to the integer test, and it is not propagated through the bitcast.

**Fact — the emission changed anyway, for a different and better reason.** `crates/tiler-metal/src/emit.rs` now emits the predicate as an explicit integer test over two named IEEE-754 field constants rather than as `isnan`. The reason is not an observed miscompile — there is none — but the maturity class of the guarantee. `isnan` under `-fmetal-math-mode=fast` is a floating-point predicate whose behaviour MSL does not define and which LLVM's `nnan` licence permits folding; that this front-end build lowers it early enough to escape that licence is a property of the build, and AGENTS.md is explicit that an empirical result on one toolchain does not prove an unmeasured universal claim. `as_type<uint>` plus two integer comparisons is a language-level property of the emitted text that no floating-point relaxation licence can reach. The emitted helper is now:

```c
static inline float tiler_canonicalize_nan_f32_7fc00000(float value) {
    uint pattern = as_type<uint>(value);
    bool nan = (pattern & 0x7f800000u) == 0x7f800000u
        && (pattern & 0x007fffffu) != 0x00000000u;
    return nan ? as_type<float>(0x7fc00000u) : value;
}
```

**Measurement — the change is visible in the shipped artifact.** Compiling the regenerated `pointwise_scale_bias.metal` golden with `-fmetal-math-mode=fast -S -emit-llvm` yields zero `fcmp` and zero `is.fpclass` occurrences at both `-O0` and `-O2`, and the two-mask test appears verbatim as `and i32 %10, 2139095040` / `icmp eq` / `and i32 %10, 8388607` / `icmp ne`. The regenerated goldens compile and return `7fc00000 7fc00000 7fc00000 40400000` for the operands `7fabcdef ffc00001 7f800001 3f800000` in all seven math-mode/contract combinations dispatched.

**Fact — the helper is still not sufficient alone, and the code says so.** Under `nnan` an arithmetic result that is a NaN has no defined value, so there is nothing well defined left for any canonicalization to map, however the predicate is spelled. `MetalNumericalRequirement::SafeMathMode` is therefore still recorded at the `ConvertOp::CanonicalizeF32Nan` site, with the reasoning corrected from "the compiler could delete the test" (measurably false) to "the operand has no defined value" (a specification-level fact). `MetalNumericalRequirement::SafeMathMode`'s own documentation was rewritten for the same reason.

### The load-bearing correctness finding: `SubnormalMode::Preserve` is unrealizable, and the emitter was claiming otherwise

**Fact — the defect.** `realization_requirements` mapped `SubnormalMode::Preserve` to `MetalNumericalRequirement::SafeMathMode`, and that variant's documentation stated `-fmetal-math-mode=safe` is "required whenever a realization ... preserves subnormals". Selecting `safe` does not preserve subnormals. The emitter was naming a flag that does not deliver the obligation, which is the "silently approximating an unsupported case" AGENTS.md forbids.

**Measurement — Apple GPU `f32` arithmetic flushes subnormals in every mode.** An emitted `x * 1.0f` returns `0x00000000` for the operand `0x00000001`; an emitted `x * 2.0f` returns `0x00000000` for `0x00400000` and for `0x00000001`; an emitted `x * 0.5f` returns `0x00000000` for `0x00800000`. Both subnormal *operands* and subnormal *results* are affected. This holds under `safe`, `relaxed` and `fast`, at every `-O` level, and identically through the offline `xcrun metal` driver and through runtime `MTLCompileOptions` compilation on the same device — so it is not an artifact of the offline flag surface. The front end records `air.compile.denorms_disable` in `air.compile_options` under every one of those combinations, including `safe`; neither `-fdenormal-fp-math=ieee` nor any other `metal` driver flag located in `metal --help`/`--help-hidden` clears it.

**Measurement — the boundary is arithmetic, not materialization.** A kernel that only loads and stores returns `00400000 00000001 80400000 007fffff` unchanged for those same operands, in every math mode and through both compilation paths. Materialization conversions preserve subnormal bit patterns exactly; only arithmetic flushes.

**Inference.** Subnormal preservation for `f32` on the governed Apple families is a hard target feasibility limit, not a compiler-flag choice and not a cost. AGENTS.md requires hard feasibility to be separated from cost and rejected with an explainable reason. It therefore must not appear in the compiler-requirement set at all.

**Fact — how it is now modelled.** `MetalTargetFacts` gained a required `subnormal_arithmetic: MetalSubnormalArithmetic` field (`FlushesToZero` | `PreservesSubnormals`), stated by the caller exactly as `buffer_binding_limit` is, with the measurement recorded on the type. Emission records `MetalNumericalGap::SubnormalFlushInArithmetic` when a kernel declares `SubnormalMode::Preserve`, the target states `FlushesToZero`, and the kernel emits at least one `f32` arithmetic operation — the last condition matching the measured materialization/arithmetic boundary, and derived from the operation vocabulary (`BinaryOp::F32Add`/`F32Multiply`) rather than from any recognized shape. The gap is exposed as `MetalTranslationUnit::numerical_gaps()`, written into the generated MSL provenance header so a caller that keeps only the text still carries it, and enforced by `MetalTranslationUnit::require_declared_realization()`, which fails closed with `MetalEmitError::UnrealizableNumericalObligation`. `NumericalRealization::preserves_subnormals()` in `tiler-metal-aot` returns `false` for every selectable realization, with the same measurement, so the driver side gives a definite answer instead of letting a caller infer that the strictest flags imply IEEE-754 subnormal behaviour.

**Proposal — one atomic decision for Tom.** Emission currently *records* the gap and rejects at the conformance step; it does not reject at `emit_translation_unit`. The strictly fail-closed alternative is to reject there. The trade is real and I did not want to make it unilaterally.

- *Reject at emission.* Strongest reading of "fail closed for an unsupported realization". Consequence: because `SubnormalMode` has exactly one variant, `Preserve`, and every `ScalarProgram` variant performs `f32` arithmetic, **no kernel of the bounded proof profile can be emitted for any Apple target at all**. The four golden fixtures, the crate doctest, and every downstream slice that consumes emitted MSL stop existing. A fixture could only survive by declaring `PreservesSubnormals` for a `MacOs` target, which is a false statement about the hardware.
- *Reject at the conformance step (implemented).* `emit_translation_unit` still produces the unit; `require_declared_realization()` is the conformance claim and rejects. The gap is un-loseable: it is in the typed record, in the generated source header, and in the crate's module documentation. Consequence: a caller that never asks for conformance never sees the rejection.

My recommendation is the implemented behaviour, on the ground that the flush is observable only when a subnormal value actually reaches an arithmetic operation, which emission cannot know, and that blocking the entire prototype on a limit shared by all Apple GPUs would stop the slice without teaching anything the record does not already state. But the decision belongs to the numerical contract, not to this backend. A third option worth considering is widening `SubnormalMode` in `tiler-ir` with a `FlushToZero` variant so a profile can *declare* the Apple behaviour and conform honestly; that is `implementation/ir` scope and this ticket cannot do it.

### What is carried by operations and what is not

**Fact — carried by the emitted operations, verified structurally in-crate and measured on device.** Exact `f32` immediates through `as_type<float>` with no decimal rendering; one arithmetic operation per statement (defeats `-ffp-contract=on`); an integer-only NaN predicate (defeats every math mode's floating-point licences); and reduction order as the IR's own bounded loop with a loop-carried dependence. `emit.rs`, `lib.rs`, and `record.rs` now state this split explicitly instead of describing all four numerical properties as one undifferentiated "explicit numerics" claim.

**Fact — not carried by any operation, reported instead.** Signed-zero results, NaN-valued arithmetic, and reassociation each require `-fmetal-math-mode=safe`; cross-statement contraction requires `-ffp-contract=off`; subnormal preservation requires nothing that exists.

**Measurement — reduction reassociation found no counterexample.** A serial reduction over three contributors seeded with `[1.0, 2^-24, 2^-24]` returns `0x3f800000` under every math-mode/contract combination, where any pairwise regrouping would return `0x3f800001`; the same holds for `[1e30, -1e30, 1.0]`, where regrouping would return `0x00000000`, and for a twelve-contributor reduction seeded with `1.0` followed by eleven `2^-24`. **Measurement boundary.** This is a bounded negative result over three fixtures on one device and one toolchain, not evidence that `reassoc` never reorders a Tiler reduction. `SafeMathMode` remains required for reassociation on the independent signed-zero evidence and on the LLVM licence itself.

**Measurement — the canonicalization is not a reliable contraction barrier.** With the canonicalization interposed between the multiply and the add, no fusion was observed even at `-ffp-contract=fast`. But `-S -emit-llvm` shows the compiler hoisting the `fadd` above the `select`, so the shipped IR is `%13 = fadd contract %9, 1.0` chained directly on the raw `%9` product — the contractible pair is restored at the IR level and only backend scheduling declined to fuse it. A control kernel with the canonicalization removed *does* fuse under `-ffp-contract=fast` (`0x3fc58f9d` against `0x3fc58f9e`), which proves the probe is sensitive. **Inference.** Do not treat the NaN canonicalization as a contraction barrier. `-ffp-contract=off` is the only thing closing that case, and the code comments say so.

### Public surface changed (Tom-reviewed boundary)

**Fact — `tiler-metal`.** `target::MetalSubnormalArithmetic` is new (`#[non_exhaustive]`, variants `FlushesToZero`/`PreservesSubnormals`, with `as_str` and `Display`). `MetalTargetFacts` gained the public field `subnormal_arithmetic`, and `MetalTargetFacts::new` gained a fifth parameter in that position — a breaking constructor change, which ADR 0074 §5 deliberately accepts for a caller-constructed input record. `record::MetalNumericalGap` is new (`#[non_exhaustive]`, one variant, with `rule` and `Display`). `MetalTranslationUnit` gained `numerical_gaps()` and `require_declared_realization()`. `diagnostic::MetalEmitError` gained `UnrealizableNumericalObligation { gap }` with rule `unrealizable-numerical-obligation`. No existing item was removed or renamed.

**Fact — `tiler-metal-aot`.** `MathMode` gained `relaxes_floating_point()` and `assumes_finite()`. `FpContract` gained `contracts_across_statements()`. `NumericalRealization` gained `preserves_signed_zero()`, `permits_reassociation()`, `preserves_nan_results()` and `preserves_subnormals()`. These are the typed predicates a consumer needs to check a `MetalNumericalRequirement` against a selected realization without string-matching flag text; the crate-edge wiring that will consume them belongs to `compile-golden-msl-through-the-aot-driver-in-the-gate`. `preserves_signed_zero` and `permits_reassociation` return the same bit for today's three modes and are still named separately, because `nsz` and `reassoc` are distinct LLVM licences a future mode could split and the crate's own documentation commits to keeping the permissions independent rather than collapsing them into one `fast` bit. No item was removed or renamed.

### Tests

**Fact — added.** In `tiler-metal`: `the_nan_predicate_is_an_integer_test_no_math_mode_can_relax` (pins the absence of a floating-point predicate and the exact emitted integer test); `the_emitted_and_host_nan_predicates_agree` (walks the exponent/significand boundaries the two shared constants separate); `subnormal_preservation_is_recorded_as_an_unrealizable_gap`; `a_subnormal_preserving_target_has_no_gap`; `every_arithmetic_kernel_records_the_subnormal_gap`; plus gap assertions folded into the empty-portfolio test, which is the only in-crate way to exercise the no-arithmetic branch because no `ScalarProgram` variant is arithmetic-free. In `tiler-metal-aot`: `each_math_mode_states_the_licences_it_applies`, `only_fast_contraction_fuses_across_statements`, `the_strict_baseline_recovers_everything_except_subnormals`, and the self-skipping `the_integer_nan_predicate_compiles_under_every_realization`, which compiles the integer-predicate form through the real `xcrun` driver in all nine math-mode × contract combinations and asserts the realizations do not all produce identical bytes, so the matrix cannot pass vacuously.

**Fact — the four goldens were regenerated.** They now carry the integer predicate, the stated `f32 arithmetic subnormals: flushes-to-zero` target fact, the operations-carried summary, and the unrealizable-obligation list.

The in-gate tests pin structure and compiler acceptance; they do not pin computed values.

**Fact — gate results.** `uv run --locked python scripts/check_repository.py` passes (`complete repository validation passed`, exit 0), as do `scripts/check_rust.py`, `scripts/docs.py validate`, `ticketsplease lint`, `git diff --check`, and `ticketsplease guard tkt/prototype-metal-numerical-realization` (13 files, affected scopes exactly the declared `implementation/metal`, `implementation/metal-aot`, `project/tickets`; the reported collisions are shared-`project/tickets` overlaps plus direct overlaps with the two already-`done` dependency tickets). ### Remaining unknowns

**Fact.** Three things this ticket did not close. Whether `reassoc` can reorder a larger or differently shaped Tiler reduction is unmeasured beyond three fixtures. Whether the runtime backend compiler that turns AIR into GPU ISA at pipeline-state creation applies any further relaxation keyed on `air.compile.fast_math_enable` beyond what was observed is untested in isolation; the end-to-end dispatch results bound it, but the stage was not probed separately. And whether any non-Apple future Metal family preserves `f32` subnormals is unknown — the `MetalSubnormalArithmetic::PreservesSubnormals` variant exists so that answer has somewhere to live, and is currently exercised only by a test.

## Correction appended 2026-07-27 — the `x * 1.0f` row is superseded

**The outcome above is left as written**, because a `done` ticket's record is evidence of what was believed at the time. This note says which one sentence of it no longer holds.

**Superseded row.** Under "Apple GPU `f32` arithmetic flushes subnormals in every mode", the outcome states that "an emitted `x * 1.0f` returns `0x00000000` for the operand `0x00000001`". It does not. `case.multiply_one.*.results` in `spikes/apple-targets/results/2026-07-24-numerics-xcode26.6-metal32023.883/record.tsv` returns `00000001` unchanged — and every other operand unchanged — under `safe`, `relaxed`, and `fast`, at both `-O0` and `-O2`. ADR 0076's re-verification reached the opposite conclusion for this kernel independently, and `docs/research/apple-targets/numerical-behaviour.md` is the record that supersedes this row.

**Why this kernel cannot support the claim, whatever it returns.** `x * 1.0f` is an identity on every operand, so it admits no execution witness: a returned subnormal is equally consistent with "the multiply ran and preserved it" and with "the multiply was folded away and nothing ran". The measurement shows both readings are live — at `-O2` the kernel retains zero floating-point operations under every mode, while at `-O0` it retains an `fmul` and *still* returns the operand unchanged, which leaves a folded multiply and a hardware special case for `1.0` indistinguishable. This is the exact shape the probe's witness guard was later built to reject, and `numerical-behaviour.md` finding 7 states it in those terms.

**The surrounding claim is unaffected**, and that was checked rather than assumed. The other two rows reproduce *with* non-subnormal execution witnesses, which is what makes them evidence: `multiply_half` returns `00000000` for `00800000` while returning `3e397ef9` for `3eb97ef9`, and `multiply_two` returns `00000000` for `00000001`, `00400000`, and `007fffff` while returning `01000000` for `00800000` and `3f397ef9` for `3eb97ef9`. In each case a non-subnormal operand changes, so the arithmetic demonstrably ran, and subnormals still went to zero. "Apple GPU `f32` arithmetic flushes subnormals in every mode" therefore stands on two rows instead of three.
