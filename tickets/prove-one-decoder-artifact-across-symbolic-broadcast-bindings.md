# Prove one decoder artifact across symbolic broadcast bindings

## User-visible outcome

The complete decoder-layer graph compiles, packages, and evaluates as one artifact identity across the admitted new-position range, including decode at one, with no row-specific graph or payload fallback.

## Work

- Assemble the decoder layer once with sourced broadcast mappings and retain one operation/value census.
- Carry live extents through ABI expressions and payload indexing using the existing symbolic-artifact delivery chain; do not compile one payload per binding.
- Compile and package the real governed path. Tests use the governed semantic registry, governed lowering/provider, real compiler/build/artifact path, and reference evaluator—no mock or fake implementation substitutes for any authority.
- Evaluate at `T = 1`, `2`, `10`, and the admitted upper boundary, including the pinned C1 prefill/decode neighbours.
- Collapse the two decoder-layer count assertions into one and preserve the old perturbation as a check that fails if row-specific spellings return.
- Update L5, L6, and L8 identity and cold-pipeline claims only after the complete proof passes.

## Acceptance

- One semantic identity, occurrence signature, selected schedule identity, artifact identity, payload population, and cache subject are observed across all four bindings.
- Runtime shapes, accessible byte ranges, launch geometry, and reference output agree at every binding.
- Perturb the mapping extent spelling, environment identity, live bound, schedule carrier, artifact identity pin, and decoder occurrence census separately; retain each failure message.
- Count at least one parametric broadcast and at least one extent-one degeneration so an empty or widening-only population cannot pass.

## Stop conditions

Stop rather than claim one artifact if any backend specializes source or payload bytes by binding, if `T = 1` selects a different semantic/schedule spelling, or if the proof can pass without executing a real parametric broadcast.
