## Context

[ADR 0078](0078-name-the-intended-public-extension-seams.md) left `frontier::PhysicalImplementationProvider` deliberately unassigned, and recorded that the question which actually decides it is architectural: does target-specific scheduling knowledge live as typed target-profile *data* consumed by in-compiler providers, or as *code* in backend crates? It also recorded why guessing was not worth it — no backend had exercised the architecture. Two bounded spikes have since exercised it from opposite ends, which is the whole of what is new.

**Measurement — the [forkless custom Metal physical provider](../../spikes/extensions/forkless-physical-provider/README.md), at `7b1e3a7e15b09dd3ea65c88759699655c462be4a`.** A separately authored crate can build a specialized Metal implementation entirely against public surfaces and cannot deliver it. Its value is in *where* it falsifies: `acme-provider` takes no dependency on `tiler-metal` at all, lowers its region through `tiler_ir::kernel::lower_scheduled_region`, emits it through `tiler_metal::emit::emit_translation_unit`, and passes `require_declared_realization` against measured Apple facts — so nothing in the emitter is in the way. The two blockers are properties of one registration seam, and its compile-fail fixture fails on a missing *method* rather than a missing item.

**Measurement — the [bounded scalar CPU backend vertical](../../spikes/target-profiles/scalar-cpu-vertical/README.md), base `488efac`.** A materially different backend went end to end through the accepted target-profile, artifact, and runtime contracts against `crates/` unmodified — its own governed profile key, backend family, and representation, a real payload, a one-way routing commit, and twelve `f32` elements agreeing bit for bit with `tiler-reference` including a negative zero, both least-magnitude subnormals, a canonicalized NaN payload, and both infinities. Deleting the `CanonicalizeF32Nan` arm from its interpreter made the run fail at the comparison naming exactly one differing element, which is what makes the agreement a result rather than a tautology. **It installed no physical provider and needed none.**

**Inference — the two spikes wanted disjoint, strict subsets of a backend, which is what makes "a backend" the wrong unit of registration.** The composition record's responsibility matrix enumerates thirteen subjects a composed backend set involves, with the role that owns each, the moment it is fixed, and whether its identity is *compared*, carried as *provenance*, or *independently selected*. The forkless provider wanted exactly one of the thirteen and reused twelve; the CPU vertical wanted seven and reused six. Neither wanted all thirteen, and a contract whose unit of registration is "a backend" would force both to declare pieces they do not own.

**Fact — ten of the thirteen subjects already have an installation mechanism, and the three that do not are nameable.** Nothing installs a physical implementation from outside the crate (matrix row 4); nothing outside the crate can register an opaque call either, though the compile path itself now carries the registry (row 5); and the build-time orchestrator has no indirection at all (row 8). Row 12, the runtime adapter, has no registry either, and that is not a gap of the same kind — independent selection *is* its mechanism, and the record eliminates giving it one. ## Decision

### 1. Target-specific scheduling knowledge is a checked combination, split at feasibility

**This is Tom's, it is the headline, and items 2 and 3 depend on it.** *Proposal.* Target profiles declare what a target *can do*; providers propose *what to do with it*; the host performs every comparison between the two. A profile carries facts, a provider carries choices, and neither may hold the other's kind of statement.

*Why the two pure positions do not survive.* Pure data fails on the forkless spike's own specialization: a 32-thread workgroup is a schedule choice that no declared axis expresses, and expressing it would mean giving the profile a "preferred workgroup width" axis whose *value* is a choice rather than a target fact — which contaminates the one vocabulary hard feasibility is decided against. It fails a second time on reach: **Fact —** every variant of `CapabilityAxis` is `pub(crate)` in `crates/tiler-compiler/src/target/feasibility.rs:211` and each of the seven admitted axes is reachable only through a `TargetProfileBuilder::declare_*` method that hard-codes it, so under pure data every new specialization is a compiler change. Pure code fails on the CPU vertical, which needed profile data and no provider code at all, and fails harder on authority: feasibility is a decision procedure ADR 0078 item 6 makes permanently internal, so removing the declared facts it compares against would move the comparison to the party being compared.

*What would refute the recommendation.* A specialization fully determined by declared target facts, which would make provider code redundant for that case and argue the split belongs later than feasibility.

### 2. The physical-implementation provider becomes an installable public seam

**Tom's, under ADR 0075's trait-and-namespace category, regardless of the recommendation.** *Proposal.* Row 4 acquires the three obligations it lacks — visibility, installation, and observability — through a frozen, per-session, immutable registry installed on the compilation request, in the idiom [ADR 0078](0078-name-the-intended-public-extension-seams.md) item 4 already closed for lowering. There is no global, no discovery, and no ambient search path; registering one `ProviderIdentity` twice is a typed installation refusal rather than a silent replacement; installation order is retained for reporting and is *not* precedence, because the frontier's admitted set is canonical and provider-order-independent.

*Grounds.* The alternative is keeping row 4 internal, which makes "contribute one specialized implementation" mean "fork `tiler-compiler`" — the exact outcome the originating work exists to prevent, and the one the forkless spike measured. The seam already satisfies ADR 0078 item 1's propose-then-re-verify test without modification: `enumerate_frontier` resubmits every proposed body through the host's own verification, and a proposal carries a body, an applicability predicate, and a cost and nothing else, so the host derives identity, exact resource requirements, and the boundary contract itself.

*What this does not settle, stated because the sketch makes it easy to assume.* Whether a third party's structural cost estimate is comparable to the governed provider's is **unmeasured**: the forkless spike could not reach `enumerate_frontier`, and the program it compiled retains a single fused alternative, so even the ordering the public surface exposes had nothing to order.

### 3. Whether ADR 0078 item 5's sharpened trigger has already fired

**Tom's, and one sentence settles it.** ADR 0078 restated his deferral trigger as *a physical implementation provider defined outside `tiler-compiler` reaching `enumerate_frontier` through `compile()`*, and flagged as an open question whether that sharpening restates his intent or narrows it. The answer decides whether items 1 and 2 are ripe now or still waiting; this record supplies the evidence and does not answer it.

### 4. Composition is per responsibility, and a partial provider is the normal case

*Proposal.* A component claims rows of the responsibility matrix and owes exactly those rows' obligations. There is no `BackendProvider`, no provider bundle, no emitter registration, and no runtime-adapter registration, and this record proposes none. Four consequences are normative and each is a place a plausible simplification would be wrong.

**Two claimants are a contradiction at the lowering seam and an alternative at the frontier, and neither rule may be generalized to the other.** ADR 0078 item 3 already states the asymmetry: exactly one authority may define what an occurrence *means*, and a region may legitimately have several correct *implementations*, retained side by side and separated by folded provenance in `ImplementationProposalIdentity`. *Measurement, forkless spike.* A specialization is identity-bearing without a new identity authority for at least one axis: `threads_per_workgroup` is free under the intrinsic verifier and is folded into `CanonicalScheduledRegionIdentity`, so a 32-thread and a 1-thread implementation of one region are distinct identities emitting byte-identical bodies under distinct entry-point symbols. *That claim is narrow* — an axis the request-subject binding does compare would be rejected, and nothing measures where that line falls.

**Reusing another backend's emitter is an ordinary Cargo edge and must stay one.** *Measurement, forkless spike.* The probe reached `tiler_ir` and `tiler_metal::emit` as public dependencies with no mediation. Routing that edge through a registry would convert a compile-time-checked reuse into a runtime-resolved one and buy nothing, since emission consumes verified kernels and knows nothing about who proposed them.

**A runtime adapter reports facts and never adjudicates them.** *Fact.* `DecodedProgram::prepare` refuses a `RouteRequirement::BackendFeature` whose owner is not the host's own `BackendKey` before any adapter is consulted — `prepare` binds the variant's route requirements at `crates/tiler-runtime/src/load.rs:448`, and the owner check that refuses is `route_requirements` at `load.rs:888-897` — and `LiveDeviceQualification::resolve_live_device_requirements` takes a host closure returning an observation while the *loader* performs the comparison — so `Unrecognized`, a wrong-shaped answer, and an unsatisfied requirement stay three distinct rejections and an adapter cannot decide a row's own comparison by answering cleverly. A composition design must not move the comparison into the adapter to save a round trip. **Corrected 2026-08-05 by [`correct-the-residual-floor-relation-prose-outside-the-artifact-scopes`](../../tickets/correct-the-residual-floor-relation-prose-outside-the-artifact-scopes.md):** the third rejection read "an unmet floor", which named a relation the one live-device dimension no longer has — [`correct-the-subgroup-threads-route-dimension-meaning`](../../tickets/correct-the-subgroup-threads-route-dimension-meaning.md) made `RouteResourceDimension::SubgroupThreads` a width equality at `77c36d5`. What the loader refuses there is `LoadRejection::UnsatisfiedRouteRequirement`, the row's own relation failing rather than a bound being undershot, so the three-way split this item depends on and the decision that the loader owns the comparison are both unchanged. **Also corrected 2026-08-05 by [`correct-the-runtime-route-requirement-relation-prose`](../../tickets/correct-the-runtime-route-requirement-relation-prose.md):** the final clause read "reverse a capacity comparison", the same staleness under a different word — "capacity" implies an ordering in which more is better, and under an equality a wider device fails exactly as a narrower one does, so there is no direction left to reverse. What survives is this item's actual content, that the *loader* performs the comparison and an adapter only reports; `RouteResourceRequirement` carries a dimension and a `required` quantity and no relation at all, the relation being `RouteResourceDimension`'s own exhaustive match. The two runtime doc comments stating the same thing — `LiveDeviceObservation` in `crates/tiler-runtime/src/load/route.rs` and the comparison table in `crates/tiler-runtime/src/adapter.rs` — moved in that ticket's single commit, so code and record still say the same thing.

**Producer provenance is never matched against adapter identity, and the join is bytes.** *Fact.* The only things a loading host states about itself are one `TargetProfileRef` of governed key plus exact descriptor identity, one `BackendKey`, one `RepresentationKey`, and one dtype-dispatch map keyed by arithmetic type; nothing in `tiler_runtime::load::ExecutionEnvironment` names which crate, provider, or process produced the payload. *Inference.* A join of the form "the adapter must have been registered by the producing provider" would have to invent an identity subject that does not exist and could not survive a process boundary. Provenance rides along as evidence about how the bytes came to be, never as a matching key. Relatedly, the backend-family and representation pair is compared *as a pair* while the profile is *classified*, so "this host cannot execute these bytes" and "this artifact is for another target" stay two refusals with two remedies.

### 5. A compilation discloses the offered and the selected physical-provider sets separately

*Proposal.* A compilation reports the complete frozen physical-provider environment it was offered, and the providers its retained plan selected, as two sets rather than one. An installed-but-never-selected provider appears in the first and is absent from the second.

*Grounds.* "This provider was available and lost on cost" and "this provider was never installed" are the two findings a composition failure most needs to tell apart, and merging the sets destroys exactly that distinction. **Fact — today neither is answerable at all**, because `Compilation::offered_providers` is populated from the lowering registry alone — `compile` fills it from `capabilities.0.lowering().providers()` at `crates/tiler-compiler/src/session.rs:2158-2159` — so the governed physical provider's own identity cannot appear; the forkless spike's `public_surface_names_no_physical_provider` asserts that against a compilation that really ran.

Both greps were re-run on 2026-08-08 at the offered half's landing commit and each still returns exactly one line, which is why the correction above reports the lowering population as *unchanged* rather than moved. The offered half's own anchors, added by the same rule and each returning one line: `grep -n "pub fn offered_physical_providers(" crates/tiler-compiler/src/session.rs` for the accessor, and `grep -n "pub(crate) fn offered_identities(" crates/tiler-compiler/src/physical_provider.rs` for the environment it reads. Neither is spelled with a bracket, so neither can fail as a silent absence the way the retired declaration line did.

### 6. An installed physical provider's identity is selected-plan provenance and never graph meaning

*Proposal.* It sits beside the lowering authority in selected-plan provenance, which is the direction [ADR 0072](0072-separate-semantic-meaning-from-provider-provenance.md) already fixes, and it is never a semantic-graph subject. *What would refute it:* a demonstration that two providers producing byte-identical plans should share one artifact identity, which would argue for excluding it.

### 7. A provider may pre-run the numerical-realization comparison; feasibility stays a rejection

*Proposal, and the asymmetry is the decision.* **Fact.** `verify_schedule_with_feasibility` (`crates/tiler-compiler/src/physical.rs`; find it with `grep -n "pub(crate) fn verify_schedule_with_feasibility"`, one line, and its own doc comment names the five in order) runs five gates in fixed order, and two of them — numerical-realization agreement and hard feasibility — are each *half*-reachable out of crate in the same shape: the provider holds one operand and the host holds the comparison. Gates 1 and 4, request authority and request-subject binding, are correctly host-only and stay so, because both are statements about a program the host normalized and a provider positioned to re-derive them would be positioned to disagree with normalization. Gate 2, whole-region intrinsic verification, is already public and is the one a provider must run on itself.

Expose the request's resolved `NumericalRealization` — a value the provider must already construct to fill its body, compared by equality. Keep hard feasibility a rejection the provider reads back, because it is a decision procedure ADR 0078 item 6 makes permanently internal and a second implementation of it invites drift.

*Why not the cheaper symmetric option.* Exposing only the rejection leaves a provider unable to distinguish "this target refuses my strategy" from "I built the body wrong" without a round trip, and makes the decline channel unusable — that channel exists to say what a provider considered and withheld *before* constructing a region, and a provider that must propose in order to learn why it should not have cannot supply it. An enumeration is complete only if it can state what was deliberately withheld.

### 8. A backend validates its own payload from bytes, before the routing commit

*Proposal, normative on every backend.* *Measurement — CPU vertical.* A payload's `code` bytes are opaque to every check the artifact layer performs; the six payload-level refusals that run measured — a foreign domain separator, truncation, trailing bytes, an out-of-range slot, an access-mode violation, and the accepted neighbour they were measured against — are all checks the backend owns. *Inference.* The artifact layer provably cannot discharge this, and [ADR 0051](0051-make-runtime-routing-commit-one-way.md) forbids selecting another plan after the commit, so an unvalidated payload has nowhere left to fail safely. A backend must also declare its representation such that a host consuming a different one is refused rather than translated; the CPU vertical's host consuming `tiler.cpu.scalar-image-v2` was refused as `runtime.unexecutable-payload`, which is that obligation working. **Corrected 2026-08-01:** the class named there no longer exists. [`select-executable-variants-across-registered-backend-families`](../../tickets/select-executable-variants-across-registered-backend-families.md) made host-relative ineligibility a filter applied before any applicability guard, so that host now excludes the variant as `VariantIneligibility::UnsupportedRepresentation` and — because the spike's artifact packages a single variant — the load is refused as `runtime.no-eligible-variant`. Re-measured at `e2da98f` on the same perturbation. The obligation this item states is unchanged, and so is the evidence for it: the representation the host consumes is still what refuses the route, and it is still refused rather than translated.

### 9. `prepare` is optional, and the exact condition is stated rather than implied

*Proposal.* **Fact — read from the loader.** `preflight` is sufficient exactly when the selected variant has zero deferred predicates *and* zero route requirements: `DecodedProgram::preflight` calls `refuse_route_requirements` then `refuse_deferred` at `crates/tiler-runtime/src/load.rs:411-412`, in that order and for that reason — a host short of a bound device learns that before it learns it is also short of a prepared pipeline. Once a caller enters `prepare`, both device stages are mandatory even when their lists are empty, because a `RoutePreparation` is reachable only through a resolved qualification. *Measurement.* The CPU vertical carried zero of both and completed correctly on `preflight` alone; Metal cannot, because only a built pipeline knows its own `maxTotalThreadsPerThreadgroup`. The natural reading of a Metal-derived pipeline is that `prepare` is universal, and a provider contract that made it so would oblige a backend to invent a pipeline object it does not have.

### 10. The `BackendKey` and `RepresentationKey` namespaces stay open, with collision a producer-side responsibility

*Proposal.* Minting stays open and this record adds no registry, because a registry over these keys reintroduces exactly the global mutable state item 12 eliminates. *Measurement — CPU vertical.* `tiler.cpu.scalar` and `tiler.cpu.scalar-image-v1` were minted without touching a registry, and that openness is what made a new backend expressible; the cost is that nothing prevents two producers minting one key for two things. *What would refute it:* two independently minted identical keys reaching one loading host, which the current single-consumer profile cannot produce but a published crate set could.

**Fact — and separately from the namespace question, the artifact layer's key grammar was weaker than the compiler's, which was a real asymmetry rather than a naming nuisance.** `validate_key`, at `crates/tiler-artifact/src/program/keys.rs:73-85` when this was written, is the entire validator behind `BackendKey`, `RepresentationKey`, `TargetProfileKey`, `FeasibilityRuleSetKey`, `CapabilityKey`, and `RouteFeatureKey`, and at drafting it checked non-empty and at most 256 bytes with no alphabet, case, separator, or namespace check in the crate — so an artifact from any producer but that compiler could carry a profile key the compiler's own constructor would refuse. **Corrected 2026-08-01:** [`reconcile-the-two-target-profile-key-grammars`](../../tickets/reconcile-the-two-target-profile-key-grammars.md) settled it — `validate_key`, found with `grep -n "fn validate_key" crates/tiler-artifact/src/program/keys.rs` (one line; the `keys.rs:125-143` span this Fact carried was accurate when written and repinned to a searchable anchor on 2026-08-08), enforces the compiler's alphabet family-wide through `admits`, refusing as `ArtifactBuildError::NoncanonicalKeyByte`, while the 128/256 byte bounds deliberately stay apart as one producer's minting bound versus this layer's admission bound; the module documentation of `keys.rs` and of `crates/tiler-compiler/src/target.rs` carries the derivation, including why the direction makes the unequal bounds safe rather than a gap. **The reproduction this Fact printed is now its own positive control:** `grep -rn "is_ascii\|InvalidByte" crates/tiler-artifact/src/` returned nothing at drafting and returns one line at `7ad2aca` — `keys.rs:121`, the `admits` closure — while the identical grep over `crates/tiler-compiler/src/target.rs` still returns six lines. The asymmetry this Fact recorded no longer exists; the namespace question above is unchanged.

### 11. The build-time orchestration seam is a promoted closure, not a backend trait

*Proposal, and it is the one item that changes the accepted packaging profile rather than describing it.* Promote the existing backend-neutral assembler, `assemble_plan_artifact`, and move `BindingKind`, the zero-work dispatch policy, and the launch preconditions into what a backend supplies, rather than introducing a trait that would re-mediate an edge a closure parameter already abstracts. The residue that was genuinely not neutral is small and named: `BindingKind::Buffer`, where the enum has one variant, and `zero_work_skips_dispatch: true` with empty launch preconditions. All three are now this backend's stated answer rather than the orchestrator's assumption, in `metal_entry_declaration` at `crates/tiler-build/src/metal_plan.rs:403-405`.

*Why this is a packaging change.* **Fact.** `tiler-build` names `tiler-metal` and `tiler-metal-aot` as unconditional dependencies with no feature gate, so a second backend today needs either a second unconditional edge into the same crate or a sibling orchestrator. A promoted closure removes the choice rather than making it, because the second backend supplies its own payload declaration and needs no edge inside `tiler-build` at all. *What would refute it:* a second backend needing to vary something `assemble_artifact` derives rather than something it delegates, which would show the split is in the wrong place. **The exposed function and its parameter types are a public boundary and therefore Tom's — accepted by him on 2026-08-05 under [`accept-the-neutral-build-orchestration-boundary`](../../tickets/accept-the-neutral-build-orchestration-boundary.md).**

### 12. What no component may do

*Proposal, and every clause is a restatement of a boundary the tree already holds.* No component may register itself globally, depend on registration order for precedence, replace an existing registration, mutate a frozen registry, declare a numerical permission the host cannot verify, stamp its own provenance or resource requirements, attribute a cost estimate to any but the governed cost-model key, or return a semantically incorrect result to preserve a fast path. Offering nothing stays a legitimate local result and stays distinguishable from a rejection; a body that fails intrinsic verification fails the whole enumeration closed rather than degrading into the empty offer that looks just like it.

### 13. Trust, linkage, and the initial device profile are inherited, not extended

*Proposal — a restatement rather than a new position.* Backend composition adds **no** linkage mode to [the operation-extension contract's trust model](../operation-extensions.md#initial-trust-and-linkage-model), accepted by [ADR 0045](0045-bound-proc-macro-providers-to-host-dependencies.md): trusted native compiler code, statically linked, supplied explicitly to a session, with compiler-process privileges and no sandbox, and the proc-macro limitation inherited whole. Native dynamic loading, a stable plugin ABI, `dlopen`-style adapter discovery, hot reload, untrusted or sandboxed providers, cross-process provider callbacks, and runtime source compilation of a kernel are jointly deferred, and no part of this design reserves a seam for any of them. The one thing that crosses a process boundary is artifact transport: bytes, validated from bytes on arrival, with no Rust object and no callback travelling with them.

*Inference — why deferring costs nothing to state now.* Every join in the matrix is already a governed key or a canonical identity rather than a Rust object; `TypeId`, vtable addresses, function pointers, and registration order appear in none of them. The artifact half of a dynamic-loading design is therefore already built, and what is deferred is only how a *process* acquires code, which shapes no identity subject. A design that instead joined producer to adapter by a shared Rust object would have to be *undone* to defer anything.

The initial device profile is the accepted one restated and narrowed by nothing: one symbolic affinity, one live device, one ordered command stream ([ADR 0047](0047-model-placement-as-physical-properties.md)). More than one live device, sharding, collectives, cross-device transfers, queue affinity, and multiple command streams stay outside this record entirely, and whether they become expressible at all is [`multi-device-and-sharding-scope-gate`](../../tickets/multi-device-and-sharding-scope-gate.md)'s product decision.

### 14. Every other unsupported case rejects explicitly

*Proposal, stated as refusals because [ADR 0069](0069-use-a-general-compilation-boundary.md)'s general compilation boundary requires an unsupported case to reject rather than approximate.* A `ProposalBody::View` is the one body variant still carrying a reservation and rejecting as `FrontierRejection::UnsupportedVariant`. Out-of-crate opaque-call registration stays compiler-owned and crate-private per ADR 0078's correction; the compile path itself now carries the registry, which [`register-opaque-calls-on-the-compile-path`](../../tickets/register-opaque-calls-on-the-compile-path.md) closed by composing the provider list and the call registry into the one authority `compile` installs, and the pair production installs registers no call because Tiler declares none of its own. Four vocabulary gaps are named rather than papered over, each measured by the CPU vertical: no availability phase names a bound host *process*, so a process-bound fact must borrow `LiveDevicePreflight` ([`name-a-host-process-availability-phase`](../../tickets/name-a-host-process-availability-phase.md)); `ArtifactExecutionPolicy` was a two-valued GPU dichotomy of which one value was unroutable — **resolved 2026-08-01** by [`route-or-refuse-the-device-translation-execution-policy`](../../tickets/route-or-refuse-the-device-translation-execution-policy.md), which retired `RequiresDeviceTranslation` rather than building a route ADR 0086 gives no authority to admit, leaving a one-valued vocabulary whose gap is now the CPU one this record's own sentence describes (no spelling for an interpreted image, a JIT input, or a dynamically linked object) rather than an unroutable value; `PayloadProvenance` requires Apple-shaped fields with no CPU meaning; and no `CapabilityAxis` names a target triple, ABI, or data layout, so both survive only inside a profile key string and payload provenance. A transport mapping is assumed in neither direction: a scalar entry's transports are its ABI slots because storage is bound by signature position, Metal's mapping is not the identity in general, and a contract assuming either is wrong for the other backend.

**Corrected 2026-08-01 — the third of the four gaps is closed.** [`generalize-payload-provenance-beyond-the-apple-shape`](../../tickets/generalize-payload-provenance-beyond-the-apple-shape.md) settled it: `PayloadProvenance` carries a `PayloadPlatform`, and which fields a payload owes follows the shape it declares rather than a platform the target may not have. A backend whose toolchain resolved against no versioned SDK states `PayloadPlatform::Unversioned` and owes no deployment minimum and no SDK field; one that did states `PayloadPlatform::VersionedSdk` and owes all four, refused by field name where the payload's identity is derived and again on decode. The direction this took is worth recording because the gap could have been closed the weaker way: the fields did **not** become optional. A Metal payload owes exactly what it owed before, to a check rather than to a convention, so the record redistributed the obligation without relaxing it — which is what keeps this a neutrality fix rather than a validation hole. The widening moved no already-encodable payload's bytes, by an appended tag whose per-tag injectivity argument the [artifact ABI](../artifact-abi.md) carries as the normative record. The other three gaps above stand as written, and their tickets are unchanged.

## Why this is one record rather than several

The graph-maintenance rule splits a decision packet into atomic records when more than one genuine product choice survives elimination. **It does not here, and the derivation is stated so a reader can refute the elimination rather than only the conclusion.** Each of the eleven decisions the composition record enumerates was tested against correctness, performance, and long-term maintainability, and in every case exactly one candidate survives: item 1 eliminates both pure positions above; item 2's alternative of keeping row 4 internal is eliminated by the fork it forces; items 5, 6, 8, 9, and 12 are each derived from an accepted decision or a measured fact with no alternative standing; item 7 eliminates both symmetric spellings and leaves the asymmetric middle; item 10's registry alternative is eliminated by the same global-state ground as ambient discovery; and item 11's trait alternative is eliminated by a neutral function that already exists and is already correct.

What is left for Tom is therefore not a choice among survivors. It is three acceptance acts of a different kind — a question about his own prior intent (item 3, which no amount of research can answer), the exact public boundaries ADR 0075 routes to him regardless of research quality (items 2, 5, and 11), and confirmation that refining his recorded deferral is what he meant (item 1). None of those is made more atomic by living in its own file, and splitting would fragment one participation model across several records, which is the duplicated-authority failure the documentation contract exists to prevent. If Tom's answer to item 1 or item 3 reopens a genuine alternative, *that* is when a second record is the right shape.

## Open questions

- **Whether a third-party physical provider's cost estimate is comparable to the governed provider's.** Unmeasured, and item 2 does not settle it. A proposal attributing its estimate to any key but the governed one is already a hard `FrontierError::MalformedCostProvenance`, so the *mechanism* forbids incomparable estimates being ranked; what is unmeasured is whether two providers' estimates under the one governed structural model are meaningfully ordered. Owned by the portfolio exercise named above.
- **Where the additivity line falls.** Item 4's identity-bearing-specialization claim rests on one axis being free under the intrinsic verifier. A specialization varying an axis the request-subject binding *does* compare would be rejected, and no case measures where that boundary is. Close this on the first provider proposal that deliberately varies an axis the request-subject binding compares; until that concrete rejection exists, moving the line would generalize beyond the one free axis the forkless spike measured.
- **Whether the artifact layer should enforce the compiler's key alphabet — resolved 2026-08-01 by [`reconcile-the-two-target-profile-key-grammars`](../../tickets/reconcile-the-two-target-profile-key-grammars.md). The answer is retained rather than deleted because the ground it turned on is what a later reader needs: being the authority for a governed key settles its *spelling* as well as its bound, since every one of these keys exists to be compared byte for byte across producers that never met, whereas a byte bound is a resource ceiling one layer may set for itself — which is why the alphabets reconciled and the 128/256 bounds deliberately did not. ## Alternatives considered

**A monolithic `Device` or `BackendProvider` trait bundling profile, physical proposals, emission, artifact production, and runtime adaptation.** Eliminated on three independent grounds. *Correctness:* the pieces are fixed at four different times — profile at compile time, emission at build time, artifact at publication, adapter at run time — so a single trait object cannot exist at all four and would either force a live-device-capable object into the compiler or split itself back into the four pieces it claimed to unify. *Maintainability:* both spikes wanted strict subsets, so the unit of registration would never match the unit of contribution. *And it breaks a working edge:* the emitter is reached today by an ordinary Cargo dependency the compiler checks, and routing it through a trait converts a compile-time-checked reuse into a runtime-resolved one. Even at row 8, the one row genuinely lacking a seam, the trait is the larger answer to the smaller question, because a narrower neutral shape already exists in private.

**Global registration or ambient discovery — a `static` registry, a link-time collector, or an environment-variable search path.** Eliminated on correctness. Compilation identity must be a deterministic function of the request, and ADR 0072 already places the complete frozen registry environment inside compilation-request provenance; a registry a linked-in crate or an environment variable can change makes two identical requests produce two identities for reasons no artifact records. It also makes the seam untestable in-process, since two tests in one binary would share it.

**Registration-order precedence, last-wins replacement, or thawing a frozen registry.** Eliminated on correctness, and the tree already refuses all three. Two matching lowering capabilities are `AmbiguousCapability` and two revisions of one provider are the same, deliberately, so no newer revision silently supersedes an older one; the frontier's admitted set is returned in canonical provider-order-independent order, so ordering has nothing to order. A replaced registration would leave the compilation-request environment either stale or silently rewritten, and a thaw would let a plan be selected under one authority and reported under another. Adding precedence converts a typed contradiction into a silent choice, which is the erosion ADR 0078 names as most likely.

**Joining build-time producer to runtime adapter by a shared Rust object, a `TypeId`, or a registration handle, or requiring the adapter's identity to match the producer's.** Eliminated on correctness. The join must survive a process boundary and no process-local value can; and there is no adapter identity in the artifact to match against, so adding one would assert that bytes may only be executed by their producer's counterpart — which is false, since the CPU vertical's payload is executable by any decoder of `tiler.cpu.scalar-image-v1`, and that substitutability is what a representation key *means*.

**Letting a provider declare its own cost model, stamp its own provenance, or accept a caller-supplied scoring callback.** Eliminated on correctness and determinism. Relaxing the governed cost key would let two providers' estimates be incomparable while still being ranked, which is a silently wrong selection rather than a refusal; self-stamped provenance makes the artifact's claim about which authority produced a plan forgeable; and a caller-supplied closure is neither deterministic across processes nor identity-bearing, so two identical requests could select different plans with nothing in the artifact recording why. Typed policy over governed cost identities is the shape that survives, and [`expose-explicit-backend-provider-and-selection-policy-composition`](../../tickets/expose-explicit-backend-provider-and-selection-policy-composition.md) already names it.

**Making the `prepare` stage mandatory for every backend.** Eliminated by measurement rather than by argument: the CPU vertical's plan carried zero deferred prepared-entry predicates and zero route requirements, and `preflight` alone was sufficient and correct.

**Treating "installed" and "visible" as one obligation, closed by a `pub` keyword.** Eliminated by the forkless spike's compile-fail evidence, which is a direct measurement rather than a reading: `no_physical_provider_installation_seam.rs` fails on a missing *method*, not a missing item, and the pass fixture beside it installs a lowering registry through the method that does exist. Publishing the `frontier` module would leave the second fixture failing exactly as it does now.

**Corrected 2026-08-08 — the elimination stands and its present-tense evidence sentence does not, because the missing method is the thing that landed.** The retired clause "`no_physical_provider_installation_seam.rs` fails on a missing *method*, not a missing item" is quoted here, so a grep hit lands inside this correction. That fixture's retained `.stderr` golden pins `error[E0599]: no method named `with_physical_providers` found for struct `CompileRequest<'a>``, and `CompileRequest::with_physical_providers` now exists, so the golden can no longer be produced. The probe takes `tiler-compiler` by `path`, so it tracks the live tree rather than the commit the Measurement is pinned to; spikes are run manually and gate nothing, so this is a stale artifact rather than a broken gate. [`refresh-the-forkless-physical-provider-spike-against-the-landed-seam`](../../tickets/refresh-the-forkless-physical-provider-spike-against-the-landed-seam.md) owns re-running it. **The elimination is unaffected**, and it is worth saying why: this alternative was rejected because installation and visibility are separate obligations, and the landing is the demonstration of that — publishing the trait was necessary and was not sufficient, and a second, independent change (`InstalledPhysicalProviders` plus the request field behind `with_physical_providers`) is what discharged installation. The Measurement in Context above stays true at its own recorded commit.

## Traceability
