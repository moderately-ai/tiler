# 0074: Use explicit conformance conventions for public Tiler APIs

**Status:** accepted. Conventions 3, 4, and 5 were amended on 2026-07-24, and two factual claims inside convention 5 about *where* it applies were corrected on 2026-07-25 without any clause changing meaning. A 2026-07-28 evidence refresh below records the first `ScalarProgram` replacement to exercise both the out-of-crate 5c recognizer and the same-crate product-match completeness guard; it changes no convention. The "Amendments" section below records exactly what changed, the evidence, and what a reader of the earlier text should un-learn.

## Context

Tiler's landed Rust surfaces already share a recognizable shape, but no document states it. Each new surface therefore rediscovers the shape by imitation and re-litigates it by per-case review. This record proposes writing the shape down so that a surface which conforms needs no bespoke design debate, and only a genuine deviation needs a decision.

The evidence is the merged code at commit `b642007`, not theory.

**Fact — the shape is already pervasive.** Every canonical *byte* identity in the workspace is an opaque newtype over `Vec<u8>` or `Box<[u8]>` whose storage is private and whose only reader is `as_bytes()`: `CanonicalIndexRegionIdentity` (`crates/tiler-ir/src/index/model.rs`), `CanonicalScheduledRegionIdentity` (`crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/model.rs`), `SemanticGraphIdentity` and the four-subject `SemanticIdentity` bundle (`crates/tiler-ir/src/semantic/identity.rs`), `SemanticOccurrenceIdentity`, `NumericalContractIdentity`, `RefinementContentIdentity`, and `IndexRefinementIdentity` (`crates/tiler-compiler/src/legality.rs`), `CanonicalReferenceRegistryIdentity` and `CanonicalScalarReferenceRegistryIdentity` (`tiler-reference`), and the crate-internal `RegionContentIdentity` and `RegionOccurrenceIdentity` (`region.rs`), `FusionLegalityContentIdentity` and `FusionLegalityIdentity` (`fusion_legality.rs`), `RegionCoverIdentity` (`cover.rs`), `ImplementationProposalIdentity` (`frontier.rs`), and `SelectedPlanIdentity` and `SelectedPortfolioIdentity` (`selection.rs`). Each encoder that derives one opens with a NUL-terminated versioned domain tag of the form `b"tiler.<subject>.v<N>\0"` and the canonical bytes carry `u64` length prefixes before variable-length runs. The historical schedule exception and its closure are recorded below.

**Fact — no error type in any library signature erases its cause.** The workspace depends on neither `anyhow` nor `thiserror`; `Box<dyn Error>` appears only inside tests and doctests. Failure kinds are hand-written enums with structured payloads, `#[non_exhaustive]`, and a `source()` that preserves the underlying typed error — `IndexBuildError::ScalarAuthority(Arc<ScalarRegistryError>)` in `crates/tiler-ir/src/index/error.rs` is representative. `CheckedBuildError<Admission, Verification>` (`crates/tiler-ir/src/convenience.rs`) is generic over each layer's own two concrete error types precisely so a shared convenience does not collapse them into one lossy error.

**Fact — construction discipline is enforced by the type system, not by review.** `IndexRegionBuilder::build(self)` consumes the draft and returns `VerifiedIndexRegion` or an `IndexRegionBuildError` carrying the intact builder and every deterministic diagnostic. `IndexRegionBuilder::build_with` delegates to the shared `build_checked` helper, which scopes the mutable draft to an `FnOnce(&mut Self)` closure so the closure cannot reach the consuming verifier. Two `trybuild` compile-fail fixtures pin the guarantee: `crates/tiler-ir/tests/index-region/fail/forge_verified.rs` fails with "cannot construct `VerifiedIndexRegion` with struct literal syntax due to private fields", and `crates/tiler-ir/tests/index-region/fail/closure_cannot_build.rs` fails with E0507 because `build` takes `self`.

**Fact — the known gap is a convention gap, and it is unevenly distributed.** `#[non_exhaustive]` is used 74 times across `tiler-ir`, `tiler-compiler`, and `tiler-reference` — on essentially every public error enum and view enum — and zero times in `crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/model.rs`, `crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/numerics.rs`, or anywhere in `tiler-metal-aot`, whose enums and output records are documented as bounded-profile placeholders that will grow. That is a rule applied consistently where it was seen and dropped where it was not. The remediation is owned by `harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive`. A companion gap — canonical encodings that discard a facet only because its enum has one inhabited variant today — is owned by `extend-canonical-identity-encodings-for-reserved-variants`.

**Claim from the originating ticket, not independently verified here.** The ticket states that over the same period per-case public-boundary review caught "essentially one substantive issue", the missing `#[non_exhaustive]`. The gap itself is verified above; the claim about what the whole review history did and did not surface is a summary of work outside this repository's recorded artifacts and is recorded as the ticket's, not as a finding of this ADR. Nothing here depends on it.

**Inference.** A rule that is discoverable only by reading sibling modules is caught systematically by a written convention and only by luck in human review. That asymmetry, not any single defect, is the argument for this record.

### A premise from the ticket that does not survive checking

The originating ticket says four independent agents converged on the private-draft authority shape "without being instructed to". The artifact was real at this record's evidence commit `b642007`: `crates/tiler-compiler/src/{explain,feasibility,fusion_legality,cover,frontier,selection}.rs` were crate-private draft authorities not yet reachable from `compile()`. That reachability statement is historical: the ordinary compiler pipeline now imports and uses all six; their crate-private visibility remains a separate public-facade question.

**Fact.** The convergence was not independent, and each agent said so in writing. The `prototype-fusion-legality-and-numerical-proof` outcome records the pattern as "matching `explain.rs`'s private-draft precedent"; `prototype-physical-implementation-frontier` records "matching the reviewed-draft posture of the sibling `feasibility` and `fusion_legality` authorities"; `prototype-region-cover-enumeration` records "mirroring the sibling `fusion_legality`/`frontier` private drafts"; and `prototype-complete-physical-plan-selection` records "mirroring the `cover`/`frontier`/`fusion_legality` convention".

**Inference.** The mechanism was imitation of an unwritten precedent found in the tree, not independent derivation. That is a weaker claim about agent judgement and a *stronger* argument for this ADR: a convention propagated only by imitation degrades silently the first time a worker reads a non-conforming sibling, and it gives a reviewer no citable rule. This record therefore does not rest on the "independent convergence" premise, and the premise should not be repeated elsewhere.

## Decision

**Proposal.** A public Tiler API — any item reachable from a `pub` module of a workspace crate — satisfies the following conventions. Each is stated so that conformance can be checked by reading the item, and a deviation is a decision to be argued rather than an oversight.

### 1. Errors are typed and non-erasing

Distinct failure kinds are distinct variants of a concrete enum. A public fallible function returns that concrete type, never `Box<dyn Error>`, an erased trait object, or a string. A variant carries the structured data a caller needs to react — the rejected entity, the exhausted resource with its attempted and permitted quantities, the expected and actual arity — not a preformatted message. When a failure wraps a lower-layer error, `Error::source()` returns that error with its own type preserved.

A helper that composes two layers is generic over both concrete error types rather than unifying them. `CheckedBuildError<Admission, Verification>` is the reference form: it keeps an insertion-time admission rejection and a whole-object verification failure structurally distinct for every layer that adopts it.

### 2. Identity types are opaque and expose canonical bytes; short digests are presentation-only

A canonical identity is a newtype whose byte storage is private and whose only public reader is `as_bytes()`. Equality, ordering, hashing, dedup, and cache keying use the canonical bytes.

An identity a crate *derives* has no public constructor: it is produced only by the encoder that establishes what it means, so no caller can assemble one that names a subject no verifier examined. An identity a crate *receives* at a boundary may have an explicit wrapping constructor, provided the constructor and the type document that the bytes are treated as opaque and are never re-derived locally. `SemanticOccurrenceIdentity::from_bytes` and `NumericalContractIdentity::from_key` in `crates/tiler-compiler/src/legality.rs` are the two present instances, and both say so: refinement "treats it as opaque bytes: it is the *selected semantic source* the region is bound to, never re-derived here". A wrapping constructor is a statement that this crate is not the authority for that subject, and it must not be used to shortcut an identity the crate is the authority for.

A surface may also offer a short bounded label for explain output and diagnostics. That label is presentation-only and is never an equality or dedup input. `RegionContentIdentity::label()` states the rule in its own doc comment: "The label is a digest of the canonical bytes and is presentation only. Equality decisions always use `as_bytes`."

**Correction to the ticket's shorthand.** The method name `key()` must not be used as the marker for this rule, because it was overloaded in the tree. In `tiler-compiler` (`region.rs`, `cover.rs`, `selection.rs`) `key()` returned a `String` digest label. In `tiler-ir` (`index/scalar.rs`, `index/model.rs`, `semantic/registry.rs`, `semantic/operation.rs`, `semantic/interface.rs`) `key()` returns a borrowed *stable semantic key* — `&ScalarOpKey`, `&OpKey`, `&OutputKey` — which is meaning, is compared, and is encoded into identity. The convention is about the role of the value, not the spelling of the accessor. `disambiguate-presentation-label-from-semantic-key-accessors` has since closed the compiler-side half of the collision by renaming those digest accessors to `label()`, and deliberately left `tiler-ir`'s borrowed semantic keys spelled `key()`, because for them the spelling is already correct. The hazard is recorded as closed rather than deleted: what it illustrates about the rule is unchanged, and the rule is still the role and not the name.

**Fact — the same hazard was already realized a second time under a different spelling, one crate deep.** Three `tiler-compiler` accessors returned the presentation digest as `stable_id` — `RegionCandidate::stable_id()` (`region.rs`), `CoverRegion::stable_id()` (`cover.rs`), and `FusionNumericalProof::candidate_stable_id()` (`fusion.rs`) — while `pipeline::ProgramAlternative::stable_id` is an author-chosen `&'static str` such as `"alternative:fused-serial-sum.v1"` that *is* compared as meaning. `select_structural_pareto` returns it as the portfolio's selection decision and `PortfolioSelection::selected_alternative_id` carries it; `verify_portfolio` dedups the alternatives on it through a `BTreeSet` and rejects the compilation with `portfolio-identity` when two collide, then rejects with `portfolio-selection` unless the recorded selection equals the recomputed one; and `record_cost_and_selection` decides `SelectionOutcome::Selected` by comparing it. So one spelling named both a presentation digest and a value that is an equality *and* a dedup input — precisely the two roles this convention says a label never has — and it did so inside a single crate rather than across the crate boundary the `key()` case straddles, with a selection decision on the compared side. The three digest accessors are now `label`; `ProgramAlternative::stable_id` and its `ExpectedAlternative` mirror deliberately keep the name.

**Inference — a spelling is not a mechanism, which is why the rule is stated as a role.** A convention carried by a name propagates by imitation of whichever sibling a worker happened to read, and that is how one collision arrived twice under two different names. Renaming the digests to `label()` is worth having because it makes the distinction visible at a call site, but it is a legibility improvement rather than the rule: a surface conforms, or fails to, on what its value is used for, whatever the accessor is called.

### 3. Canonical encodings are domain-separated, length-prefixed, ordinal-free, and exhaustively matched

An encoder writes a versioned domain tag before any content, so bytes produced for one subject can never be mistaken for another subject's. It writes a fixed-width length before every variable-length run, so no concatenation of fields is ambiguous. It excludes transient identifiers — arena indices, builder insertion order, graph-local ordinals, planning identifiers — wherever the represented semantics are equivalent without them; `encode_identity` in `crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/model.rs` documents excluding the transient `RegionId` for exactly this reason.

It matches every encoded enum exhaustively, with no wildcard arm and no silently omitted field. A single-variant enum is destructured irrefutably (`let ContributorOrder::OriginalAxisLexicographic = order;`) rather than matched with a catch-all, so adding a variant is a compile error at the encoding site. This is the fail-closed property: a widened enum must stop the build, never produce two structurally distinct subjects that share identity bytes.

**Amended 2026-07-24 by `reconcile-adr-records-with-the-widened-numerical-vocabulary` — an enum reaches canonical bytes through an exhaustive match, never through its discriminant.** An `as` cast of an enum is forbidden in an encoder, and so is any other tag read from ordinal position rather than written by an arm: `std::mem::discriminant`, a `#[repr(u8)]` value read as data, or a helper that returns an index. The paragraph above constrains the *arms* of a match, so as stated it does not reach a site that writes no match at all — `bytes.push(value as u8)` has no wildcard arm and omits no field, and it still defeats the fail-closed property the paragraph exists to protect. It defeats it in a strictly worse way. A wildcard arm is stable under a variant reorder and mis-encodes only variants that did not exist when it was written; a discriminant cast reads whatever ordinal position each variant happens to occupy, so **inserting or reordering a variant silently re-encodes every subject the encoder has ever produced**, changing identity bytes for values whose meaning did not change, with no missing arm, no dropped field, no diagnostic, and no build error. The tag an arm writes is a stated constant a reviewer can compare against a fixture; the tag a cast writes is a consequence of declaration order that nobody chose and no reader of the encoder can see. The rule is therefore about the *mechanism that produces the tag*, not about the shape of a match, and it applies wherever the enum is defined — a same-crate cast is as ordinal-dependent as a cross-crate one.

**Fact — the form was realized, in canonical identity, and only an unrelated accident exposed it.** `VerifiedRequestSubject::canonical_explain_subject_bytes` in `crates/tiler-compiler/src/request.rs` encoded all four dimensions of a request's numerical contract by discriminant — `bytes.push(self.numerical_contract.input_subnormals as u8)` and three siblings over `tiler_ir::schedule::SubnormalMode` and `NumericalPermission` — into the canonical explain subject that `FusionNumericalProof::canonical_explain_evidence_bytes` builds on. It was found by `widen-numerical-vocabulary-and-complete-identity`, which had not predicted it, and it stopped the build only because the variant that ticket added was a *struct* variant, `FlushToZero { zero_sign }`: an enum with a field-carrying variant cannot be cast at all, so `rustc` rejected the four casts outright. Had the added variant been fieldless, the tree would have stayed green. Had it been inserted before `Preserve` rather than after, every previously encoded request subject's bytes would have shifted with nothing to report it. The four casts are now `subnormal_tag` and `permission_tag`, two `const fn` exhaustive matches the crate shares with its fusion encoder.

**Inference — that is why this is a convention and not a review item.** The defect was not caught by the rule, by the reviewer of the encoder, or by a test; it was caught by an incidental property of the variant that happened to be added next. A hazard whose detection depends on which shape of variant arrives first is undetected. Convention 5's amendment records the same asymmetry one level up — a wildcard is loud when you remove the attribute and silent when you add it — and a discriminant cast is the case where neither the attribute nor the match arms say anything at all.

**Historical fact at acceptance — two sites did not yet meet this.** `push_numerical` omitted `input_subnormals` and `result_subnormals` from `CanonicalScheduledRegionIdentity`, and `fusion_legality::effect_tag` mapped every non-`Pure` `OperationEffect` to `u8::MAX` through a wildcard arm. Both were unobservable only while the enums had one inhabited variant. `extend-canonical-identity-encodings-for-reserved-variants` was assigned to close them.

**Evidence refresh 2026-07-24 — at that point the first site was closed and the second remained owned.** `widen-numerical-vocabulary-and-complete-identity` (`1f78223`) landed ADR 0076 items 1 and 6 together, because widening `SubnormalMode` is what made the omission observable. `crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/model.rs` encoded both subnormal dimensions through `push_subnormal` and both permissions through `push_permission`, each an exhaustive match with no wildcard arm, and the two derived `permits_*` booleans it wrote in place of the permissions were gone — a boolean is a projection of the enum, and a projection cannot fail closed when its source grows, which is the same defect one layer of indirection away. Both encoders lived in the crate that defined every enum they matched, so the amended convention 5 cost them nothing. `fusion_legality::effect_tag` was untouched: `OperationEffect` remained `#[non_exhaustive]`, the wildcard remained mandatory across that crate boundary, and `extend-canonical-identity-encodings-for-reserved-variants` owned it under the amended note below.

**Amended 2026-07-24 — the second site could not be closed in place under the convention 5 this record originally stated.** `effect_tag` lived in `crates/tiler-compiler/src/fusion_legality.rs` and encoded `tiler_ir::semantic::OperationEffect`, which then carried `#[non_exhaustive]` at `crates/tiler-ir/src/semantic/operation.rs`. Its wildcard arm was not an oversight the encoder could delete: `#[non_exhaustive]` makes a cross-crate match without a wildcard a hard `E0004`, so this convention and the original convention 5 were in direct contradiction at that one site from the day both were accepted. Amended convention 5b resolved it in this convention's favour, which meant closing the site required either removing `#[non_exhaustive]` from `OperationEffect` or moving the encoder into the crate that defines it. `extend-canonical-identity-encodings-for-reserved-variants` was assigned to record which resolution it chose.

**Evidence refresh 2026-07-24 — a third resolution exists and is already in the tree, so the choice above is between three options rather than two.** `crates/tiler-artifact/src/program/model.rs` encodes three `#[non_exhaustive]` `tiler_ir::kernel` vocabularies from outside their defining crate and keeps the mandatory wildcard, but makes the wildcard a **typed rejection** instead of a derived value: `element_type_tag`, `address_space_tag`, and `buffer_access_tag` each return `Err(ArtifactDiagnostic::UnrecognizedForeignVariant { subject })` rather than a tag, and the encoder's own comment states the reasoning — "Rejecting is the only remaining fail-closed behaviour: an unrecognized element type must never share identity bytes with a recognized one." That preserves what convention 3 protects, because no two subjects can share bytes, while giving up what 5b prefers, because a variant the encoder has not been taught silently becomes unencodable rather than a build failure, and nothing reports the omission until an artifact carrying it is refused at run time. It is a genuine third option with a genuine cost, not a way around the tie-break. Ordering, so the correction is attributable: `f6da4c4` amended these conventions at 12:28 on 2026-07-24 and `d5b6381` added the rejecting encoders at 13:01 the same day, so the two-option enumeration was accurate when written and incomplete 33 minutes later.

**Evidence refresh 2026-07-29 — the second identity site is closed.** `extend-canonical-identity-encodings-for-reserved-variants` removed `#[non_exhaustive]` from `OperationEffect` under convention 5b and made both cross-crate `effect_tag` encoders exhaustive with no wildcard arm. `Pure` retains tag `1`, so the closure changed no existing identity byte; a future effect must now choose a tag at both encoding sites as a compile error. The ownership and open-state statements in the preceding chronology are historical.

**Evidence refresh 2026-07-29 — the two tag-form deviations are closed.** `encode_identity` now opens with the NUL-terminated `b"tiler.schedule.v2\0"` domain, and `push_numerical` length-prefixes `profile_key`; the v2 step makes every older encoding incomparable rather than silently changing bytes under v1. `prototype-quantized-value-vertical` rebaselined the dependent identities and fixtures on the merged tree. The uniform NUL-terminated, length-prefixed form this convention selected is therefore implemented at this former exception.

### 4. Construction is a transactional builder plus a consuming terminal yielding an unforgeable immutable product

*Amended 2026-07-24 by `resolve-non-exhaustive-recognizer-hole`. The rule now states the property it always meant — a terminal that consumes its builder — instead of prescribing the verb `build`, and the scope qualifier this record dropped when it restated ADR 0071 is restored. "Amendments" below records what changed and why.*

This is ADR 0071's lifecycle, restated here as one item of a conformance checklist rather than re-decided. A public builder owns private storage and checks local invariants on each insertion, leaving the draft unchanged when an insertion is rejected. The terminal consumes the builder by taking `self`, runs whole-object verification, and returns either an opaque immutable product or a typed failure. **The property is that the terminal consumes; a terminal that leaves the builder usable after producing a product is the violation, whatever it is called.**

**In ADR 0071's own scope — the shared target-neutral IR layers — the terminal is `build(self)` and its failure carries the diagnostics and recoverable builder ownership.** That is realized without exception: `IndexRegionBuilder::build`, `ScheduledRegionBuilder::build`, `KernelBuilder::build`, and `SemanticProgramBuilder::build` all take `self`, and `IndexRegionBuildError`, `ScheduledRegionBuildError`, `KernelVerificationError`, and `ProgramBuildError` each return the intact builder through `into_parts`. ADR 0058 records the rationale for the recoverable form: a large arena-backed draft must be inspectable, correctable, and retryable rather than discarded. Within this scope, `build` and not `freeze` remains the vocabulary, exactly as ADR 0071 states it.

**Amended 2026-07-24 — the registry family is a second terminal shape, and it conforms.** A registry that accumulates authority and then publishes an immutable cheap-clone snapshot names its terminal `freeze`, and `docs/ir.md` already states that lifecycle normatively ("consumes the builder at freeze", "freeze diagnostics are deterministic"). Five such terminals exist and every one takes `self` and yields an opaque newtype over private `Arc` storage: `SemanticRegistryBuilder::freeze`, `ScalarRegistryBuilder::freeze`, `LoweringCapabilityRegistryBuilder::freeze`, `ReferenceRegistryBuilder::freeze`, and `ScalarReferenceRegistryBuilder::freeze`. Each satisfies the property this convention protects. Two are infallible and three return a typed error without recoverable builder ownership; whether that asymmetry with the IR layers should converge is recorded as an open question below rather than settled here.

The immutable product cannot be forged: its fields are private, so struct-literal construction fails to compile, and it offers no mutation, thawing, unchecked constructor, or mutable access to its draft. A closure convenience delegates to that same builder and the same consuming verifier — it does not re-implement verification — and it scopes the draft by mutable borrow so the closure body cannot itself reach the consuming step.

### 5. `#[non_exhaustive]` on public enums and output records documented as growing, unless a crate outside the defining crate has to match them completely

*Amended 2026-07-24 by `resolve-non-exhaustive-recognizer-hole`. The accepted rule and its input-record asymmetry are preserved verbatim as 5a; clauses 5b and 5c are new; the convention's closing sentence is withdrawn. The "Amendments" section records the evidence, the measurement, and what changed for a reader who has already applied the earlier text.*

**5a — the accepted rule, unchanged.** A public enum whose variant set is a bounded-profile placeholder, and a public output record that will gain fields, carries `#[non_exhaustive]` so the addition lands additively rather than breaking a downstream `match` or struct literal. The doc comment that says the type is bounded and the attribute that makes growth additive belong together; a comment alone does not fail closed.

The rule is deliberately asymmetric. It does not extend to caller-constructed *input* records: a caller must be able to write the literal, and growing such a type is a constructor-signature change regardless, so `#[non_exhaustive]` buys nothing and costs construction ergonomics. `harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive` records the same split for the concrete `tiler-metal-aot` inputs.

5a is the whole rule for a type that no crate outside the defining crate has to match completely: an error, diagnostic, or provenance enum; an output record; a view enum read through `if let` or `matches!`; and any enum a consumer only constructs. `#[non_exhaustive]` costs such a consumer nothing, because it never had to enumerate the variants, and a new variant genuinely is additive for it. It is also the whole rule for a type that has no consumer outside its defining crate at all. A crate qualifies only while nothing outside it matches, casts, or recognizes its types, and a development dependency is enough to end that — `#[non_exhaustive]` binds every out-of-crate consumer regardless of dependency kind, so a crate's own manifest does not settle the question and only its consumers do.

*Corrected 2026-07-25 by [`correct-adr-0074-driver-vocabulary-consumers`](../../tickets/correct-adr-0074-driver-vocabulary-consumers.md). This paragraph previously closed by naming `tiler-metal-aot` as such a crate and concluded that the `tiler-metal-aot` half of `harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive` was untouched by the clauses below. That was true when written and false 85 minutes later. The driver's out-of-crate consumers are enumerated under 5b and 5c below, and "Amendments" records the ordering.*

**5b — a vocabulary that a crate outside the defining crate maps *totally* is not `#[non_exhaustive]`.** A total map is a match in which every variant must contribute its own correct result and no wildcard value is derivable from the variant it would cover. Convention 3 already requires such a match to be exhaustive with no wildcard arm; across a crate boundary `#[non_exhaustive]` makes that requirement uncompilable, so where 3 and 5 meet, 3 wins. The asymmetry that decides it is not stylistic. The failure 5a prevents is one in-workspace source edit, which `cargo check` enumerates exhaustively and ADR 0075 records as costing near nothing while no crate is publishable. The failure 5b prevents is a wrong artifact: two structurally distinct subjects sharing identity bytes, or a compiled kernel built with flags that do not preserve the numerical contract it declares.

**Fact — three such sites exist.** `tiler_compiler::fusion::FusionNumericalProof::canonical_explain_evidence_bytes` encodes `tiler_ir::schedule::NumericalPermission` with `bytes.push(match permission { NumericalPermission::Forbidden => 1 })`; `tiler_compiler::fusion_legality::effect_tag` encodes `tiler_ir::semantic::OperationEffect`; and `tiler_metal::emit::realization_requirements` derives the Metal compiler requirements a kernel's declared numerics impose by matching `NumericalPermission` twice and `SubnormalMode` once. The third already states the dependency in its own doc comment: "Each vocabulary matched here is *not* `#[non_exhaustive]`, so widening the numerical contract is a compile error at this site rather than a silently dropped requirement." A wildcard there has no honest value — the flag a subnormal mode requires is a property of that exact mode — so the arm would either drop the requirement or invent a conservative guess, and either way the emitted source and the reported requirements would stop agreeing.

**Amended 2026-07-24 by `reconcile-adr-records-with-the-widened-numerical-vocabulary` — an `as` cast counts as a total map when choosing a clause.** 5b asks what a consumer's match has to do, so a consumer that writes no match at all could be read as falling outside the clause entirely, leaving the "how to tell which clause applies" test below with nothing to run on. It does not fall outside. A discriminant cast is a total map whose arms are all implied rather than written, and the value it derives is precisely the identity tag that test names as the 5b signature. A vocabulary that an out-of-crate encoder casts is a 5b type and must not be `#[non_exhaustive]`.

**Measurement — at such a site the attribute is inert, so 5b's tie-break against convention 3 does not arise.** `#[non_exhaustive]` constrains construction and pattern matching; it does not constrain `as`. `spikes/extensions/non-exhaustive-visibility/consuming/tests/ui/pass/cross_crate_discriminant_cast.rs` writes `value as u8` on a marked enum from outside its defining crate and compiles, while `fail/cross_crate_discriminant_tag_match.rs` — the same total map written as the convention-3 match, deriving the same tag for the same variant across the same boundary — fails `E0004`. The pair is the evidence, not either half: a cross-crate cast compiles cleanly, the wildcard the attribute is otherwise famous for forcing never appears, and marking the enum would not have prevented the defect recorded under convention 3 above. The compile error that closes an `as` cast is the one convention 3 now requires — replace the cast with a match — and it is available whatever the attribute says. A second pair measures the escape hatch recorded below: `pass/cast_ignores_denied_omitted_patterns.rs` denies `non_exhaustive_omitted_patterns` crate-wide under its feature gate and still compiles the cast, and `fail/omitted_patterns_denied_at_crate_level.rs` is the control showing that same crate-level level firing on a match and reporting nothing about the cast beside it, so a stricter lint level does not close the hole either. Bounded to the recorded compiler; the record's fail-closed channel comparison forces a fresh run at the next pin migration.

**Fact — the enumeration above is a snapshot, and five more 5b sites have landed since it was written.** Each maps a `tiler_ir` vocabulary totally from outside `tiler-ir`, and none existed when convention 5b was amended at 12:28 on 2026-07-24. **Fact — this enumeration is a snapshot and there is no mechanism that completes it.** The paragraph above already says so for the five sites it added, and it holds for all seven: nothing in the repository enumerates 5b sites, so a reader cannot obtain the complete set from this record or from a command. What a reader *can* do is apply the test under "How to tell which clause applies" to a specific type, which is decidable by reading that type's out-of-crate consumers. Treat the list as evidence that the convention is live and as a set of worked examples, not as a closed inventory; the sentence below that makes the ticket adding a consumer responsible for re-checking the classification is what keeps it approximately current, and it is a convention rather than a check.

**5c — a vocabulary that a crate outside the defining crate matches to decide *support* is not `#[non_exhaustive]` while Tiler is pre-alpha.** For a *recognizer* — a match whose arms are the cases the consumer supports and whose unknown arm is a typed rejection — `#[non_exhaustive]` is fail-closed but silently incomplete, which is the harder failure to notice. Because the attribute obliges every out-of-crate consumer to carry a wildcard, adding a variant compiles cleanly at every one of them and reroutes the new case into reject-unknown with no error, no warning, and no test failure: the capability exists in the IR and never reaches the consumer, and the refusal looks like a considered capability statement when it is an unreviewed default. The compile error is the mechanism that keeps a recognizer complete, and while Tiler has no external consumer the additive growth `#[non_exhaustive]` buys protects nobody, so it is not worth trading the mechanism for.

**Fact — the recognizers of the shared IR vocabulary.** `tiler_compiler::physical::verify_region_subject_binding` matches `tiler_ir::schedule::ScalarProgram` exhaustively over its three variants; `tiler_compiler::program` matches `Option<&LogicalAccess>` exhaustively at two sites; and `tiler_metal::emit` matches the twelve `#[non_exhaustive]` vocabulary enums of `tiler_ir::kernel` to decide what it can emit. The first two are 5c types and must stay exhaustive. The third is the same class one layer down and is recorded as a named non-conformance below rather than left implicit.

**Fact — a 5c recognizer over the driver vocabulary, added 2026-07-25 by `correct-adr-0074-driver-vocabulary-consumers`.** [`crates/tiler-metal/src/golden_compilation.rs`](../../crates/tiler-metal/src/golden_compilation.rs)'s `resolved_toolchain` matches `tiler_metal_aot::diagnostic::DriverError` out of crate with no wildcard, splitting `ToolchainUnavailable` and `SdkUnavailable` — a host with no Apple toolchain, which self-skips unless the require-toolchain environment variable is set — from `ToolFailure`, `Host`, and `EmptyArtifact`, which panic because a failure that is not an absent toolchain is a defect. That is exactly the 5c shape: the arms are the cases the consumer supports, and the split is a support decision. Marking `DriverError` `#[non_exhaustive]` would force a wildcard, and a new variant would then route silently into whichever half the wildcard chose — turning a real driver defect into a skipped test, or an absent toolchain into a panic, with no error, no warning, and no test failure. `DriverError` therefore stays exhaustive while Tiler is pre-alpha.

**Growth announcement is the compiler, and the alternatives have a trigger.** A 5c enum announces its growth by failing the build at every recognizer that has not decided what to do with the new variant. That is a deliberate source-breaking change, accepted in full: ADR 0075 records that the workspace sets `version = "0.0.0"` and `publish = false`, that no crate is publishable, and that Tom rejected the compatibility framing outright on 2026-07-24. Two mechanisms would restore the compile error without giving up the attribute, and both are recorded with their cost rather than adopted now — a declared per-consumer capability list checked by a conformance test against an `ALL`-style constant the defining crate builds from a same-crate exhaustive match, and the `non_exhaustive_omitted_patterns` lint measured in "Amendments" below. Reconsider on the trigger ADR 0075 already names: an external consumer, a publishable crate, or an approached release. Until then the compiler is the cheapest correct mechanism and needs nothing kept in sync.

**Withdrawn.** The accepted convention closed with: "Marking a recognized enum non-exhaustive forces its consumers to grow an explicit reject-unknown arm. That is the intended fail-closed posture, not a regression." That sentence is withdrawn as stated. It is true that the posture is fail-closed and true that a reject-unknown arm is not a regression in itself; what it missed is that the arm the attribute forces is *unconditional*, so it also silently absorbs every future variant the consumer should have recognized. The original reasoning is preserved above rather than deleted, because it remains correct for the 5a case it was written about — a consumer that partially classifies a growing enum genuinely does want a stable catch-all — and the amendment narrows its scope rather than reversing its judgement.

**How to tell which clause applies.** Ask what the wildcard arm would have to do. Where the consumer writes no match — a discriminant cast, or any other tag read from ordinal position — ask it of the match the cast would have to become under convention 3, because the cast's implied arms are what the clause is classifying. If a wildcard is never written because the consumer only constructs, forwards, or partially classifies the value, the type is 5a. If a wildcard would have to invent a value that the variant alone determines — an identity tag, a required target obligation — it is 5b. If a wildcard would be a typed rejection that is correct today and wrong the moment the profile widens to cover the new variant, it is 5c. The classification is a property of the consumers that exist, so a type moves clause when a consumer is added; the ticket that adds such a consumer owns re-checking it.

**Fact — the hole is not exclusively an attribute problem.** `tiler_ir::schedule::builder::verify_access_and_semantics` recognizes `ScalarProgram` inside the crate that defines it and still ends in `_ => return Err(ScheduledRegionDiagnostic::NumericalOrAccessRefinement)`, because it matches a three-way product of `ScalarProgram`, `ReductionTopology`, and `LogicalAccess`, and a product match needs a catch-all whatever the attributes say. A fourth scalar program is silently rejected there today. Convention 5 governs the attribute; it cannot govern the match shape, so a product-match recognizer needs its own explicit completeness argument and is not made safe by the absence of `#[non_exhaustive]`.

**Evidence refresh 2026-07-28 — the total-match mechanism was exercised, and the product-match hole now has an explicit guard.** Replacing `ScalarProgram::MultiplyThenAdd` with `ScalarProgram::PointwiseF32(PointwiseF32Expression)` stopped the out-of-crate total match in `tiler_compiler::physical::verify_region_subject_binding` and the dedicated same-crate `kernel::tests::body_shaping_vocabulary_is_closed` match until both named the new case. The production three-way product match in `schedule::builder::verify_access_and_semantics` also gained the exact `PointwiseF32` arm, while its structural catch-all necessarily remains. The dedicated test is the explicit completeness mechanism the paragraph above said a product recognizer needs: it matches every body-shaping vocabulary without a wildcard and names this deferred gate, so another `ScalarProgram` variant stops compilation even though the production product match could otherwise route it silently to rejection. The nested `PointwiseF32Node` vocabulary is itself intentionally exhaustive and is mapped totally by schedule identity and structured-KIR lowering; adding a node therefore stops both authorities until its identity and physical meaning are supplied. This is evidence that conventions 3 and 5c worked as designed, not an amendment to either.

**Fact — one class of type is knowingly non-conforming under 5c.** The twelve vocabulary enums of `crates/tiler-ir/src/kernel/model.rs` carry `#[non_exhaustive]` and are recognized by `crates/tiler-metal/src/emit.rs`. Reading both files at commit `37f1350`, ten of the emitter's wildcard arms are unreachable today — every variant of `KernelType`, `Builtin`, `KernelConstant`, `BinaryOp`, `CompareOp`, `ConvertOp`, `OperationView`, `BarrierOrdering`, `ExecutionScope`, and `BufferAccess` under `AddressSpace::Device` is already listed explicitly — and exist only because the attribute forces them. Three are live and each already names in a comment exactly which known variants it rejects: `address_space_declaration` rejects `Workgroup` and `InvocationPrivate`, its `Constant` branch rejects `BufferAccess::Write`, and `fence_flag` rejects `Constant` and `InvocationPrivate`. One more is structural — `barrier_call`'s match on the `(ExecutionScope, MemoryScope)` pair — and no attribute affects it. Conforming therefore deletes ten dead arms and transcribes three documented comments into explicit patterns; it is a net simplification, not a cost. `harden-kernel-vocabulary-recognizer-completeness` owns it.

### 6. Verified products expose no `pub` fields; leaf value-data descriptors may

A type whose invariants were established by a verifier exposes no public fields. It exposes borrowed accessors, iterators, and view types that yield meaning without yielding storage. `VerifiedIndexRegion` holds a single private `Arc<VerifiedIndexRegionData>` and reads through `*Ref<'_>` views; `VerifiedScheduledRegion` holds three private fields and reads through `region()`, `requirements()`, and `canonical_identity()`.

A *leaf value-data descriptor* — a plain record with no cross-field invariant that a producer legitimately assembles or reads field by field, and which becomes trustworthy only once a verifier binds it into a verified product — may expose `pub` fields. `crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/model.rs` states this posture in its own module doc: "The descriptor structs are read-transparent value data; only `ScheduledRegionBuilder::build` can bind a region into an opaque `VerifiedScheduledRegion`."

The two rules compose safely only because opacity is enforced at the verified boundary. Which of the two forms `tiler-ir` should use for its descriptors is not settled; see the open question below.

### 7. A new authority is crate-private until its facade is reviewed

A landed authority that is not yet reachable from its crate's entry point is a private `mod` whose items are `pub(crate)`, with a module-level `#![allow(dead_code, reason = "…")]` whose reason names what the surface reserves and, where it is known, which slice will consume it. It becomes `pub` only when Tom accepts the exact facade; a module that is already `pub` while under review says so in its module documentation, as `tiler_compiler::capability` and `tiler_compiler::legality` do ("Every public item here is a reviewed *draft* boundary").

This is the staging rule that makes the other six affordable: a surface can be built, tested, and reviewed at full fidelity without spending public-API commitment before the boundary is accepted.

## Amendments

An amendment changes a stated convention of this accepted record without reopening the decision to write the conventions down. Each entry names what the record said before, what it says now, and the evidence that moved it, so a reader who applied the earlier text can see precisely what to revise.

### 2026-07-24 — convention 5 is split by what the consumer's match has to do (`resolve-non-exhaustive-recognizer-hole`)

**What the record said.** Convention 5 stated one rule for every growing public enum — mark it `#[non_exhaustive]` so a later variant lands additively — and closed by naming the consequence for a consumer: "Marking a recognized enum non-exhaustive forces its consumers to grow an explicit reject-unknown arm. That is the intended fail-closed posture, not a regression."

**Why it was amended.** The two statements pull apart for a *recognizer*, an out-of-crate match whose arms are the cases a consumer supports. `#[non_exhaustive]` obliges every such consumer to carry a wildcard, so adding a variant compiles cleanly everywhere and silently reroutes the new case into reject-unknown. The compile error was the mechanism keeping recognizers complete; the attribute removes it and replaces a loud, mechanical prompt with a behavioural change from accept to reject that nothing reports. The result is still fail-closed but silently incomplete — a capability the IR gained and no consumer ever sees — and it is harder to notice than a build failure because the tree stays green.

**Measurement — what `#[non_exhaustive]` does and does not constrain.** Two crates were compiled directly with the repository's pinned toolchain, `rustc 1.99.0-nightly (eff8269f7 2026-07-18)` selected by `rust-toolchain.toml`'s `nightly-2026-07-19`, edition 2024, on macOS arm64. A defining crate exported a two-variant `#[non_exhaustive]` enum and matched it exhaustively with no wildcard: exit 0, so the attribute constrains only other crates and a same-crate encoder keeps its compile-time guard. A consuming crate matching both variants without a wildcard failed with `error[E0004]: non-exhaustive patterns: '&_' not covered`, whose note reads "marked as non-exhaustive, so a wildcard `_` is necessary to match exhaustively". This is the fact `extend-canonical-identity-encodings-for-reserved-variants` depends on, and it holds for the encoders in `crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/model.rs`, which live in the crate that defines every enum they encode. It does not hold for the two encoders that do not — see convention 3's amended note and convention 5b — so that ticket must handle those two separately rather than relying on the same-crate property.

**Measurement — the mechanism that would keep both the attribute and the compile error.** On the same toolchain, `#![feature(non_exhaustive_omitted_patterns_lint)]` with `#[deny(non_exhaustive_omitted_patterns)]` on a cross-crate match makes a recognizer that keeps its wildcard but omits a known variant fail to compile: "some variants are not matched explicitly … pattern `&Growing::B` not covered". Listing every variant alongside the wildcard compiles clean. The lint is unstable (tracking issue rust-lang/rust#89554), so it costs a `#![feature]` gate in every consumer crate that uses it, where today only `tiler-ir` carries feature gates; without the gate the attribute is inert and produces only an `unknown_lints` warning, which the repository gate's warning-free requirement converts into a failure rather than a silent hole. It is recorded as the proven migration path for the day an external consumer makes `#[non_exhaustive]` genuinely necessary, and deliberately not adopted now, because dropping the attribute buys the same compile error with no unstable surface and nothing to keep in sync.

**Evidence — the harness for both measurements above, added 2026-07-24 by `preserve-non-exhaustive-visibility-probe`.** Both measurements were originally taken with two crates written in an agent scratchpad and deleted afterwards, which left an accepted amendment resting on a run nobody could repeat. The equivalent probe is now checked in at [`spikes/extensions/non-exhaustive-visibility/`](../../spikes/extensions/non-exhaustive-visibility) and every diagnostic quoted above is retained there byte for byte as a `trybuild` expectation rather than paraphrased: [`cross_crate_total_map.stderr`](../../spikes/extensions/non-exhaustive-visibility/consuming/tests/ui/fail/cross_crate_total_map.stderr) for `E0004`, [`omitted_patterns_denied.stderr`](../../spikes/extensions/non-exhaustive-visibility/consuming/tests/ui/fail/omitted_patterns_denied.stderr) for the denied unstable lint, and [`omitted_patterns_inert_without_feature.stderr`](../../spikes/extensions/non-exhaustive-visibility/consuming/tests/ui/fail/omitted_patterns_inert_without_feature.stderr) for the ungated attribute being inert. The reproduction agreed with this record on every point, on the same `rustc 1.99.0-nightly (eff8269f7 2026-07-18)` and edition 2024, macOS arm64; the exact environment is recorded beside the fixtures in [`results/`](../../spikes/extensions/non-exhaustive-visibility/results). Because the lint is unstable, the harness refuses to reuse a measurement whose toolchain channel is no longer the pinned one, so a compiler that changes this behaviour fails the probe instead of quietly redefining what this record cites. `spikes/extensions/run.py --self-test`, which the repository gate runs, checks the retained diagnostics against that record without invoking Cargo. **Updated 2026-07-24 by `compile-extension-spike-fixtures-in-the-gate`:** the gate now also compiles the workspace, so the fixtures are re-derived under the pinned toolchain on every invocation rather than only when the suite is run by hand, and a fixture edited until it no longer fails for its recorded reason fails the gate instead of leaving this record citing a diagnostic nothing still produces. That is an evidence refresh, not an amendment: no convention changes, and what this record cites is the same measurement, now checked in both directions.

**Fact — the accepted record already contradicted itself at one site.** `tiler_ir::semantic::OperationEffect` carries `#[non_exhaustive]`, and `tiler_compiler::fusion_legality::effect_tag` encodes it into a canonical identity from a different crate. Convention 3 requires that match to be exhaustive with no wildcard arm; convention 5 as accepted made the wildcard mandatory. The site's `_ => u8::MAX` was therefore never an oversight the encoder could delete on its own, and the ticket that owns closing it could not have done so under the unamended rule. Amended convention 5b decides the collision in convention 3's favour.

**Fact — two more out-of-crate sites map a schedule vocabulary totally**, and one of them already documents that it depends on the attribute's absence. They are named in convention 5b. Both encode or derive from `NumericalPermission` and `SubnormalMode`, which the `harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive` list would have marked `#[non_exhaustive]`; doing so would have forced a wildcard into a canonical explain encoding and into the derivation of the Metal compiler flags a kernel's declared numerics require. The first is an identity collision, the second a compiled artifact whose flags and whose reported requirements disagree. Neither is a rejection, so neither would have failed closed.

**A premise from the originating ticket that does not survive checking.** `harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive` states that marking `ScalarProgram` non-exhaustive "forces `tiler-compiler`'s recognizer (`verify_region_subject_binding`, `verify_access_and_semantics`) to grow an explicit reject-unknown arm". Only the first of the two is in `tiler-compiler`. `verify_access_and_semantics` is in `crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/builder.rs`, the crate that defines `ScalarProgram`, where the attribute has no effect at all; and it already carries a catch-all because it matches a three-way product. The correction matters beyond bookkeeping: it is the evidence that a product match reintroduces the same hole with no attribute involved, which convention 5 records but cannot govern.

**Why this is an amendment and not a superseding decision.** No accepted convention is reversed and none is withdrawn. Conventions 1, 2, 4, 6, and 7 are untouched; convention 3 gains a note about a site it already named; convention 5 keeps its rule for the case it was written about and gains two exceptions plus a stated tie-break against convention 3. The record's `applies_to`, evidence, and authority are unchanged, and ADR 0075's no-approval categories still depend on this record being the single place the conventions live. A superseding ADR would fork a seven-convention checklist across two documents, which is the duplicated-authority failure the documentation contract exists to prevent. The boundary is explicit: narrowing a convention's scope and withdrawing one sentence of its reasoning is an amendment; reversing the convention, or changing which contracts this record governs, would require supersession.

**What a reader of the pre-amendment text should un-learn.** Not "mark every growing public enum `#[non_exhaustive]`", but "mark every growing public enum `#[non_exhaustive]` unless some crate outside the defining crate has to match it completely". A reader who applied the earlier text to a recognizer or to a total map should revisit that item; a reader who applied it to an error, provenance, output record, or partially classified view enum changed nothing that needs revisiting.

### 2026-07-24 — convention 4 states the consuming-terminal property instead of prescribing the verb (`resolve-non-exhaustive-recognizer-hole`)

### 2026-07-24 — convention 3 forbids the discriminant, not only the wildcard arm (`reconcile-adr-records-with-the-widened-numerical-vocabulary`)

**What the record said.** Convention 3 required that an encoder "matches every encoded enum exhaustively, with no wildcard arm and no silently omitted field", and gave the fail-closed property as "a widened enum must stop the build, never produce two structurally distinct subjects that share identity bytes". Both sentences describe a match. Neither says anything about an encoder that produces its tag without writing a match at all, and convention 5b's classification test — "ask what the wildcard arm would have to do" — has no wildcard arm to ask about at such a site.

**Why it was amended.** The gap is not hypothetical and it is not benign. `VerifiedRequestSubject::canonical_explain_subject_bytes` encoded four dimensions of a request's numerical contract as `bytes.push(self.numerical_contract.input_subnormals as u8)` and three siblings, over two `tiler_ir::schedule` vocabularies, straight into a canonical explain subject. Read against the record as accepted, the site conformed: it had no wildcard arm and omitted no field. It nonetheless violated the property the convention states, and violated it more severely than the wildcard case the convention does cover. A wildcard arm is stable under a variant reorder and can only mis-encode a variant that did not exist when it was written. A discriminant cast reads ordinal position, so inserting or reordering a variant re-encodes *every subject the encoder has ever produced* — identity bytes move for values whose meaning did not change, with no missing arm, no dropped field, and no diagnostic of any kind.

**Fact — how it was found, which is the argument for writing the rule down.** `widen-numerical-vocabulary-and-complete-identity` did not predict the site; its ticket named two encoders and this was not one of them. The build broke only because the variant that work added was a struct variant, `SubnormalMode::FlushToZero { zero_sign }`, and an enum carrying a field in any variant cannot be cast at all. A fieldless second variant would have compiled. A variant inserted before `Preserve` rather than after would have compiled *and* silently shifted every previously encoded subject. Detection depended on an incidental property of whichever variant happened to arrive next, which is not a mechanism.

**Fact — the class is empty today, and the check is one line.** `grep -rn " as u8" crates/` matches nothing; every remaining integer cast under `crates/*/src/` is over a numeric index, length, or bound. So this amendment retrofits nothing and its effect is entirely prospective.

**Measurement — `#[non_exhaustive]` would not have closed it, which is why the rule belongs to convention 3 rather than to convention 5.** The attribute constrains construction and pattern matching; it does not constrain `as`. Marking the two vocabularies would have left the four casts compiling unchanged and produced none of the wildcards the attribute is otherwise criticized for forcing. Convention 5 decides whether a vocabulary carries the attribute; only convention 3 decides how an encoder turns a value into bytes, and only convention 3 can reach a site that writes no match. Convention 5b gains a classification sentence so a cast is not read as falling outside the clause, and nothing else in convention 5 changes. Measured on the pinned toolchain by the `cross_crate_discriminant_cast.rs` / `cross_crate_discriminant_tag_match.rs` pair cited under convention 5b above.

**Why this is an amendment and not a superseding decision.** It is the same boundary as the two entries above. No convention is reversed and none is withdrawn: convention 3 keeps every requirement it stated and gains one more mechanism under the property it always named, and convention 5b gains a sentence about which clause a cast falls in without changing what any clause requires. Conventions 1, 2, 4, 6, and 7 are untouched, `applies_to` is unchanged, and the record remains the single place the conventions live, which ADR 0075's no-approval categories depend on. Extending a convention to a form it did not anticipate is an amendment; reversing a convention, or moving the conventions out of this record, would require supersession.

**What a reader of the pre-amendment text should un-learn.** Not "an encoder without a wildcard arm conforms", but "an encoder conforms when every enum it writes reaches the bytes through an arm that states the tag". A reader who checked an encoder for wildcard arms and stopped should re-read it for `as` casts, `std::mem::discriminant`, and any helper that returns an ordinal. A reader who checked for exhaustive matches over stated constants changed nothing that needs revisiting.

### 2026-07-25 — `tiler-metal-aot` has out-of-crate consumers, and two of its types are named (`correct-adr-0074-driver-vocabulary-consumers`)

This entry corrects two factual claims about where the conventions apply. **No clause of any convention changes meaning**, which is why it is an amendment and, unusually for this section, not even a change to a stated rule — the rules were right and the record's account of the sites they reach was wrong.

**What the record said.** Convention 5a closed with: "It is also the whole rule for a type that has no consumer outside its defining crate at all, which is why the `tiler-metal-aot` half of `harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive` is untouched by the clauses below." Separately, 5b's site enumeration opened "**Fact — three such sites exist**" and listed `FusionNumericalProof::canonical_explain_evidence_bytes`, `fusion_legality::effect_tag`, and `tiler_metal::emit::realization_requirements`.

The clause naming `tiler-metal-aot` as such a crate is withdrawn. 5b gains `crates/tiler-metal/src/target_correspondence.rs` as a site and 5c gains `crates/tiler-metal/src/golden_compilation.rs::resolved_toolchain`.

**Fact — the two consumers.** `target_correspondence.rs` maps `tiler_metal_aot::input::{MslVersion, ApplePlatform}` onto `tiler_metal::target::{MslLanguageVersion, MetalPlatform}` totally and in both directions, with no wildcard and with pair tables declared at a matching count, and its own module documentation cites convention 5b for exactly that reason. `golden_compilation.rs::resolved_toolchain` matches `tiler_metal_aot::diagnostic::DriverError` out of crate with no wildcard, splitting an absent Apple toolchain from a driver defect. Both are 5b and 5c sites by this record's own classification test, so both driver vocabularies must stay exhaustive.

**Fact — the ordering, so the correction is attributable rather than a reproach.** `f6da4c4` amended conventions 4 and 5 on 2026-07-24 at 12:28, when the claim was true. `45d9827` added the checked correspondence at 13:53 the same day, 85 minutes later. `fbe0b4f` and `8194c94` touched this record afterwards for the measurement probe and a naming resolution and neither revisited these two claims. This is the third time an enumeration in this record has decayed because a consumer landed after it was written, which is the pattern the snapshot paragraph under 5b and `harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive`'s own "the defect is the form, not the entries" both name.

**Fact — the operational risk was already contained, and only the authority conflict needed closing.** `tickets/harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive.md` has carried a revision block since 2026-07-24 stating in terms that "the premise that `tiler-metal-aot`'s types 'have no consumer outside `tiler-metal-aot` at all' did not survive checking", removing `MslVersion` from its mark list, recording that `ApplePlatform` was never on it, and adding `DriverError` as a 5c type. So a worker who read the ticket got the right answer while a worker who read this accepted record got the wrong one, and the ticket could not fix the stale text because it lives here. That is the duplicated-authority failure the documentation contract names, and closing it is the whole point of this entry.

**Fact — the rest of convention 5 was read in full for further claims resting on the same premise, and two were checked rather than assumed.** The "known gap" fact in Context observes that `#[non_exhaustive]` appears zero times in `tiler-metal-aot` and routes remediation to `harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive`; that is a statement about the attribute's absence, not about consumers, and it survives unchanged — with the remediation now correctly excluding the three types the ticket's revision block names. ## Consequences

- A conforming surface is reviewable against a citable list instead of against a reviewer's memory of sibling modules. A deviation becomes an explicit argument in a ticket outcome or a superseding decision.
- Most violations are visible by reading the item, without running anything: `Box<dyn Error>` in a public signature (1), a public constructor on a derived identity (2), a missing domain tag, a wildcard arm, or an `as` cast of an enum in an encoder (3), a non-consuming terminal, whatever its verb, or a forgeable verified product (4), a growing public enum without `#[non_exhaustive]` (5a), a `pub` field on a verified product (6), and a `pub` module without an accepted facade (7). Three points still need judgement rather than inspection: whether a record is a leaf value-data descriptor or an invariant-bearing type (6), whether a type is documented as growing (5), and whether an identity is derived or received at a boundary (2).
- **Amended 2026-07-24.** Convention 5 is no longer checkable from the item alone. Deciding between 5a, 5b, and 5c requires reading every out-of-crate match on the type, because the clause follows what those matches have to do. `cargo` answers it mechanically in one direction — removing `#[non_exhaustive]` and rebuilding names each recognizer that was relying on a wildcard — but adding the attribute has no such check, so the burden falls on the ticket that introduces or moves a consumer. This is the price of the amendment and it is paid deliberately: the alternative was a rule that reads cleanly and silently drops a capability.
- Writing the conventions down does not make the codebase conform. Two identity encodings, two crates' public enums, and — under amended convention 5c — the twelve `tiler_ir::kernel` vocabulary enums are known non-conforming, each with a named owner. **Evidence refresh 2026-07-24.** One of the two identity encodings, `schedule::model::push_numerical`, now conforms; `fusion_legality::effect_tag` does not and cannot until its owner picks one of the three resolutions convention 3's note above records. A third encoding defect that no convention named — four `as` discriminant casts in `VerifiedRequestSubject::canonical_explain_subject_bytes` — was found and repaired in the same work, and convention 3's amendment above is what makes the class citable rather than a one-off repair. **Fact — the class is currently empty:** `grep -rn " as u8" crates/` matches nothing, and every remaining integer cast under `crates/*/src/` is over a numeric index, length, or bound.
- The rules constrain shape, not semantics. None of them decides what an operation means, which plan is cheapest, or which layer owns a concept; those remain with the IR, optimizer, and architecture contracts.

## Implementation boundary

**This ADR records conventions and retrofits nothing.** It changes no code, alters no existing public surface, and does not by itself make any current API conforming. The conforming work is owned by separate implementation tickets — `harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive`, `extend-canonical-identity-encodings-for-reserved-variants`, `harden-kernel-vocabulary-recognizer-completeness`, and `unify-schedule-index-region-with-verified-index-region` — which may in turn amend or close the open questions below.

**Amended 2026-07-24.** Neither amendment changes code. The convention 5 amendment changes what two tickets must do: `harden-public-enums-non-exhaustive` no longer marks the 5b and 5c types, and `extend-canonical-identity-encodings-for-reserved-variants` must choose how to make a cross-crate encoder exhaustive over an enum that is `#[non_exhaustive]` today. `harden-kernel-vocabulary-recognizer-completeness` is new and owns the `tiler_ir::kernel` vocabulary named in convention 5c. The convention 4 amendment retrofits nothing at all: it reclassifies five landed `freeze` terminals from apparent deviation to conformance, and removes an obstacle from `propagate-accepted-api-conventions-into-governed-contracts`, which would otherwise have had to reword a `docs/ir.md` lifecycle that was already correct.

**Amended again 2026-07-24 — convention 3's discriminant rule retrofits nothing either, because the only site it names was already repaired.** The four `as` casts it forbids were replaced by exhaustive tag helpers in `1f78223`, before the rule was written, and no other enum cast remains in `crates/`. The rule's whole effect is prospective: a reviewer now has something to cite, and a worker writing a new encoder has a stated form to follow. It also narrows `extend-canonical-identity-encodings-for-reserved-variants` by half — the `schedule::model::push_numerical` site it owned is closed — while leaving its `effect_tag` half open with a third resolution now on the table.

`implementation_status: "partial"` reflects the state of the decided behaviour, not of this document: most landed surfaces already satisfy most conventions, and the exceptions above are named with owners.

Conventions 1 through 6 are realized in `tiler-ir` and, for identity and errors, in `tiler-compiler`'s draft authorities. The closure convenience of convention 4 exists only for `tiler_ir::index::IndexRegionBuilder`; `ScheduledRegionBuilder` and `SemanticProgramBuilder` expose the transactional builder and consuming `build()` without a `build_with`, and `CheckedBuildError` plus the toy-builder tests in `crates/tiler-ir/src/convenience.rs` are the evidence that the shared shape generalizes to a second layer. That is a reservation, not implemented support.

## Open questions

These are recorded unresolved on purpose, and none was settled by this ADR itself. A question a later ticket answers is retained here with its resolution and the owner that supplied it, rather than deleted, so the reasoning that produced the answer stays attached to the question it answers.

- **Descriptor accessor style within `tiler-ir`.** `tiler_ir::schedule` exposes leaf descriptors (`IndexRegion`, `Access`, `KernelSchedule`, `BoundsProof`, `OwnershipProof`, `NumericalRealization`) with `pub` fields, while the sibling `tiler_ir::index` keeps its data types `pub(super)` and reads through `*Ref<'_>` view accessors. Both satisfy convention 6, and this is not a soundness gap: opacity is enforced at `VerifiedScheduledRegion` and the descriptors are reachable only through `&ScheduledRegion`. It is nonetheless two styles in one crate, and this ADR deliberately does not pick a winner. The choice is owned by `unify-schedule-index-region-with-verified-index-region`, which must decide whether the unified form adopts view accessors (preferred if it needs field-level invariants) or records the pub-field value-data form as intended for schedule descriptors.
- **Whether `applies_to` should also name `tiler.contract.optimizer`.** This record names `tiler.contract.ir` (whose "Shared IR construction lifecycle" section is where conventions 1 through 6 would become normative) and `tiler.contract.architecture` (whose component-boundary and packaging sections are where convention 7 would). Convention 7 also constrains `tiler-compiler` modules that `docs/compiler/optimizer.md` describes, but that contract owns search semantics rather than Rust surface shape, and the compiler's public facade does not exist yet. Naming it now would claim authority this ADR has not earned. Revisit when the reviewed compiler facade lands.
- **Naming for presentation-only digest labels — resolved 2026-07-24 by `disambiguate-presentation-label-from-semantic-key-accessors`. The settled spelling is `label()`.** As recorded, the question was that `key()` named both a presentation-only digest (`tiler-compiler`) and a stable semantic key (`tiler-ir`); that a distinct spelling — `label()`, `display_id()`, or similar — would make the distinction visible at the call site, against the cost that renaming touches explain-record construction and its fixtures; and that no owner was assigned. An owner was assigned and the rename landed. This record's own first candidate won, on evidence the question did not yet have: every one of the affected doc comments already used the word — "Returns a bounded explain label", "The label is a digest of the canonical bytes and is presentation only" — so `label()` makes each accessor match the contract it was already documenting instead of introducing a third vocabulary. Eight `tiler-compiler` accessors were renamed, covering both spellings convention 2 above now records; `tiler-ir`'s borrowed semantic-key accessors were deliberately untouched, as was `pipeline::ProgramAlternative::stable_id`, because for those the existing name states the role correctly. **Measurement — the rename moved no label value, and none could move.** Every label is a `format!` over unmodified canonical bytes, so renaming its accessor cannot change what it renders; the owning ticket confirmed it by rendering the same explain trace before and after the rename and comparing the two byte-identical results, and no expected fixture value changed anywhere. Convention 2's citation and its correction paragraph were brought to the landed spelling by `record-presentation-label-naming-resolution`. No convention changed, so that update is a citation and evidence refresh rather than an amendment; the rule convention 2 states is the one it always stated.
- **Whether a failing registry `freeze` should return recoverable builder ownership.** Added 2026-07-24 with the convention 4 amendment. Every shared-IR-layer `build` returns its intact builder through `into_parts`, for the reason ADR 0058 records: a large arena-backed draft is worth inspecting, correcting, and retrying rather than discarding. The registry family splits. `ScalarRegistryBuilder::freeze` and `LoweringCapabilityRegistryBuilder::freeze` are infallible and raise no question. The other three return a typed error and drop the builder: `ReferenceRegistryBuilder::freeze` and `ScalarReferenceRegistryBuilder::freeze` fail only with `EmptyRegistry`, where there is nothing worth recovering and the asymmetry is arguably right, but `SemanticRegistryBuilder::freeze` also fails on unsatisfied type-authority closure and governed resource bounds — failures a caller could plausibly correct by registering the missing authority and retrying, and exactly the case ADR 0058's rationale describes. This ADR deliberately does not decide it, because converging the shape means changing three public signatures across two crates and that is a boundary Tom reviews. What would settle it is one real caller that wants to retry a failed `freeze`; until such a caller exists, the difference is recorded rather than removed, and the conformance rule stays the consuming-terminal property alone.
- **Whether a conformance check should be mechanized.** Several conventions are grep-shaped (`Box<dyn` in a `pub fn` signature, a `pub` field on a `Verified*` type, an encoder without a domain tag). Whether that belongs in the repository gate, in a Clippy configuration, or in review only is unresolved; a check that fires on the leaf-descriptor exception would be worse than none.

## Alternatives considered

Leaving the conventions implicit is the status quo, and it is what produced the `#[non_exhaustive]` gap: the rules propagated by imitation, each worker citing the sibling it happened to read, with nothing to cite when a sibling was wrong.

Encoding the conventions as an accepted contract section instead of a proposed ADR would skip the acceptance step this repository reserves for public boundaries. Acceptance is Tom's, and propagation into `docs/ir.md` and `docs/architecture.md` is a deliberate follow-up rather than a side effect of drafting.

Enforcing the conventions with a lint or gate before writing them down would freeze the leaf-descriptor exception and the digest-label naming before either was settled, and would report a violation without a citable rule to explain it.

Retrofitting the non-conforming surfaces inside this record would mix a conventions decision with identity re-baselining across two crates. Those edits change canonical bytes and belong to tickets that can rebaseline fixtures deliberately and state that the change is intentional rather than drift.
