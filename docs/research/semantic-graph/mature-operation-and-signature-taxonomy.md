## Purpose and boundary

This document enumerates the tensor operation families a mature tensor compiler should eventually be able to *express*, together with the exact signature obligations each family imposes. It is the operation analogue of the [mature dtype taxonomy](../numerics/mature-dtype-taxonomy.md): a catalog, not a support promise, and not a schedule.

**Enumerating a family is not admitting one, and the distinction is load-bearing.** The [sequence-extending tensor family](../shapes/sequence-extending-tensor-family.md) record states the objection this document must answer, in its own words: admitting a family with no occurrence "would be a contract written from imagination". That objection is correct and this record does not weaken it. What it constrains is *registration* — fixing a key, a schema, an inference routine, and a normative reference, all of which bind future readers to a contract nobody's workload asked for. Enumeration does the opposite job: it names the space so that when a workload does arrive, the reader can tell whether the family is a new question or an already-mapped one, and can tell an accidental gap from a deliberate exclusion. The [support matrix](../../roadmap.md#operation-family-support-matrix) already carries the same posture for the rows it tracks — twenty when this record was written and twenty-two since the [delivery graph](operation-family-delivery-graph.md) split two over-broad ones on 2026-08-05, with no family gaining a row and no rung moving — "Listing a family authorizes nothing, and widening any row is separate, explicitly scheduled work" — and this record extends the posture to the families that have no matrix row at all.

Three things this record therefore does not do. It does not assign a maturity rung to any family; rungs live in the support matrix and move only there. It does not propose an `OpKey`, an attribute schema, or a Rust spelling; those are public boundaries reserved to Tom under [ADR 0075](../../decisions/0075-scope-public-boundary-approval-by-change-category.md). And it does not select a first profile; [Q-SEM-003](../../open-questions.md) owns that and closes on a named production consumer, not on an inventory.

**Evidence classes.** This record is `primary-source-synthesis` and nothing else. It takes no measurement, runs no program, and reports no execution. Where a family's classification depends on an observation Tiler has not made, the row says `RQ-OP-nn` and [Research questions and their closure tests](#research-questions-and-their-closure-tests) states exactly what evidence would close it. A row that guesses would be worse than a row that asks, because the delivery-graph ticket consumes these rows as though they were settled.

## What a signature must carry

The eight dimensions this record records per family are not invented here. They are the dimensions [Operation extensions](../../operation-extensions.md) already requires of any mandatory operation definition — "the bounded operand/result/value-kind schema, attribute schema, initial pure effect signature, deterministic inference and semantic validation, normative semantic specification identity, conformance vectors, and stable host-readable names" — plus the numerical and lowering obligations the [initial operation conformance matrix](../numerics/operation-conformance-matrix.md) and the [transformer operation and shape surface](../shapes/transformer-operation-and-shape-surface.md) each own for their own subsets. This record's contribution is applying all eight uniformly to a space wider than any one workload.

| Dimension | What the cell states | Authority that owns the answer once adopted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D1 Atomicity** | Whether the family is one atomic semantic operation or a composition over smaller governed operations. | [IR](../../ir.md) and the family's own decomposition capability. |
| **D2 Arity** | Ordered operand count and ordered result count, including multi-result and bounded-variadic forms. | [Contract memo](contract-memo.md) `### Structure`; [Operation extensions](../../operation-extensions.md) schema rules. |
| **D3 Dtype signature and promotion** | Which exact resolved value types the family admits, and what happens when operands disagree. | [Mature dtype taxonomy](../numerics/mature-dtype-taxonomy.md) for identity; [ADR 0009](../../decisions/0009-resolved-numerical-typing.md) and [ADR 0026](../../decisions/0026-dtype-representability-vs-operation-support.md) for the resolution and representability rules. |
| **D4 Rank/shape and typed attributes** | Rank and extent constraints, and the canonical attributes that carry the family's structure. | [IR](../../ir.md) shape environment; [Operation extensions](../../operation-extensions.md) `## Host-owned canonical attributes`. |
| **D5 Numerical and exceptional-value contract** | Rounding, accumulation, NaN, signed zero, subnormals, overflow, and division-by-zero behaviour. | [Numerical semantics](../../numerical-semantics.md); the [operation conformance matrix](../numerics/operation-conformance-matrix.md). |
| **D6 Purity, effects, determinism** | Whether the family is pure, what effect or state it would need, and what determinism it can promise. | [Contract memo](contract-memo.md) `### Purity and determinism`; [ADR 0013](../../decisions/0013-scoped-determinism.md). |
| **D7 Reference realization and physical fallback** | The minimum correct host oracle, and the minimum physical route that is not a fast path. | [Index and access model](../indexing/index-access-model.md); `tiler-reference`. |
| **D8 Extension interaction and unsupported cases** | How the family behaves through the extension seams, and which of its cases fail closed. | [Operation extensions](../../operation-extensions.md) `## Capability coherence` and `### What a seam is not`. |

### The atomicity vocabulary

**D1 uses the vocabulary the [transformer operation and shape surface](../shapes/transformer-operation-and-shape-surface.md) established in its `## Family-by-family disposition` table**, extended by one value that a broad enumeration needs and a single-workload derivation did not.

- **Atomic** — one semantic operation family whose meaning is not recoverable from smaller governed operations without losing an optimization, a numerical guarantee, or an identity the compiler depends on.
- **Atomic with a declared decomposition capability** — atomic identity, plus an optional capability that rewrites it into governed operations when a pass needs the smaller form. Registration of the decomposition is separate from registration of the family, per [Operation extensions](../../operation-extensions.md).
- **Composition over governed keys** — no key of its own; the frontend emits the smaller operations. Choosing this is a real decision, because it fixes what the optimizer must rediscover.
- **Frontend sugar** — not a graph operation at any maturity. It is a spelling that resolves before the graph exists.
- **Neither, today** — the family needs a graph mechanism (an effect, a region, a non-tensor value kind, a data-dependent extent) that the accepted model does not have, so its atomicity is not yet a meaningful question.

**Fact.** *Frontend sugar* is forced by the Array API standard rather than chosen. Python array API standard 2025.12 defines `zeros`, `ones`, `empty`, `zeros_like`, `full_like`, `eye`, `linspace`, `meshgrid`, `arange`, and `asarray` as creation functions, and `broadcast_arrays`, `broadcast_shapes`, `expand_dims`, `flatten`-style reshaping, `moveaxis`, and `squeeze` as manipulation functions. **Inference.** Several of these denote no computation at all once rank and extents are resolved — `broadcast_shapes` is a shape computation over metadata, `zeros_like` is `zeros` at an already-known type, and `expand_dims` is one `Reindex` mapping form. Admitting them as operations would put frontend convenience into durable graph identity, which the [contract memo](contract-memo.md) already forbids in a narrower form when it rules that shapes and dtypes are derived facts rather than trusted attributes. The classification is therefore a positive contract boundary, not an omission.

## A. Value production and data movement

| Family | D1 Atomicity | D2 Arity | D3 Dtype and promotion | D4 Rank/shape and attributes | D5 Numerical and exceptional | D6 Purity/effects/determinism | D7 Reference and physical fallback | D8 Extension and unsupported |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **F-01** dense constant | Atomic | 0 operands, 1 result | Exactly one resolved value type; no promotion is definable because there is no second operand | Static rank and extents; the payload is a canonical attribute whose bytes are the value, not a re-encoding of it | Bit-exact; no rounding occurs, so an unrepresentable literal is a construction refusal rather than a rounded value | Pure, deterministic | Reference returns the stored bits; physical fallback is an embedded or bound buffer | Extension types supply their own canonical byte encoding; a payload whose length disagrees with the declared extents fails closed |
| **F-02** structural index generation | Atomic | 0 operands, 1 result | Integer or float result type; the generated value is a coordinate, so the admissible set is narrower than the catalog | Static rank; one axis attribute selecting which coordinate is materialized | Exact for every coordinate representable in the result type; a coordinate that is not exactly representable is a refusal | Pure, deterministic | Reference computes the coordinate directly; physical fallback is an index expression, which the [index model](../indexing/index-access-model.md) already admits as affine | Materializing an index that a fused consumer would have computed is a cost question, never a legality one |
| **F-03** identity and bit-preserving copy | Atomic with a declared decomposition capability | 1 operand, 1 result | Result type equals operand type exactly | Result shape equals operand shape exactly | No numerical content; it is bit-preserving by definition, including NaN payloads and signed zero | Pure, deterministic | Reference is the identity; physical fallback is a copy, and eliding it is an optimization with a written-ownership obligation | An identity over an extension type must not silently canonicalize the payload, which is the failure mode that makes this family worth naming |
| **F-04** bit reinterpretation | Atomic | 1 operand, 1 result | Source and destination are an ordered pair; no promotion exists and no pair is implied by width alone | Rank changes when the widths differ — a narrowing reinterpretation appends a trailing axis | Bit-preserving, so it is the one family where a NaN payload must survive; that directly tensions with [ADR 0018](../../decisions/0018-portable-bitwise-nans.md) | Pure, but deterministic only where the physical representation is fixed | Reference reinterprets the exact stored bits; physical fallback needs a declared storage encoding, so it is not realizable over an unspecified carrier | Sub-byte and block-scaled carriers are explicitly unsupported here; see `RQ-OP-02` |

**Fact — the rank consequence of F-04 is normative in a primary source, not a design flourish.** StableHLO `bitcast_convert` (`stablehlo-spec-v1.18.0`) states that when `num_bits(E') < num_bits(E)` the result gains a trailing axis with `dim(result, R) * num_bits(E') = num_bits(E)`, and when `num_bits(E') > num_bits(E)` the operand's trailing axis is consumed. The same section states that `bits` "returns in-memory representation of a given value, and its behavior is implementation-defined because the exact representation of tensors is implementation-defined". **Inference.** A bit reinterpretation is therefore the first family in this inventory whose *semantic* result depends on a physical fact, which is exactly the separation [ADR 0046](../../decisions/0046-separate-logical-access-from-storage-addressing.md) exists to protect. Admitting it requires either fixing the carrier in the operation's own identity or accepting that the family is only meaningful under a declared storage encoding. That tension is `RQ-OP-02` and this record does not resolve it.

**Fact — F-04 and [ADR 0018](../../decisions/0018-portable-bitwise-nans.md) are in genuine tension and the tension is stated rather than smoothed.** ADR 0018 canonicalizes arithmetic NaNs so that a bitwise result is portable. A bit-preserving reinterpretation that canonicalized its input would not be bit-preserving, and one that did not would let a non-canonical payload reach a bitwise consumer. **Inference.** The resolution is that canonicalization is a property of *arithmetic result materialization*, which F-04 is not, so F-04 preserves and the boundary is that F-04's output is not an arithmetic result. That is an inference from two accepted positions, not an accepted rule, and it belongs in the ADR that eventually admits the family.

Primary sources: StableHLO `constant`, `iota`, `dynamic_iota`, `bitcast_convert` (`stablehlo-spec-v1.18.0`); ONNX `Constant`, `ConstantOfShape`, `Identity`, `Range`, `EyeLike`, `BitCast` (`onnx-operators-v1.22.0`); TOSA `CONST`, `IDENTITY`, `CONST_SHAPE` (`tosa-spec-1.0.1`); Array API `arange`, `eye`, `linspace`, `full`, `zeros`, `ones` (`array-api-2025.12`).

## B. Elementwise arithmetic over numbers

| Family | D1 Atomicity | D2 Arity | D3 Dtype and promotion | D4 Rank/shape and attributes | D5 Numerical and exceptional | D6 Purity/effects/determinism | D7 Reference and physical fallback | D8 Extension and unsupported |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **F-05** unary float arithmetic | Atomic per operation, not one keyed family | 1 operand, 1 result | One resolved float type in and the same type out; no promotion | Result shape equals operand shape; no attributes | Exactly rounded per [ADR 0024](../../decisions/0024-initial-arithmetic-rounding.md) where the operation is algebraic; `abs`, `negate`, and `sign` are exact and must state signed-zero and NaN behaviour separately | Pure, deterministic | Reference is exact-rational then one rounding at materialization; physical fallback is a scalar kernel op | An extension float supplies its own value set; an accuracy contract over a non-ordered type is intentionally invalid, per `ULP_FORMAT_RULES` |
| **F-06** binary float arithmetic | Atomic per operation | 2 operands, 1 result | Both operands and the result carry one identical resolved type; **no ambient promotion or autocast** | Equal shapes exactly, with the narrow rank-zero scalar admission [IR](../../ir.md) owns; no implicit broadcast | Separate rounding per operation under [ADR 0024](../../decisions/0024-initial-arithmetic-rounding.md); `divide` states its zero-divisor and inexactness behaviour; `remainder` states its sign convention | Pure, deterministic | Reference is exact-rational with one rounding; physical fallback is a scalar kernel op | A mixed-precision spelling is refused by name rather than promoted, exactly as the registered BF16 signatures refuse `bf16.binary.mixed-precision` |
| **F-07** fused multiply-add | Atomic, and specifically not a composition | 3 operands, 1 result | One identical resolved float type across all three operands and the result | Equal shapes; no attributes | **One** rounding, which is the entire reason the family exists; [ADR 0015](../../decisions/0015-fma-vs-contraction.md) forbids lowering it to separate roundings | Pure, deterministic | Reference must be exact-rational, because a host `f32` route double-rounds and is wrong here; physical fallback requires a target that offers single-rounding | A target that cannot honour single rounding makes the operation infeasible, never approximately feasible |
| **F-08** integer arithmetic | Atomic per overflow discipline; wrapping, saturating, checked, and widening are four families, not one mode-selected family | 2 operands, 1 result; the checked form is `RQ-OP-01` on whether it is 1 or 2 results | One identical resolved integer type, or an explicitly stated wider result for the widening form | Equal shapes; no attributes | [ADR 0039](../../decisions/0039-explicit-integer-overflow-operations.md) makes overflow part of identity; required-no-overflow is a discharged proof or a runtime validation obligation, never undefined behaviour | Pure, deterministic; integer addition is associative, so its reassociation legality is the inverse of the float case | Reference is exact integer arithmetic at unbounded precision, then the family's own overflow rule; physical fallback is a scalar kernel op | Sub-byte widths need a packing contract before a physical route exists; the semantic family does not |
| **F-09** integer division and remainder | Atomic per rounding convention; [ADR 0040](../../decisions/0040-specialize-integer-division-families.md) accepts signed truncating, floor, Euclidean, ceiling, canonical unsigned, and exact as separate families | 2 operands, 1 result | One identical resolved integer type | Equal shapes; no attributes | Each family fixes quotient rounding, matched remainder sign and range, zero-divisor behaviour, signed quotient overflow, and the standalone `MIN rem -1` result | Pure, deterministic | Reference is exact integer division under the family's stated convention; physical fallback is a scalar kernel op, and the conventions differ from the host's | A division whose divisor cannot be proved nonzero is a value assumption under [ADR 0021](../../decisions/0021-validated-value-assumptions.md), not a silently defined result |
| **F-10** extrema and clamp | Atomic; [ADR 0023](../../decisions/0023-floating-point-extrema-semantics.md) accepts propagating and number-preferring as separate families | 2 operands for extrema, 3 for clamp, 1 result | One identical resolved type | Equal shapes; no attributes | Deterministic `-0.0 < +0.0` ordering; the two families differ exactly in NaN handling and must never be one mode-selected operation | Pure, deterministic | Reference implements the stated total order; physical fallback is a scalar kernel op, and vendor `fmin`/`fmax` are number-preferring with order-dependent NaN behaviour | A clamp whose bounds are not ordered is a construction refusal, not a defined empty interval |
| **F-11** elementary transcendentals | Atomic with a declared decomposition capability | 1 or 2 operands, 1 result | One identical resolved float type | Equal shapes; no attributes | An accuracy contract under [ADR 0016](../../decisions/0016-transcendental-accuracy-contracts.md) and [ADR 0042](../../decisions/0042-use-typed-transcendental-accuracy-contracts.md), plus an **independent** exceptional-value policy; an accuracy bound never implies special-value behaviour | Pure; deterministic only within the declared accuracy envelope, so bitwise portability is not promised | Reference is a correctly rounded or explicitly bounded oracle; physical fallback is a vendor function whose own guarantee must refine the declared contract | A target with no published bound yields `Unknown`, which fails closed rather than defaulting to the vendor's behaviour |
| **F-12** named activations and normalizations | Atomic with a declared decomposition capability, and the atomicity is the point | 1 to 3 operands, 1 result | One identical resolved float type; an internal accumulator type is a declared operation fact under [ADR 0091](../../decisions/0091-separate-bf16-float-conversion-families-and-keep-the-accumulator-an-operation-fact.md), never a schedule choice | Reduced-axis and epsilon-placement attributes carry the decisions a composition would leave implicit | The family pins what the usual spelling hides: whether a maximum is subtracted, where `eps` sits, which extrema family is used, and the reduction order | Pure, deterministic given a stated reduction order | Reference evaluates the pinned formula, not a mathematically equivalent one; physical fallback is a fused or multi-pass kernel | A frontend that emits a composition instead gets a different canonical graph, which is a different identity, not an alternative spelling |

**Fact — the promotion policy this record uses is already owned and is only cited here.** The [operation conformance matrix](../numerics/operation-conformance-matrix.md) `## Cross-operation invariants` fixes value and computation dtype as "Resolved before semantic optimization; no ambient promotion or autocast", and the [sequence-extending tensor family](../shapes/sequence-extending-tensor-family.md) restates the same posture for a structural family: it grants "no promotion, no weak-scalar rule, and no dtype permission, exactly as the binary elementwise signatures do not". **Inference.** Every D3 cell in this record is therefore an *admissible set* rather than a promotion lattice, and a mixed-type program is a frontend's explicit conversion problem rather than a compiler inference problem.

**Fact — the primary sources disagree with each other about promotion, and the disagreement is why the policy above is the correct one rather than merely the conservative one.** Array API 2025.12 specifies promotion within a kind — signed integers widen along `int8 → int16 → int32 → int64`, floats along `float32 → float64` — and then states of the cross-kind case that "Mixed integer and floating-point type promotion rules are not specified because behavior varies between implementations", that "Type promotion of non-numerical data types to numerical data types is unspecified (e.g., `bool` to `intxx` or `floatxx`)", and that mixing a Python `float` with an integer array "may give `float32`, `float64`, or raise an exception". **Inference.** The most portable array specification in the ecosystem leaves the interesting half of promotion undefined. A compiler that inferred a promotion there would be choosing one implementation's behaviour and calling it semantics; one that refuses returns a typed error the frontend can fix at the only place the answer is known. The refusal is the correct design, and this is the evidence for it rather than an appeal to caution.

**Fact — F-12's atomicity has a worked precedent and a stated reason.** The [transformer operation and shape surface](../shapes/transformer-operation-and-shape-surface.md) `## Family-by-family disposition` classifies softmax, RMS normalization, and SiLU by exactly this dimension, and the [support matrix](../../roadmap.md#operation-family-support-matrix) records that the registered `tiler::softmax-f32@1` "pins the four decisions a composition would leave open: the row maximum is subtracted before the exponential, the extrema family is the NaN-propagating `Maximum` with `-0.0 < +0.0`, the denominator" ordering, and so on. **Inference.** The general rule this generalizes to is that a family is atomic when a composition would leave a *numerical* decision to whichever pass happened to run, and it is a composition when the smaller operations already determine the answer. That rule, not a taste for large operations, is what decides D1 for every row in group B.

## C. Predicates, logic, and bits

| Family | D1 Atomicity | D2 Arity | D3 Dtype and promotion | D4 Rank/shape and attributes | D5 Numerical and exceptional | D6 Purity/effects/determinism | D7 Reference and physical fallback | D8 Extension and unsupported |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **F-13** comparison | Atomic; one family carrying a direction attribute, or six families — `RQ-OP-03` | 2 operands, 1 result | Both operands one identical resolved type; the **result is a predicate type**, which is why this family is gated on `RQ-OP-03` | Equal shapes; a canonical direction attribute and, for floats, a comparison-kind attribute | Total versus partial order over NaN is a semantic choice, not a target detail; signed zero compares equal while ordering `-0.0 < +0.0` in extrema is a deliberate inconsistency that must be stated | Pure, deterministic | Reference implements the stated order; physical fallback is a scalar predicate | A comparison over a type with no defined order — a complex or an unordered extension type — is intentionally invalid, not unsupported |
| **F-14** logical operations | Atomic per operation | 1 or 2 operands, 1 result | Predicate operands and a predicate result only; an integer operand is F-15, not this family | Equal shapes; no attributes | No numerical content; two-valued and exact | Pure, deterministic | Reference is boolean algebra; physical fallback needs a decided predicate carrier | The overload that lets one spelling mean both logical and bitwise depending on operand type is intentionally rejected here |
| **F-15** bitwise and shift | Atomic per operation | 1 or 2 operands, 1 result | Identical resolved integer types; shift amount type and range are part of the signature | Equal shapes; no attributes | An out-of-range shift is a defined result or a refusal, never undefined; arithmetic and logical right shift are separate operations | Pure, deterministic | Reference is exact bit manipulation at the declared width; physical fallback is a scalar kernel op | Bitwise operations over a float type are intentionally invalid; the coherent spelling is F-04 then F-15 |
| **F-16** classification predicates | Atomic per predicate | 1 operand, 1 result | Float operand, predicate result | Equal shapes; no attributes | These are the operations that *observe* exceptional values rather than produce them, so [ADR 0020](../../decisions/0020-value-only-floating-point-exceptions.md)'s value-only exception model is what makes them expressible at all | Pure, deterministic | Reference inspects the exact value; physical fallback is a scalar predicate | A subnormal-detecting predicate interacts with [ADR 0019](../../decisions/0019-split-subnormal-handling.md) flush contracts and is not admissible under a flushing target |
| **F-17** elementwise selection | Atomic | 3 operands, 1 result | A predicate condition operand plus two identically typed value operands; the result carries the value type | Equal shapes, or a rank-1 condition against a batched value under some precedents — this record takes the equal-shape form and treats the relaxation as unsupported | Bit-preserving on the chosen branch; it must not canonicalize a selected NaN, because it performs no arithmetic | Pure, deterministic | Reference selects; physical fallback is a predicated scalar select | The [glossary](../../glossary.md) separates four unrelated `Select`s in this corpus; this row is the tensor one only |

**Fact — every family in group C is gated on the same missing decision, and that is why they are one group.** The [dtype support ledger](../../dtype-support.md) records that the governed catalog registers `tiler::bool@1` as two-valued with deliberately no logical width, that `tiler::i1@1` has no authority, and that "no registered operation admits a `bool` operand at any arity". It further records that `KernelType::Bool` and `AbiType::Boolean` "are control predicates, not logical bool tensor values, and registration did not change that". **Inference.** Comparison, logical operations, classification predicates, elementwise selection, and boolean masking all produce or consume a predicate tensor. None of them is expressible until a predicate tensor is a graph value with a decided storage and ABI, so they rise and fall together rather than one at a time. `RQ-OP-03` states the closure test for the whole group, and the ledger's own trigger — admit the first vertical "only for a named `Select`, comparison, logical reduction, or frontend workload" — is the same gate seen from the dtype side.

**Fact — the logical-versus-bitwise overload is a real hazard in the sources and not a hypothetical one.** StableHLO uses `and`, `or`, `not`, and `xor` for both the boolean and the integer case, resolving by operand type. TOSA separates them into `LOGICAL_AND`/`LOGICAL_OR`/`LOGICAL_NOT`/`LOGICAL_XOR` and `BITWISE_AND`/`BITWISE_OR`/`BITWISE_NOT`/`BITWISE_XOR`. Array API 2025.12 does the same, with `logical_and` and `bitwise_and` as distinct functions. **Inference.** Two of three authorities separate them, and the separation is what lets each family state its own D3 admissible set and its own D5 row without a conditional. This record follows TOSA and the Array API; the cost is two families where StableHLO has one, and the benefit is that neither family's signature contains "depending on the operand type".

**Fact — the directional-pair decision in F-18 is accepted for one pair, and the generalization this record originally inferred from it has since been tested and refuted in part.** [ADR 0091](../../decisions/0091-separate-bf16-float-conversion-families-and-keep-the-accumulator-an-operation-fact.md) decides that BF16/binary32 conversion is "two directional families carrying disjoint field sets", that a pointwise mixed-width program spells its conversions explicitly, and that the accumulator lives in the operation's registered facts and hence its identity.

**Superseded inference, retained because `RQ-OP-04` is the record of its refutation.** This record originally inferred that the reason the fields are disjoint is that narrowing owes a rounding rule, an overflow rule, and a subnormal rule that widening does not, while widening owes an exactness claim narrowing cannot make — and that "that asymmetry is a property of the direction rather than of BF16, so the same shape should hold for every pair". **Fact — 2026-08-05.** [Conversion family decomposition across pairs](../numerics/conversion-family-decomposition-across-pairs.md) walked twenty ordered pairs over the five IEEE-convention binary float layouts the [dtype taxonomy](../numerics/mature-dtype-taxonomy.md) states, and eighteen fall into ADR 0091's two field sets while two do not: `bf16 → f16` owes `{rounding, overflow, underflow}` and `f16 → bf16` owes `{rounding, NaN mapping}`, so the two directions' field sets **intersect** at `rounding`. **Inference.** The empty-and-full split is a property of the pair's value sets being *comparable*, not of direction; `bf16` and `f16` are the first pair in the recognized catalog where neither format contains the other, `bf16` having the wider exponent range and `f16` the wider significand. What survives is the per-ordered-pair key in F-18's D1 cell; what does not survive is naming the two families for a direction.

**Fact — TOSA keeps `CAST` and `RESCALE` in one category and they are not the same kind of operation.** TOSA `2.13. Type Conversion` contains exactly `CAST` and `RESCALE` (`tosa-spec-1.0.1`). ONNX separates `Cast`, `CastLike`, `QuantizeLinear`, `DequantizeLinear`, and `DynamicQuantizeLinear` (`onnx-operators-v1.22.0`). StableHLO separates `convert`, `uniform_quantize`, `uniform_dequantize`, and `reduce_precision` (`stablehlo-spec-v1.18.0`). **Inference.** The grouping that survives all three is by *what the transition preserves*: F-18 and F-19 change the type, F-20 changes the numeric interpretation while the code type may be unchanged, and F-21 changes neither. Those are three different obligations, and collapsing any two of them puts an unstated rounding into a family whose signature does not mention rounding.

**Fact — the out-of-bounds question in F-24 is a real divergence among the authorities and cannot be settled by adopting one of them.** ONNX `Slice` clamps out-of-range start and end values to the axis extent (`onnx-operators-v1.22.0`: `starts[i]` "is clamped into the range `[0, dims[axes[i]]]`"). StableHLO `slice` constrains the indices at verification instead, and its `dynamic_slice` adjusts the start indices to keep the slice in bounds. Array API 2025.12 does neither: it declines to specify. Its indexing chapter states that the specification "does not require \"clipping\" out-of-bounds slice indices. This is in contrast to Python slice semantics where `0:100` and `0:10` are equivalent on a list of length `10`", and gives the rationale that implementations "may choose to clip..., raise an exception, return junk values, or some other behavior depending on device requirements and performance considerations" (`array-api-indexing-2025.12`); `take` carries the same refusal, "the behavior for out-of-bounds indices is unspecified and thus implementation-defined", explicitly since 2023.12 (`array-api-indexing-functions-stub-2025.12`). **Inference.** Two of the three conventions produce a *different tensor* for the same program rather than a different diagnostic, and the third makes the result portable-undefined, so inheriting any of them silently would make a frontend's meaning depend on which specification its author had read. The correct posture for a compiler that fails closed is to make the bound a validated obligation — proved at construction where extents are static, and a typed host-side pre-dispatch requirement where they are symbolic, which is exactly the three-outcome path [the shape environment contract](../shapes/shape-environment-contract.md) already accepted for extent agreement. This record proposes that posture and does not adopt a clamp.

**Fact — F-25's pad value is not neutral, and the corpus already has the counterexample.** [Numerical semantics](../../numerical-semantics.md) keeps empty result, algebraic identity, and safe physical padding as three separate facts, and gives the exact case: a strict floating sum may return `+0.0` for an empty domain, yet adding `+0.0` to a singleton `-0.0` under round-to-nearest produces `+0.0`, so `+0.0` is not bitwise-neutral padding for that reduction even though it is its empty result. **Inference.** A padding family therefore owes its consumers a stated pad value rather than an implied zero, and any pass that elides the materialization owes a neutrality proof under the selected contract. This is the same obligation a tiled contraction owes for a ragged contracted extent, which is why the two rows cite the same rule.

**Fact — the reduction signature schema in F-28 is not proposed here; it is quoted from an already-partially-adopted record.** [Reduction semantics and legality](../numerics/reduction-semantics-and-legality.md) fixes the schema `Reduction { input, axes, reducer, input_conversion, accumulator_dtype, initial, empty_without_initial, result_conversion, result_dtype, result_value_policy, order, determinism }`, fixes the canonical axis set as unique, sorted, in range, and nonempty, fixes `keepdims` as frontend sugar, and fixes the reassociation-by-permutation legality table. It is `adopted_by` ADRs 0012, 0013, 0014, 0022, and 0025. **Inference.** F-28's row is that schema restated at the granularity this inventory uses, and F-31, F-32, and F-33 each inherit it and add exactly one thing — a retained prefix, a shared reduction domain, and a window respectively. Naming the inheritance is what stops each of the three from re-deriving an accumulator policy.

**Fact — the same record explicitly protects the families this inventory adds.** Its `## First vertical slice` excludes argmin and argmax, mean, variance, norms, log-sum-exp, scans, windows, segments, histograms, and scatters from the first slice "without making them impossible". **Inference.** F-29, F-30, F-31, and F-33 are therefore not contradictions of an adopted position; they are the enumeration that position deliberately left room for, and the phrase quoted above is the licence.

**Fact — StableHLO's `reduce` is variadic and multi-result, and its ordering is explicitly implementation-defined.** The spec states that the reduction "order of reductions is implementation-defined, which means that `body` and `init_values` must form a monoid to guarantee that the operation produces the same results for all inputs on all implementations. However, this condition doesn't hold for many popular reductions. E.g. floating-point addition for `body` and zero for `init_values` don't actually form a monoid because floating-point addition is not associative" (`stablehlo-spec-v1.18.0`). **Inference.** The most widely deployed portable tensor IR states, in its own normative text, that its most common reduction is not portable. That is the strongest available argument for the corpus's opposite choice — a stated contributor order in the operation, with reassociation as an explicit permission — and it is evidence rather than preference. It is also why `RQ-OP-06` asks whether the *variadic* shape is worth adopting even though the ordering model is not.

**Fact — F-32's index-structure decision is accepted and its multi-operand question is not.** [ADR 0087](../../decisions/0087-model-contraction-as-one-keyed-family-with-an-index-structure.md) accepts the single keyed family carrying a renaming-invariant canonical index structure, with five structural rules rejecting at construction. The roadmap's `#### Decisions reserved for Tom` records the second question — whether a semantic contraction may consume more than two operands — as still reserved, and notes that the answer "determines what an association is a choice *about*, not whether it needs a permission Tiler cannot currently express". **Inference.** This record's F-32 row therefore states arity as two-with-a-reservation rather than guessing, and points at [Q-SEM-015](../../open-questions.md) rather than opening a duplicate question of its own.

**Fact — StableHLO's `dot_general` carries the structure separately from the operands, and the field list is instructive.** It takes `lhs_batching_dimensions`, `rhs_batching_dimensions`, `lhs_contracting_dimensions`, `rhs_contracting_dimensions`, and a `precision_config` whose "semantics of these enum values is underspecified" by the spec's own admission (`stablehlo-spec-v1.18.0`). **Inference.** The structural half is exactly ADR 0087's index structure under a different spelling, which is independent confirmation. The precision half is the field the corpus's accepted mixed-precision decision replaces with named computation, accumulator, and result types — and the source's own "underspecified" is the reason to replace it rather than adopt it.

**Fact — F-35's purity claim is the corpus's position and it is mechanically enforced today.** The [sequence-extending record](../shapes/sequence-extending-tensor-family.md) records that `OperationEffect` "has exactly one variant and is deliberately not `#[non_exhaustive]`, so a second effect class is a compile error at three encoders — the mechanism that makes mutation unrepresentable rather than merely unimplemented". **Inference.** A scatter is therefore admissible as a pure family and inadmissible as an in-place update, and the difference is not stylistic: the pure form produces a value the optimizer may materialize where it chooses, while the in-place form would need alias analysis the accepted model has no vocabulary for. Every ecosystem spelling that reads as mutation — `index_put_`, `scatter_`, `dynamic_update_slice` — resolves to the pure form or is out of scope.

**Fact — the aliasing obligation F-35 inherits is already specified.** The [index and access model](../indexing/index-access-model.md) `## Aliasing and write ownership` states that read maps may be many-to-one, that ordinary write maps need coverage plus unique ownership, that reduction updates and atomics use separate contracts, and that initial outputs use fresh non-aliasing allocations. **Inference.** F-35's physical fallback is therefore not "a kernel that writes"; it is a kernel that either proves unique ownership or uses the separate atomic-combine contract, and a scatter admitted without choosing between those two would be admitted without a lowering.

**Fact — F-36's blocker is a shape mechanism rather than an operation design.** ONNX defines `NonZero`, `Compress`, `Unique`, and `TensorScatter` (`onnx-operators-v1.22.0`); Array API 2025.12 defines `nonzero`, `unique_all`, `unique_counts`, `unique_inverse`, `unique_values`, and `count_nonzero`; StableHLO defines none of them, which is itself evidence — a compiler-facing portable IR omits the whole class. **Inference.** The family is coherent and widely used at the framework level, and it is not expressible as a *tensor* result in a system whose allocations are planned before execution. That is why its D1 is "Neither, today" rather than "unsupported": the missing thing is a bounded-extent-plus-validity-count representation or a future value, not an operation definition. `RQ-OP-10` names both candidates and the test that would pick one.

Primary sources: StableHLO `gather`, `dynamic_gather`, `scatter`, `dynamic_update_slice`, `select_and_scatter` (`stablehlo-spec-v1.18.0`); TOSA `2.11. Scatter/Gather Operators` (`tosa-spec-1.0.1`); ONNX `Gather`, `GatherElements`, `GatherND`, `Scatter`, `ScatterElements`, `ScatterND`, `TensorScatter`, `Compress`, `NonZero`, `Unique`, `OneHot` (`onnx-operators-v1.22.0`); Array API `take`, `take_along_axis`, `nonzero`, `where`, set functions (`array-api-2025.12`).

**Fact — the sources agree that they do not define float sort order.** Array API 2025.12 states of `sort` and `argsort` that "For floating-point input arrays, the sort order of NaNs and signed zeros is unspecified and thus implementation-dependent". StableHLO's `sort` takes a caller-supplied `comparator` and an `is_stable` flag, pushing the whole ordering question to the caller, and defines equality as `comparator(e1, e2) = comparator(e2, e1) = false` (`stablehlo-spec-v1.18.0`). **Inference.** Neither source supplies an order Tiler could adopt; one declines to specify and the other delegates. A sort family therefore cannot be admitted by reference to a precedent, and its D5 cell is a decision the corpus must make — most naturally by reusing the deterministic `-0.0 < +0.0` ordering [ADR 0023](../../decisions/0023-floating-point-extrema-semantics.md) already accepted for extrema, so that two families do not carry two float orders. That reuse is a proposal, and `RQ-OP-11` is where it would be tested rather than assumed.

**Fact — a comparator region is not a small addition.** [IR](../../ir.md) states that the initial compilation unit "has no semantic functions/calls, recursion, region-bearing control flow, data-dependent branches, or semantic loops", and the [contract memo](contract-memo.md) records that "General sequences, maps, optional values, graph-valued attributes, nested regions, calls, and control flow are not required to prove the tensor optimizer architecture". **Inference.** Adopting StableHLO's comparator shape would introduce a nested region into the public graph, with consequences for canonical identity, verification, and the extension seam that far exceed one family. The fixed-order alternative costs expressiveness — co-sorting by a composite key becomes the caller's problem — and costs nothing structurally. `RQ-OP-11` states the test rather than this record picking.

## Intentionally invalid versus merely unsupported

The ticket requires these to be distinguishable, and the corpus already has the vocabulary: [Operation extensions](../../operation-extensions.md) fixes that "an unenumerated capability fails closed as `Unknown`", and the [dtype taxonomy](../numerics/mature-dtype-taxonomy.md) fixes that "Unknown remains distinct from unsupported". This record adds the third class the operation axis needs.

| Class | What it means | What a program spelling it gets | What would change it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intentionally invalid** | The cross-product denotes nothing coherent, or denotes something the semantic model forbids by construction. | A construction-time refusal with a named rule. It is a malformed program, not an unmet request. | Nothing short of changing the semantic model, and the change would be a supersession rather than a widening. |
| **Merely unsupported** | The cross-product is coherent and could be admitted; no authority fixes it today. | A typed unsupported failure naming the missing authority. | A named producer and consumer, followed by the ordinary admission path. |
| **Unknown** | Evidence does not establish whether the cross-product is honourable on the named target or under the named contract. | A fail-closed `Unknown`, which keeps a candidate in explain and search state only and never reaches an executable program. | A measurement or a normative guarantee for the exact `(family, dtype, target, contract)` tuple. |

**The distinction is not cosmetic.** An intentionally invalid case must never become a ticket, because filing one invites a future worker to "fix" a refusal that is load-bearing. A merely unsupported case must never be reported as invalid, because that tells a frontend author their program is wrong when it is only early. The [dtype support ledger](../../dtype-support.md) already makes this mistake impossible on its own axis by giving deliberately-excluded families an `absent/unsupported` cell that names the exclusion; this table is the operation-axis equivalent.

### Intentionally invalid cross-products, enumerated

These are the cases this pass identified where the cross-product is incoherent rather than early. Each names the family and the reason, so a reader can refute the classification rather than only the list.

- **A ULP accuracy contract over an integer, predicate, or unordered type** (F-05, F-11 over F-08's types). Adjacent-value distance is not defined for a type with no ordered floating value set. The corpus already refuses this with `UnrecognizedClass`, and the [dtype support ledger](../../dtype-support.md) records that the refusal "is the right answer".
- **A bitwise or shift operation over a floating-point type** (F-15). The coherent spelling is F-04 followed by F-15; admitting the direct form would silently fix a storage representation inside an arithmetic family.
- **An ordering comparison over a type with no defined total order** (F-13 over complex or an unordered extension type). StableHLO's own deprecation list includes complex comparisons, which is corroboration rather than the reason.
- **An index repeated within a single operand of a contraction** (F-32). This is a diagonal or trace extraction — a many-to-one read inside one operand — with a different access relation and a different meaning. It is one of ADR 0087's five structural rejections.
- **An index summed over but appearing in only one operand of a contraction** (F-32). That is a reduction of that operand, not a contraction, and it has a different access relation. Also an ADR 0087 structural rejection.
- **A duplicated axis in a reduction's canonical axis set** (F-28, F-31, F-33). The set is unique and sorted by definition; a duplicate denotes no reduction.
- **A concatenation whose operands disagree in rank, in resolved value type, or on any axis other than the concatenated one** (F-26). The result extent is defined as the exact operand sum along one axis and equality elsewhere; disagreement denotes no tensor.
- **A split mapping whose factors do not multiply to the original extent** (F-22). The map would not be a bijection, which is the family's defining property.
- **An operation declaring `Pure` while threading hidden state** (F-43 versus F-44). This is the one entry enforced by the type system rather than by a validator, and [Operation extensions](../../operation-extensions.md) states it directly: "No extension may smuggle an effect through a `pure` declaration."
- **A mixed-precision binary elementwise application** (F-06, F-08, F-26). Refused by name at application rather than promoted, because the promotion the caller wants is unspecified in the one standard that defines promotion at all.
- **Implicit broadcast at a binary operation** (F-06 without F-23). The map is an operation; inferring it would put a structural decision into an arithmetic family's inference routine.
- **Substituting one float-to-integer conversion family for another because the target realizes it natively** (F-19). [ADR 0076](../../decisions/0076-declare-target-honourable-numerical-realizations.md) forbids narrowing, weakening, or substituting the caller's stated contract to make a target feasible.

**What is merely unsupported, by contrast**, includes every family in this inventory that carries no registered key — which is most of them — plus every dtype the [dtype support ledger](../../dtype-support.md) marks `absent/unsupported` at the operation-signature layer, plus every `(family, dtype)` pair outside the narrow F32, BF16-triple, and strict-affine U4/U8 profiles. Those are early, not wrong, and each has an admission path.

## Cross-family invariants

These hold across every row above and are stated once rather than repeated forty-seven times. Each cites the authority that owns it; none is introduced here.

1. **No ambient promotion or autocast.** Value and computation dtypes are resolved before semantic optimization. A family's D3 cell is an admissible set, and a program outside it is refused rather than coerced.
2. **Structural families grant no dtype permission.** F-22 through F-27 preserve the resolved type exactly. A structural operation is never a place where a conversion hides.
3. **Reduction topology is physical, never semantic.** F-28 and every family inheriting it state a contributor order and a permission; the tree, the pass count, and the lane binding belong to the selected plan.
4. **Reassociation and operand permutation are independent permissions,** each requiring both an operation capability and an effective numerical permission. Distributivity is a third dimension no expressible contract grants.
5. **An accumulator type is an operation fact, hence part of identity** — never a schedule choice and never a resolved numerical contract field.
6. **Accuracy and exceptional-value behaviour are independent.** A declared ULP envelope says nothing about NaN, infinity, or subnormal results, and a family owes both.
7. **Every operation has at least one result,** and results are ordered and independently typed. Program outputs are a separate ordered named list, not synthetic `Output` operations.
8. **Canonical attributes are host-owned and structure-carrying.** An authored label — an einsum subscript string, an axis name — is a transient identifier that must normalize away before hashing, or two spellings of one computation become two identities with no cache reuse.
9. **Missing capability is conservative at the seam and fails closed at resolution.** A family with no fusion role yields no legality; an occurrence with no lowering capability stops compilation with a typed cause rather than degrading.
10. **A reservation is not a capability.** Nothing in this record is evidence of support, and a variant that only rejects is not evidence of the support it names.

## Inventory conclusions, not support decisions

1. **The signature is eight-dimensional and every dimension has an owner.** A family described by fewer than eight is underspecified against an obligation the corpus has already accepted somewhere, and the most commonly omitted one is D5's independence of accuracy from exceptional-value behaviour.
2. **Atomicity is decided by whether a composition leaves a numerical decision unowned,** not by operation size. That rule is derived from the three registered composite families and generalizes to F-30 and F-33, where `RQ-OP-08` and `RQ-OP-09` apply it.
3. **The no-promotion policy is supported by the primary sources rather than merely conservative,** because the one standard that specifies promotion leaves cross-kind promotion explicitly unspecified.
4. **Predicate tensors gate an entire group of five families plus two cases,** and no member of that group can be admitted alone. This is the single highest-leverage unblocking decision in the inventory.
5. **Data-dependent extents gate a second group,** and their blocker is a shape and allocation mechanism rather than any operation design. A compiler-facing portable IR — StableHLO — omits the whole class, which is corroboration that the difficulty is structural.
6. **Two families are pure despite reading as stateful** — scatter and counter-based random generation — and both are pure for the same reason: the state is a value. Every ecosystem spelling that reads as mutation resolves to the pure form or is out of scope.
7. **Effect, region, and non-tensor-value support are three separate reservations,** not one "advanced features" bucket. Sorting needs a region and no effect; scatter needs neither; collectives need effects and tokens; control flow needs regions and changes what determinism means.
8. **Twenty-five enumerated families have no maturity ledger row,** which is the concrete gap this record closes and the number the delivery graph should be sized against. Corrected from twenty-three on 2026-08-05; the [join table](#join-key-for-the-delivery-graph) states both reasons and the one-line check that refutes either.
9. **The intentionally-invalid list is short and mostly already enforced,** which is evidence that the semantic model is coherent rather than merely restrictive: twelve entries, of which ADR 0087 owns two, the type system owns one, and existing validators own several more.
10. **Nothing here selects, schedules, or registers anything.** The next step is not implementation; it is a delivery-graph pass that reads the join table above and files work only where a named producer and consumer exist.
