# Preserved primary sources

These records keep the documents behind Tiler's recognized dtype catalog — and, since the third wave, behind its operation taxonomy, since the fifth, behind its certified-bounds survey, and since the sixth, behind its float-to-integer conversion precedents — reproducible when an upstream URL moves, changes, or disappears. Preservation is licence-aware rather than uniform: a document whose own terms permit dissemination is vendored here byte-for-byte, and a document whose terms do not — or whose terms could not be read — keeps bibliographic identity, a retrieval fingerprint where one exists, and an official acquisition route, with no bytes checked in.

**Retrieval date for most records below: 2026-07-31.** Both of the first two preservation waves — the format-defining specifications and the ecosystem precedents added after them — were retrieved on that date. Three later additions were retrieved on **2026-08-05**: the array API standard in a third wave, the MLIR Linalg definition files in a fourth, and the verified floating-point error-analysis literature in a fifth. A sixth wave — the WebAssembly execution-semantics files, the Rust reference's operator chapter, and the C++ working draft — was retrieved on **2026-08-06**, as was the single-row seventh addition that closed the one conversion claim the sixth wave's declared population had left uncovered. Each of those records states that date again where it matters.

**On 2026-08-06 two existing rows changed classification rather than being added, and no eighth wave is declared for them.** Tom bought IEEE Std 754-2019 and Higham's *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms* and relayed both files. Neither may be vendored — both are for-sale publisher-copyrighted documents — so both rows are now `metadata-only` with a digest over the exact purchased bytes, which is the outcome each row had already named as its realistic best. **The manifest's `pending-acquisition` count is therefore back to zero**, and the row that made it one is the one this change closes.

**The fifth wave inverts this record's usual ratio, and the reason is worth stating once rather than fifteen times.** The first four waves are specifications and open-source project documentation, which are overwhelmingly vendorable; the fifth is academic papers, which are overwhelmingly published under publisher copyright with no redistribution grant. Thirteen of its fifteen rows are therefore metadata-only — twelve of them from the start, the thirteenth being the monograph that was `pending-acquisition` until it was purchased on 2026-08-06 — and the two that are vendored are vendored because each carries a Creative Commons grant in its own text rather than in a venue's reputation. That is the expected shape for a literature survey and not a gap in the acquisition — the same finding [the region-search source record](../../region-search/sources/README.md) reached independently over a different literature.

**The sixth wave is the first that mostly did not need to acquire anything, and that is its finding rather than an accident.** The record it repairs cited seven documents as bare URLs. Three of the seven were already preserved here for other reasons and had simply never been named — both LLVM claims live in `llvm-langref-llvmorg-22.1.8`, the StableHLO claim in `stablehlo-spec-v1.18.0`, the PTX claim in the metadata-only `nvidia-ptx-isa-cuda-13.3.0` — so half the repair was citation work, not acquisition, and every one of those three claims was re-checked against the bytes rather than assumed from the id's existence. The other three were acquired: WebAssembly and the Rust reference vendored under grants read in their own copies, the C++ working draft metadata-only under ISO/IEC copyright. **No source in this wave was unreachable, so it files no acquisition request** — stated explicitly because "nothing to flag" and "nobody looked" read identically in a record that only lists what it found.

**The seventh addition is one row, and what produced it is worth keeping.** The sixth wave declared its population as the seven citations its record carried, and that boundary was honest — but the record made *eight* claims, and the eighth ("PyTorch documents platform variation") had never been cited at all, in either the pre- or post-hardening list. It was found by counting claims against citations rather than by re-reading the citations, which is the check that catches an uncited claim; a citation-first sweep cannot, because an uncited claim has nothing to sweep. The claim survived acquisition — PyTorch documents it in the `Tensor.to` docstring, preserved here as `pytorch-tensor-docs-v2.13.0` — but that outcome was not the expected one: the ticket that filed the gap predicted the likelier result was correcting the sentence, and had the search come back empty the correction would have landed instead.

**Identity was checked from the document in every fifth-wave row, and this is why.** Five candidate retrievals in that wave returned an entirely unrelated paper under a guessed identifier: `arXiv:1507.03931` is a Whitney-extension theorem in `math.CA` rather than the FPTaylor conference paper, `arXiv:1612.02186` is Cherenkov-telescope instrumentation rather than FPBench, `arXiv:2206.06554` is a Minkowski inequality in `math.DG` rather than a Higham survey, MIMS EPrint 2018.31 is a face-recognition paper rather than a probabilistic rounding-error analysis, and NTRS 20180001740 is a NASA satellite-communications talk rather than PRECiSA. Every one returned HTTP 200 with a well-formed PDF, so nothing but reading the first page distinguished them from the intended document, and each was discarded rather than recorded. **No row below rests on a guessed identifier**; each names the route that produced its bytes and the field of the document that confirms it. Reproduce any of the five negatives with `curl -sL https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.03931 | pdftotext - - | head -3`.

Digests are deliberately not repeated in this file. `expected-sources.tsv` is the single authority for the expected population, each record's classification, and each SHA-256; `verify-sources.sh` enforces it. A digest written twice drifts silently, and the copy nothing checks is the one a reader would trust.

## What these records claim, and what they do not

- **Preserved source.** The bytes under this directory are an unmodified copy of what the named upstream source served for the named document at the named version. Nothing here is transcribed, reflowed, summarized, or corrected; a specification excerpt in Tiler prose is never the authority, the preserved file is. "Unmodified" is a claim about this repository, not about the delivery path: one record's byte stream carries content the publisher's CDN injects per request, and that record states the consequence for reproducing its digest.
- **Normative guarantee.** What a preserved specification requires of a conforming implementation. Preserving a document does not extend its guarantees to Tiler, nor claim Tiler implements it.
- **Tiler inference.** Every classification, alias rejection, and admission conclusion drawn from these documents lives in the research and ADR text that cites them, not here. This directory records provenance only.

Vendor documentation preserved under an open-source licence (NVIDIA Transformer Engine below) is a primary description by the format's owner, not a standards-body specification; the distinction survives the fact that both are preserved the same way.

## Population boundary

This record closes over every document the dtype research cites as a primary source, in two classes that stay distinguishable because a reader acts differently on each, plus a third class the [operation and signature taxonomy](../../semantic-graph/mature-operation-and-signature-taxonomy.md) contributed later — and [the float-to-integer conversion precedents record](../float-to-integer-conversion-precedents.md) contributed to again in the sixth wave — and a fourth [the certified-bounds record](../certified-bounds-as-rewrite-permissions.md) contributed after that.

**Format-defining sources** are the **numerical-format and quantization-semantics** specifications cited by [the mature dtype taxonomy](../mature-dtype-taxonomy.md) and by the accepted dtype ADRs 0026–0038 (the `dtypes-quantization` catalog group) through their evidence documents — documents that *define* a format's value set, encoding, or quantization contract. Losing one of these would leave an admitted Tiler identity without its normative definition.

**Ecosystem precedents** are cited by the same documents for how an existing system *spells*, *exposes*, or *governs* a format rather than for what a format means: PyTorch, NumPy, JAX, Arrow, the GCC manual's decimal float extension, SPIR-V, WGSL, and the NVIDIA TensorRT accuracy guide, joined by two namespace-governance precedents — the StableHLO compatibility page and the MLIR language reference. None of them defines a value set, so losing one cannot invalidate an admitted identity; they carry the alias-resolution and naming evidence instead — that `complex64` means different things in different ecosystems, that a PyTorch shell dtype proves representation can precede arithmetic — which is why they are worth preserving rather than citing from a live URL.

**Operation-semantics sources** are cited for what an *operation* means rather than for what a format means: the Python array API standard, added in the third wave, the MLIR Linalg definition files added in the fourth, and the float-to-integer conversion precedents added in the sixth — the WebAssembly execution semantics, the Rust reference's operator chapter, and the C++ working draft, cited by [the float-to-integer conversion precedents record](../float-to-integer-conversion-precedents.md) for how an existing system totalizes a conversion whose mathematical result does not fit. That record is the `evidence` document for ADRs 0010 and 0041, so what those three documents carry is the *contrast set* the four Tiler conversion families are separated against: a trapping form, a saturating form, a NaN-to-zero form, and an undefined form are four different answers to one question, and the argument that they are genuinely four rather than one with variations is not re-derivable without them. Their three companions in that record's citation list needed no new rows, because the wave found them already preserved for other reasons — both LLVM claims in `llvm-langref-llvmorg-22.1.8`, the StableHLO claim in `stablehlo-spec-v1.18.0`, the PTX claim in the metadata-only `nvidia-ptx-isa-cuda-13.3.0`. Neither defines a value set Tiler admits, and neither defines a numerical format. The array API standard carries function inventories, a type-promotion chapter, and the chapter notes where the standard declines to specify a behaviour — and it is those refusals, not its definitions, that the operation taxonomy leans on; losing it would leave the no-promotion policy's strongest supporting evidence resting on a live URL. The Linalg files carry the *form* a structured operation takes — an iteration domain connected to its operands by affine maps held in an attribute, with the destination operand part of the signature — which is what the taxonomy's contraction and windowed-reduction rows are argued against; losing them would return that argument to a rendered page that tracks the project's main branch, which is the state this record's fourth wave existed to end. The seventh addition, `pytorch-tensor-docs-v2.13.0`, joins this class for the same reason and puts PyTorch in two classes at once: its three older rows are ecosystem *spelling and exposure* precedents, and this one is cited for what a *conversion* does, which is an operation claim. The two are kept apart deliberately — a reader who took the ecosystem rows as covering conversion behaviour would be wrong, and this record's own gap is the proof that they do not.

**Error-analysis sources** are cited for what a *rounding error* is bounded by rather than for what a format or an operation means, and they arrived in the fifth wave with [the certified-bounds record](../certified-bounds-as-rewrite-permissions.md). They split in two. The **tooling** group — FPTaylor, FPTuner, Gappa and its Coq pairing, Daisy, Rosa, Anton, Herbie, Precimonious, HiFPTuner, and Real2Float — is what that record's admission-rule elimination is argued against: each row is preserved for what its tool bounds, what it refuses, and whether its result is a checkable certificate or a trusted analyzer's word. The **classical analysis** group — the Acta Numerica survey, Jeannerod and Rump's inner-product bounds, and the Higham monograph the survey itself defers to — is what the record's worked online-softmax bound is *derived from*, so losing it would leave a `sound-proof` claim resting on a remembered constant. One further row, the online-softmax paper, defines the algorithm the worked example is about and belongs to neither group. None of these documents defines a value set or an operation, and none is normative for Tiler in any degree; they are the evidence a derivation is checked against.

Keeping all four classes in one manifest is deliberate: the check's value comes from a single declared population, and separate manifests would each be able to agree with themselves. A further wave of citations extends this record and restates the boundary here rather than starting a second one — which is what the fourth wave did by joining the operation-semantics class, what the fifth did by opening a class of its own because no existing one described it, and what the sixth did by joining the operation-semantics class again rather than opening a "conversion" one, since a conversion is an operation and a fifth class would have split the population along a line no reader acts on. The directory's name predates the third wave and is now considerably narrower than its contents; the manifest and this boundary section are the authority on what the population is, not the path.

Apple Metal language and capability evidence stays under [its own record](../../apple-targets/sources/README.md), and the optimizer-search literature under [the region-search record](../../region-search/sources/README.md). Nothing here duplicates a row from the region-search manifest, which matters because both records reach the rewriting literature at their edges and were assembled independently — reproduce with `comm -12 <(grep -v '^#' docs/research/numerics/sources/expected-sources.tsv | cut -f1 | sort) <(grep -v '^#' docs/research/region-search/sources/expected-sources.tsv | cut -f1 | sort)`, which prints nothing. The Apple record carries no manifest of its own, so there is no comparable check to run against it and none is claimed.

Two ids may legitimately share a digest: `jax-license-v0.11.0` and `onnx-license-v1.22.0` are both the stock Apache-2.0 text. The check requires unique ids, not unique digests, which is why that agreement is a consistency signal rather than a failure.

Adding or refreshing a source means updating the manifest row, the counts declared at the top of `verify-sources.sh`, and the record above in the same change.

`.gitattributes` here marks the preserved files `-text -whitespace -diff`. The first two protect the bytes: end-of-line conversion on a checkout would silently break every recorded digest, and `git diff --check` would otherwise report trailing whitespace that belongs to the upstream document and must not be removed.

**`-diff` arrived with the fifth wave on 2026-08-05, and it is prophylactic here rather than remedial — which is the honest description and not the one first written.** Git's content heuristic renders some PDFs as text, and in [the region-search source record](../../region-search/sources/README.md) one vendored document did exactly that, arriving as 3 052 added lines in review; that record added the attribute to stop it. **Neither PDF this wave adds triggers the heuristic.** Reproduce with `git -c core.attributesfile=/dev/null diff --numstat --no-index /dev/null docs/research/numerics/sources/acta-numerica-fp-2023/floating-point-arithmetic.pdf`, which answers `-	-` for a binary file rather than a line count; the same check answers `-	-` for the Daisy chapter and for both PDFs this directory already held. The attribute is set anyway so the next document vendored here cannot reintroduce the problem silently, and the reasoning is recorded rather than the outcome so that a future reader does not infer a failure that did not occur. It never reaches the stored bytes either way: every blob is checked against its manifest digest.

The record's own four files re-enable all three attributes by name, `diff` included, so this file's own prose stays reviewable — which is the opposite of what the glob's `-diff` is for.

An upstream revision that changes a semantic fact is not handled by refreshing bytes. Refreshing a preserved document updates evidence only; changing what Tiler recognizes requires an explicit contract or ADR review.
