# CPU vector realization facts

Every claim labelled **Fact** is inspected source in this repository at base commit `cb5d86a` or a primary vendor specification. The schedule half of this design — what a lane binding may bind, what its verifier obligations are, and what it lowers to — is [the CPU vector-lane tier](../scheduling/cpu-vector-lane-tier.md). This record owns the target-declaration half and answers one question: what a CPU target profile declares about its vector units, with what provenance, at which availability phase, and how much of it a feasibility authority can settle without executing backend code.

## Conclusion

**A vector realization is one atomic subject matched by equality, exactly as a synchronization realization is, and for the same reason.** It is not a quantitative capability axis, and the bound comparison a capability axis would apply to lane width is not merely a poor fit but actively unsound. **Fixed-width ISA presence is a compile-target attribution at `AvailabilityPhase::CompileProfile`**, sourced from the vendor's architecture specification under `FactAuthority::ExternalProfile`, and runtime feature detection never enters feasibility at all — it lives in the runtime's existing device-free variant-eligibility filter, which the bounded scalar CPU vertical already exercised end to end. **The whole feasibility surface is therefore answerable with no backend code executed, for both binding kinds**, because the concrete scalable vector length is a cost fact and no legality predicate consumes it. **The lane tier consequently does not trigger the missing host-process availability phase**, which is a deliberate negative result: the seam the scalar vertical named is real, but this is not what fires it.

## Why the fact is atomic, and why it is not a capability axis

**Fact.** `CapabilityAxis` is explicitly the *quantitative* space: "every axis has a `u64` bound, a `Quantity` unit, and a comparison `Relation`", and the module states why numerical behaviour is deliberately excluded — "a bound comparison can report whether an obligation is met and never by what means, and the means is what an emulated dimension's emitted operations depend on" (`crates/tiler-compiler/src/target/feasibility.rs`). The synchronization predicate is excluded on the parallel ground recorded on the feasibility rule-set key: "the new predicate is not a quantitative axis, because a subject is matched by equality and has no bound to compare".

**A lane width looks quantitative and is not.** The relation a capability axis would give it is `CapabilityRelation::AtMost`, and `satisfies(AtMost, required, available)` is `required <= available`. Under it, a schedule requiring 4 `f32` lanes would be admitted by a profile declaring 8. **That is unsound.** Realizing 8-lane arithmetic is not realizing 4-lane arithmetic: whether the ISA has a narrower form of the same operation is a separate architectural fact, whether the narrower form has the same numerical behaviour is a separate fact again, and on a scalable ISA at a given implemented length there may be no fixed narrower form to select at all. `Exact` fares no better, because it makes width the only dimension and says nothing about masking, about the operation class, or about the numerical row. The unsoundness is the disproof; the poor fit is only the symptom.

**And the dimensions must be matched together, which is the whole content of atomicity.** The argument is `DeclaredSynchronizationRealization`'s, transferred: "each of its five dimensions is separately true of some realization on some machine… so a profile declaring them independently would let their conjunction be inferred from facts none of which is about it." A concrete counterexample exists in this space and it is the one that settles it. **Fact — Arm A-profile A64 ISA, DDI 0602 2026-06.** AArch64 Advanced SIMD realizes 4-lane `f32` packed arithmetic and provides no predicated or masked vector load; the Advanced SIMD instruction list contains 337 distinct mnemonics and none has a predicate, mask, or fault-suppression role. **Fact — Intel SDM Volume 2, 325383-085US.** `VMASKMOVPS` is a fault-suppressing masked load for 32-bit elements, under the `AVX` feature flag rather than `AVX2`. **Fact — DDI 0602 2026-06.** Arm SVE provides `FADDA`, a strictly-ordered horizontal `f32` add reduction. Three separate true statements about three different machines; a profile declaring "4-lane f32 arithmetic", "masked load", and "ordered horizontal reduction" as independent rows would let a schedule needing all three be admitted by a target that has each of them in isolation and none of them together. A NEON profile declaring the first and, from some unrelated row, the second, would admit a predicated tail that NEON cannot execute.

**The dimension list is not this record's to invent, and saying so is what keeps the proposal narrow.** ADR 0043's accepted text already fixes it: "vector legality depends on operation, dtype, masks, address space, width, and alignment." Six dimensions, decided. What ADR 0043 does *not* decide is whether they are six independently declarable facts or one indivisible one, and that — together with the lane shape the same sentence separately requires by deciding that "fixed and scalable vector shapes are distinct" — is the whole of what this record proposes.

**Proposal.** The declared subject is one value with no per-dimension accessor and no per-dimension declaration method, mirroring `declare_synchronization_realization`'s deliberate absence of "no `declare_barrier_execution_scope`, no `declare_fenced_spaces`". Its dimensions, which are ADR 0043's list with the shape distinction its own sentence requires:

| Dimension | Content | Why it must be inside the subject rather than beside it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lane shape | fixed with a literal lane count, or scalable | A fixed realization and a scalable one are different instruction families; a target may have either, both, or neither, and neither implies the other. |
| Element arithmetic type | an `ArithmeticType` | Subnormal behaviour is already measurably per-dtype rather than per-target, which is why every honourability key in the workspace carries one; a packed row inherits that requirement unchanged. |
| Operation class | lane arithmetic, masked load, masked store, gather, or ordered horizontal accumulate | The NEON counterexample above is exactly a target that realizes one class and not another at the same shape and dtype. |
| Masking | unmasked only, store-masked, or fault-suppressing masked access | The dimension that decides whether a predicated tail is legal, and the one no bound can express. |
| Address space | a governed `AddressSpace` | A masked load from device memory and one from a private staging allocation are different realizations, and the kernel IR already keys every buffer by address space. |
| Alignment requirement | the alignment the access requires, in bytes | ADR 0043 decides that "instruction alignment" is a hard requirement rather than an estimate, and the adopted scheduled-region model separates the three failure classes: "A failed alignment guard is an applicability miss; an unsupported vector operation is hard target infeasibility; a low estimated benefit is only a cost rejection." |

**Two-valued, not a presence marker.** The verdict is `Realized` or `Unrealizable`, following `SynchronizationRealization`'s stated reason: "a measured negative is a fact worth recording, and a profile that could only stay silent about what it cannot do would make 'unsupported' and 'unmeasured' one state. They are not — the first is a typed rejection a caller can act on, the second is `FeasibilityOutcome::Unknown`." A NEON profile must be able to *say* that it does not realize a masked load, so that a predicated schedule is `Rejected` with a reason rather than `Unknown`.

**No deferred query path, and this is a decision rather than an omission.** `resolve_synchronization` has no `Later` arm, and its recorded reason transfers verbatim: there is no query vocabulary in which a runtime can be asked "do you realize a fault-suppressing 8-lane `f32` masked load from device memory at 4-byte alignment", so "deferring without a query contract would be a promise nothing can keep, so the unresolved case is `Unknown` and fails closed". A profile that does not speak to a subject leaves it `Unknown`, which is what makes an unenumerated realization fail closed instead of defaulting to supported.

## Provenance and availability phase

### Fixed-width ISA presence is a compile-target attribution, and there is no other kind

**The compile target is the authority.** A build is compiled for an exact target triple with an exact feature set, and that pair is what licenses an instruction. The corresponding vocabulary already exists and needs no widening: `FactAuthority::ExternalProfile` is "a named external producer's normative target-family declaration… available at the compile-profile phase but is not a compiler proof. Its source record carries both the producer identity and the versioned specification or guarantee it relies on" — which is exactly a vendor architecture reference manual cited by version. `FactValidityScope::PortableProfile` scopes the claim to the declared target family rather than to a host. The phase is `AvailabilityPhase::CompileProfile`, and `authority_matches_phase` already binds the two.

This is the same shape the accepted Metal compile profile uses for its own architectural rows, where the buffer-binding limit and the 64-bit-arithmetic row are `CompileProfile` facts sourced from Apple's feature-set tables and scoped to a named family. **Inference.** A CPU profile's ISA rows are not a new authority class; they are the existing `ExternalProfile` class pointed at a different vendor's specification. The one thing that must not happen is a row sourced from the *host the compiler is running on*, which would attribute a claim about the compile target to an unrelated machine.

**A row that is mandatory in the base architecture takes `GovernedProfile` instead**, which is "a governed, conservative compile-time profile guarantee". A profile keyed on an AArch64 triple may declare its Advanced SIMD rows this way, because the base architecture requires them; a profile keyed on an x86-64 triple may not declare AVX2 this way, because AVX2 is an extension the triple alone does not imply, and it needs an `ExternalProfile` row naming the feature the build was compiled with.

**Numerical rows are measured, and are a different authority from the architectural ones.** Whether a target's packed `f32` arithmetic preserves subnormals is a `MeasuredProfile` fact at `FactValidityScope::MeasuredEnvironment`, exactly as the workspace's existing numerical honourability rows are — and the scalar CPU vertical already demonstrated the pattern for the scalar unit, measuring "the subnormal behaviour of its actual floating-point arithmetic" and refusing "a route whose declared realization this process does not deliver". **The architectural row and the numerical row are two facts about one unit and must not be collapsed:** an ISA manual states that a packed multiply exists, and it does not state what a given implementation does with a subnormal operand under a given floating-point control state.

### Runtime feature detection never enters feasibility

**Elimination.** *Feasibility asks the host at compile time* is eliminated immediately: the compile host is not the execution host, and a cross-compiled artifact would be assessed against the wrong machine.

*Feasibility defers the ISA-presence predicate to a later phase and a runtime query answers it* is eliminated on two grounds. First, it needs a query contract for a predicate that has none, for the reason recorded above. Second and decisively, **the mechanism it would duplicate already exists and is already device-free.** A build declares one profile per ISA level, compiles a variant against each, and packages them in one artifact; the loader filters variants against what the host execution context states it can execute, before any applicability guard is evaluated. **Fact.** The bounded scalar CPU vertical exercised exactly this path and perturbed it: a host stating it executes `metallib` and a host consuming `tiler.cpu.scalar-image-v2` are both refused as `runtime.no-eligible-variant` with `VariantIneligibility::UnsupportedRepresentation` naming the backend and representation the host stated, and each probe asserts the exclusion's fields rather than only its class. **Fact.** That same vertical carried "**zero** deferred prepared-entry predicates" and completed on `preflight` alone, because "this profile declares its workgroup bound as an available compile-time fact rather than a prepared-pipeline query".

**Inference.** Host feature detection is variant *eligibility*, not feasibility. Feasibility asks whether a schedule is realizable by a declared target; eligibility asks whether this host is that target. Putting CPUID into feasibility would answer the second question with the first question's machinery, and would make a compile-time authority depend on a runtime observation it has no phase for.

**And this is a different mechanism from the backend-scoped route requirement, which must not be confused with it.** [Backend-scoped route-requirement answers](../runtime/backend-scoped-route-requirement-answers.md) derives that a CPU ISA row travels as a `Feature(bool)` decided by the owning backend, that "an ISA probe is `std::arch::is_aarch64_feature_detected!`, a macro over a string literal the backend can hold itself, so a CPU backend's observation function takes no callback at all", and that such a row costs a borrowed availability phase and forces the `prepare` path. That is a route requirement carried on a *packaged variant* and answered at load time — a third mechanism beside feasibility and eligibility, and the right one for a qualitative live-host fact a backend wants to gate on. **The lane tier needs none of the three beyond the first two**, because a variant compiled for a declared ISA either is eligible on this host or is not, and nothing about its lanes remains to be asked afterwards.

### What the feasibility surface can answer without executing backend code: all of it

Walk every legality predicate the lane tier raises and ask what it needs.

| Predicate | What it needs | Executed backend code? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The lane arithmetic is realizable at this shape, dtype, and address space | one declared subject, matched by equality | none |
| A predicated tail's masked load is realizable | one declared subject whose masking dimension is fault-suppressing | none |
| A scalar-epilogue schedule has one numerical realization | a row covering every path that realizes its arithmetic — one row where a target governs both paths together, two where it does not | none |
| The region's declared numerical dimensions are honoured by the path that executes them | the existing per-dimension honourability resolution | none |
| Coverage and ownership under a fixed lane width | intrinsic verification with a literal width | none, and not a target question at all |
| Coverage under a scalable lane width | the width-agnostic strip-mining relation | none, and the concrete length is never read |
| Contributor coverage under a lane partition | `ContributorPartition::covers`, refused outright for a scalable width | none |
| Alignment | the declared alignment requirement, composed as a guard | none |

**Inference, and it is the answer to the ticket's fourth question.** Every row is settled from a declared profile at `CompileProfile`. Nothing in the lane tier requires a live device, a prepared pipeline, a bound host process, or an executed probe. That is a stronger property than the Metal profile has — the Metal profile cannot state its workgroup capacity without a prepared pipeline, because "only a built pipeline knows its own `maxTotalThreadsPerThreadgroup`" — and it is the same property the scalar CPU vertical already demonstrated for the scalar tier and inferred as a general shape: "the `prepare`/`resolve_target_properties` stage is correctly *optional* rather than universal, and a provider contract must not make it mandatory." The lane tier extends that property rather than spending it.

### The negative result: the lane tier does not fire the host-process phase

**Fact.** `AvailabilityPhase` has five variants — `CompileProfile`, `ArtifactEvidence`, `LiveDevicePreflight`, `PreparedKernelPreflight`, `LaunchPreflight` — and none names a bound host *process*. **Fact.** The scalar CPU vertical's finding 5 records that "a fact known once a *host process* is bound has to borrow `LiveDevicePreflight`. This is a naming seam rather than a functional one today, and it becomes a real one the moment a profile wants to distinguish 'known once a device exists' from 'known once a process exists'." **Fact.** `name-a-host-process-availability-phase` is an existing open ticket owning that gap.

**The obvious reading is that a scalable vector length fires it, and that reading is wrong.** A scalable length is a value with no compile-time answer, readable only once a process exists, so it looks like exactly the fact the missing phase is for. But the schedule half of this design establishes that no legality predicate consumes the number: a scalable schedule's coverage is a width-agnostic relation, its tail is unconditional predication rather than a compile-time remainder, and it is refused from binding a contributor partition precisely because that would need the number. **A value used only to estimate a trip count and rank a candidate is a cost fact, and cost facts do not enter the phased feasibility model at all** — ADR 0043's whole separation is that "a cost estimate can neither prove nor disprove feasibility".

**So the trigger stays unfired, and stating that is worth as much as firing it would be.** The phase becomes necessary when a CPU profile needs a *value* — not a presence — observed from a process, for a *legality* predicate. The lane tier deliberately has none. The backend-scoped route-requirement record's ISA row does borrow the phase, and that record already carries the deferral and names the owning ticket; nothing here should file a second one.

## Illustrative rows, in the authority-ledger form

These are the shape a real CPU profile's rows take. They are **Proposal**, not a declared profile: no profile is constructed by this record, and each row's per-implementation numerical half is deliberately left to measurement.

### AArch64 Advanced SIMD — 4-lane `f32` arithmetic, device address space

- **Owner:** the vector realization subject; shape fixed at 4 lanes, `ArithmeticType::F32`, operation class lane arithmetic, masking unmasked-only, address space `Device`.
- **Verdict:** `Realized`.
- **Authority:** Fact — `GovernedProfile`. Advanced SIMD is part of the base AArch64 architecture, so a profile keyed on an AArch64 triple may declare it as a conservative compile-time guarantee without naming a feature flag.
- **Validity:** `PortableProfile`, scoped to the declared triple.
- **Phase:** `CompileProfile`.

### AArch64 Advanced SIMD — masked load

- **Owner:** the same shape and dtype, operation class masked load, masking fault-suppressing.
- **Verdict:** **`Unrealizable`.**
- **Authority:** Fact — `ExternalProfile`, from the Arm architecture reference manual. AArch64 Advanced SIMD has no predicate register file and no predicated load; partial access is done with narrower or single-element load forms, which is a different operation with a different address computation and is not a mask.
- **Why the row exists at all.** Without it, a predicated schedule against a NEON profile is `Unknown` and fails closed with no reason. With it, the schedule is `Rejected` and the explanation names the realization the target does not have — which is what lets a planner select a scalar-epilogue schedule instead of reporting that it could not decide.

### AVX — 8-lane `f32` masked load

- **Owner:** shape fixed at 8 lanes, `ArithmeticType::F32`, operation class masked load, masking fault-suppressing, address space `Device`.
- **Verdict:** `Realized`.
- **Authority:** Fact — `ExternalProfile`, from the Intel SDM Volume 2 (325383-085US) page for `VMASKMOVPS`: "Faults will not occur due to referencing any memory location if the corresponding mask bit for that memory location is 0." **The row is licensed by the feature the build was compiled with, not by the triple**, so a profile declaring it must name the feature in its own key and its source record must cite the compile-target feature set.
- **The feature is `AVX`, not `AVX2`, and getting this wrong is a live hazard rather than pedantry.** The SDM's CPUID Feature Flag column reads `AVX` for all four `VMASKMOVPS`/`VMASKMOVPD` forms; only the integer siblings `VPMASKMOVD`/`VPMASKMOVQ` are `AVX2`. A profile declaring this row under `AVX2` would refuse an AVX-only target that realizes it, and — worse in the other direction — a profile that reasoned "we compiled for AVX2, so masked loads are available" would be right by accident and wrong the moment the element type became integer.
- **Validity:** `PortableProfile`, scoped to the declared feature level.
- **Phase:** `CompileProfile`.
- **Two further caveats the same SDM page states, and a profile must not silently drop:** "In cases where mask bits indicate data should not be loaded or stored paging A and D bits will be set in an implementation dependent way", and "VMASKMOV should not be used to access memory mapped I/O and un-cached memory". Neither affects a device-memory tensor buffer, which is why the row is `Realized` and not conditioned — but a profile widening the address-space dimension would have to revisit both.
- **Eliminated:** declaring it from a triple alone (an x86-64 triple does not imply AVX); declaring it from a host CPUID observation taken in the compiling process (the compile host is not the execution host).

### Arm SVE — scalable `f32` lane arithmetic with predication

- **Owner:** shape scalable, `ArithmeticType::F32`, operation class lane arithmetic and, as a second subject, masked load; masking fault-suppressing.
- **Verdict:** `Realized`, as two subjects and not one — the second sentence of the atomicity argument, instantiated.
- **Authority:** Fact — `ExternalProfile`, from DDI 0602 2026-06 and DDI 0487 M.c: SVE has 16 governing predicate registers `P0`–`P15` (with most encodings restricting the *governing* predicate to `P0`–`P7`), predicated loads whose inactive elements "will not cause a read from Device memory or signal a fault, and are set to zero in the destination vector", and `WHILELT` to generate a predicate from a loop counter against a bound.
- **Phase:** `CompileProfile`.
- **The implemented vector length is deliberately not a row.** DDI 0487 M.c §B1.4.2 fixes the architectural set as "all powers of two from 128 to 2048 bits inclusive" — narrowed from H.a's "multiple of 128 bits", which is the wording still in wide circulation — and the value is read at run time through `RDVL` or `CNTB`. No legality predicate here consumes it. Adding it as a fact would create a compile-time claim about a value the compile target does not fix.

### Any target — the packed numerical row

- **Owner:** the numerical honourability vocabulary, keyed by dimension and `ArithmeticType`.
- **Authority:** Measurement, `MeasuredProfile`, `MeasuredEnvironment`, tied to an exact toolchain, flag set, and floating-point control state.
- **Whether the key must gain an execution-path component is a narrower question than it first appeared, and the narrowing is a correction to this record's own first draft.** The scalar-epilogue tail policy requires that every path realizing the region's arithmetic honour the region's one declared realization, and the obvious worry is that a target's packed path and its scalar path differ. **Fact — Arm ARM DDI 0487 M.c §A1.5.6.** On AArch64 the flush-to-zero rules name no scalar-versus-Advanced-SIMD distinction at all; `FPCR.FZ` and `FPCR.FIZ` govern scalar FP, Advanced SIMD, and SVE alike. The "Advanced SIMD always flushes" rule is **AArch32's** (§E1.3.5.1). **Fact — Intel SDM Volume 1 §11.5.1.** "SIMD floating-point exceptions are those exceptions that can be generated by SSE/SSE2/SSE3 instructions that operate on packed **or scalar** floating-point operands", under one MXCSR with one `FTZ` and one `DAZ` bit.
- **So on both ISAs examined the two paths share one control, and the divergences that do exist are per-instruction rather than per-class:** on AArch64, `FPCR.AH == 1` makes the reciprocal-estimate family flush unconditionally regardless of `FZ`, the min/max family and `FABS`/`FNEG` are exempt from output flushing, and half-precision has its own `FZ16`; on x86, x87 has no `FTZ`/`DAZ` at all, so a toolchain routing scalar arithmetic through x87 has a never-flushing path beside flushing SSE ones. A target profile still cannot *state* any of that, because the current key has no path component — but the case for widening it now rests on those instances rather than on a class distinction that does not exist.
- **Boundary.** No such measurement is taken here, and the paragraphs above are specification reading rather than observation of any machine — a manual saying one control governs both paths is not evidence that a given implementation obeys it. The scalar CPU vertical measured one process's scalar behaviour on one host in one interval and re-measures per run rather than caching; a packed row needs the same discipline and the same disclaimer.

## Public-boundary items, enumerated for Tom and not self-accepted

1. **A `VectorRealizationSubject` type and its six dimensions**, its `Realized`/`Unrealizable` verdict, and the declaration method that takes the whole subject as one argument.
2. **Where the subject lives.** `SynchronizationSubject` is in `tiler-ir`'s schedule module because the schedule derives it; a vector subject is derived from a schedule the same way and would follow, which puts a target-facing vocabulary in the IR crate exactly as the precedent already does.
3. **Whether the numerical honourability key gains an execution-path component**, which is a *question* for Tom rather than a proposal, because the class-level divergence that would have forced it is refuted on both ISAs examined and only per-instruction instances survive. If it is taken, it changes a governed descriptor and therefore steps `PROFILE_DESCRIPTOR_DOMAIN`, and changes the vocabulary the feasibility rules range over and therefore mints a new `GOVERNED_FEASIBILITY_RULE_SET_KEY` rather than bumping its revision — both by the rules those constants' own documentation states. Not taking it means a scalar-epilogue schedule is admitted on the strength of one row covering both paths, which is true on AArch64 and on x86-SSE/AVX and is an unstated assumption everywhere else.
4. **`ResourceRequirements` gains a derived vector requirement**, `Option<VectorRealizationSubject>`-shaped, whose `None` is the canonical absence a non-vector schedule derives — no requirement, no query, no explain row, no artifact field — following the synchronization field's stated discipline.
5. **Whether a target may declare more than one subject at one phase**, and what the canonical sort and uniqueness key is. The synchronization fact keys on `(subject, phase)` and deliberately excludes the verdict so that a contradiction cannot coexist; a vector fact needs the same treatment and the same reasoning.
6. **Whether `docs/backends/cpu.md` moves off proposed.** This record answers its vector-legality paragraph and answers none of its threading or cache paragraphs. That file is `contracts/artifacts`.

## Deferrals, each with the evidence that would close it and a trigger

- **Do packed and scalar floating-point arithmetic ever resolve a numerical dimension differently on one machine?** **Partly answered, and the answer narrowed the question.** Not as a class, on either ISA examined: AArch64's `FPCR.FZ`/`FIZ` and x86's `MXCSR.FTZ`/`DAZ` each govern both paths. What survives is per-instruction — the `FPCR.AH`-governed reciprocal-estimate family, the min/max exemptions, `FZ16`, and x87's absence of `FTZ`/`DAZ` entirely. Closes with a *measurement* on one target confirming that the specification's uniformity is the implementation's, across the subnormal, signed-zero, and NaN-canonicalization dimensions under a fixed control state, since a manual is not an observation. Trigger: the first scalar-epilogue schedule proposed for a real CPU profile.
- **Does the `ZCR_EL1.LEN` encoding matter to anything here?** SVE's architectural length set narrowed from "any multiple of 128 bits" (DDI 0487 H.a) to "any power of two from 128 to 2048" (M.c), while the register encoding still reads `(LEN+1)*128`. Nothing in this design consumes the length, so nothing depends on which reading is right — but a cost model that enumerates candidate lengths would, and it should read M.c rather than the widely repeated older wording. Closes with the first cost model that enumerates lengths. Trigger: scalable-length calibration.
- **Does a gather need its own subject or is it an operation class of the existing one?** It is listed as an operation class above, which is the cheaper answer; a gather also carries an index element type and an index width that no other class has. Closes by attempting to state a gather subject without adding a dimension. Trigger: the first lane-partition schedule under a permutation-forbidding contract, which is the one that needs a gather.
- **Is the alignment requirement a subject dimension or a separate applicability guard?** The adopted scheduled-region model puts a failed alignment guard in a different class from an unsupported operation, which argues for separating them, while ADR 0043 lists both instruction alignment and alignment-dependent vector legality among the hard requirements, which argues for one predicate; carrying it in the subject argues that a target realizing an unaligned access and one requiring alignment are different realizations. Closes with a target that realizes the same operation at two alignments. Trigger: the first profile declaring an alignment-sensitive access.
- **Does a CPU profile ever need a value observed from a bound process for a legality predicate?** The lane tier has none, which is why the host-process phase stays unfired here. Closes with such a predicate. Trigger: any CPU legality predicate whose right-hand side is a number no compile target fixes.

## Measurement boundary and unsupported cases

- **No profile is constructed and no measurement is taken.** Every row above is a Proposal about the shape a declaration takes, and every architectural claim is a vendor specification rather than an observation of any machine.
- **The architectural rows are claims about instruction sets, not about hosts.** Whether a host implements an extension, and what its floating-point control state is when a region executes, are runtime observations belonging to the execution context, which the scalar CPU vertical re-measures per run rather than caching — "a process can change its own floating-point control state after the probe, so the binding is evidence about that interval".
- **`f32` only, and the per-dtype requirement is why.** Every row names one `ArithmeticType`, and a row for one dtype states nothing about another; the workspace already carries that requirement on every honourability key because subnormal behaviour was measured to differ per dtype on one Apple row.
- **No cost model and no calibration.** Preferred width, cache fit, register pressure, spills, task granularity, and oversubscription are cost facts and appear nowhere here.
- **Three ISAs, chosen to make the atomicity argument decidable.** They are not a survey. A fourth ISA could realize a combination none of them does, which is the argument for the subject being atomic rather than evidence against it.
- **Where the ISA claims were read, and two that were refuted.** Arm claims from the A-profile A64 ISA (DDI 0602, version 2026-06) and the Architecture Reference Manual (DDI 0487 M.c, with H.a consulted for the superseded wording); x86 claims from the Intel SDM Volume 2 (325383-085US) and Volume 1 (253665-078US). **This record's first draft asserted two things that are wrong and are corrected in place:** that `VMASKMOVPS` is an AVX2 instruction, when its CPUID feature flag is `AVX`; and that a packed-versus-scalar numerical divergence was a class distinction a profile must always state, when on both ISAs examined one control governs both paths and only per-instruction divergences survive. The second correction is why item 3 of the public-boundary list is a question rather than a proposal.
