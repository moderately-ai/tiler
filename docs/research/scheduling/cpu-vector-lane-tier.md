# The CPU vector-lane tier

Every repository claim here is read at base commit `cb5d86a`, and every claim labelled **Fact** is either inspected source in this repository — cited by symbol, which is the durable reference — or a primary vendor specification cited by document and version. Claims are labelled **Fact**, **Inference**, **Proposal**, and **Measurement**; there is no Measurement in this record, and that absence is itself a boundary — nothing here is timed, executed, or emitted, and the ticket's non-goals forbid all three. Three ISA claims an earlier draft made were checked against primary manuals and found wrong; they are corrected in place and listed under the measurement boundary rather than removed, because a reader who believes one of them is better served by seeing it refuted than by never seeing it.

The target-declaration half of the design — what a CPU profile says about its vector units, with what provenance and at which availability phase — is [CPU vector realization facts](../target-profiles/cpu-vector-realization-facts.md). This record owns the schedule half and cites that one where a worked example needs a target verdict. They are two records because they are two authorities, which is the split the workgroup tier already took: the dataflow and its obligations live in `tiler-ir` (`crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/cooperative.rs`, `synchronization.rs`) and the target's verdict on them lives in `tiler-compiler` (`DeclaredSynchronizationRealization`).

## Conclusion

**A CPU vector lane may bind the map or the contributor partition, and never the combine order.** Binding the map — one lane per output coordinate — consumes no numerical permission at all, so a strict-order fold *is* vectorizable, across its outputs, under a contract that forbids reassociation outright. Binding the partition — lanes splitting one output's contributor sequence — is the same construct `MultiPass` and `CooperativeWorkgroup` already are and consumes reassociation exactly as they do; a strided lane assignment additionally consumes permutation, which forces the numerically legal layout to be the cache-hostile one. The third case, a vector load feeding an order-preserving horizontal accumulate, changes nothing the schedule owns and must therefore get no schedule construct at all.

`FixedVectorLane` and `ScalableVectorLane` do **not** share one answer, and the difference is not the spelling of the width. A fixed width is a literal, a tail is a compile-time quantity, and each of the four tail policies is available to the case it fits. A scalable width is symbolic, there is no compile-time tail so predication is the only policy, and the contributor-coverage obligation is a product over an unknown — so a scalable lane may bind the map and may *not* bind the partition, under any contract. The CPU worker-thread scope stays explicitly out, with a trigger. Lowering needs new kernel-IR constructs, enumerated and deferred.

## The line this tier turns on: map, partition, combine order

A `KernelSchedule` owns two things a lane binding can touch and one it does not.

- **The map** — which execution instance evaluates which output coordinate. In the adopted scheduled-region model this is `work_assignment`; in the implemented vocabulary it is `ExecutionBinding` together with the `OwnershipProof` that each output position has exactly one writer (`OwnershipProofKind::OneGlobalInvocationPerOutput`).
- **The partition** — how one output position's declared contributor sequence is split across execution instances. In the implemented vocabulary this is `ContributorPartition`, and `ReductionTopology::MultiPass` and `ReductionTopology::CooperativeWorkgroup` are its two realizations: the first splits across a dispatch boundary, the second across one workgroup's invocations.
- **The combine order within one partition** — which the schedule does *not* own. It is fixed by the region's `ContributorOrder` and by the registered operation's own fold-order fact. **Fact.** `tiler::strict-serial-sum-f32@1` declares `SERIAL_SUM_F32_FACT_FOLD_ORDER` as `"strict-left-fold"` and `SERIAL_SUM_F32_FACT_ACCUMULATION` as `"binary32-each-step"` (`crates/tiler-ir/src/semantic/registry.rs`, the `strict_serial_sum_f32_op` registration). **Fact.** `tiler::strict-tensor-contraction-f32@1` (since [ADR 0112](../../decisions/0112-replace-the-strict-contraction-key-with-a-permission-indexed-successor.md): `tiler::tensor-contraction-f32@1`, same contributor-sequence fact) declares `CONTRACTION_F32_FACT_CONTRIBUTOR_SEQUENCE` as `"ascending-lexicographic-over-the-canonically-ordered-contracted-index-space"`, `CONTRACTION_F32_FACT_SEED` as `"none-the-accumulator-starts-at-the-first-product"`, and both `CONTRACTION_F32_FACT_REASSOCIATION_PERMITTED` and `CONTRACTION_F32_FACT_PERMUTATION_PERMITTED` as `false` (`crates/tiler-ir/src/semantic/contraction.rs`, `contraction_f32_facts`).

A lane binding is therefore exactly one of three things, and only two of them are schedule facts.

1. **A lane over the map.** Lane `v` owns output coordinate `base + v` and evaluates the region's whole scalar program for it, including — for a reduction — its own complete fold over the full contributor sequence in the declared order.
2. **A lane over the partition.** Lane `v` accumulates a subsequence of one output's contributors, and a horizontal step combines the `W` partial accumulators.
3. **A lane under the combine order.** A vector *load* of `W` contributors feeding a horizontal accumulate that visits them in ascending lane order with one rounding per lane — the declared left fold, executed out of a vector register instead of `W` scalar loads.

**The ticket's hint separates (1) from (2) and is right as far as it goes; the line it does not draw is between (2) and (3), and that is the one that decides what construct the vocabulary gains.** Case (3) is not hypothetical, and the instruction set that proves it also proves the two cases are different values rather than different spellings.

**Fact — Arm A-profile A64 ISA, DDI 0602, version 2026-06.** `FADDA` is titled "Floating-point add strictly-ordered reduction, accumulating in scalar" and its description reads: "This instruction adds the floating-point values in a SIMD&FP scalar source and all active lanes of the vector source and destructively places the result in the SIMD&FP scalar source register. **Vector elements are processed strictly in order from low to high, with the scalar source providing the initial value.** Inactive elements in the source vector are ignored." Its Operation pseudocode is a left fold with one `FPAdd` per active lane. `FADDV` is titled "Floating-point add recursive reduction to scalar" and reads: "This instruction adds horizontally the floating-point values over all lanes of a vector using a **recursive pairwise reduction**… Inactive elements in the source vector are treated as `+0.0`." Its shared function halves the input and recurses, which is a balanced tree.

**They are two instructions because they compute two values**, and that is exactly the (3)-versus-(2) line rendered in silicon: `FADDA` is the declared left fold and consumes no permission; `FADDV` replaces the combine tree and consumes reassociation. Case (3) preserves the region's declared combine tree exactly; case (2) replaces it.

**And `FADDV`'s inactive-lane rule is a hardware instance of the padding-identity trap derived below.** It substitutes `+0.0` for inactive lanes, and `+0.0` is not a two-sided identity of binary32 addition — so a partially predicated `FADDV` over lanes that are all `-0.0` returns `+0.0` where the strict fold returns `-0.0`. The trap is not a Tiler modelling artefact; it is in the reduction instruction a CPU backend would reach for first. A single schedule construct spelled "reduce across lanes" would have to cover both, and would then have to be admitted under a strict contract because one of its realizations preserves the fold and refused under it because the other does not. **That is one name for two numerically different realizations, which is the `block_reduce` opacity the adopted scheduled-region model already refuses by name:** "An opaque `block_reduce` is insufficient because it does not determine numerical order, visibility, barriers, or resource use."

**Inference, and it is the record's load-bearing one.** A lane binding earns a schedule construct only when it changes something the schedule owns. Case (3) changes neither the map nor the partition; it changes how the contributors of one unchanged fold reach the arithmetic unit. That is a memory and instruction-selection decision, so it belongs below the schedule boundary, in structured-kernel lowering and in cost — exactly where the adopted scheduled-region model already puts the analogous claim that "whether a private temporary becomes a register or spills is a lowering/target fact". A backend realizing a `ReductionTopology::Serial` loop with `FADDA` is making a legal choice whose legality follows from the loop it is realizing, and it needs no permission and no new topology to make it.

**A note on attribution, because the two are easy to conflate.** ADR 0007's own accepted text names none of `FixedVectorLane`, `ScalableVectorLane`, `SubgroupTree`, `WorkgroupTree`, or `block_reduce`; those live in [the scheduled-region model](scheduled-region-model.md), the research record ADR 0007 lists as its evidence and whose `disposition` is `adopted`. Quotations above and below are the *model's*, and this record cites it as adopted-track design rather than as decided text. Where an accepted ADR itself decides something this record needs, it is cited by name — and ADR 0043 decides more of it than a reader would expect.

## What the implemented vocabulary already has, and what it does not

**Fact.** `ExecutionBinding` has exactly one variant, `GlobalLinearInvocation` (`crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/model.rs`). It is `#[non_exhaustive]` under ADR 0074 convention 5a, so a binding lands additively.

**Fact.** `TailPolicy` has exactly one variant, `Exact` — "the launch geometry covers the domain exactly with no tail". It is `#[non_exhaustive]` and carries a `compile_fail,E0004` doctest proving an out-of-crate exhaustive match breaks, so a tail policy also lands additively. **The implemented vocabulary therefore has no tail at all today**, in any tier.

**Fact.** `ReductionTopology` has five variants — `None`, `Serial`, `MultiPass`, `Contraction`, `CooperativeWorkgroup` — and none of them is a vector topology. `ContributorPartition` carries `partitions` and `contributors_per_partition`, and `ContributorPartition::covers` requires their product to equal the contributor count exactly, rejecting a ragged final partition rather than approximating it.

**Fact.** Both split topologies consume reassociation as a hard admission condition, not merely as a recorded field. `verify_cooperative_semantics` (`crates/tiler-ir/src/schedule/builder.rs`) refuses the topology when `!*permits_reassociation`, with the stated reason "Reassociation is what a split consumes, exactly as it is for a multi-pass one, and it is required rather than merely recorded: a contract that forbids reassociation forbids this strategy outright." The multi-pass arm carries the same check.

**Fact.** No identity padding is admitted anywhere. Neither `TailPolicy::IdentityPadded` nor any padding value exists; the cooperative verifier requires `partition.covers(contributors)` exactly and refuses an empty contributor domain outright (`CooperativeTileRule::EmptyContributorDomain`).

**Fact.** The `empty_identity_bits` every reducing scalar program carries is required by the schedule verifier to be `0.0_f32.to_bits()` — that is, `+0.0`, the positive zero — at each of the serial, multi-pass, and cooperative arms, with the comment "The strict sum's empty result is `+0.0`, and every family here shares it… so a tile cannot introduce a second empty-domain answer."

**Fact.** `KernelType` in the structured kernel IR is `Bool | U8 | Index | F32 | I32`, all scalar. `Builtin` is `GlobalInvocationIndex | LocalInvocationIndex`. There is no vector type, no lane index, no mask type, and no masked memory operation.

**Inference.** Nothing in the implemented vocabulary represents a vector lane, and nothing in it *forbids* one either. Every construct this design needs is an additive widening of a seam that was built to be widened, and the two that are not — the kernel-IR type and builtin vocabularies — are deliberately `non_exhaustive`-free total maps whose widening is a build error at every encoder, which is the mechanism, not an obstacle.

## 1. Verifier obligations for the lane tier

The obligations divide by which of the three cases the binding is, because the cases have almost nothing in common.

### A lane over the map consumes no numerical permission

**Derivation.** A region's meaning is: for each coordinate of `iteration_shape`, evaluate `scalar_program` and commit one owning write. The value at output coordinate `i` is a function of the input elements the region's access maps address from `i`. It is not a function of which other output coordinates are being computed, nor of the order in which they are computed — and the `OwnershipProof` is what makes that true rather than assumed, because `OneGlobalInvocationPerOutput` establishes that no output write is an input to any other output's evaluation. Assigning `W` output coordinates to `W` lanes executing in lockstep therefore changes no operand of any arithmetic operation, no rounding site, and no combining order inside any fold. The only thing it changes is which execution instance evaluates a given site, and that is the same freedom `GlobalLinearInvocation` already exercises across an arbitrary number of threads.

**So a strict-order fold is vectorizable, and the thing it vectorizes is the map.** A region computing `y[m] = strict_serial_sum(n, x[m,n])` under a contract with `reassociation: Forbidden` may put four rows in four lanes; each lane still performs the declared strict left fold over its own row, one rounding per contributor, in ascending contributor order. Nothing about the fold moved. This is the answer to "what a strict-order fold may vectorize at all", and it is a positive answer rather than the negative one the question's framing invites.

**Three obligations survive, and none of them is a numerical permission.**

- **Coverage and ownership.** The lane axis participates in the map, so the composed work assignment must still cover every coordinate of `iteration_shape` and assign each exactly one owner. With `W` lanes and extent `N`, this is the tail obligation below, and it is the *whole* of what the lane binding adds to the intrinsic verification of a pointwise region.
- **Bounds.** Every lane's loads and stores must be provably in range under the active predicate. This is where the tail policies stop being interchangeable — see below.
- **Uniform numerical realization.** The region declares exactly one `NumericalRealization`, and the verifier's job is to make sure the region *has* one realization rather than two. This is an obligation on the tail policy, not on the lane binding, and it is the obligation a scalar epilogue violates.

### A lane over the partition consumes reassociation, and a strided one also consumes permutation

**Derivation.** Lane `v` accumulating a subsequence of one output's contributors, followed by a horizontal combine of the `W` partials, is a `ContributorPartition { partitions: W, contributors_per_partition: k }` and nothing else. It stands to the fold exactly as `MultiPass` and `CooperativeWorkgroup` do, and the existing rule transfers verbatim: it must be admitted only when `permits_reassociation` holds, at the same place and by the same check.

**The layout decides the second permission, and this is where the lane tier differs from its two siblings.** `ContributorPartition`'s own contract states that partition `p` covers the contiguous contributor range `p * contributors_per_partition .. (p+1) * contributors_per_partition` and that the partials combine in ascending `p`, and concludes that "a multi-pass split is therefore a *reassociation* of the declared contributor sequence and never a permutation of it". Read the combine tree's leaves left to right under that rule and you get the declared sequence exactly; only the parenthesization moved. **A strided lane assignment — lane `v` taking contributors `v, v+W, v+2W, …` — does not have that property.** Its leaf order read left to right is `0, W, 2W, …, 1, W+1, …`, which is a permutation of the declared sequence, so it consumes `permutation` in addition to `reassociation`.

**Inference, and it is the tier's sharpest practical consequence.** On a CPU the strided assignment is the one with unit-stride contiguous vector loads, and the contiguous-block assignment is the one whose lanes are `k` elements apart and therefore needs a gather. **The permission structure forces the numerically legal layout to be the cache-hostile one.** A contract permitting reassociation but forbidding permutation — which is the common case, because they are independent dimensions and permitting one never implies the other (ADR 0011, and `NumericalPermission`'s own doc: "A permission is granted per dimension and never implies another") — admits only the gather layout. A planner that reached for the contiguous-load layout because it is obviously faster would be pricing meaning.

**The coverage obligation is the one that bites, and it is arithmetic rather than numerical.** `ContributorPartition::covers` requires `partitions * contributors_per_partition == contributors` exactly. For a lane partition with `W` lanes over `C` contributors this requires `W | C`. When it does not divide, the split is not merely suboptimal — it is unstatable, because a ragged final partition needs a second extent and a second trip count that the type does not have and deliberately refuses to approximate. The only repair is identity padding, which no construct in the implemented vocabulary provides, and which carries its own obligation derived below.

### The tail: masked, epilogue, and padded are three different target requirements

The scheduled-region model names the policies — `Exact` derives a divisibility obligation, `Predicated` carries an in-domain active predicate, `IdentityPadded` "is legal only with a proved reduction identity and padding safety". Carrying that into the lane tier turns each into a *different, decidable, per-target* requirement rather than three spellings of one choice.

**`Exact` — the divisibility obligation.** For a lane over the map at extent `N` and width `W`, `Exact` requires `W | N`. It is the only policy with no target requirement beyond the lane arithmetic itself, and it is the policy a specializing planner should reach for when the extent is a compile-time constant that happens to divide. It is not available for a symbolic extent, because `N mod W` is then not a compile-time quantity.

**`Predicated` — requires a fault-suppressing masked load, which is a hard per-ISA fact.** Masking suppresses the store; it does not, on its own, make the load safe. Lane `W-1` at a tail-bearing extent addresses an element past the region's declared bound, and `BoundsProofKind::LinearRange { element_count }` is exactly the proof that says it must not. So a predicated tail is admissible only when the target realizes a load whose inactive lanes fault-suppress.

**Fact — Intel 64 and IA-32 SDM, Volume 2, order number 325383-085US.** `VMASKMOVPS` (`_mm256_maskload_ps`) takes its mask from "the most significant bit of that element in the first source operand" and states: "Faults occur only due to mask-bit required memory accesses that caused the faults. **Faults will not occur due to referencing any memory location if the corresponding mask bit for that memory location is 0.**" **Its CPUID feature flag is `AVX`, not `AVX2`** — only the integer siblings `VPMASKMOVD`/`VPMASKMOVQ` are AVX2, and a profile keyed on the wrong feature would be declaring a row it cannot license.

**Fact — Intel SDM, Volume 1, §15.6.1.** AVX-512 opmask predication: "For some instructions with a memory operand, memory faults are suppressed for elements with a mask bit of 0", with `k1` through `k7` addressable as predicate operands and `k0` not.

**Fact — DDI 0602, 2026-06.** SVE's `LD1W` states "Inactive elements will not cause a read from Device memory or signal a fault, and are set to zero in the destination vector", and `WHILELT` "generates a predicate that starting from the lowest numbered element is true while the incrementing value of the first, signed scalar operand is less than the second scalar operand and false thereafter". First-faulting and non-faulting variants (`LDFF1W`, `LDNF1W`) exist beside it.

**Fact — AArch64 Advanced SIMD has no masked load, established by enumeration rather than by a failed search.** The Advanced SIMD instruction list in DDI 0602 2026-06 contains 467 pages over 337 distinct mnemonics, and none has a predicate, mask, or fault-suppression role; the complete load set is the `LD1`–`LD4` structure families, `LDR`/`LDUR`/`LDP` and their variants, and the floating-point atomic read-modify-write families. There is no predicate register file in Advanced SIMD. A tail is handled by single-lane loads, narrower loads, or an overlapping full-width load — never by predication.

**So a predicated lane tail is `Realized` on AVX, AVX-512, and SVE, and `Unrealizable` on NEON.** This is a hard feasibility fork between two targets in Tom's own priority list — the Apple Silicon host the scalar vertical ran on is NEON — and it is exactly the kind of fact that must reject with an explainable reason rather than hide behind a cost.

**`ScalarEpilogue` — requires that every path realizing the region's arithmetic honour its one declared realization. The obligation is real; the shape it takes is not the obvious one.** A scalar epilogue needs no masking and therefore runs on NEON. Its cost is elsewhere: the region declares one `NumericalRealization`, and under this policy outputs `0 .. N - r` are produced by the vector unit while outputs `N - r .. N` are produced by the scalar unit. If those two paths resolve any dimension of that realization differently, the region produces observably different results for two ranges of one output tensor.

**And the obvious form of that worry is refuted on both ISAs checked.** **Fact — Arm ARM DDI 0487 M.c §A1.5.6.** On AArch64 the flush-to-zero controls are stated with **no scalar-versus-Advanced-SIMD distinction at all**: "If FPCR.FIZ == 1, or both FPCR.AH == 0 and FPCR.FZ == 1, then for floating-point instructions other than FABS and FNEG, for all inputs other than half-precision, denormalized inputs are flushed to zero." The "Advanced SIMD always flushes" rule a reader may remember is **AArch32's** (§E1.3.5.1: "For Advanced SIMD floating-point instructions, all single-precision and double-precision inputs that are denormalized numbers are flushed to zero. For scalar floating-point instructions… are not flushed to zero"), and the manual's own data-type tables say so explicitly: "Denormalized numbers are always flushed to zero in Advanced SIMD processing in AArch32 state. They are optionally flushed to zero in floating-point processing and in Advanced SIMD processing in AArch64 state." **Fact — Intel SDM Volume 1 §11.5.1.** "SIMD floating-point exceptions are those exceptions that can be generated by SSE/SSE2/SSE3 instructions that operate on packed **or scalar** floating-point operands", and one MXCSR carries one FTZ bit and one DAZ bit for both.

**Inference, and it is a correction to this record's own first framing.** The divergence is not between *packed and scalar as classes* — on AArch64 and on x86-SSE/AVX the two share one control. It is **per-instruction and per-execution-path**, and the real instances are narrower and stranger: on AArch64, `FPCR.AH == 1` makes `FRECPE`, `FRECPS`, `FRECPX`, `FRSQRTE`, and `FRSQRTS` flush unconditionally regardless of `FZ`, the min/max family and `FABS`/`FNEG` are exempt from output flushing, and half-precision is governed by a separate `FZ16`; on x86, **x87 has no FTZ or DAZ at all**, so a toolchain that routes any scalar arithmetic through x87 has a path that never flushes beside SSE paths that do.

**So the obligation stands and its discharge is usually cheap.** The correct statement is not "declare a packed row and a scalar row and require them equal" but "**every path that realizes any of the region's arithmetic must honour the region's one declared realization**" — which on the two ISAs checked is discharged by one control governing both paths, and which fails only in the per-instruction cases above. A profile that wants to *state* a divergence still cannot, because `NumericalHonourabilityFact` is keyed by dimension and arithmetic type with no path component; whether that widening is warranted now rests on a much narrower case than this record first claimed, and the profile record says so.

**`IdentityPadded` — and the padding identity is not the empty identity.** For a lane over the partition, the repair for `W ∤ C` is to extend the contributor sequence to a multiple of `W` with a value that is an identity of the combine operation. **The value is `-0.0`, not `+0.0`.** **Fact — IEEE Std 754-2019, clause 6.3 "The sign bit".** "When the sum of two operands with opposite signs (or the difference of two operands with like signs) is exactly zero, the sign of that sum (or difference) shall be +0 under all rounding-direction attributes except roundTowardNegative; under that attribute, the sign of an exact zero sum (or difference) shall be −0. However, under all rounding-direction attributes, when x is zero, x + x and x − (−x) have the sign of x." Under roundTiesToEven this makes `x + (-0.0) = x` for every non-NaN `x`, including both signed zeros — `(-0.0) + (-0.0) = -0.0` by the second sentence and `(+0.0) + (-0.0) = +0.0` by the first — while `+0.0` is not a two-sided identity, because `(-0.0) + (+0.0) = +0.0`. **The restriction to roundTiesToEven is load-bearing:** under roundTowardNegative *neither* zero is an identity, since `(+0.0) + (-0.0) = -0.0` there. Tiler's admitted rounding is `MaterializationRounding::NearestTiesToEven` and nothing else, so the derivation holds for every contract this build can express, and a directed-rounding contract would have to redo it. **Fact.** The value the schedule verifier currently requires in `empty_identity_bits` is `0.0_f32.to_bits()`, that is `+0.0`. **Inference.** A padded lane fold that reused `empty_identity_bits` as its padding value would be silently wrong on exactly the rows whose true sum is `-0.0` — every contributor `-0.0`, which is reachable from ordinary data — and correct everywhere else, which is the worst possible failure profile. The two values have different roles: `empty_identity_bits` is the result committed when there are *no* contributors, and a padding identity is a value combined *into* a nonempty fold. They are allowed to differ and here they must.

**And the reason the padding value must be a stated, checked field rather than a derivation is that the derivation would be right half the time.** Under `signed_zero: Forbidden` the two zeros are observably different and only `-0.0` works. Under `signed_zero: Permitted` the contract authorizes eliminating the distinction, so both work and the choice is free. A rule that derived the padding from `empty_identity_bits` would therefore produce correct results on every reassociation-permitted, signed-zero-permitted contract — the ones a first implementation is most likely to test — and silently wrong ones on the stricter contract it was never exercised against. A stated field with a checked identity obligation fails closed instead.

## 2. How width enters the schedule, and why the two bindings do not share an answer

### `FixedVectorLane` carries a literal width. This is forced, not chosen.

**Elimination.** *Width-symbolic, resolved at feasibility* is eliminated on two independent grounds, either sufficient.

First, **identity**. `CanonicalScheduledRegionIdentity` is a pure function of normalized schedule content, and it is what an artifact, a cache entry, and a compiled payload are keyed by. Two schedules that differ only in lane width have different launch geometry, different tail structure, and different derived target requirements — they are two programs. A symbolic width would give them one identity, so a cache would hit across them and an artifact would claim to be the bytes of a program it is not. The domain-separator discipline the encoders carry exists precisely to make identity injective; a symbolic width would break injectivity from inside.

Second, **intrinsic verification is target-independent by construction.** ADR 0007 decides that "Verification is layered. Intrinsic verification proves domain coverage, ownership, bounds under predicates… tail/vector behavior, reduction contributor and numerical-order legality… It then derives exact or proven resource requirements and typed predicates for the separate phased target-feasibility assessment", and the adopted model states the consequence as "Failure here is a hard schedule rejection independent of target performance". The tail obligation is an intrinsic one: whether there is a tail at all is `N mod W`. With `W` symbolic there is no answer at intrinsic verification, so either the tail obligation moves into target feasibility — which would make a coverage failure a target verdict, collapsing a hard intrinsic rejection into a phased one — or it is not checked. Both are refused.

*Width absent from the schedule, chosen by the backend* is eliminated by the ticket's own non-goal and by the model: a schedule that relies on the emitter picking a width is not a schedule that states lanes, and the derived resource requirements would then be a function of something the identity does not carry.

### `ScalableVectorLane` is width-symbolic. This is also forced.

**Elimination.** *A literal width for the scalable binding too* is eliminated because it is not the scalable binding. **Fact — Arm ARM DDI 0487 M.c §B1.4.1 and §B1.4.2.** SVE's vector length is implementation-defined; "the smallest architecturally defined maximum length is 128 bits. The largest architecturally defined maximum length is 2048 bits. **The length is a power of two**", and "the architecturally defined NSVL set is all powers of two from 128 to 2048 bits inclusive". **A correction worth carrying, because the older wording is widely repeated:** DDI 0487 H.a (2022) said "an IMPLEMENTATION DEFINED **multiple of** 128 bits", and Arm narrowed it between issues; the `ZCR_EL1.LEN` encoding still reads as `(LEN+1)*128`, which is where the multiple-of-128 reading comes from. `grep -c "multiple of 128"` over the full text of M.c returns 0. The length is readable at run time through `RDVL` and `CNTB`. **Fact — RISC-V unprivileged ISA manual, chapter 9.** RVV sets an active vector length per iteration with `vsetvli` and reports `VLEN/8` through the read-only `vlenb` CSR.

**Inference, labelled because the normative text does not say it.** No rule in DDI 0487 M.c asserts that one binary runs at any implemented vector length; "vector-length agnostic" is Arm programmer's-guide vocabulary rather than an architectural clause. What the architecture does supply is the mechanism — no instruction encodes the length, `RDVL`/`CNTB`/`INCB` read it, and `WHILELT` derives predicates from a loop bound — and that mechanism is what the elimination actually rests on. Pinning a literal makes the schedule a fixed-width schedule that happens to be emitted with scalable instructions, which is `FixedVectorLane` with extra steps and forfeits the property the mechanism exists to provide.

*Width-symbolic with no compile-time obligation at all* is eliminated as fail-open: a schedule whose coverage cannot be established is `Unknown`, and ADR 0043 decides that "an `Unknown` candidate may remain in search/explain state but cannot enter an executable frontier or manifest".

**And the distinction itself is already decided rather than proposed.** ADR 0043's accepted text states: "Execution and vector models are explicit. GPU workgroup/subgroup scopes are not aliases for CPU worker/vector scopes. **Fixed and scalable vector shapes are distinct**, and vector legality depends on operation, dtype, masks, address space, width, and alignment." What follows is therefore not an argument that the two bindings differ — that is settled — but a derivation of *where* they differ, which the ADR does not say.

**What survives is width-symbolic with a width-agnostic covering relation**, and its content is precise: the work assignment states that every coordinate `i < N` is active in exactly one iteration of a predicated loop whose trip count is `ceil(N / W)`, and that statement is discharged *as a relation*, for all `W ≥ 1`, without evaluating it at any `W`. This is exactly the strip-mining idiom the scalable ISAs are built around. **Fact — RISC-V unprivileged ISA manual §9.1.5.3 and §9.1.5.4.** RVV's strip-mining loop needs no scalar epilogue, and the canonical loop in the manual has none; the hardware chooses `vl` under the stated constraints and **may split the last two iterations rather than emitting one full and one short one** — "this permits an implementation to set vl = ceil(AVL / 2) for VLMAX < AVL < 2*VLMAX in order to evenly distribute work over the last two iterations of a strip-mine loop." That the exact split is implementation-chosen is precisely why the covering relation must be discharged for all admissible `vl` rather than for a computed one. SVE's `WHILELT` produces a predicate that is partially false on the final iteration and the loop body is unchanged.

### The three consequences that make Fixed and Scalable different answers

1. **The tail vocabulary differs.** A scalable schedule has no compile-time tail, because `N mod W` is not a compile-time quantity. `Exact` is therefore unstatable for it, and so is a scalar epilogue, because the epilogue's trip count is `N mod W`. **A scalable binding admits `Predicated` and nothing else**, and its predication is not a tail policy applied to a last iteration — every iteration is predicated identically. The word "tail" does not survive the translation, which is the strongest possible sign that the two bindings do not share an answer.
2. **A scalable lane may not bind the partition, under any contract.** `ContributorPartition::covers` is `partitions * contributors_per_partition == contributors`, a product over `partitions = W`. With `W` symbolic the equality is undecidable at intrinsic verification, so the contributor-coverage obligation — "reduction contributors appear exactly once unless proved identity padding is used" — cannot be discharged. **This is a hard structural refusal that holds even when the contract permits reassociation and permutation outright.** The two bindings therefore differ in *what they may vectorize*, not merely in how their width is written.
3. **The concrete scalable length is a cost fact, never a legality one.** Every legality predicate the tier raises is either width-agnostic by construction (coverage under the strip-mining relation), independent of the width (a predicated load's element alignment, the lane arithmetic's numerical realization), or refused outright (the partition). Nothing left needs the number. The number is needed to estimate trip counts and to rank candidates, and that is cost. This is the finding that keeps a whole availability-phase question closed; it is developed in the profile record.

## 3. The CPU worker-thread scope: explicitly out, with a trigger

**Elimination.** *In, in this design* requires, at minimum: a governed worker scope kind distinct from `Workgroup`; a synchronization subject whose execution and visibility scopes range over OS threads rather than GPU invocations; a fenced-space vocabulary for a machine whose address space is single and coherent, where `FencedSpaces { workgroup: true, device: false }` has no referent; a threading-runtime ownership contract; and a task-granularity model. Every one of those is a public boundary, and none of them is needed by anything in this record.

*Out* costs a CPU backend its thread-level parallelism, which is a performance ceiling — and performance claims are this ticket's stated non-goal, so the cost is not payable in this ticket's currency in either direction.

**The decisive argument is that the two are orthogonal in the schedule, and only one of them is missing.** A vector lane binds *within* one execution instance's private register file: it introduces no second instance, no shared allocation, no cross-instance visibility edge, and therefore consumes no synchronization authority — `cooperative_synchronization_requirement` returns `None` for it, which is the canonical absence rather than a zero. A worker thread is *another execution instance*, and the schedule already has a vocabulary for that: `LaunchPlan { grid_threads, threads_per_workgroup }` and `GlobalLinearInvocation`. A threaded CPU backend that maps global linear invocations onto a work-stealing pool needs **no new binding at all**; what it needs is a profile that declares more than one thread per workgroup, which is a declaration and not a construct.

**Inference.** Including the worker scope here would add a construct that is not required to express threading, while the construct that *is* required for lanes has no representation. And it would be the first thing to compete for the `SynchronizationScope::Workgroup` spelling, whose current meaning is a GPU threadgroup — which is precisely the synonym **ADR 0043 already refuses in its accepted text**: "GPU workgroup/subgroup scopes are not aliases for CPU worker/vector scopes." The adopted scheduled-region model states the same refusal at more length ("GPU threadgroups and CPU worker/vector scopes are likewise different governed scope kinds… the common schema does not pretend they are synonyms"), but the decided form is the ADR's, and it is what makes conflating them a violation rather than a departure from a proposal.

**Trigger, stated so a future reader can fire it without re-deriving this.** The worker scope enters when a CPU schedule needs a *cross-instance handoff* — concretely, the first time a `CooperativeWorkgroup`-shaped topology is proposed for a CPU target, because that is the first construct whose visibility edges cannot be discharged by program order within one instance. Until then the scope has no obligation to discharge, and a scope kind with no obligation is a name, not a design.

## 4. What the CPU target profile declares

Answered in full in [CPU vector realization facts](../target-profiles/cpu-vector-realization-facts.md). Its four results, stated here because the worked examples below consume them: the target fact is one **atomic** `VectorRealizationSubject` matched by equality, exactly as `SynchronizationSubject` is; fixed-width ISA presence is a **compile-target attribution** at `AvailabilityPhase::CompileProfile` and never a runtime observation, with host feature detection living in the runtime's existing device-free variant-eligibility filter rather than in feasibility at all; feasibility is therefore answerable **with no backend code executed**, for both binding kinds; and the lane tier **does not** trigger the missing host-process availability phase, which is a deliberate negative result.

## 5. Lowering: what a CPU emission target consumes

**Elimination.** *The kernel IR as it stands, with a lane-annotated schedule the backend reads* is eliminated, and not on grounds of taste. The kernel-IR module states its own purpose: "The layer exists so a backend never reconstructs graph-specific semantics… Predicates. Iteration-domain guarding is an explicit `OperationView::Predicated` region, so tail behaviour is visible rather than implied by a launch geometry." Under this candidate the lane-to-element mapping and the lane mask would be schedule facts the kernel body does not contain, so the two things the kernel verifier proves about effects — predicate dominance of every effect, and bounds-witness provenance for every load — would both be proved against a body whose real predication is invisible to it. **A kernel whose actual predication is not in the kernel is the failure the layer exists to prevent**, and it would land the fault-suppression obligation of a masked load in a backend with nothing checking that it was met.

*New constructs in the kernel IR* survives. The minimum set follows from the obligations above and from nothing else.

1. **A lane-shaped value type.** `KernelType` gains a fixed vector shape carrying element type and lane count, and — separately — a scalable vector shape carrying element type alone. Two shapes rather than one with an optional count, because an optional count would let a fixed-width operation consume a scalable value; the whole point of the Q2 answer is that those are different programs.
2. **A lane-mask type, distinct from `Bool`.** `Bool` is a whole-block control predicate that `OperationView::Predicated` consumes. A lane mask is per-lane and is consumed by memory operations. Conflating them would let a block predicate be passed where a mask is required and vice versa, in a vocabulary whose entire discipline is that a type means one thing.
3. **Masked load and masked store**, each naming its mask alongside the buffer, the offset, and the `BoundsWitnessId` or `OwnershipWitnessId` it already names. This is what makes the fault-suppression obligation a derived requirement the profile is asked about, rather than an assumption.
4. **A mask-generating comparison of the lane index against a bound**, the `WHILELT`-shaped primitive, plus a lane broadcast. Without the first, the predicated covering relation of the scalable binding is not expressible in the body; without the second, a loop-invariant scalar cannot enter a lane computation.
5. **Element-wise widening of the existing arithmetic operations to lane-shaped operands**, rather than a parallel family of vector operations. The operation's *meaning* is unchanged — `BinaryOp::F32Add` over lanes is the same rounding rule applied lane-wise — and a parallel family would duplicate every numerical rule and every honourability obligation into a second set that could drift.

**And one construct that is deliberately absent: there is no horizontal-reduce operation.** By the map/partition/combine-order line, an order-preserving horizontal accumulate is below the schedule boundary, and its kernel-IR spelling already exists — it is the `SerialLoop` with a typed loop-carried accumulator that a `ReductionTopology::Serial` region already lowers to. A backend that realizes that loop with an ordered horizontal instruction is making an instruction-selection choice whose legality follows from the loop's own declared arithmetic. Adding a horizontal-reduce operation would create a second, unchecked spelling of the same fold.

Every one of these is a public vocabulary widening in `tiler-ir` and is out of this record's scope. They are deferred to their own implementation ticket, enumerated below.

## Worked example A — a strict-order fold at a tail-bearing extent

The program, in both examples:

```text
x : f32[7, 101]                      (one input tensor)
y : f32[7]
y[m] = strict_serial_sum(n, x[m, n]) 0 <= m < 7, 0 <= n < 101
```

The region, in the implemented vocabulary: `iteration_shape = [7]`; one `ReductionContributor` read with `input_shape = [7,101]`, `output_shape = [7]`, `axes = [1]`, `order = OriginalAxisLexicographic`; one `LinearIdentity` owning write; `OwnershipProofKind::OneGlobalInvocationPerOutput { output_count: 7 }`; `ScalarProgram::StrictSerialSum { axes: [1], order: OriginalAxisLexicographic, canonical_nan_bits: 0x7fc0_0000, empty_identity_bits: 0x0000_0000 }`.

**The contract in example A** is the strict one the workspace already uses (`tiler.doc.strict-f32`): `input_subnormals: Preserve`, `result_subnormals: Preserve`, `contraction: Forbidden`, `reassociation: Forbidden`, `permutation: Forbidden`, `signed_zero: Forbidden`, both exceptional-value assumptions `MakeNoAssumption`.

**Both extents bear a tail, and they are different tails.** The output extent is `7` and the contributor extent is `101`; at `W = 4`, `7 mod 4 = 3` and `101 mod 4 = 1`. Which one is the tail depends on which case the lane binding is, and that is the first thing the example demonstrates.

### A1 — lane over the map, `FixedVectorLane(4)`, `Predicated`

The map: lane `v` of iteration `q` owns output `m = 4q + v`, `q ∈ {0, 1}`, active predicate `m < 7`. Iteration 0 has all four lanes active; iteration 1 has lanes 0, 1, 2 active and lane 3 inactive. Each active lane performs the complete 101-step strict left fold over its own row.

| Obligation | Discharge |
| --- | --- |
| Reassociation | **Not consumed.** Each lane's combine tree is the declared left fold, unchanged; `SERIAL_SUM_F32_FACT_FOLD_ORDER = "strict-left-fold"` is preserved per lane. The topology stays `ReductionTopology::Serial { permits_reassociation: false, permits_permutation: false }`, which the verifier already admits under this contract. |
| Permutation | Not consumed. |
| Coverage | `ceil(7/4) = 2` iterations × 4 lanes, predicate `m < 7`, covers `0..7` exactly once. |
| Ownership | Each active lane is the sole writer of its `m`; `OneGlobalInvocationPerOutput { output_count: 7 }` holds with the lane as the invocation. |
| Bounds | **The binding obligation.** Lane 3 of iteration 1 addresses `x[7, n]`, which is outside `BoundsProofKind::LinearRange { element_count: 707 }`. The schedule therefore derives a requirement for a fault-suppressing masked load. |
| Numerical realization | One realization for the whole region; the profile is asked to honour `Preserve`/`Preserve` and the four `Forbidden` permissions for **packed** `f32` arithmetic at width 4. |

**Verdicts, and they differ.** On a profile declaring AVX, the masked-load subject is `Realized` (`VMASKMOVPS` suppresses faults on masked-off elements) and A1 is `Proven`. On a profile declaring AArch64 Advanced SIMD, the subject is `Unrealizable` — NEON has no predicated load — and A1 is **`Rejected`**, at hard feasibility, with an explainable named reason. Same schedule, same contract, two targets, two verdicts, and the difference is legality rather than cost.

### A2 — lane over the map, `FixedVectorLane(4)`, `ScalarEpilogue`

One full vector iteration covering `m ∈ 0..4`, then a scalar loop covering `m ∈ 4..7`.

| Obligation | Discharge |
| --- | --- |
| Reassociation, permutation | Not consumed, for the reason A1 gives. |
| Coverage, ownership | `4 + 3 = 7`, disjoint, one owner each. |
| Bounds | **No out-of-range address is generated at all.** Every vector lane and every epilogue iteration is in range by construction. This is the whole advantage of the policy. |
| Numerical realization | **The binding obligation, and it is the one nobody states.** Outputs `0..4` are produced by the packed path and outputs `4..7` by the scalar path. The region declares one realization, so **both paths** must honour `Preserve`/`Preserve`. On AArch64 and on x86-SSE/AVX one control governs both, so the obligation is discharged by the same fact A1 needed; it becomes two facts only on a target whose paths are separately controlled, and the x87 routing case is the one instance found. |

**Verdicts.** On NEON, A2 is admissible where A1 is not — the epilogue is exactly how a NEON backend does a tail. On AVX both are admissible and the choice is cost. On an x86 profile whose floating-point environment sets FTZ/DAZ, both A1 and A2 are `Rejected` before the lane question is reached, because a flushing path cannot honour `Preserve`; that rejection is a property of the contract and the target and has nothing to do with lanes, and the example includes it to show which rejections the lane tier owns and which it does not.

**Inference — the naive ordering of A1 against A2 is still wrong, though less dramatically than this record first claimed.** A scalar epilogue looks like the conservative choice because it involves no exotic instruction. It is not: it introduces a second execution path into a region that declares one numerical realization, and the fact that the two ISAs checked happen to govern both paths with one control is a *measured convenience*, not a property of the policy. A mask introduces no second path and needs one architectural fact. The asymmetry survives the correction; the size of it does not.

**Inference.** A1 and A2 are not two spellings of one decision. They demand different target facts, and each is the only admissible policy on some target in Tom's priority list. A tail policy is therefore a first-class schedule field with a per-target verdict, not an emitter detail.

### A3 — lane over the partition, `FixedVectorLane(4)` over the contributor axis

**Rejected at intrinsic verification, and twice over.** First: `ReductionTopology`'s split arms require `permits_reassociation`, and `verify_cooperative_semantics`'s check `!*permits_reassociation` is exactly the rule; this contract sets it `Forbidden`, so the topology is refused outright with the phase `Numerical` and the rule `schedule.reduction.numerical_order`. Second, and independently: `ContributorPartition { partitions: 4, contributors_per_partition: k }.covers(101)` requires `4k = 101`, which has no integer solution, so the split is not statable even with the permission. The second refusal is what makes the tail on the *contributor* axis visible: it is not a masking question at all, it is a coverage question, and its only repair is identity padding.

**This is the answer to the ticket's first question, in one line: under a reassociation-forbidding contract, a strict-order fold may vectorize its map and may not vectorize its fold, and the two tails those choices create are different obligations with different discharges.**

## Worked example B — the same program under a reassociation-permitted contract

Identical program and identical region, one dimension changed: `reassociation: Permitted`. Everything else — including `permutation: Forbidden` and `signed_zero: Forbidden` — is unchanged. This is the realistic shape, because the dimensions are independent and permitting one never implies another.

### B1 — lane over the map

**Unchanged from A1 and A2 in every respect.** The map binding consumed no permission, so granting one changes nothing about it. This is worth stating because it is the check on the Q1 derivation: if the map's obligations had moved when a permission was granted, the claim that it consumes none would have been false.

### B2 — lane over the partition, contiguous blocks

Now admissible in principle, and the obligations bite in a specific order.

1. **Reassociation:** consumed and available. The topology's admission check passes.
2. **Permutation:** the layout must be contiguous blocks combined in ascending lane order, because `ContributorPartition`'s contract is what makes such a split "a reassociation of the declared contributor sequence and never a permutation of it". A strided assignment consumes `permutation`, which this contract forbids, so **the strided layout is rejected and the gather layout is the only legal one** — the consequence derived above, now instantiated.
3. **Coverage:** `4k = 101` still has no solution. The ragged repair — three partitions of 26 and one of 23 — is refused by `covers` by construction, and deliberately: "a ragged final partition would need a second extent and a second trip count, and `ContributorPartition::covers` rejects one rather than approximating it."
4. **Identity padding** is therefore the only repair: extend the contributor sequence to 104 with three padding contributors and give lane 3 the range `78..104` of which `101..104` are padding.
5. **And the padding value is `0x8000_0000`, not `0x0000_0000`.** With `signed_zero: Forbidden`, `+0.0` is not an identity of `f32` addition: a row `m` whose 101 contributors are all `-0.0` has true sum `-0.0`, and padding lane 3's accumulator chain with `+0.0` returns `+0.0`. The two differ in one bit and are observably different values. `-0.0` is the two-sided identity and is correct for every row. **The value the region already carries — `empty_identity_bits = 0x0000_0000` — is the wrong one, and it is the one an implementer would reach for**, because it is right there on the scalar program and is named "identity".

**So B2's differing result against B1 is not a permission and not a target fact: it is a construct that does not exist.** `TailPolicy::IdentityPadded` needs a *stated padding value* that is not derived from `empty_identity_bits`, plus a proof obligation that the stated value is an identity of the combine operation under the contract's own signed-zero permission. Both are deferred below.

### B3 — the same partition on `ScalableVectorLane`

**Rejected, and the contract is irrelevant.** `covers` is a product over `partitions`, and `partitions = W` is symbolic. The obligation "reduction contributors appear exactly once unless proved identity padding is used" cannot be discharged at intrinsic verification for any `W`, and moving it to target feasibility would convert a hard intrinsic rejection into a phased one, which ADR 0007's layered verification forbids by placing "reduction contributor and numerical-order legality" inside intrinsic verification. **A reassociation-permitted contract therefore admits the fold split on `FixedVectorLane` and refuses it on `ScalableVectorLane`** — the clearest demonstration that the two bindings are two answers, since here they differ under an identical contract on an identical program with no target involved.

## Deferrals, each with the evidence that would close it and a trigger

- **Does the horizontal-accumulate case ever need to become a schedule fact?** It is below the boundary because it changes neither the map nor the partition. It would rise above the boundary if a target offered an ordered horizontal accumulate whose *numerical* realization differed from the scalar loop it replaces — then the choice would price meaning and would have to be stated. Closes with a measured comparison of an ordered horizontal reduction against the equivalent scalar chain on one target. Trigger: the first CPU emission target that has such an instruction and a reason to use it.
- **Can a symbolic contributor partition be made verifiable?** `covers` is a product; a coverage *relation* over a symbolic `W` — every contributor in exactly one partition, partitions combined in ascending order — is statable and is what would let `ScalableVectorLane` bind the fold. Closes with a partition vocabulary carrying a relation rather than a product, and a verifier that discharges it without evaluating `W`. Trigger: a workload whose reduction is long enough that the scalable map binding leaves the fold as the dominant cost.
- **Does the strided lane layout have a contract that admits it?** It consumes reassociation *and* permutation, and every registered reducing family currently declares both `false`. Closes with a registered family that permits both, or with a decision that Tiler will not offer one. Trigger: a caller asking for the fast layout.
- **What does a mixed vector/scalar region's numerical realization mean when the two units agree only on some dimensions?** This record requires agreement on every declared dimension and stops there. A partial agreement is expressible and its verdict is not derived. Closes with the profile-side key widening enumerated in the profile record. Trigger: the first target profile declaring two execution units with different numerical rows.
- **Is `SubnormalFreedom` a repair for the epilogue obligation?** A region whose arithmetic is provably bounded away from the subnormal range has nothing for two units to differ about on the subnormal dimensions, so `subnormal_freedom` could discharge the vector/scalar agreement obligation for those two dimensions alone. The current freedom is `StrictAffineNormalScaleDecode` and applies to one scalar program. Closes with a derivation extending the freedom to a reducing program. Trigger: a scalar-epilogue schedule refused only on the subnormal dimensions.

**Title:** Bind vector lanes to the map or the contributor partition, never to the combine order

**Frontmatter:** `decision_status: proposed`, `implementation_status: not-started`, `catalog_group: "physical-planning-lowering"`, `applies_to: ["tiler.contract.fusion-and-scheduling", "tiler.contract.cpu-backend", "tiler.contract.ir"]`, `evidence: ["tiler.research.scheduling.cpu-vector-lane-tier", "tiler.research.target-profiles.cpu-vector-realization-facts"]`, `depends_on: ["ADR-0007", "ADR-0011", "ADR-0019", "ADR-0043", "ADR-0074", "ADR-0076", "ADR-0090"]`, `ticket: "land-the-cpu-vector-lane-tier-adr"`.

### Context

ADR 0043 decides that fixed and scalable vector shapes are distinct, that GPU workgroup and subgroup scopes are not aliases for CPU worker and vector scopes, and that vector legality depends on operation, dtype, masks, address space, width, and alignment. ADR 0007 makes the normalized schedule authoritative and puts tail and vector behaviour, contributor coverage, and numerical-order legality inside intrinsic verification. The adopted scheduled-region model behind ADR 0007 goes further and names `FixedVectorLane` and `ScalableVectorLane` as bindings distinct from subgroup lanes.

**None of that says which of a schedule's structures a vector lane may bind**, and the implemented vocabulary has no vector construct at all: one execution binding, one tail policy, five reduction topologies of which none is a vector topology, and a scalar-only kernel type vocabulary. CPU is one of the two target families Tom prioritized on 2026-08-01, and the bounded scalar CPU vertical deliberately covered the scalar half alone, leaving every vector, scalable-vector, and mask/tail claim undeclared and therefore `Unknown`.

### Decision

1. **A schedule owns the map and the contributor partition; it does not own the combine order within a partition.** A vector-lane binding is admitted when and only when it changes one of the first two. A lane binding that changes neither — a vector load feeding an accumulate that visits contributors in the declared order with one rounding per contributor — is an instruction-selection and memory decision below the schedule boundary, and gains no schedule construct, no topology, and no kernel-IR horizontal-reduce operation.
2. **A lane over the map consumes no numerical permission.** Output positions are pairwise independent by the region's own ownership proof, so assigning them to lanes changes no operand, no rounding site, and no combine order. A strict-order fold is therefore vectorizable across its outputs under a contract that forbids reassociation, contraction, permutation, and signed-zero elimination outright.
3. **A lane over the contributor partition is a `ContributorPartition` and consumes reassociation**, admitted by the same check `MultiPass` and `CooperativeWorkgroup` already apply. A contiguous-block layout consumes reassociation alone; a strided layout additionally consumes permutation, because its combine tree's leaves read in a permuted order. The two are distinguished in the representation, never inferred.
4. **Tail policies are distinct target requirements, not spellings of one choice.** `Exact` requires divisibility and nothing else; a predicated tail requires a fault-suppressing masked load, which some SIMD ISAs realize and some do not; a scalar epilogue requires that **every execution path realizing any of the region's arithmetic** honour every dimension of the region's one declared numerical realization, which is one fact where a target governs both paths together and two where it does not; an identity-padded tail requires a stated padding value proved to be an identity of the combine operation under the contract's own signed-zero permission. A padding value is never derived from the empty-domain identity: under a contract forbidding signed-zero elimination the additive identity is `-0.0` and the empty-domain result is `+0.0`.
5. **`FixedVectorLane` carries a literal width and `ScalableVectorLane` is width-symbolic, and they are not one answer.** A literal is forced by canonical schedule identity and by the tail obligation being intrinsic. A symbolic width is forced by the vector-length-agnostic model the scalable ISAs exist to provide. A scalable binding admits predication only — it has no compile-time tail — and may bind the map and **not** the partition, because contributor coverage is a product over the unknown width and cannot be discharged at intrinsic verification.
6. **The concrete scalable vector length is a cost fact and never a feasibility fact**, because every legality predicate the tier raises is width-agnostic, width-independent, or refused outright. A CPU profile therefore answers vector feasibility with no backend code executed and at the compile-profile phase alone, and the lane tier does not require a host-process availability phase.
7. **The CPU worker-thread scope stays out of the lane tier.** A vector lane binds within one execution instance and raises no cross-instance visibility edge, so it consumes no synchronization authority; thread-level parallelism needs a profile declaring more than one thread per workgroup, not a new binding. The worker scope enters when a CPU schedule first requires a cross-instance handoff.

### Consequences

- A CPU backend can vectorize strict, reassociation-forbidding programs — the ones Tiler's numerical contracts default to — rather than being limited to relaxed ones, because the map is always available.
- The numerically legal fold layout on a permutation-forbidding contract is the one needing gathers, so a CPU cost model must be able to price a gather before the fold split is worth selecting.
- One schedule can be `Proven` on one CPU profile and `Rejected` on another for its tail policy alone, which makes tail policy a planning dimension rather than an emitter detail.
- The kernel IR gains lane-shaped and mask types, masked memory operations, and a lane index, and gains no horizontal-reduce operation.
- Nothing here admits a threaded CPU backend, a cache model, or any performance claim.

### Alternatives considered

- **One "reduce across lanes" construct covering both the ordered and the tree-ordered realization.** Rejected: it would have to be both admitted and refused under a strict contract, since one realization preserves the declared fold and the other replaces it — the `block_reduce` opacity the adopted scheduled-region model refuses by name.
- **A width-symbolic fixed-width schedule resolved at feasibility.** Rejected on canonical identity (two widths are two programs and must not share identity bytes) and on layering (the tail obligation is intrinsic and target-independent, and a symbolic width moves it into target feasibility or drops it).
- **A literal width for the scalable binding, enumerated per implemented length.** Rejected: it forfeits the one property the binding exists to provide, and the implemented lengths are a range rather than a small set.
- **The kernel IR unchanged, with the backend reading lane facts off the schedule.** Rejected: the kernel verifier proves predicate dominance and bounds-witness provenance over the kernel body, so a body whose real predication lives in the schedule is verified against something it is not.
- **Admitting the CPU worker scope in the same design.** Rejected as unnecessary rather than wrong: nothing in the lane tier raises a cross-instance obligation, and the construct threading actually needs is a profile declaration that already exists.

## Measurement boundary and unsupported cases

- **Nothing here was executed, emitted, compiled, or timed.** Every claim is inspected source at `cb5d86a` or a primary vendor specification. There is no measurement in this record and therefore no measured bound on anything it claims.
- **The per-ISA rows are architectural facts, not host facts.** "AVX provides a fault-suppressing masked load" is a claim about the instruction set. Whether a given host implements the extension, and what its floating-point control state is at the moment a region runs, are runtime observations the scalar CPU vertical already showed belong to the execution context and are re-measured per run rather than cached.
- **Where the ISA claims were read, and one that was refuted.** The Arm claims are from the A-profile A64 ISA (DDI 0602, version 2026-06) and the Architecture Reference Manual (DDI 0487 M.c); the x86 claims from the Intel SDM Volume 2 (325383-085US) and Volume 1 (253665-078US); the RISC-V claims from the unprivileged ISA manual, chapter 9; the IEEE claim from IEEE Std 754-2019 clause 6.3. **Three claims this record originally made were wrong and are corrected in place rather than quietly dropped:** `VMASKMOVPS` is an AVX instruction and not AVX2; SVE's vector length is a *power of two* from 128 to 2048 bits and not any multiple of 128, which was the DDI 0487 H.a wording Arm has since narrowed; and there is no AArch64 "Advanced SIMD always flushes subnormals" rule, that being AArch32's, so the packed-versus-scalar numerical divergence this record first asserted as a class distinction is per-instruction and per-path instead.
- **The IEEE clause is quoted from a copy of a paywalled standard.** IEEE Std 754-2019 is distributed through IEEE Xplore; the text above was read from a hosted PDF carrying the IEEE copyright footer rather than from an authorized distribution point. The quotation is of the primary document, and a reader with Xplore access should confirm clause 6.3 rather than treat this citation as the source of record.
- **`f32` only.** Every numerical derivation — the additive identity, the two-sided `-0.0` argument, the packed/scalar agreement obligation — is stated for IEEE 754 binary32. The identity argument transfers to any IEEE binary format, and the per-ISA realization rows do not: a masked load for 32-bit elements says nothing about 16-bit ones, and `ArithmeticType` exists as a separate key on every honourability row for exactly that reason.
- **One reduction shape.** The examples use a single-axis sum over the trailing axis of a rank-2 input. A multi-axis reduction, a contraction whose contracted index sits at different axes of its two operands, and a reduction over a non-trailing axis each raise coordinate-reconstruction questions this record does not touch.
- **No cost model.** Which of two admissible schedules is faster is not decided anywhere here, and the one place this record touches cost — that the numerically legal fold layout needs gathers — is a statement about what a cost model must be able to price, not an estimate.
- **No threading, no caches, no scalable length.** The worker scope is out by decision, CPU caches remain transparent cost-model levels, and the concrete scalable vector length is deliberately not consumed by any predicate here.
