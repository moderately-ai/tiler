# Preserved primary sources for the measured-feedback tuning-loop design

These records keep the autotuning and adaptive-execution literature behind [the measured-feedback tuning loop](../measured-feedback-tuning-loop.md) reproducible when an upstream URL moves, changes, or disappears. The discipline is the one the [rewrite-search source record](../../region-search/sources/README.md) and the [numerics source record](../../numerics/sources/README.md) established, with one deliberate difference stated up front.

**Every record here is metadata-only with a retrieval fingerprint, and none is vendored.** That is a *deliberate uniform classification and not a licence reading*: no document below has had its licence terms assessed, so no verdict about redistribution has been reached for any of them, and vendoring one without that reading would be exactly the unchecked assertion this discipline exists to prevent. Several are arXiv submissions whose grant is plausibly permissive, and that plausibility is not evidence. [`vendor-the-tuning-loop-primary-sources-after-reading-each-licence`](../../../../tickets/vendor-the-tuning-loop-primary-sources-after-reading-each-licence.md) owns the reading and any subsequent vendoring; until it lands, every row's digest is the identity a re-acquisition is checked against and nothing here claims permission.

**Retrieval date for every reachable record below: 2026-08-07**, by `curl` from the recorded URL on the coordination host, followed by `pdftotext` (Poppler) into the text actually read. The digest in each row is a SHA-256 over the exact retrieved PDF byte stream. Those bytes were read and then discarded; they are not under this directory.

## What these records claim, and what they do not

- **Retrieval fingerprint.** The recorded digest pins the exact byte stream that was read when the design record's claims about that document were checked. It is evidence that a re-acquired copy is or is not the same bytes. It is not permission, and its absence from this directory is deliberate.
- **Quotation.** Every sentence [the design record](../measured-feedback-tuning-loop.md) quotes from one of these documents was read in the extracted text of the exact byte stream digested here, not in an abstract, a summary, or a secondary description. Where the design record could not verify a claim it says so and names the row.
- **Tiler inference.** Every decision, elimination, and mapping drawn from these documents lives in the design record, not here. Preserving a reference does not endorse its conclusions, and a measurement inside a paper stays bounded by that paper's own environment.

## Population boundary

This record closes over every document the design record cites as primary evidence, in four groups matching its own sections. A citation the design record adds later extends this record and restates the boundary here rather than starting a second manifest.

**Tensor-program autotuning** — AutoTVM, Ansor, TVM, and the two Halide autoschedulers. These carry the measured-search loop the design record's *where measurement enters* section is decided against: the measurement budget an operator-level tuner actually spends, the surrogate-model-plus-hardware-batch structure, the transfer mechanism and its stated invariance requirement, and — from the two Halide records read together — the one directly comparable pair of a no-benchmarking analytic scheduler and a learned one with optional ground-truth autotuning.

**General program autotuning** — OpenTuner. It is the group of one that carries a tuning *store* as an explicit architectural component rather than an implementation detail, which is the subject of the design record's identity-discipline section.

**Adaptive execution and parametric plans** — the adaptive-query-processing survey, Kabra and DeWitt's mid-query re-optimization, Ioannidis et al.'s parametric query optimization, Reddy and Haritsa's plan diagrams, Markl et al.'s progressive optimization, and Cole and Graefe's dynamic query evaluation plans. These carry the twenty-year prior art on the shape-transfer question: what a plan's validity region is and how one is actually computed, when an observed parameter invalidates a cached plan, why keeping one plan per parameter point is refused, the measured geometry of how coarse the winner partition actually is, and the measured cost of the one design that ships every potentially optimal plan in a single artifact and picks among them later.

**Benchmarking statistics** — Hoefler and Belli. It is the source of the twelve rules the design record's statistical section is written against, including the two it records itself as failing.

### `markl-pop-sigmod-2004`

- **Document:** Volker Markl, Vijayshankar Raman, David Simmen, Guy Lohman, Hamid Pirahesh, Miso Cilimdzic, "Robust Query Processing through Progressive Optimization", ACM SIGMOD 2004, Paris, France, pp. 659–670. DOI `10.1145/1007568.1007642` (Crossref-verified 2026-08-07: title, container, and page range all match).
- **Retrieved from:** `https://www.cse.iitb.ac.in/infolab/Data/Courses/CS632/2015/2009/Papers/sigmod04-markl.pdf`, 2026-08-07, HTTP 200, plain `curl`, no access barrier of any kind. 12 pages, matching the cited page range.
- **Cited for:** the construction of a validity range, which is the mechanism [the design record's](../measured-feedback-tuning-loop.md) shape-transfer section previously had to take from the [adaptive-query-processing survey's](#aqp-survey-fntdb-2007) description rather than from the paper. The paper defines it and states its direction of conservatism: a validity range for a plan edge is "an upper and lower bound on the number of rows flowing through e, such that if the range is violated at run-time, we can guarantee P is suboptimal with respect to the optimizer's cost model", and "This range is defined conservatively, i.e., even within the validity range P may become suboptimal with respect to alternative QEP we do consider. This conservative definition is fine, since we only want to avoid needless re-optimization." The construction is a by-product of pruning: "POP computes validity ranges during the plan enumeration and pruning phases of the optimizer through a plan sensitivity analysis. It iteratively narrows the validity range for each input to an operator of the currently optimal plans, when pruning alternative plans during optimization." Concretely, when the surviving plan prunes an alternative, "we determine if there exists an input cardinality c > e such that the cost functions cost(Palt, c) and cost(Popt, c) intersect. We do this by solving for the root of cost(Palt , c) – cost(Popt , c) = 0", by a modified Newton-Raphson iteration capped in practice at three steps, because analytic inversion is rejected: "it is not practical to analytically compute the validity ranges because the formulas will have to be continually updated as the code changes." The paper also states the construction's limits — "Our method is limited as it only detects suboptimality of the root operator and Popt and Palt must share the same input edges" — and its advantage over enumerating parametric plans: "we need not enumerate beforehand all possible optimal plans under all possible parameter values – we only need the cardinality ranges within which the chosen plan remains optimal."
- **What it settles, and what it leaves standing.** The design record's Rule 2 requires a Tiler validity region to be *declared* rather than derived and explicitly declines to claim that this matches POP. Having read the paper, that caution was **warranted**, and it now rests on a stated reason rather than on absence of evidence. Three properties of the POP construction do not transfer. First, a POP range is a claim about the *optimizer's own cost model* — the quoted definition says so — so it bounds where the model changes its mind, not where an observation of a machine remains valid; deriving a measured result's validity region from Tiler's analytic model would bound the wrong thing. Second, POP's conservatism runs in the direction that is safe *only because a recovery path exists*: the range is deliberately wider than the true optimality region, and a plan inside its range may already be suboptimal, which costs POP a missed re-optimization and nothing worse. Tiler AOT-compiles one artifact with no re-optimization, so a region wider than the truth means a stored result speaks for subjects it should not — the silently-wrong direction the design record forbids throughout. Third, the derivation consumes the pruned alternatives and their cost functions at the moment of pruning, and Tiler discards alternatives after selection. **No conclusion in the design record changes.** The paper is also the source of the record's earlier characterisation of the survey's account as incomplete, which it was: the survey states that ranges exist, and this paper states how one is computed and how conservative it is.
- **Also read, and not relied upon:** the paper's measured results (TPC-H robustness within a factor of two of optimal; a real-world DMV workload with "cardinality estimation errors ... exceed six orders of magnitude"; "incurring only a negligible overhead of around 2-3% of the overall query execution time for queries not benefiting from POP"; a worst observed regression of a factor of five). These are bounded to that paper's DBMS, schema, and hardware and no Tiler claim rests on them.
- **Reported about a source this record has not read:** the paper states, of [`graefe-ward-sigmod-1989`](#graefe-ward-sigmod-1989--awaiting-retrieval), that because optimizer cost functions "are also not smooth, not even always continuous", "simple binary-search techniques as in [GW89] will not work." That is Markl et al.'s characterisation of Graefe and Ward, not a reading of Graefe and Ward, and it is recorded here only to help whoever retrieves that document.

### `cole-graefe-sigmod-1994`

- **Document:** Richard L. Cole, Goetz Graefe, "Optimization of Dynamic Query Evaluation Plans", ACM SIGMOD 1994, Minneapolis, Minnesota, USA, pp. 150–160. DOI `10.1145/191839.191872` for the proceedings article; the same article also carries `10.1145/191843.191872` as an ACM SIGMOD Record 23(2) item. Both were Crossref-verified 2026-08-07 and resolve to the same title and page range.
- **Retrieved from:** `https://www.csd.uoc.gr/~hy460/pdf/p150-cole.pdf`, 2026-08-07, HTTP 200, plain `curl`, no access barrier of any kind. 11 pages, ending at printed page 160, matching the cited page range.
- **Byte-stream caveat, stated because it bounds what may be quoted.** This PDF is a 1997 scan of the printed proceedings, and `pdftotext` output carries visible OCR damage in places — dropped inter-word spaces, `sec` rendered as `see`, and occasional mangled words. Every sentence quoted below was taken from a passage where the extracted text is unambiguous. Numeric results are reported as measurements rather than quoted verbatim wherever a subscript or unit was damaged.
- **Cited for:** what a run-time `choose-plan` operator actually is and what it costs. The deferral target is start-up time, not arbitrary run time: "The choose-plan operator allows postponement of the choice among two or more equivalent, alternative plans until start-up-time, when the decision can be based on up-to-date knowledge, e.g., the bindings of user variables unbound at compile-time." The enabling concept is compile-time cost *incomparability*: "we acknowledge that missing run-time bindings may render it impossible to calculate and compare the costs of alternative plans at compile-time. If so, alternative plans are only partially ordered by cost (instead of being totally ordered as in traditional query optimization) and we delay the choice between such plans until start-up-time", where costs are modelled as intervals and "two overlapping cost intervals are declared incomparable." The decision procedure is deliberately not an inverted cost function — "it is not easy to build these minimal decision procedures since it requires building inverses for all cost functions", and "In an extensible system, we consider it entirely unrealistic to assume that inverses of cost functions can be provided" — but a re-evaluation of the same cost functions with bindings supplied: "The decision procedure is now merely a cost comparison of the plan alternatives with run-time bindings instantiated; thus, the reasons for incomparability of costs at compile-time have vanished." The guarantee it buys is stated as "Plans not included in a dynamic plan cannot possibly be optimal for any run-time binding of the unknown parameters, and a dynamic plan is guaranteed to include all potentially optimal plans for all run-time bindings", and it requires a shared representation: "all plans and alternative plans must be represented as directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) with common subexpressions, not as trees."
- **Measurement (this paper's, on a DECstation 5000/125).** For its ten-way join with eleven uncertain parameters, "the difference in plan size is 14,090 versus 21 operator nodes"; optimization time rose by under a factor of three ("27.1 sec versus 80.6 sec for query 5"), attributed to "the reduced effectiveness of branch-and-bound pruning"; start-up CPU for that largest dynamic plan was 5.8 s; and break-even against static plans was reached at a single invocation, against run-time optimization at two to four. These are bounded to that prototype and that host.
- **What it settles, and what it leaves standing.** The design record names the choice of the repository's [`AvailabilityPhase`](../../../decisions/0043-use-typed-phased-target-feasibility.md) ladder over a run-time choose-plan operator as the one place a reader should expect the argument to be thinner. Having read the paper, the conclusion **stands, and the argument is now stronger rather than merely unopposed**, for a reason the design record did not have: a choose-plan decision is not a measured-feedback mechanism at all. Its decision procedure re-evaluates the *analytic* cost functions once the missing bindings arrive, so what choose-plan varies is *when a cost-model parameter becomes known* — which is precisely the axis `AvailabilityPhase` already models — and it additionally requires carrying every potentially optimal alternative in the shipped artifact. The paper's own measurement puts a number on that additional requirement: 14,090 operator nodes against 21 for the same query. So the two designs are not peers on one axis with the ladder chosen arbitrarily; they address the same axis, and choose-plan adds an artifact-size and retention obligation on top.
- **Two consequences flagged rather than acted on.** First, the paper's optimality guarantee depends on *retaining* incomparable alternatives through optimization; the design record states that Tiler's alternatives are enumerated, reported in explain, and then discarded, so anything choose-plan-shaped would require the frontier to retain incomparable alternatives and not merely report them. Second, the paper's cost intervals and its "incomparable" comparison verdict are the same construct as a dominance relation that may decline to order two plans, and the measured price it paid for admitting incomparability broadly was eroded branch-and-bound pruning — which is independent corroboration of the design record's [section 0](../measured-feedback-tuning-loop.md#0-the-state-this-design-starts-from) concern that a second cost key entering dominance would make "the non-dominated set ... silently become the whole set".
- **Reported about a source this record has not read:** the paper states that the choose-plan primitive was introduced in [`graefe-ward-sigmod-1989`](#graefe-ward-sigmod-1989--awaiting-retrieval) and that the earlier work "left two all-important questions unanswered, namely how to choose which optimization decisions to delay and how to engineer a query optimizer that efficiently creates dynamic plans for arbitrarily complex queries at compile-time." It also attributes to that earlier work the minimal-decision-procedure proposal it goes on to reject as unrealistic. Both are Cole and Graefe's characterisation of Graefe and Ward, not a reading of it.

- **What changes if it says something different — and why this lookup is low priority.** Almost certainly nothing, and the reason is stated inside a document that *was* read. [`cole-graefe-sigmod-1994`](#cole-graefe-sigmod-1994) is by one of the same authors, continues the same line, and says of this paper that it introduced the choose-plan primitive but "left two all-important questions unanswered, namely how to choose which optimization decisions to delay and how to engineer a query optimizer that efficiently creates dynamic plans for arbitrarily complex queries at compile-time." The 1994 paper is therefore the one that answers the design question, and it has been read in full. What the 1989 paper would add is the primitive's original framing and the minimal-decision-procedure proposal — building inverses of the cost functions — that the 1994 paper explicitly rejects as unrealistic in an extensible system, and that [`markl-pop-sigmod-2004`](#markl-pop-sigmod-2004) separately reports as a binary search that "will not work" against non-smooth cost functions. Both characterisations are secondary; a reading could correct either. **The concrete way this lookup could matter:** if the 1989 minimal decision procedure turns out to be cheaper or more general than its two successors describe, it would be a candidate mechanism for resolving a deferred cost row at a later availability phase without carrying alternatives — which is the one thing that would make the choose-plan line genuinely competitive with the ladder rather than a superset of it. Nothing currently suggests that, and no claim in the design record rests on this document either way.
