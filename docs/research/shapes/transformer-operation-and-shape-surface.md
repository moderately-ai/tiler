# Transformer operation and shape surface derivation

**Status:** durable derivation record for rung L2 of the language-model conformance track. It is a research outcome, not a capability: nothing here registers an operation, admits a key, or authorizes implementation. Every family it names sits where the [operation-family support matrix](../../roadmap.md#operation-family-support-matrix) already places it — at R1 or R2 except the residual addition and the attention scale, which were already at R6 — and this record moves no row.

## What L1 handed over, and what this record adds

**Fact.** L1's traced surface names the per-layer computation at the pinned `transformers` v4.51.0 reference, the six weight shape classes, the nine contractions per layer plus the vocabulary projection, the per-head q/k RMSNorm, the half-split RoPE form, the 8→16 GQA repetition, the SwiGLU MLP, the softmax over the key axis, the tied-embedding gather, and the two varying extents `T` and `S`. Every statement in this record about what the reference computes is that trace's, not a fresh reading of the reference; nothing here asserts a new fact about `modeling_qwen3.py`.

**Inference — the trace is not the derivation, and the gap is the whole of this rung.** A list of family names does not say which names are one semantic operation and which are a shape of graph; it does not say which requirements Tiler can already express; and it does not distinguish a family that needs a new key from one that needs only a key that other work already owes. This record answers those three questions and turns the residue into capability tickets.

## The three contraction index structures

**Inference — derived from L1's traced shapes and the pinned configuration.** Every one of the 253 contraction occurrences has one of exactly three index structures. `T` is the number of new positions, `S` the total context length; `g = 8` key/value groups, `r = 2` repetitions, `d` the contracted index.

| # | Structure | Operand shapes | Result | Occurrences |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `td,od->to` | `[T, D_in] × [D_out, D_in]` | `[T, D_out]` | 197 — seven weighted projections per layer × 28, plus the vocabulary projection |
| 2 | `grtd,gsd->grts` | `[8, 2, T, 128] × [8, S, 128]` | `[8, 2, T, S]` | 28 |
| 3 | `grts,gsd->grtd` | `[8, 2, T, S] × [8, S, 128]` | `[8, 2, T, 128]` | 28 |

The six distinct weight shape classes L1 recorded — `[2048, 1024]`, `[1024, 1024]`, `[1024, 2048]`, `[3072, 1024]`, `[1024, 3072]`, `[151936, 1024]` — all instantiate structure 1. **Inference — six shape classes are one index structure, and conflating the two counts is how the keyed-family question gets answered wrongly.** Milestone 6 reserves whether a contraction is one keyed family carrying an index-structure attribute or a set of fixed-arity keys per shape class. A reader who counts shape classes concludes this workload needs six keys; a reader who counts index structures concludes three. The second is the number that matters, because a key is identity and an extent is not.

**Inference — none of the three is `[M, K] × [K, N]`.** The checkpoint stores every projection weight as `[out_features, in_features]`, so the contracted index is the *last* axis of both operands in structure 1. A fixed matmul key over `[M, K] × [K, N]` therefore requires a transposing `Reindex` on all 197 weighted occurrences, or a pre-transposed copy of all 2.22 GiB of F32 weights. A family carrying an index structure requires neither. This is workload evidence for the reserved question and is deliberately not an answer to it: the question is Tom's under [Q-SEM-015](../../open-questions.md#q-sem-015--tensor-contraction-matmul-batched-matmul-and-einsum), and [`scope-einsum-contraction-support`](../../../tickets/scope-einsum-contraction-support.md) owns it.

**Fact — all three structures pass the framing's structural admission rules.** No output index is absent from every operand; no summed index appears in only one operand (`d` appears in both, in all three); no index repeats within one operand; no output index is duplicated, and each output order is a permutation of that structure's free indices; no index appears in more than two operands. The extent agreements they then require — `D_in` between operands in structure 1, `128` and `S` in structures 2 and 3 — are the ordinary three-outcome shape requirement the [shape environment contract](shape-environment-contract.md) already fixes.

**Inference — structure 1 is weight-stationary and structures 2 and 3 are not.** In structure 1 every extent except `T` is static, so only the output's first axis moves. In structures 2 and 3 the contracted extent (`128` then `S`) differs: structure 3 contracts over the *growing* axis. Only structure 3's reduction extent grows during decode, which is what makes decode-time attention a symbolic-extent reduction rather than a static one.

## Extent classes

**Fact — restated from L1.** Batch is the constant 1. Hidden 1024, intermediate 3072, head dimension 128, head counts 16 and 8, layer count 28, and vocabulary 151,936 are static. Exactly two extents vary: `T` and `S`. Both are bounded per workload row and both sit inside `max_position_embeddings = 32768`.

| Class | Members | Bound | Where it must be expressible |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Static | 1024, 3072, 128, 16, 8, 2, 28, 151936 | literal | everywhere; already expressible |
| Bounded symbolic | `T` | `1 ≤ T ≤ 8192` for the B1 matrix; `T = 10` then `T = 1` for C1 | tensor boundary extents, iteration-domain dimensions |
| Bounded symbolic, growing | `S` | `T ≤ S ≤ 8320` for the B1 matrix; `S ≤ 18` for C1 | tensor boundary extents, and one *reduction* domain dimension in structure 3 |
| Unconstrained dynamic | none | — | not required; the roadmap's deferral stays intact rather than triggered |

**Fact — the semantic layer can already state these bounds, and the public index layer cannot yet carry them.** `crates/tiler-ir/src/shape/env/constraint.rs` implements `ExtentInterval` with `lower` and `upper` and a solver that seeds every symbol at `0..=MAX_EXTENT` and narrows, so a bounded symbolic extent is expressible in `ShapeEnv`. In the index layer, `crates/tiler-ir/src/index/sourced.rs` implements `SourcedExtent` and `SourcedShape` for both iteration dimensions and tensor boundaries, but `DomainDimensionRef::sourced_extent`, `TensorRef::sourced_shape`, and `VerifiedIndexRegion::extent_sources` are all `pub(crate)`, each carrying an `#[allow(dead_code)]` whose `reason` records that the symbolic profile is a crate-internal draft. The doc comments state the consequence exactly: "No public constructor produces a symbolic dimension yet, so every region a public caller can build still answers `Some` for every dimension."

**Inference — the workload's extent requirement is a promotion, not a new capability.** `T` and `S` need the already-implemented sourced-extent profile to reach a public boundary; they do not need semi-affine coefficients, piecewise maps, dynamic rank, or device-produced shapes. [`promote-the-symbolic-index-profile-to-a-public-boundary`](../../../tickets/promote-the-symbolic-index-profile-to-a-public-boundary.md) already owns exactly that promotion, so this record files nothing for it and instead supplies the workload evidence that the promotion is load-bearing: without it, every `T` and every `S` becomes a separate compiled artifact.

**Inference — the KV-cache append needs a symbolic *offset*, and that one is already admitted.** Writing the new `T` rows at position `S − T` of the cache is addition of a parameter-only expression, which [IR](../../ir.md)'s bounded initial index vocabulary explicitly admits alongside negation and multiplication by a parameter-only expression. No semi-affine coefficient and no symbolic divisor appears anywhere in this workload, so [`admit-semi-affine-index-expression-class`](../../../tickets/admit-semi-affine-index-expression-class.md) is not a prerequisite for it.

## No slice and no concatenate is required inside a layer

**Inference.** The corpus defines neither. `Reindex`'s admitted forms are bijective permutations, splits, merges, unit-axis insertion or removal, and the within-axis reversal D-10 admitted; a slice is injective but not surjective, so it is outside them; a concatenate is multi-operand with an output partitioned by operand and is outside `Reindex` and `Broadcast` both. Neither appears as a row on the support matrix, which means neither is even at R2 — no normative contract fixes their obligations.

That absence would be a blocking finding if the workload needed them, and the derivation above establishes that it does not. `rotate_half` reduces to a split, a permutation, a broadcast multiply, and a merge. The head split and merge are bijective reshapes. The GQA repetition is either free or a `Broadcast`. **The one place a concatenate genuinely appears is the KV-cache append**, which crosses the state boundary and belongs to L5. Recording this is the point: the naive reading of the same trace files two capability tickets, and both would be wrong. *Corrected 2026-08-04:* there is no Tiler state boundary for it to cross. Under [`supersede-the-runtime-owned-kv-state-design`](../../../tickets/supersede-the-runtime-owned-kv-state-design.md) the cached `K` and `V` are the consumer's retained tensors, bound as ordinary program inputs, and the append is an ordinary value-producing `Concatenate` at the block boundary — `tiler::concatenate-f32@1`, registered since. The claim this sentence was making survives with its owner corrected, and it is the load-bearing half: the append is the one occurrence *outside* a layer's interior, which is exactly why no slice and no concatenate family is required inside one.

## The causal mask is a feasibility crossover, not a convenience

**Inference.** Two routes exist and they fail at different lengths.

Supplying the additive mask as an F32 program input of shape `[T, S]` needs no family that is not already required: it is one `Broadcast` over the head axes and one `Add`. It costs `T × S × 4` bytes of materialized input, which is 720 bytes at C1 and **268,435,456 bytes at B1-d** (`T = S = 8192` at prefill). L1 records that a materialized-score prefill already reaches 4.00 GiB at that row; a quarter-gibibyte mask on top of it is not the binding term, but it is a transfer and a residency cost that exists only because the mask cannot be derived.

Deriving the mask from iteration coordinates needs a comparison over index expressions and a selection between two values. Neither exists. The index-domain predicate vocabulary [ADR 0084](../../decisions/0084-reference-canonical-index-expressions-from-domain-predicates.md) fixes is the two atoms `NonNegative` and `LessThanExtent`, and the ADR states that it "contains no arbitrary Boolean composition, provider-defined atom, physical guard, runtime check, or proof result" — [IR](../../ir.md) renders the same rule as "no Boolean escape hatch". The support matrix places a tensor `Select` at R1 with no key, and its own trigger records that closure "needs an admitted predicate value type, which does not exist because the registry admits no boolean dtype". `crates/tiler-ir/src/kernel/model.rs` carries `CompareOp` in the structured-kernel vocabulary but no selection construct over F32 values.

**Inference — the two routes are not a preference.** The input route is correct and expressible at every row this workload declares, so nothing is blocked today. It is a materialization that grows as `T × S` against a program whose other terms grow as `T` or `S`, which means the crossover is real and reachable, not hypothetical. That makes the predicate-and-select family a tracked capability with a stated activation trigger rather than an omission. It is not filed here: L3′ owns masking by name, and this crossover is the evidence handed to it below.

## Typed refusals

Each unsupported case below must reject with a typed, explainable diagnostic rather than approximate. They are stated as refusals because the ticket requires it and because every one of them is a place where a silent approximation would return a plausible tensor.

- An extent symbol with no proved upper bound refuses rather than compiling a generic program. `ExtentInterval::states_no_upper_bound` already exists for exactly this, and its doc comment records that the remedy "is to state a constraint rather than to retry".
- A context length beyond the checkpoint's declared 32,768 positions refuses. The tokenizer's `model_max_length: 131072` is not the position budget, and L1 records the disagreement rather than resolving it silently.
- A contraction occurrence with no resolvable index-access lowering capability refuses at resolution, which is the current behaviour and must stay the current behaviour: `resolve_lowering` is on the ordinary `compile()` path and a program whose occurrences no installed capability covers fails closed.
- A malformed contraction index structure refuses at construction, by each of the five named structural rules separately, so the diagnostic says which rule and not merely that the structure is invalid.
- A token ID outside `0..151936` refuses or is validated at a named enforcement boundary; it never reads out of bounds and never clamps.
- A reduction whose contributor order, reassociation, or permutation permission is not established refuses rather than selecting a faster tree. [Reduction semantics and legality](../numerics/reduction-semantics-and-legality.md) fixes that permission is necessary and never sufficient, and that the reducer's own algebraic capability is the second half.
- A target that does not honour the effective numerical realization refuses. ADR 0076 forbids any authority from narrowing, weakening, or substituting the caller's stated contract to make a plan feasible, and the qualified Apple9/F32 row's subnormal flush is a declared realization rather than a tolerance.

## What this record does not decide

- **Whether a semantic contraction node may consume more than two operands.** Also reserved. This workload contains no contraction with more than two operands, so it supplies no evidence either way, and saying so is more useful than inventing pressure.

## Consequences for the ladder

**Inference.** L3's activation trigger — "L2 lists the contraction shapes, and milestone 6 settles the keyed-family question" — is now half satisfied: the shapes are listed above, and the keyed-family question remains Tom's. What L2 delivers is a derivation record, in the same sense that L1 delivered a workload record: a research outcome, to which the four-claim maturity vocabulary does not apply.
